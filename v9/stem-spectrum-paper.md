## Abstract

Covariant observables in causal-set sequential growth (CSG) were characterized by Brightwell–Dowker–García–Henson–Sorkin (BDGHS): the σ-algebra of relabeling-invariant events on the space of completed causal sets is generated, *up to sets of measure zero*, by **stem events** — questions of the form "does the finite causet b occur as an ancestor-closed sub-causet?" The measure-zero caveat is unavoidable: "rogue" pairs of distinct completed causets realize identical stems. We point out an identification that, to our knowledge, appears nowhere in the causal-set or duality literature: the labeled history space is a **light profinite set** (a countable inverse limit of finite sets), and the Boolean algebra generated by stem events is a countable algebra whose **Stone spectrum X** — the *stem spectrum* — is again light profinite. Under the canonical evaluation map φ from histories to X: (i) fibers are exactly the stem-equivalence classes, so the BDGHS rogue phenomenon *is* the non-injectivity of φ — and BDGHS's own exact characterization of the rogues (Θ = Γ, the causets with a level containing infinitely many non-maximal elements) becomes an exact fiber theorem: the union of the non-trivial fibers is a named Borel set, null for every dynamics of the generalized-percolation family, over which the fibers elsewhere are single relabeling classes — while in a known deterministic degenerate model that set carries *full* measure even though the generation theorem still holds there, so nullity and generation genuinely come apart; (ii) for the dynamics BDGHS cover, their generation theorem becomes a **measure-algebra isomorphism** between the covariant algebra mod null and the Borel algebra of X mod null; (iii) by compactness of X, *every* bounded finitely-additive state on the stem algebra extends uniquely to a Radon measure on X — for scalar charges the extension problem dissolves on the spectrum, exactly as it already does on the cylinder algebra (Surya–Zalel's bounded-variation criterion is the quantitative form of the same compactness), and for decoherence functionals (Dowker–Johnston–Surya) the surviving obstruction is strong additivity of the associated vector measure (Carathéodory–Hahn–Kluvánek), with bounded variation its scalar shadow: the problem is relocated — not cured — into classical vector-measure theory on one compact zero-dimensional space, now for the *covariant* algebra.

(iv) The distributive-lattice (Priestley) version realizes the spectrum as a profinite *poset* in the sense of Speed, and the covtree of Dowker–Imambaccus–Owens–Sorkin–Zalel — whose level-n nodes are the realizable families of n-element stems — is precisely the tower of finite quotients of the spectrum: an elementary extension lemma makes exact-rank and cumulative stem signatures interchangeable, the level-n nodes are in canonical bijection with the atoms of the rank-≤n stem algebra, and covtree's own order map is the algebraic refinement map, so X is the inverse limit of the covtree levels along covtree's own order — the space of infinite covtree paths — and φ is *surjective* by the published path-certificate theorem: the spectrum has no ghost points. The receipts confirm the published level-3 census mechanically (22 nodes; the node realizing all five 3-orders has seven-element minimal certificates) and record that finite proto-rogues (distinct n-causets sharing all proper stems) are abundant: 5, 11, 37 pairs at n = 4, 5, 6. The results are assemblies of classical duality theory (Stone 1936; Priestley 1970; Speed 1972) with the published CSG theorems; their value is structural: the "up to measure zero" of covariant causal-set physics is the geometry of one compact, totally disconnected space.

## 1. Setting and imports

**CSG and the history space.** Following BDGHS [1], a *naturally labeled causet* on {0,…,n−1} is a partial order in which i ≺ j implies i < j; sequential growth adds element n above a down-set of the existing order. Let Ω̃(n) denote the (finite) set of naturally labeled n-causets and let e_n : Ω̃(n+1) → Ω̃(n) delete the top-labeled element. The *labeled history space* is the inverse limit Ω̃ = lim← (Ω̃(n), e_n), whose elements are completed labeled causets; the *cylinder* [b̃] ⊆ Ω̃ is the set of histories with prefix b̃ ∈ Ω̃(n). BDGHS take R̃ = σ(cylinders) and define the covariant algebra R ⊆ R̃ as the relabeling-invariant events. A *stem* of a causet is a finite down-set (ancestor-closed subset), taken up to isomorphism; the *stem event* is stem(b) = {c ∈ Ω̃ : b embeds as a stem of c}. **The covered dynamics classes.** BDGHS prove their theorems for classes they themselves delimit [1, §2 and §7], and the two theorems have *different* scopes. Write **P** for the *generalized percolation* family of Rideout–Sorkin CSG dynamics — those with coupling t_k > 0 for some k ≥ 2 — and **P⁺** for P together with the two remaining degenerate families, which are deterministic in the sense that the unlabeled history is almost surely a single fixed causet (only t₀ > 0: the infinite antichain; only t₀, t₁ > 0). Coverage of any further non-generic random models is BDGHS's stated conjecture ([1] §7). With that fixed: BDGHS prove [1] that stem events are countable unions of cylinders; **(BDGHS Theorem 1)** the stem events generate R up to measure zero, precisely **(BDGHS Corollary 1)**: for every A ∈ R there is B ∈ R(S) := σ(stem events) with μ(A △ B) = 0 — for P, with a single B serving all μ ∈ P simultaneously (the strong quantifier order, genuine because A △ B sits inside the rogue set); for the deterministic models of P⁺, per model and trivially (every covariant event is μ-equivalent to ∅ or everything). The inclusion R(S) ⊊ R is strict, witnessed by *rogue* pairs — non-isomorphic completed causets with identical stem sets. BDGHS in fact characterize the rogues *exactly* ([1] §4, Lemmas 1–2): writing Θ for the set of all rogues and Γ for the set of causets having a level with infinitely many non-maximal elements, **Θ = Γ**, and **(BDGHS Proposition 1)** μ(Θ) = 0 for μ ∈ P — the Borel–Cantelli step of their proof is where t_k > 0 for some k ≥ 2 enters. The scope split is not pedantry: *nullity and generation come apart at the seam*. In the deterministic t₀,t₁ model the rogue set has full measure (§4), so the clause in BDGHS's own §7 — that the remaining models "either are deterministic or preclude infinite antichains, whence the rogues would be of measure zero" — fails for the deterministic disjunct, even as the generation statement it was invoked to support survives there. Accordingly, nullity-type statements below (Theorem 3(iii)) are asserted for P, and generation-type statements (Theorem 4) for P⁺. Dowker–Johnston–Surya [2] prove that *quantum* measures (decoherence functionals) on the cylinder algebra need not extend to R̃: "the decoherence functional, and hence the quantum measure, extends if and only if the associated vector measure does," and a quantum sequential growth model witnesses failure; Surya–Zalel [10] give the exact scalar-side criterion (a complex measure on the cylinder event algebra extends iff it has bounded variation). Covtree [4] — introduced by Dowker–Imambaccus–Owens–Sorkin–Zalel — re-founds covariant dynamics directly on finite families of stems: its level-n nodes are the realizable sets of n-element stems.

**Duality background.** Stone duality: countable Boolean algebras ↔ second-countable Stone spaces (compact, Hausdorff, totally disconnected) — i.e., **light profinite sets**, the countable-limit site of Clausen–Scholze's light condensed mathematics [7] (we use only the identification, none of the condensed machinery). Priestley duality [5]: bounded distributive lattices ↔ Priestley spaces; Speed [6]: the Priestley spaces are exactly the profinite posets (inverse limits of finite posets). Birkhoff: finite distributive lattices ↔ finite posets via join-irreducibles/down-sets.

## 2. The history space is light profinite (T1)

**Theorem 1.** Ω̃ = lim← Ω̃(n) with the inverse-limit topology is a light profinite set; its clopen algebra is the algebra of finite unions of cylinders; its Borel σ-algebra is R̃.

*Proof.* Each Ω̃(n) is finite and discrete and the tower is countable, so the limit is a countable inverse limit of finite sets: compact, Hausdorff, totally disconnected, second countable — light profinite by definition. Cylinders are the preimages of points under the projections Ω̃ → Ω̃(n); they form a clopen basis, and every clopen set, being compact and open, is a finite union of basis elements. Hence Clopen(Ω̃) = finite unions of cylinders, and σ(Clopen) = σ(cylinders) = R̃; on a second-countable space *with a clopen basis* (zero-dimensional, as here), σ(clopens) contains every open set and hence is the Borel algebra. ∎

Nothing here is difficult; the point is the identification. In the CSG literature the space carries only its measure-theoretic description; the words "profinite", "Stone", "totally disconnected" do not occur (checked against [1–4] and the surrounding literature). Every construction below flows from taking the topology seriously.

## 3. The stem spectrum (T2)

Let **B** ⊆ P(Ω̃) be the Boolean algebra of subsets generated by the stem events {stem(b)}. Since stems are finite posets up to isomorphism, B is countable. Let **X = Stone(B)**: the space of Boolean homomorphisms B → 2 (equivalently ultrafilters of B), with the topology generated by â = {u : u(a) = 1}. Define the **evaluation map** φ : Ω̃ → X by φ(c)(a) = 1 iff c ∈ a.

**Theorem 2.** X is a light profinite set. φ is Borel measurable (not, in general, continuous), φ⁻¹(Clopen(X)) = B, and σ(stem events) = φ⁻¹(Borel(X)).

*Proof.* B countable gives X second countable, hence light profinite. Each c ∈ Ω̃ evaluates the concrete algebra B ⊆ P(Ω̃) to {0,1} preserving Boolean operations, so φ(c) ∈ X is well defined; φ⁻¹(â) = a for a ∈ B by construction, giving φ⁻¹(Clopen(X)) = B (the clopens of X are exactly the â). Stem events are countable unions of cylinders [1], hence Borel in Ω̃; Boolean combinations preserve Borelness, so B ⊆ Borel(Ω̃) and φ is Borel. Since X is second countable and zero-dimensional, Borel(X) = σ(Clopen(X)), whence φ⁻¹(Borel X) = σ(B) = σ(stem events). Continuity fails in general because complements of stem events need not be open in Ω̃: no finite stage of a history can certify that a stem is *absent* (elements added later may complete it), so stem(b) is open but not closed — explicitly, the infinite antichain realizes no 2-chain stem, yet every one of its finite stages has extensions that do, so the antichain lies in the closure of stem(2-chain). ∎

Two remarks. First, φ factors through relabeling (stems are isomorphism-invariant), so everything below is automatically covariant. Second, X is *not* a topological quotient of Ω̃: it is the measurable spectrum of the covariant question algebra. That is a feature — it is the space the *observables* see. (It is nonetheless fully realized: φ is surjective, by the corollary in §7, so X carries no idealized points beyond the actual histories.)

## 4. Rogues are the fibers (T3)

Call a completed causet a *rogue* if some non-isomorphic completed causet realizes exactly the same stems [1]. Recall from §1 BDGHS's exact characterization: Θ (all rogues) = Γ (some level contains infinitely many non-maximal elements), where the *level* of an element x is the maximum length of a chain with top element x ([1]'s convention) — a quantity determined by any finite stage containing x's past. Write Γ̃ ⊆ Ω̃ for the labeled copy of Γ.

**Theorem 3 (the exact fiber theorem).** (i) φ(c) = φ(c′) iff c and c′ realize the same set of stems. (ii) The union of the fibers of φ containing more than one relabeling class is exactly Γ̃, a Borel subset of Ω̃; over non-rogues, every fiber is a single relabeling class. (iii) μ(Γ̃) = 0 for every μ ∈ P (BDGHS Proposition 1); by contrast μ(Γ̃) = 1 in the deterministic t₀,t₁-only model (in P⁺ ∖ P), and μ(Γ̃) = 0 trivially in the t₀-only (dust) model. (iv) No covariant event separates two histories in a common fiber except on a null set: for A ∈ R and c, c′ with φ(c) = φ(c′), any B ∈ R(S) with μ(A △ B) = 0 satisfies 1_B(c) = 1_B(c′).

*Proof.* (i) Two homomorphisms on B agree iff they agree on the generators stem(b). (ii) A fiber contains two relabeling classes iff it contains a rogue, and BDGHS Lemma 2 ([1] §4: outside Γ, stem-equivalence implies isomorphism) gives both halves: fibers over Γ̃ are non-trivial (Lemma 1 supplies the stem-equivalent partner) and fibers off Γ̃ are single relabeling classes. Borel-ness: "element i has level k" is decided by any cylinder fixing the past of i (clopen); "element i is non-maximal" is the countable union over j of the cylinder-decided events i ≺ j (open); "level k contains infinitely many non-maximal elements" is a countable lim-sup of these, and Γ̃ is the countable union over k — Borel at low finite rank. (iii) The first clause is [1] Proposition 1 (their proof runs through Θ ⊆ {some level is infinite} and Borel–Cantelli, which is where t_k > 0 for some k ≥ 2 enters). For the t₀,t₁-only model, the a.s. history is an infinite disjoint union of trees whose level 0 is infinite with all its elements non-maximal, so Γ̃ has full measure; for the t₀-only model the history is a.s. the infinite antichain, whose every element is maximal — not in Γ, and indeed stem-determined (its stems are exactly the finite antichains, and any order relation anywhere would create a 2-chain stem). (iv) B ∈ R(S) = σ(stem events) cannot separate stem-equivalent histories (the class of sets that cannot separate them is a σ-algebra containing the generators); the rest is BDGHS Corollary 1. ∎

So the non-injectivity of φ is not merely exemplified by BDGHS's rogue families — it is *exactly located*: one named Borel set, null throughout P, and of full measure in a deterministic model of P⁺ where generation nevertheless holds — the nullity hypothesis is load-bearing and separates cleanly from the generation hypothesis (§1's seam). The receipts add a finite-size fact worth recording: **proto-rogues are abundant** — distinct unlabeled n-causets sharing *all proper stems* (every stem of size ≤ n−1) number 5, 11, and 37 pairs at n = 4, 5, 6 respectively (r2), so the phenomenon behind the measure-zero caveat is visible at every finite size, not an artifact of the infinite limit. What the spectrum adds to [1] is *location*: the "up to measure zero" is not an analytic accident scattered through the σ-algebra; it is carried entirely by the failure of one map to be injective, on one compact space.

## 5. The measure-algebra isomorphism (T4)

**Theorem 4.** Let μ be a dynamics of P⁺ and ν = φ∗(μ|_{σ(B)}) its pushforward to X. Then E ↦ φ⁻¹(E) induces an isomorphism of measure algebras
Borel(X) / ν-null  ≅  R / μ-null.

*Proof.* The map is a Boolean homomorphism commuting with countable operations, and ν(E) = μ(φ⁻¹E) makes it well defined and injective on classes (ν(E △ E′) = μ(φ⁻¹E △ φ⁻¹E′)). Its image is σ(B) mod null by Theorem 2; BDGHS Corollary 1 says every class of R mod μ-null contains a representative in σ(stem events) = σ(B) — for the deterministic models of P⁺ trivially, every covariant event being μ-equivalent to ∅ or Ω̃ — so the image is all of R mod μ-null. ∎

*Remark (the hypothesis is load-bearing).* Theorem 4 is not a fact about arbitrary probability measures on (Ω̃, R̃): take a rogue pair c ≇ c′ (Theorem 3) and let μ = ½(δ_c̃ + δ_c̃′) for fixed labelings. Every B ∈ σ(stem events) satisfies 1_B(c̃) = 1_B(c̃′) (Theorem 3(iv)'s separation argument), so the covariant event "relabelings of c," which μ weights ½, differs from every such B on a set of measure ≥ ½ — surjectivity fails. The isomorphism inherits exactly the scope of BDGHS's dynamical input, which is what excludes such measures: rogues are null throughout P, and the deterministic models of P⁺ concentrate on a single unlabeled history rather than splitting mass across a rogue pair.

This is BDGHS Theorem 1 *restructured*: not "stems generate, with an unavoidable caveat," but "the covariant measure algebra of every dynamics of P⁺ **is** the measure algebra of one fixed compact zero-dimensional space." All these dynamics live on the same spectrum; only the measure moves.

## 6. Automatic extension on the spectrum, and where the quantum obstruction goes (N3)

**Theorem 5.** Every bounded finitely-additive charge m : B → ℝ or ℂ extends uniquely to a (complex) Radon measure on X. (For scalar charges on a Boolean algebra, boundedness and bounded variation are equivalent — |m|(1) ≤ 4·sup|m| — so no separate variation hypothesis is needed.) In particular every finitely-additive *state* on the stem algebra is a Radon probability measure on the stem spectrum.

*Proof.* Transport m to the clopen algebra of X via Stone. On a compact space, a countable disjoint family of clopens covering a clopen set is finite (compactness of the covered set; each part is open), so finite additivity on Clopen(X) *implies* countable additivity there. Carathéodory extension (applied to the positive part, or to a complex charge via its total-variation decomposition) yields a unique countably-additive extension to σ(Clopen) = Borel(X); on a compact metrizable space Borel measures are Radon. Uniqueness follows from the π-system generated by clopens. ∎

**An honest accounting of what is new here.** By Theorem 1, the *cylinder* algebra is already the clopen algebra of the compact space Ω̃ — so the vacuous-σ-additivity trick above was always available on the original algebra, and indeed it is the topological content of Surya–Zalel's extension criterion [10, Thm 2.1]: a complex measure on the cylinder event algebra extends to the generated σ-algebra **iff it has bounded variation** (their proof runs through the nesting/finest-partition property of cylinders, which is this compactness in algebraic garb). What the spectrum adds is not the trick but its *reach*: on X the **covariant** algebra B becomes clopen, so the same dissolution now applies to covariant states — the algebra BDGHS showed carries the physics.

**The quantum relocation (stated with care).** Dowker–Johnston–Surya's obstruction [2] is that a decoherence functional D on the cylinder algebra may fail to extend σ-additively; their theorem traces the failure to the associated (Hilbert-space-valued) vector measure. For vector measures the exact extension condition is not bounded variation but **strong additivity**: a bounded vector measure on a field, vacuously weakly countably additive as here, extends to the generated σ-field iff it is strongly additive (the Carathéodory–Hahn–Kluvánek theorem [14]; Surya–Zalel state and use it for this very problem as their Theorem B.1). Bounded variation is the scalar specialization — sufficient in general, and equivalent in finite dimensions, which is the setting of DJS's variation analysis [2]; in infinite dimensions DJS themselves note a weaker condition must take its place. On the stem spectrum the situation is now structural: any decoherence-functional-like bi-charge *defined on B × B* is automatically countably additive on clopens, and its extension question is exactly classical strong additivity on a compact zero-dimensional space. (We do not claim the cylinder-algebra problem transports to B: stem events are countable unions of cylinders, so B and the cylinder algebra are incomparable, and a decoherence functional on cylinders need not restrict to B without assuming the very extension at issue. The covariant extension problem is the *analogue*, not a translation.) The spectrum does not cure the obstruction; it strips away the σ-additivity half and leaves the failure named in textbook terms — strong additivity of a vector measure — on a space where the classical theory directly applies. Whether a given growth dynamics' decoherence functional is strongly additive on B is a well-posed question per dynamics; Sorkin's canonical-sequence extension [3] is the constructive tool where extension fails.

## 7. The ordered version and covtree (N4)

Let **L** be the bounded distributive lattice generated by the stem events under union and intersection (no complements). Its Priestley dual X_≤ is a Priestley space, and since L is countable, X_≤ is — by Speed's theorem [6] — a **profinite poset**: a countable inverse limit of finite posets, i.e., a light profinite poset. Its order is inclusion of prime filters: informally, one idealized stem-theory lies below another when every stem-question the first affirms, the second affirms. At each finite truncation (stems of size ≤ k, evaluated on n-element causets) the lattice is finite and Birkhoff duality is effective; the receipts verify the machinery mechanically (r3) and compute the finite stages.

**The tower correspondence.** Covtree [4] is the growth structure whose level-n nodes are the realizable sets of n-element stems. Two elementary facts make its relation to the spectrum exact.

**Lemma (extension).** Let c be a causet with at least k elements and b a stem of c of size j < k. Then b extends to a stem of c of size j + 1, hence to one of size k. Consequently, for causets with ≥ k elements — in particular for every completed history — *the exact-size-k stem signature determines the full size-≤ k signature*: b is a stem of c iff b is a stem of some k-stem of c (stems of stems are stems).

*Proof.* c ∖ b is non-empty; pick y ∈ c ∖ b. The set past(y) ∩ (c ∖ b) is finite (local finiteness); if empty take x = y, else take x minimal in it. Then every element below x lies in b, so b ∪ {x} is a down-set of size j + 1. Iterate. ∎

**Theorem 6 (covtree levels are the finite quotients of the spectrum).** For each n let B_n ⊆ B be the finite subalgebra generated by the stem events of size ≤ n. Then the map sending a history to its set of exact-n stems induces a canonical bijection between the non-empty atoms of B_n (as subsets of Ω̃) and the level-n nodes of covtree; under it, the atom-refinement maps Spec(B_{n+1}) → Spec(B_n) are covtree's own order map 𝒪₋, defined in [4] by 𝒪₋(Γ_n) := {B : B is an (n−1)-stem of some A ∈ Γ_n}. Consequently X = Stone(B) = lim← Spec(B_n) exhibits the stem spectrum as **the inverse limit of covtree along its own order — the space of infinite covtree paths.**

*Proof.* By the extension lemma, the atoms of B_n on Ω̃ — the classes of histories with a common size-≤ n signature — are exactly the classes with a common exact-n signature. Every history's exact-n set is a covtree node: a history has finitely many n-stems (there are finitely many n-orders), each realized at some finite stage, so some finite stage realizes them all, and that stage's n-stems are precisely the history's (stems of a stage are stems of the history). Conversely every level-n node is realized by a history: take a finite certificate c₀ of size ≥ n (nodes are certified by causets, and a node with a certificate has a finite one [4]) and extend it to a history by repeatedly adding a new element above *all* existing elements — each addition creates no new stem of size ≤ n (a down-set containing the new element contains its entire past, of size > n) — so the resulting history's exact-n set is the node's. Distinct nodes give distinct atoms by the determination clause of the lemma. The refinement maps: for a history c, every n-stem of c extends to an (n+1)-stem of c (the lemma), i.e., is an n-stem of a member of its exact-(n+1) set Q′, and conversely every n-stem of a member of Q′ is an n-stem of c — so the atom of Q′ refines the atom of ⋃_{q ∈ Q′}{n-stems of q}, which is exactly 𝒪₋(Q′) as defined in [4]. B = ⋃ B_n gives X = lim← Spec(B_n) by Stone duality for directed unions. ∎

**Corollary (φ is surjective).** Every infinite covtree path has a certificate — a completed causet realizing exactly its tower of nodes ([4]; Zalel [4, §2.3]) — and covtree's completed causets are the countable past-finite orders, which admit type-ω natural labelings, i.e., points of Ω̃. In spectral terms: every point of X is φ of some history. Together with Theorem 3, φ maps Ω̃ *onto* X with fibers the stem-equivalence classes: the spectrum has no ghost points, and X = φ(Ω̃) exactly.

Two published facts should be positioned here. Zalel records ([4], fn. 4) that the certificate events cert(Γ_n) generate the same σ-algebra as the stem events; in the present language the cert-events *are* the B_n-atoms read as events, and Theorem 6 is the structural form of that observation. And DIOSZ compute covtree level 3 exactly ([4], Fig. 10 and App. B.1): **22 nodes** of the 31 candidate stem-families, with a certificate for each and the nine non-nodes identified; their Figs. 7–8 exhibit minimal certificates — of seven and eight elements — for the node realizing *all five* 3-orders. The receipts confirm this census mechanically and independently (§8): all 96,428 labeled 7-causets enumerated and independently recounted yield exactly 22 exact-3 signatures, the last being the all-five node with a seven-element witness, and an interface state machine over minimal-element interface states, cross-validated exactly against the brute-force census, finds nothing further through twelve elements (the witness bounds of Gutzeit–Shaban–Yeats–Zalel [4] give a second completeness route). Within six-element causets only 21 of the 22 signatures are visible (r5) — a concrete reminder that fixed-size censuses undercount nodes. The determination of the cumulative theory by the exact-rank signature is not an empirical coincidence but the extension lemma (mechanized at n ≤ 7, rB2), so the identification of covtree's exact-rank slices with the spectrum's cumulative tower holds at *every* rank by Theorem 6.

One consequence worth stating for the dynamics program: DIOSZ construct covariant dynamics as walks on covtree, inducing measures on σ(cert events) = σ(stem events); composed with Theorem 5, every such dynamics *is* a Radon measure on X. Covtree dynamics are, canonically, measures on the stem spectrum.

## 9. Related work

BDGHS [1] and the companion problem-of-time paper are the foundation; this paper adds no new dynamics and changes none of their theorems — it changes the *category* they are stated in. Dowker–Surya [11] is the direct continuation of [1], extending the observables analysis to a wider class of percolation-type models; our P/P⁺ scoping follows [1] and should be read alongside it. Covtree [4] is the closest active line: a stem-first combinatorial dynamics; §7 gives it a spectral home (Theorem 6: the stem spectrum is the space of infinite covtree paths), its published level-3 census is confirmed mechanically by our receipts, and the witness-size bounds of the 2026 follow-up [4] supply an independent completeness route. Surya–Zalel's extension criterion [10] is, in the light of Theorem 1, the compactness of the history space expressed measure-theoretically — §6 credits and builds on it. Brightwell–Łuczak [12] study order-invariant measures on infinite causets from the random-orders side — the same covariant σ-algebra approached from exchangeability — and are the natural interlocutor for the measure-geometry questions of §10(b); the observables analysis has also been extended to cyclic cosmologies [13]. Martin–Panangaden [8] reconstruct globally hyperbolic spacetimes from countable *causally dense* suborders via domain theory — an adjacent program whose density hypothesis locally finite orders never satisfy; the ideal completion of the stem system (a Scott domain, by the configurations-form-a-domain theorem of event-structure theory [9]) is the natural meeting point, and we regard "for μ-almost-every growth history, the completion of its interval basis is a manifold" as the right form of their closing open question for genuinely discrete orders. Light condensed mathematics [7] supplies the name of the category in which both the history space and its spectrum live; we deliberately use only the identification. The p-adic/adelic tradition (Volovich 1987 and successors) shares the intuition of total disconnectedness at the fundamental level but derives no Lorentzian structure from it; here causal order is primitive and the disconnected object is the *history space*, not spacetime.

## 10. Outlook

Four directions. (a) *The extension question per dynamics*: Surya–Zalel [10] exhibit a large family of complex CSG dynamics whose measures extend on the cylinder σ-algebra, and any such extension restricts to σ(B) ⊆ σ(cylinders) — for that family the covariant extension question is already answered affirmatively by restriction. The genuinely open, receipt-sized question §6 isolates is the gap: is there a growth dynamics whose decoherence functional fails strong additivity on the cylinder algebra (hence admits no labeled σ-extension, by Carathéodory–Hahn–Kluvánek [14]) yet is strongly additive on the coarser stem algebra B — a dynamics with no labeled σ-extension but a covariant one? (b) *The measure-geometry of X*: CSG dynamics = Radon measures on one fixed space; comparisons between dynamics (transitive percolation vs. others) become measure-theoretic geometry on X. (c) *The level-4 census*: covtree's level 3 is computed in [4] and confirmed here; level 4 appears in no literature we know. The interface-machine method of rB3 extends — a richer interface state, the same monotone-signature design — with the witness bounds of [4] capping the search: a finite, well-posed computation whose output would be the next stage of the spectrum's tower. (d) For the host program that motivated this note: record webs are CSG-class growth processes, their down-set machinery is L, and the bridge from finite record combinatorics to continuum physics can now be phrased as: which Radon measures on the stem spectrum concentrate on histories whose stem-ideal completions are manifolds — the probabilistic form of Martin–Panangaden's question, with volume supplied by counting.
