## Abstract

Covariant observables in causal-set sequential growth (CSG) were characterized by Brightwell–Dowker–García–Henson–Sorkin (BDGHS): the σ-algebra of relabeling-invariant events on the space of completed causal sets is generated, *up to sets of measure zero*, by **stem events** — questions of the form "does the finite causet b occur as an ancestor-closed sub-causet?" The measure-zero caveat is unavoidable: "rogue" pairs of distinct completed causets realize identical stems. We point out an identification that, to our knowledge, appears nowhere in the causal-set or duality literature: the labeled history space is a **light profinite set** (a countable inverse limit of finite sets), and the Boolean algebra generated by stem events is a countable algebra whose **Stone spectrum X** — the *stem spectrum* — is again light profinite. Under the canonical evaluation map φ from histories to X: (i) fibers are exactly the stem-equivalence classes, so the BDGHS rogue phenomenon *is* the non-injectivity of φ — named, localized, and (by BDGHS) invisible to every CSG dynamics; (ii) BDGHS's generation theorem becomes a **measure-algebra isomorphism** between the covariant algebra mod null and the Borel algebra of X mod null, for every CSG dynamics; (iii) by compactness of X, *every* bounded finitely-additive state on the stem algebra extends uniquely to a Radon measure on X — the countable-additivity half of the well-known quantum-measure extension problem (Dowker–Johnston–Surya) dissolves on the spectrum, and the surviving obstruction is exactly classical bounded variation for complex charges, relocating the problem into textbook vector-measure theory; (iv) the distributive-lattice (Priestley) version realizes the spectrum as a profinite *poset* in the sense of Speed, whose finite stages are computable — we exhibit them mechanically to six elements and compute their relation to the recently introduced covtree of Surya–Zalel, whose nodes are finite families of stems — at the computed sizes the two coincide — and we record that finite proto-rogues (distinct n-causets sharing all proper stems) are abundant: 5, 11, 37 pairs at n = 4, 5, 6. The results are assemblies of classical duality theory (Stone 1936; Priestley 1970; Speed 1972) with the published CSG theorems; their value is structural: the "up to measure zero" of covariant causal-set physics is the geometry of one compact, totally disconnected space.

## 1. Setting and imports

**CSG and the history space.** Following BDGHS [1], a *naturally labeled causet* on {0,…,n−1} is a partial order in which i ≺ j implies i < j; sequential growth adds element n above a down-set of the existing order. Let Ω̃(n) denote the (finite) set of naturally labeled n-causets and let e_n : Ω̃(n+1) → Ω̃(n) delete the top-labeled element. The *labeled history space* is the inverse limit Ω̃ = lim← (Ω̃(n), e_n), whose elements are completed labeled causets; the *cylinder* [b̃] ⊆ Ω̃ is the set of histories with prefix b̃ ∈ Ω̃(n). BDGHS take R̃ = σ(cylinders) and define the covariant algebra R ⊆ R̃ as the relabeling-invariant events. A *stem* of a causet is a finite down-set (ancestor-closed subset), taken up to isomorphism; the *stem event* is stem(b) = {c ∈ Ω̃ : b embeds as a stem of c}. BDGHS prove [1]: stem events are countable unions of cylinders; **(BDGHS Theorem 1)** the stem events generate R up to measure zero in every CSG dynamics, precisely **(BDGHS Corollary 1)**: for every A ∈ R there is B ∈ R(S) := σ(stem events) with μ(A △ B) = 0 for every CSG measure μ; the inclusion R(S) ⊊ R is strict, witnessed by *rogue* pairs — non-isomorphic completed causets with identical stem sets — and **(BDGHS Proposition 1)** their exhibited rogue family is μ-null in every CSG dynamics. Dowker–Johnston–Surya [2] prove that *quantum* measures (decoherence functionals) on the cylinder algebra need not extend to R̃: "the decoherence functional, and hence the quantum measure, extends if and only if the associated vector measure does," and a quantum sequential growth model witnesses failure. Surya–Zalel's covtree [4] re-founds covariant dynamics directly on finite families of stems.

**Duality background.** Stone duality: countable Boolean algebras ↔ second-countable Stone spaces (compact, Hausdorff, totally disconnected) — i.e., **light profinite sets**, the countable-limit site of Clausen–Scholze's light condensed mathematics [7] (we use only the identification, none of the condensed machinery). Priestley duality [5]: bounded distributive lattices ↔ Priestley spaces; Speed [6]: the Priestley spaces are exactly the profinite posets (inverse limits of finite posets). Birkhoff: finite distributive lattices ↔ finite posets via join-irreducibles/down-sets.

## 2. The history space is light profinite (T1)

**Theorem 1.** Ω̃ = lim← Ω̃(n) with the inverse-limit topology is a light profinite set; its clopen algebra is the algebra of finite unions of cylinders; its Borel σ-algebra is R̃.

*Proof.* Each Ω̃(n) is finite and discrete and the tower is countable, so the limit is a countable inverse limit of finite sets: compact, Hausdorff, totally disconnected, second countable — light profinite by definition. Cylinders are the preimages of points under the projections Ω̃ → Ω̃(n); they form a clopen basis, and every clopen set, being compact and open, is a finite union of basis elements. Hence Clopen(Ω̃) = finite unions of cylinders, and σ(Clopen) = σ(cylinders) = R̃; on a second-countable space σ(clopens) is the Borel algebra. ∎

Nothing here is difficult; the point is the identification. In the CSG literature the space carries only its measure-theoretic description; the words "profinite", "Stone", "totally disconnected" do not occur (checked against [1–4] and the surrounding literature). Every construction below flows from taking the topology seriously.

## 3. The stem spectrum (T2)

Let **B** ⊆ P(Ω̃) be the Boolean algebra of subsets generated by the stem events {stem(b)}. Since stems are finite posets up to isomorphism, B is countable. Let **X = Stone(B)**: the space of Boolean homomorphisms B → 2 (equivalently ultrafilters of B), with the topology generated by â = {u : u(a) = 1}. Define the **evaluation map** φ : Ω̃ → X by φ(c)(a) = 1 iff c ∈ a.

**Theorem 2.** X is a light profinite set. φ is Borel measurable (not, in general, continuous), φ⁻¹(Clopen(X)) = B, and σ(stem events) = φ⁻¹(Borel(X)).

*Proof.* B countable gives X second countable, hence light profinite. Each c ∈ Ω̃ evaluates the concrete algebra B ⊆ P(Ω̃) to {0,1} preserving Boolean operations, so φ(c) ∈ X is well defined; φ⁻¹(â) = a for a ∈ B by construction, giving φ⁻¹(Clopen(X)) = B (the clopens of X are exactly the â). Stem events are countable unions of cylinders [1], hence Borel in Ω̃; Boolean combinations preserve Borelness, so B ⊆ Borel(Ω̃) and φ is Borel. Since X is second countable, Borel(X) = σ(Clopen(X)), whence φ⁻¹(Borel X) = σ(B) = σ(stem events). Continuity fails in general because complements of stem events need not be open in Ω̃ — a stem, once absent from every finite stage, can still appear later, so stem(b) is open but not closed. ∎

Two remarks. First, φ factors through relabeling (stems are isomorphism-invariant), so everything below is automatically covariant. Second, X is *not* a topological quotient of Ω̃: it is the measurable spectrum of the covariant question algebra. That is a feature — it is the space the *observables* see.

## 4. Rogues are the fibers (T3)

**Theorem 3.** (i) φ(c) = φ(c′) iff c and c′ realize the same set of stems. (ii) The non-injectivity of φ is exactly the BDGHS rogue phenomenon: a fiber of φ containing two relabeling classes is a rogue pair, and BDGHS's examples witness that such fibers exist. (iii) BDGHS Proposition 1 shows their rogue family is null in every CSG dynamics; and by Corollary 1, no covariant event separates two histories in a common fiber except on a null set: for A ∈ R and c, c′ with φ(c) = φ(c′), any B ∈ R(S) with μ(A △ B) = 0 satisfies 1_B(c) = 1_B(c′).

*Proof.* (i) Two homomorphisms on B agree iff they agree on the generators stem(b). (ii) is the definition of rogue in [1] read through (i). (iii) B ∈ R(S) = σ(stem events) cannot separate stem-equivalent histories (the class of sets that cannot separate them is a σ-algebra containing the generators); the rest is BDGHS Corollary 1. ∎

We do not claim the union of all non-trivial fibers is measurable or null — only the exhibited families are known null, and (iii) is the precise covariant-invisibility statement. The receipts add a finite-size fact worth recording: **proto-rogues are abundant** — distinct unlabeled n-causets sharing *all proper stems* (every stem of size ≤ n−1) number 5, 11, and 37 pairs at n = 4, 5, 6 respectively (r2), so the phenomenon behind the measure-zero caveat is visible at every finite size, not an artifact of the infinite limit. What the spectrum adds to [1] is *location*: the "up to measure zero" is not an analytic accident scattered through the σ-algebra; it is carried entirely by the failure of one map to be injective, on one compact space.

## 5. The measure-algebra isomorphism (T4)

**Theorem 4.** Let μ be any CSG dynamics (a probability measure on (Ω̃, R̃)) and ν = φ∗(μ|_{σ(B)}) its pushforward to X. Then E ↦ φ⁻¹(E) induces an isomorphism of measure algebras
Borel(X) / ν-null  ≅  R / μ-null.

*Proof.* The map is a Boolean homomorphism commuting with countable operations, and ν(E) = μ(φ⁻¹E) makes it well defined and injective on classes (ν(E △ E′) = μ(φ⁻¹E △ φ⁻¹E′)). Its image is σ(B) mod null by Theorem 2; BDGHS Corollary 1 says every class of R mod μ-null contains a representative in σ(stem events) = σ(B), so the image is all of R mod μ-null. ∎

This is BDGHS Theorem 1 *restructured*: not "stems generate, with an unavoidable caveat," but "the covariant measure algebra of every CSG dynamics **is** the measure algebra of one fixed compact zero-dimensional space." All CSG dynamics live on the same spectrum; only the measure moves.

## 6. Automatic extension on the spectrum, and where the quantum obstruction goes (N3)

**Theorem 5.** Every bounded finitely-additive charge m : B → ℝ (or ℂ, of bounded variation) extends uniquely to a Radon measure on X. In particular every finitely-additive *state* on the stem algebra is a Radon probability measure on the stem spectrum.

*Proof.* Transport m to the clopen algebra of X via Stone. On a compact space, a countable disjoint family of clopens covering a clopen set is finite (compactness of the covered set; each part is open), so finite additivity on Clopen(X) *implies* countable additivity there. Carathéodory extension (applied to the positive part, or to a bounded-variation complex charge via its total-variation decomposition) yields a unique countably-additive extension to σ(Clopen) = Borel(X); on a compact metrizable space Borel measures are Radon. Uniqueness follows from the π-system generated by clopens. ∎

**The quantum relocation (stated with care).** Dowker–Johnston–Surya's obstruction [2] is that a decoherence functional D on the cylinder algebra — equivalently, by Theorem 4's translation, a bi-additive complex charge on B × B — may fail to extend σ-additively; their theorem traces the failure to the associated vector measure, i.e., to *unbounded variation*. On the spectrum the situation clarifies: countable additivity on clopens is automatic (as in the proof above), so **the entire surviving obstruction is bounded variation of the complex charge** — the classical extension condition of vector-measure theory. The spectrum does not cure the obstruction; it strips away the σ-additivity half and leaves the failure named in textbook terms, on a compact zero-dimensional space where the classical theory (Kluvánek-type extension theorems) directly applies. Whether a given growth dynamics' decoherence functional has bounded variation on B is a well-posed question per dynamics; Sorkin's canonical-sequence extension [3] is the constructive tool where it fails.

## 7. The ordered version and covtree (N4)

Let **L** be the bounded distributive lattice generated by the stem events under union and intersection (no complements). Its Priestley dual X_≤ is a Priestley space, and since L is countable, X_≤ is — by Speed's theorem [6] — a **profinite poset**: a countable inverse limit of finite posets, i.e., a light profinite poset. Its order is inclusion of prime filters: informally, one idealized stem-theory lies below another when every stem-question the first affirms, the second affirms. At each finite truncation (stems of size ≤ k, evaluated on n-element causets) the lattice is finite and Birkhoff duality is effective; the receipts verify the machinery mechanically (r3) and compute the finite stages. **Covtree** [4] is the growth structure whose level-n nodes are the realizable sets of n-element stems; the receipts (r5) compute, within six-element causets, the relation between covtree's exact-size-3 signatures and the spectrum's finite quotients (the size-≤ 3 stem-theory classes): **they coincide at this size — 21 classes each, with the exact-rank signature determining the cumulative theory in all 21 cases** — so covtree's exact-rank slices and the spectrum's cumulative tower are, at least at the computed sizes, the same finite approximations of the same object, the stem spectrum. (Whether the determination persists at all sizes is left open; the receipt claims only the computed range.)

## 9. Related work

BDGHS [1] and the companion problem-of-time paper are the foundation; this paper adds no new dynamics and changes none of their theorems — it changes the *category* they are stated in. Covtree [4, and the 2026 witness-size follow-up] is the closest active line: a stem-first combinatorial dynamics; §7 gives it a spectral home. Martin–Panangaden [8] reconstruct globally hyperbolic spacetimes from countable *causally dense* suborders via domain theory — an adjacent program whose density hypothesis locally finite orders never satisfy; the ideal completion of the stem system (a Scott domain, by the configurations-form-a-domain theorem of event-structure theory [9]) is the natural meeting point, and we regard "for μ-almost-every growth history, the completion of its interval basis is a manifold" as the right form of their closing open question for genuinely discrete orders. Light condensed mathematics [7] supplies the name of the category in which both the history space and its spectrum live; we deliberately use only the identification. The p-adic/adelic tradition (Volovich 1987 and successors) shares the intuition of total disconnectedness at the fundamental level but derives no Lorentzian structure from it; here causal order is primitive and the disconnected object is the *history space*, not spacetime.

## 10. Outlook

Three directions. (a) *The extension question per dynamics*: does a given growth dynamics' decoherence functional have bounded variation on the stem algebra? — now a well-posed, receipt-sized question (§6). (b) *The measure-geometry of X*: CSG dynamics = Radon measures on one fixed space; comparisons between dynamics (transitive percolation vs. others) become measure-theoretic geometry on X. (c) For the host program that motivated this note: record webs are CSG-class growth processes, their down-set machinery is L, and the bridge from finite record combinatorics to continuum physics can now be phrased as: which Radon measures on the stem spectrum concentrate on histories whose stem-ideal completions are manifolds — the probabilistic form of Martin–Panangaden's question, with volume supplied by counting.
