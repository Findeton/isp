## Abstract

Covariant observables in causal-set sequential growth (CSG) were characterized by Brightwell–Dowker–García–Henson–Sorkin (BDGHS): the σ-algebra of relabeling-invariant events on the space of completed causal sets is generated, *up to sets of measure zero*, by **stem events** — questions of the form "does the finite causet b occur as an ancestor-closed sub-causet?" The measure-zero caveat is unavoidable: "rogue" pairs of distinct completed causets realize identical stems. We point out an identification that, to our knowledge, appears nowhere in the causal-set or duality literature: the labeled history space is a **light profinite set** (a countable inverse limit of finite sets), and the Boolean algebra generated by stem events is a countable algebra whose **Stone spectrum X** — the *stem spectrum* — is again light profinite. Under the canonical evaluation map φ from histories to X: (i) fibers are exactly the stem-equivalence classes, so the BDGHS rogue phenomenon *is* the non-injectivity of φ — and BDGHS's own exact characterization of the rogues (Θ = Γ, the causets with a level containing infinitely many non-maximal elements) becomes an exact fiber theorem: the union of the non-trivial fibers is a named Borel set, null for every dynamics in BDGHS's covered class (the generalized-percolation family), of full measure in a known excluded model, and over non-rogues the fibers are single relabeling classes; (ii) for the covered class, BDGHS's generation theorem becomes a **measure-algebra isomorphism** between the covariant algebra mod null and the Borel algebra of X mod null; (iii) by compactness of X, *every* bounded finitely-additive state on the stem algebra extends uniquely to a Radon measure on X — for scalar charges the extension problem dissolves on the spectrum, exactly as it already does on the cylinder algebra (Surya–Zalel's bounded-variation criterion is the quantitative form of the same compactness), and for decoherence functionals (Dowker–Johnston–Surya) the surviving obstruction is strong additivity of the associated vector measure (Carathéodory–Hahn–Kluvánek), with bounded variation its scalar shadow: the problem is relocated — not cured — into classical vector-measure theory on one compact zero-dimensional space, now for the *covariant* algebra; (iv) the distributive-lattice (Priestley) version realizes the spectrum as a profinite *poset* in the sense of Speed, and the covtree of Dowker–Imambaccus–Owens–Sorkin–Zalel — whose level-n nodes are the realizable families of n-element stems — is precisely the tower of finite quotients of the spectrum: an elementary extension lemma makes exact-rank and cumulative stem signatures interchangeable, the level-n nodes are in canonical bijection with the atoms of the rank-≤n stem algebra, and X is the inverse limit of the covtree levels along explicit refinement maps. The receipts compute the tower mechanically: covtree level 3 has exactly 22 nodes — the 22nd, realizing all five 3-orders, first appears at seven elements — and finite proto-rogues (distinct n-causets sharing all proper stems) are abundant: 5, 11, 37 pairs at n = 4, 5, 6. The results are assemblies of classical duality theory (Stone 1936; Priestley 1970; Speed 1972) with the published CSG theorems; their value is structural: the "up to measure zero" of covariant causal-set physics is the geometry of one compact, totally disconnected space.

## 1. Setting and imports

**CSG and the history space.** Following BDGHS [1], a *naturally labeled causet* on {0,…,n−1} is a partial order in which i ≺ j implies i < j; sequential growth adds element n above a down-set of the existing order. Let Ω̃(n) denote the (finite) set of naturally labeled n-causets and let e_n : Ω̃(n+1) → Ω̃(n) delete the top-labeled element. The *labeled history space* is the inverse limit Ω̃ = lim← (Ω̃(n), e_n), whose elements are completed labeled causets; the *cylinder* [b̃] ⊆ Ω̃ is the set of histories with prefix b̃ ∈ Ω̃(n). BDGHS take R̃ = σ(cylinders) and define the covariant algebra R ⊆ R̃ as the relabeling-invariant events. A *stem* of a causet is a finite down-set (ancestor-closed subset), taken up to isomorphism; the *stem event* is stem(b) = {c ∈ Ω̃ : b embeds as a stem of c}. **The covered dynamics class.** BDGHS prove their theorems for the class they themselves delimit [1, §2 and §7]: the *generalized percolation* family of Rideout–Sorkin CSG dynamics — those with coupling t_k > 0 for some k ≥ 2 — together with the degenerate deterministic models, where the statements hold trivially; coverage of the remaining non-generic random models is their stated conjecture ([1] §7). Throughout this paper "the covered class" means exactly that, and the scoping is not pedantry: for the fiber statement below, the conjecture must fail in at least one direction — in the excluded model with only t₀, t₁ > 0 the rogue set has full measure (§4). With that fixed: BDGHS prove [1] that stem events are countable unions of cylinders; **(BDGHS Theorem 1)** the stem events generate R up to measure zero, precisely **(BDGHS Corollary 1)**: for every A ∈ R there is B ∈ R(S) := σ(stem events) with μ(A △ B) = 0 for every measure μ of the covered class (their quantifier order — one B serving all covered μ simultaneously — is the strong form, and it is genuine: A △ B sits inside the rogue set). The inclusion R(S) ⊊ R is strict, witnessed by *rogue* pairs — non-isomorphic completed causets with identical stem sets. BDGHS in fact characterize the rogues *exactly* ([1] §4, Lemmas 1–2): writing Θ for the set of all rogues and Γ for the set of causets having a level with infinitely many non-maximal elements, **Θ = Γ**, and **(BDGHS Proposition 1)** μ(Θ) = 0 for the covered class. Dowker–Johnston–Surya [2] prove that *quantum* measures (decoherence functionals) on the cylinder algebra need not extend to R̃: "the decoherence functional, and hence the quantum measure, extends if and only if the associated vector measure does," and a quantum sequential growth model witnesses failure; Surya–Zalel [10] give the exact scalar-side criterion (a complex measure on the cylinder event algebra extends iff it has bounded variation). Covtree [4] — introduced by Dowker–Imambaccus–Owens–Sorkin–Zalel — re-founds covariant dynamics directly on finite families of stems: its level-n nodes are the realizable sets of n-element stems.

**Duality background.** Stone duality: countable Boolean algebras ↔ second-countable Stone spaces (compact, Hausdorff, totally disconnected) — i.e., **light profinite sets**, the countable-limit site of Clausen–Scholze's light condensed mathematics [7] (we use only the identification, none of the condensed machinery). Priestley duality [5]: bounded distributive lattices ↔ Priestley spaces; Speed [6]: the Priestley spaces are exactly the profinite posets (inverse limits of finite posets). Birkhoff: finite distributive lattices ↔ finite posets via join-irreducibles/down-sets.

## 2. The history space is light profinite (T1)

**Theorem 1.** Ω̃ = lim← Ω̃(n) with the inverse-limit topology is a light profinite set; its clopen algebra is the algebra of finite unions of cylinders; its Borel σ-algebra is R̃.

*Proof.* Each Ω̃(n) is finite and discrete and the tower is countable, so the limit is a countable inverse limit of finite sets: compact, Hausdorff, totally disconnected, second countable — light profinite by definition. Cylinders are the preimages of points under the projections Ω̃ → Ω̃(n); they form a clopen basis, and every clopen set, being compact and open, is a finite union of basis elements. Hence Clopen(Ω̃) = finite unions of cylinders, and σ(Clopen) = σ(cylinders) = R̃; on a second-countable space *with a clopen basis* (zero-dimensional, as here), σ(clopens) contains every open set and hence is the Borel algebra. ∎

Nothing here is difficult; the point is the identification. In the CSG literature the space carries only its measure-theoretic description; the words "profinite", "Stone", "totally disconnected" do not occur (checked against [1–4] and the surrounding literature). Every construction below flows from taking the topology seriously.

## 3. The stem spectrum (T2)

Let **B** ⊆ P(Ω̃) be the Boolean algebra of subsets generated by the stem events {stem(b)}. Since stems are finite posets up to isomorphism, B is countable. Let **X = Stone(B)**: the space of Boolean homomorphisms B → 2 (equivalently ultrafilters of B), with the topology generated by â = {u : u(a) = 1}. Define the **evaluation map** φ : Ω̃ → X by φ(c)(a) = 1 iff c ∈ a.

**Theorem 2.** X is a light profinite set. φ is Borel measurable (not, in general, continuous), φ⁻¹(Clopen(X)) = B, and σ(stem events) = φ⁻¹(Borel(X)).

*Proof.* B countable gives X second countable, hence light profinite. Each c ∈ Ω̃ evaluates the concrete algebra B ⊆ P(Ω̃) to {0,1} preserving Boolean operations, so φ(c) ∈ X is well defined; φ⁻¹(â) = a for a ∈ B by construction, giving φ⁻¹(Clopen(X)) = B (the clopens of X are exactly the â). Stem events are countable unions of cylinders [1], hence Borel in Ω̃; Boolean combinations preserve Borelness, so B ⊆ Borel(Ω̃) and φ is Borel. Since X is second countable and zero-dimensional, Borel(X) = σ(Clopen(X)), whence φ⁻¹(Borel X) = σ(B) = σ(stem events). Continuity fails in general because complements of stem events need not be open in Ω̃: no finite stage of a history can certify that a stem is *absent* (elements added later may complete it), so stem(b) is open but not closed — explicitly, the infinite antichain realizes no 2-chain stem, yet every one of its finite stages has extensions that do, so the antichain lies in the closure of stem(2-chain). ∎

Two remarks. First, φ factors through relabeling (stems are isomorphism-invariant), so everything below is automatically covariant. Second, X is *not* a topological quotient of Ω̃: it is the measurable spectrum of the covariant question algebra. That is a feature — it is the space the *observables* see.

## 4. Rogues are the fibers (T3)

Call a completed causet a *rogue* if some non-isomorphic completed causet realizes exactly the same stems [1]. Recall from §1 BDGHS's exact characterization: Θ (all rogues) = Γ (some level contains infinitely many non-maximal elements), where the *level* of an element is the height of its past — a quantity determined by any finite stage containing that past. Write Γ̃ ⊆ Ω̃ for the labeled copy of Γ.

**Theorem 3 (the exact fiber theorem).** (i) φ(c) = φ(c′) iff c and c′ realize the same set of stems. (ii) The union of the fibers of φ containing more than one relabeling class is exactly Γ̃, a Borel subset of Ω̃; over non-rogues, every fiber is a single relabeling class. (iii) μ(Γ̃) = 0 for every dynamics of the covered class (BDGHS Proposition 1); by contrast μ(Γ̃) = 1 in the excluded t₀,t₁-only model, and μ(Γ̃) = 0 trivially in the t₀-only (dust) model. (iv) No covariant event separates two histories in a common fiber except on a null set: for A ∈ R and c, c′ with φ(c) = φ(c′), any B ∈ R(S) with μ(A △ B) = 0 satisfies 1_B(c) = 1_B(c′).

*Proof.* (i) Two homomorphisms on B agree iff they agree on the generators stem(b). (ii) A fiber contains two relabeling classes iff it contains a rogue, and BDGHS Lemma 2 ([1] §4: outside Γ, stem-equivalence implies isomorphism) gives both halves: fibers over Γ̃ are non-trivial (Lemma 1 supplies the stem-equivalent partner) and fibers off Γ̃ are single relabeling classes. Borel-ness: "element i has level k" is decided by any cylinder fixing the past of i (clopen); "element i is non-maximal" is the countable union over j of the cylinder-decided events i ≺ j (open); "level k contains infinitely many non-maximal elements" is a countable lim-sup of these, and Γ̃ is the countable union over k — Borel at low finite rank. (iii) The first clause is [1] Proposition 1 (their proof runs through Θ ⊆ {some level is infinite} and Borel–Cantelli, which is where t_k > 0 for some k ≥ 2 enters). For the t₀,t₁-only model, the a.s. history is an infinite disjoint union of trees whose level 0 is infinite with all its elements non-maximal, so Γ̃ has full measure; for the t₀-only model the history is a.s. the infinite antichain, whose every element is maximal — not in Γ, and indeed stem-determined (its stems are exactly the finite antichains, and any order relation anywhere would create a 2-chain stem). (iv) B ∈ R(S) = σ(stem events) cannot separate stem-equivalent histories (the class of sets that cannot separate them is a σ-algebra containing the generators); the rest is BDGHS Corollary 1. ∎

So the non-injectivity of φ is not merely exemplified by BDGHS's rogue families — it is *exactly located*: one named Borel set, null for the covered dynamics, and provably not null beyond it (the t₀,t₁ model shows the covered-class hypothesis is load-bearing, not an artifact of proof technique). The receipts add a finite-size fact worth recording: **proto-rogues are abundant** — distinct unlabeled n-causets sharing *all proper stems* (every stem of size ≤ n−1) number 5, 11, and 37 pairs at n = 4, 5, 6 respectively (r2), so the phenomenon behind the measure-zero caveat is visible at every finite size, not an artifact of the infinite limit. What the spectrum adds to [1] is *location*: the "up to measure zero" is not an analytic accident scattered through the σ-algebra; it is carried entirely by the failure of one map to be injective, on one compact space.

## 5. The measure-algebra isomorphism (T4)

**Theorem 4.** Let μ be a dynamics of the covered class and ν = φ∗(μ|_{σ(B)}) its pushforward to X. Then E ↦ φ⁻¹(E) induces an isomorphism of measure algebras
Borel(X) / ν-null  ≅  R / μ-null.

*Proof.* The map is a Boolean homomorphism commuting with countable operations, and ν(E) = μ(φ⁻¹E) makes it well defined and injective on classes (ν(E △ E′) = μ(φ⁻¹E △ φ⁻¹E′)). Its image is σ(B) mod null by Theorem 2; BDGHS Corollary 1 says every class of R mod μ-null contains a representative in σ(stem events) = σ(B), so the image is all of R mod μ-null. ∎

*Remark (the hypothesis is load-bearing).* Theorem 4 is not a fact about arbitrary probability measures on (Ω̃, R̃): take a rogue pair c ≇ c′ (Theorem 3) and let μ = ½(δ_c̃ + δ_c̃′) for fixed labelings. Every B ∈ σ(stem events) satisfies 1_B(c̃) = 1_B(c̃′) (Theorem 3(iv)'s separation argument), so the covariant event "relabelings of c," which μ weights ½, differs from every such B on a set of measure ≥ ½ — surjectivity fails. The isomorphism inherits exactly the scope of BDGHS's dynamical input, which is what kills such measures: rogues are null for the covered class.

This is BDGHS Theorem 1 *restructured*: not "stems generate, with an unavoidable caveat," but "the covariant measure algebra of every covered CSG dynamics **is** the measure algebra of one fixed compact zero-dimensional space." All covered dynamics live on the same spectrum; only the measure moves.

## 6. Automatic extension on the spectrum, and where the quantum obstruction goes (N3)

**Theorem 5.** Every bounded finitely-additive charge m : B → ℝ or ℂ extends uniquely to a (complex) Radon measure on X. (For scalar charges on a Boolean algebra, boundedness and bounded variation are equivalent — |m|(1) ≤ 4·sup|m| — so no separate variation hypothesis is needed.) In particular every finitely-additive *state* on the stem algebra is a Radon probability measure on the stem spectrum.

*Proof.* Transport m to the clopen algebra of X via Stone. On a compact space, a countable disjoint family of clopens covering a clopen set is finite (compactness of the covered set; each part is open), so finite additivity on Clopen(X) *implies* countable additivity there. Carathéodory extension (applied to the positive part, or to a complex charge via its total-variation decomposition) yields a unique countably-additive extension to σ(Clopen) = Borel(X); on a compact metrizable space Borel measures are Radon. Uniqueness follows from the π-system generated by clopens. ∎

**An honest accounting of what is new here.** By Theorem 1, the *cylinder* algebra is already the clopen algebra of the compact space Ω̃ — so the vacuous-σ-additivity trick above was always available on the original algebra, and indeed it is the topological content of Surya–Zalel's extension criterion [10, Thm 2.1]: a complex measure on the cylinder event algebra extends to the generated σ-algebra **iff it has bounded variation** (their proof runs through the nesting/finest-partition property of cylinders, which is this compactness in algebraic garb). What the spectrum adds is not the trick but its *reach*: on X the **covariant** algebra B becomes clopen, so the same dissolution now applies to covariant states — the algebra BDGHS showed carries the physics.

**The quantum relocation (stated with care).** Dowker–Johnston–Surya's obstruction [2] is that a decoherence functional D on the cylinder algebra may fail to extend σ-additively; their theorem traces the failure to the associated (Hilbert-space-valued) vector measure. For vector measures the exact extension condition is not bounded variation but **strong additivity**: a bounded vector measure on a field, vacuously weakly countably additive as here, extends to the generated σ-field iff it is strongly additive (the Carathéodory–Hahn–Kluvánek theorem [14]; Surya–Zalel state and use it for this very problem as their Theorem B.1). Bounded variation is the scalar specialization — sufficient in general, and equivalent in finite dimensions, which is the setting of DJS's variation analysis [2]; in infinite dimensions DJS themselves note a weaker condition must take its place. On the stem spectrum the situation is now structural: any decoherence-functional-like bi-charge *defined on B × B* is automatically countably additive on clopens, and its extension question is exactly classical strong additivity on a compact zero-dimensional space. (We do not claim the cylinder-algebra problem transports to B: stem events are countable unions of cylinders, so B and the cylinder algebra are incomparable, and a decoherence functional on cylinders need not restrict to B without assuming the very extension at issue. The covariant extension problem is the *analogue*, not a translation.) The spectrum does not cure the obstruction; it strips away the σ-additivity half and leaves the failure named in textbook terms — strong additivity of a vector measure — on a space where the classical theory directly applies. Whether a given growth dynamics' decoherence functional is strongly additive on B is a well-posed question per dynamics; Sorkin's canonical-sequence extension [3] is the constructive tool where extension fails.

## 7. The ordered version and covtree (N4)

Let **L** be the bounded distributive lattice generated by the stem events under union and intersection (no complements). Its Priestley dual X_≤ is a Priestley space, and since L is countable, X_≤ is — by Speed's theorem [6] — a **profinite poset**: a countable inverse limit of finite posets, i.e., a light profinite poset. Its order is inclusion of prime filters: informally, one idealized stem-theory lies below another when every stem-question the first affirms, the second affirms. At each finite truncation (stems of size ≤ k, evaluated on n-element causets) the lattice is finite and Birkhoff duality is effective; the receipts verify the machinery mechanically (r3) and compute the finite stages.

**The tower correspondence.** Covtree [4] is the growth structure whose level-n nodes are the realizable sets of n-element stems. Two elementary facts make its relation to the spectrum exact.

**Lemma (extension).** Let c be a causet with at least k elements and b a stem of c of size j < k. Then b extends to a stem of c of size j + 1, hence to one of size k. Consequently, for causets with ≥ k elements — in particular for every completed history — *the exact-size-k stem signature determines the full size-≤ k signature*: b is a stem of c iff b is a stem of some k-stem of c (stems of stems are stems).

*Proof.* c ∖ b is non-empty; pick y ∈ c ∖ b. The set past(y) ∩ (c ∖ b) is finite (local finiteness); if empty take x = y, else take x minimal in it. Then every element below x lies in b, so b ∪ {x} is a down-set of size j + 1. Iterate. ∎

**Theorem 6 (covtree levels are the finite quotients of the spectrum).** For each n let B_n ⊆ B be the finite subalgebra generated by the stem events of size ≤ n. Then the map sending a history to its set of exact-n stems induces a canonical bijection between the non-empty atoms of B_n (as subsets of Ω̃) and the level-n nodes of covtree. Consequently X = Stone(B) = lim← Spec(B_n) exhibits the stem spectrum as the inverse limit of the covtree levels, along the explicit refinement maps Q′ ↦ ⋃_{q ∈ Q′} {n-stems of q} (from level n + 1 to level n).

*Proof.* By the extension lemma, the atoms of B_n on Ω̃ — the classes of histories with a common size-≤ n signature — are exactly the classes with a common exact-n signature. Every history's exact-n set is a covtree node: a history has finitely many n-stems (there are finitely many n-orders), each realized at some finite stage, so some finite stage realizes them all, and that stage's n-stems are precisely the history's (stems of a stage are stems of the history). Conversely every level-n node is realized by a history: take a finite certificate c₀ (size ≥ n) and extend it to a history by repeatedly adding a new element above *all* existing elements — each addition creates no new stem of size ≤ n (a down-set containing the new element contains its entire past, of size > n) — so the resulting history's exact-n set is the node's. Distinct nodes give distinct atoms by the determination clause of the lemma. The refinement formula: for a history c with ≥ n+1 elements realized, every n-stem of c extends to an (n+1)-stem of c (the lemma), i.e., is an n-stem of a member of its exact-(n+1) set Q′, and conversely every n-stem of a member of Q′ is an n-stem of c. B = ⋃ B_n gives X = lim← Spec(B_n) by Stone duality for directed unions. ∎

We claim the bijection of node *sets* level by level and the displayed refinement maps; whether covtree's own parent relation [4] agrees edge-for-edge with these refinement maps is a finite combinatorial check per level that we have not performed and do not claim (if it does — as the certificate-based definitions suggest — then X is precisely the space of infinite covtree paths, the spectral form of covtree's completeness).

**The computed tower, and a correction.** The receipts compute the level-3 tower mechanically. Within six-element causets there are 21 exact-size-3 signatures (r5), and the first version of this paper reported the covtree contact as "21 = 21." That count is a **six-element artifact**: enumerating all 96,428 labeled 7-causets (rB1, independently recounted) yields a 22nd signature — the full set of all five 3-orders (3-chain, 3-antichain, L, V, Λ), first realizable at seven elements; an explicit 7-element witness is printed and re-verified in the receipt. An interface state machine (adopted from the round-38 review after exact cross-validation against brute force, rB3) finds no further signatures through twelve elements, and the witness-size bound of Gutzeit–Shaban–Yeats–Zalel [4] certifies completeness beyond that: **covtree level 3 has exactly 22 nodes.** The determination of the cumulative theory by the exact-rank signature, reported empirically as "21/21" in the first version, was never contingent — it is the extension lemma, mechanized at n ≤ 7 as rB2 — so the coincidence of covtree's exact-rank slices with the spectrum's cumulative tower holds at *every* rank by Theorem 6, not merely in the computed range.

## 9. Related work

BDGHS [1] and the companion problem-of-time paper are the foundation; this paper adds no new dynamics and changes none of their theorems — it changes the *category* they are stated in. Dowker–Surya [11] is the direct continuation of [1], extending the observables analysis to a wider class of percolation-type models; our "covered class" scoping follows [1] and should be read alongside it. Covtree [4] is the closest active line: a stem-first combinatorial dynamics; §7 gives it a spectral home (Theorem 6), and the witness-size bounds of the 2026 follow-up [4] are what certify our level-3 census. Surya–Zalel's extension criterion [10] is, in the light of Theorem 1, the compactness of the history space expressed measure-theoretically — §6 credits and builds on it. Brightwell–Łuczak [12] study order-invariant measures on infinite causets from the random-orders side — the same covariant σ-algebra approached from exchangeability — and are the natural interlocutor for the measure-geometry questions of §10(b); the observables analysis has also been extended to cyclic cosmologies [13]. Martin–Panangaden [8] reconstruct globally hyperbolic spacetimes from countable *causally dense* suborders via domain theory — an adjacent program whose density hypothesis locally finite orders never satisfy; the ideal completion of the stem system (a Scott domain, by the configurations-form-a-domain theorem of event-structure theory [9]) is the natural meeting point, and we regard "for μ-almost-every growth history, the completion of its interval basis is a manifold" as the right form of their closing open question for genuinely discrete orders. Light condensed mathematics [7] supplies the name of the category in which both the history space and its spectrum live; we deliberately use only the identification. The p-adic/adelic tradition (Volovich 1987 and successors) shares the intuition of total disconnectedness at the fundamental level but derives no Lorentzian structure from it; here causal order is primitive and the disconnected object is the *history space*, not spacetime.

## 10. Outlook

Three directions. (a) *The extension question per dynamics*: Surya–Zalel [10] exhibit a large family of complex CSG dynamics whose measures extend on the cylinder σ-algebra, and any such extension restricts to σ(B) ⊆ σ(cylinders) — for that family the covariant extension question is already answered affirmatively by restriction. The genuinely open, receipt-sized question §6 isolates is the gap: is there a growth dynamics whose decoherence functional fails bounded variation on the cylinder algebra (hence does not extend there, per [10]) yet is strongly additive on the coarser stem algebra B — a dynamics with no labeled continuum limit but a covariant one? (b) *The measure-geometry of X*: CSG dynamics = Radon measures on one fixed space; comparisons between dynamics (transitive percolation vs. others) become measure-theoretic geometry on X. (c) For the host program that motivated this note: record webs are CSG-class growth processes, their down-set machinery is L, and the bridge from finite record combinatorics to continuum physics can now be phrased as: which Radon measures on the stem spectrum concentrate on histories whose stem-ideal completions are manifolds — the probabilistic form of Martin–Panangaden's question, with volume supplied by counting.
