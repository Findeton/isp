\[
M\longmapsto M\otimes_A X

\[

\[
\mathcal L_A(M)
\cong
\mathcal L_B(M\otimes_A X).

Consequently, a classical action

\[

\[
\lambda^X=\Theta_X\lambda.

Products, adjoints, orthogonality and the fine/coarse map are preserved. Proposition 4.5 at lines 502–519 is sound.

# Ranked findings

## Finding 1 — Critical: the operational Morita localization is only named

**Type:** missing categorical construction  
**Affects:** `RQ0-L0-MORITA-INVARIANT-W3-SEAMS` and every later rung

Definition 3.6 at lines 391–408 introduces

\[
\mathbf{OpMor}(D)
=
\mathbf{Pres}_D[\mathcal W_{\mathrm{Mor}}^{-1}],
\]

but neither side has the data required to use this expression.

The paper does not define:

- the objects of \(\mathbf{Pres}_D\) beyond “presentations”;
- its marked correspondence 1-morphisms;
- its 2-morphisms and their compatibility with markings;
- horizontal composition of marked correspondences;
- the associator and unitors after marking;
- the localization pseudofunctor;
- a universal property of the localization; or
- the induced action of every localized 1- and 2-morphism on W3 objects.

Moreover, \(\mathcal W_{\mathrm{Mor}}\) is said at lines 404–406 already to consist of adjoint equivalences. Localizing a bicategory at 1-morphisms that are already internal equivalences is biequivalent to the original bicategory. On that rigorous reading, the localization is harmless but does not itself remove presentations or construct a new quotient.

The subsequent W3 construction confirms the gap. Definition 5.7 defines W3 objects but no morphisms. Nevertheless Theorem 7.1 at lines 757–769 asserts an equivalence of categories

\[
\mathsf{W3}_{\rm ci}(D)
\simeq
\mathsf{W3}_{\rm ci}(D')
\]

“and marked intertwiners.” No definition of a marked W3 intertwiner, its identity, composition or 2-cell appears before this theorem.

Definition 7.4 at lines 829–843 then declares a Grothendieck category of a pseudofunctor on \(\mathbf{OpMor}(D)\). No such pseudofunctor has been defined. Theorem 7.1 only provides objectwise transport along marked Morita equivalences; it does not define transport along every 1-morphism of the stated localized bicategory or naturality against its 2-morphisms.

Thus the paper has a correct object-level invariance theorem under a supplied marked imprimitivity equivalence, but it has not constructed W3 objects *on the localized base*.

### Exact associator counterexample

Let

\[
{}_AX_B,\qquad {}_BY_C
\]

be composable imprimitivity bimodules. Then

\[
(M\otimes_A X)\otimes_B Y
\]

and

\[
M\otimes_A(X\otimes_B Y)
\]

are canonically unitarily isomorphic, but they are not equal. A W3 transport pseudofunctor therefore requires a compositor

\[
F_YF_X
\Longrightarrow
F_{X\otimes_B Y}
\]

built from the interior-tensor associator, together with pentagon and unit coherence on classical actions, instruments and W3 morphisms.

Lines 805–807 merely say that associators supply coherence. They do not define the compositor or check its action on any W3 hom-set. Since the W3 hom-sets themselves are absent, this appeal cannot complete the proof.

## Finding 2 — Major: the correspondence and operational layers are not assembled into the claimed typed bicategory

**Type:** typing gap  
**Affects:** marked localization and transport

The paper correctly distinguishes:

- C*-correspondences and adjointable bimodule intertwiners at lines 270–282; and
- CP maps, UCP maps and instruments in the operational marking.

But it never defines how those two directions form \(\mathbf{Pres}_D\).

A C*-correspondence is not a CP channel, and an adjointable bimodule map is not an instrument branch. The only operation actually constructed between the two layers is conjugation of endomorphism algebras by an *invertible* imprimitivity equivalence.

There are therefore two coherent options:

1. restrict the base to the bigroupoid of marked imprimitivity equivalences; or
2. construct a double category/equipment whose horizontal arrows are correspondences, vertical arrows are operational CP maps and squares express their compatibility.

The paper does neither. It calls the base a bicategory of “marked correspondences” while using only its invertible part and separately transporting CP maps by algebra isomorphisms.

This does not damage the complete-instrument equations or the objectwise imprimitivity theorem. It prevents the richer localization and pseudofunctor claims.

## Finding 3 — Major: the spectator stability theorem does not construct the claimed spectator kernel

**Type:** missing arrows and false isotropy inference  
**Affects:** spectator stability and effective rigidification

The matrix-amplification module at lines 851–873 is correct:

\[
X=\mathbb C^{1\times n},
\qquad
\mathcal L_{M_n}(H\otimes X)\cong B(H).
\]

Transporting the marking through this isomorphism gives a correct objectwise equivalence. It avoids the earlier ill-typed rule that tensor-multiplied scalar preparations by \(I_n\).

But the mandatory isotropy claim is not established.

For the row imprimitivity module \(X=\mathbb C^{1\times n}\), an adjointable right \(M_n\)-module endomorphism lies in

\[
\mathcal L_{M_n}(X)\cong\mathbb C.
\]

In particular, right multiplication by a general \(u\in U(n)\) is not a right-module map:

\[
R_u(xa)=xau,
\qquad
R_u(x)a=xua,
\]

which differ unless \(u\) is central.

An inner automorphism \(\operatorname{Ad}_u\) of the coefficient algebra can be represented by a twisted correspondence and an intertwiner, but the paper never constructs that twisted presentation as an object or arrow of \(\mathfrak S(D)\). Control 12.2’s assertion that the raw amplified presentation “admits inner \(U(n)\) changes” therefore does not follow from the declared 2-morphism type.

Theorem 8.1 proves equivalent W3 object data under an induced module presentation. It does not identify the raw spectator automorphism group or place its inner transformations inside the raw marked W3 groupoid.

### The physical-spectator discriminator is misstated

Lemma 3.2 says

\[
\Theta_X:\mathcal L_A(M)
\longrightarrow
\mathcal L_B(M\otimes_A X)
\]

is surjective. Proposition 8.2 at lines 896–906 assumes an operator of the transported module lies outside the image of \(\Theta_X\). Such an operator cannot exist on that same transported boundary chart.

The correct possible failure is that the operator’s unique preimage is not in the original **admitted marking**, not that it is outside the algebraic image of \(\Theta_X\).

Control 12.3 obtains a genuine physical spectator only by replacing the transported right \(M_n\)-module with the ordinary Hilbert space \(H\otimes\mathbb C^n\) over \(\mathbb C\). That is a different boundary chart with the larger algebra \(B(H)\otimes M_n\). The control is physically useful, but it does not prove Proposition 8.2 as written.

The paper also proves amplification only explicitly for

\[
\mathbb C\longleftrightarrow M_n.
\]

The pin required the general finite amplification

\[
A\longleftrightarrow A\otimes M_n.
\]

The general standard equivalence is available, but it is not constructed or checked against the complete marking here.

## Finding 4 — Critical: strict ineffective isotropy and natural Morita ineffectivity are conflated

**Type:** false implication and missing 2-categorical rigidification  
**Affects:** `RQ0-L0-EFFECTIVE-W3-SEAM-STACK`

Definition 9.2 at lines 927–939 places \(g\) in \(K_s\) only when its induced action is literally the identity on every marked datum and every addressability object and morphism.

Corollary 9.6 at lines 1001–1005 then says that inner spectator transformations **naturally isomorphic** to identity lie in \(K_s\).

That conclusion does not follow. Equality of a functor and natural isomorphism to the identity are different conditions.

### Exact counterexample

Let \(\mathcal C\) be the groupoid with two distinct isomorphic objects \(0,1\) and a unique arrow between any ordered pair. The swap functor

\[
F(0)=1,\qquad F(1)=0
\]

is not the identity functor, but there is a unique natural isomorphism

\[
F\Rightarrow\operatorname{Id}_{\mathcal C}.
\]

Under the strict criterion of Definition 9.2, the swap is not in the kernel because it moves both objects. Under the naturally-isomorphic criterion invoked in Corollary 9.6, it is ineffective.

Choosing the latter requires a 2-categorical kernel with specified trivializing 2-cells and coherence. The paper instead forms an ordinary quotient groupoid by strict equalities.

An even sharper fiber example is the one-object category \(BG\) for a nonabelian group \(G\). An inner automorphism

\[
g\longmapsto hgh^{-1}
\]

is naturally isomorphic to the identity functor on \(BG\), but acts nontrivially on morphisms. It cannot simultaneously be “identity on every addressability morphism” and merely naturally isomorphic to identity.

This is exactly the situation the spectator claim must resolve. It does not.

## Finding 5 — Major: the ineffective family is not proved to be a normal system at the stated weak-categorical scope

**Type:** missing subgroup proof and missing conjugation coherence  
**Affects:** rigidification

Lemma 9.3 at lines 941–953 proves conjugation stability only after assuming that every arrow \(f:s\to t\) strictly transports every marked datum and every addressability object and morphism.

The paper does not first prove that \(K_s\) is a subgroup. That conclusion would be immediate if there were a strict group action on a fixed product of data sets, but no such action is constructed. At Morita scope the transport is pseudofunctorial and depends on associators and quasi-inverses.

### Objectwise normality is insufficient

Consider a groupoid with isomorphic objects \(s,t\), automorphism groups

\[
\operatorname{Aut}(s)\cong C_2,
\qquad
\operatorname{Aut}(t)\cong C_2,
\]

and an isomorphism \(f:s\to t\). Let

\[
K_s=C_2,
\qquad
K_t=\{1\}.
\]

Each \(K_s\) and \(K_t\) is normal in its local isotropy group, but

\[
fK_sf^{-1}\ne K_t.
\]

The double-coset quotient does not define a groupoid. This shows why actual conjugation-stable transport, not local normality, is required.

The paper’s proof would exclude this example if the complete strict action existed. Since the W3 and addressability pseudofunctors are not constructed, the required hypothesis is unavailable.

Conditional on a genuine conjugation-stable family of subgroups, the double-coset computation at lines 983–995 is correct.

## Finding 6 — Major: the addressability pseudofunctor is not defined before it is used for rigidification

**Type:** circular order and missing descent data  
**Affects:** effective stack and full fibration

The ineffective kernel at lines 927–936 is defined using every object and morphism of the addressability category “defined below.” The rigidified base is then constructed. Only afterward, at lines 1027–1101, is the addressability category and its alleged pseudofunctor introduced.

A forward reference is not inherently circular. The coherent construction would be:

1. define \(\mathsf{Addr}\) on the raw marked seam groupoid;
2. define \(K_s\) as the kernel of that complete raw action;
3. prove strict or coherently trivial action of \(K\);
4. form the quotient; and
5. descend \(\mathsf{Addr}\).

The paper does not perform these steps. Theorem 10.4 says descent holds because ineffective automorphisms act identically “by Definition 9.2.” That is true only by having placed the desired descent conclusion inside the definition of \(K_s\). It does not construct the raw action or its coherence.

### Kernel-on-morphisms counterexample

Let the raw base contain an involution \(k\), and let a fiber be the one-object category \(BC_3\). Let \(k\) fix the sole object but act on fiber morphisms by inversion:

\[
g\longmapsto g^{-1}.
\]

If a kernel test observes only fiber objects, \(k\) appears ineffective, but the pseudofunctor does not descend through the quotient because it changes morphisms.

Definition 9.2 attempts to avoid this by mentioning every morphism. The paper never defines the raw pseudofunctor whose morphism action would make that test meaningful.

## Finding 7 — Major: the full addressability category has an undefined compatibility predicate

**Type:** incomplete category definition  
**Affects:** `RQ0-L0-FULL-ADDRESSABILITY-FIBRATION`

At lines 1043–1056, a morphism is an admitted CP map satisfying

\[

and

No equation defines that second condition.

Possible inequivalent meanings include:

\[

Proposition 10.3 proves closure of the corner equation, then says only that “record-interface compatibility composes.” Without a definition, category closure cannot be checked.

Theorem 10.4 also assumes that every marked Morita seam equivalence conjugates this unspecified condition, so its category equivalence and pseudofunctoriality are unproved.

### Variance check

The variance statement itself is correct conditionally:

- a contravariant pseudofunctor
  \[
  \mathfrak S_{\rm eff}^{op}\to\mathbf{Cat}
  \]
  yields a Grothendieck fibration;
- the covariant form yields an opfibration;
- because the base is a groupoid and transition functors are equivalences, both descriptions can coexist.

The paper does not make the common error of calling an arbitrary covariant Grothendieck construction a fibration.

A noninvertible base arrow would invalidate the automatic conversion by inverse. The chosen base is explicitly the maximal groupoid at lines 919–925, so noninvertible base arrows are outside the claim. This part passes.

The fibration still fails because its input pseudofunctor has not been constructed.

## Finding 8 — Moderate: the branch \(S_4\) conclusion is plausible, but the displayed lift is false

**Type:** exact proof error  
**Affects:** branch rigidification control

Lemma 13.5 at lines 1513–1545 claims that for every output permutation matrix \(P\),

\[
T_2=P,
\qquad
T_1=V^*PV,
\qquad
T_0=U^*T_1U
\]

satisfies all four intertwining equations.

This is false.

Use the paper’s ordered input basis, the availability basis at the cut and the corresponding ordered output basis. In these coordinates,

\[
N=I,\qquad V=I,\qquad U=A/2.
\]

The no-write and preserving intertwining equations force

\[
T_0=T_1=T_2.
\]

The write equation then requires

\[
PA=AP.
\]

Take \(P\) to transpose labels \(0\) and \(1\). Then

\[
(PA)_{0,2}=A_{1,2}=-1,
\qquad
(AP)_{0,2}=A_{0,2}=1.
\]

Hence \(PA\ne AP\), and the displayed \(T_i\) do not satisfy the claimed equations.

An independent monomial calculation shows why the high-level result may still be salvageable: for every underlying permutation \(P\), there is a diagonal sign correction \(D_P\), unique up to common phase, for which

\[
T_P=P D_P
\]

commutes with \(A\). Suitable phased adjacent-transposition lifts satisfy the \(S_4\) Coxeter relations. Thus the abstract \(U(1)\times S_4\) result is compatible with a corrected signed lift.

But that signed construction is not the proof in the immutable paper. The asserted direct substitution and the displayed homomorphism are wrong as written. Consequently Theorem 13.6 is not self-containedly established by Lemma 13.5, even though its two orbit types are consistent with the corrected action.

### Physical stabilizers

A symmetry can stabilize a coarse partition while permuting its fine alternatives. For example, the transposition within the two-element block of a \(2+1+1\) partition fixes the coarse object and acts nontrivially on the fine action. Definition 9.2 correctly excludes such an automorphism from \(K_s\). If the effective action were constructed correctly, this stabilizer would remain.

## Finding 9 — Moderate: “finite stack” is not justified for the general object

**Type:** scope inflation

The word *stack* is explicitly restricted to a retained-isotropy groupoid, and the paper does not infer a site, sheaf, topology or spatial atlas. That terminology ceiling is disciplined.

The adjective *finite* is not generally justified.

Finite-dimensional C*-algebras and finite outcome sets can still have:

- continuous automorphism groups;
- continuous families of classical actions;
- infinitely many marked Morita presentations; and
- infinite W3 object groupoids.

The branch benchmark has nine seam objects and, after removing its ineffective \(U(1)\), a finite effective \(S_4\) action. That one control supports a finite action groupoid. Definitions 9.1–9.5 do not restrict the general \(\mathfrak S(D)\) or its effective quotient to be finite.

The narrow phrase “groupoid-valued moduli object” would be supported conditionally. The registered “finite rigidification” is only demonstrated, at best, in the branch control.
