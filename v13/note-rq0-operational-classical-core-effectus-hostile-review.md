## 1. Reconstruction of the order-ideal argument

Let \(V\) be a finite-dimensional ordered real vector space with pointed generating cone \(V_+\), and suppose

\[
P^2=P,\qquad P\ge0,\qquad I-P\ge0.
\]

For \(0\le y\le x\) with \(x\in\operatorname{ran}P\),

\[
0\le(I-P)y\le(I-P)x=0.
\]

Pointedness gives \((I-P)y=0\), hence \(y\in\operatorname{ran}P\). Thus \(\operatorname{ran}P\) is solid.

It is also generated by its positive cone. If \(v\in\operatorname{ran}P\), write \(v=a-b\) with \(a,b\in V_+\). Then

\[
v=P(v)=P(a)-P(b),
\]

and both \(P(a)\) and \(P(b)\) are positive elements of the range. The same proof applies to \(\operatorname{ran}(I-P)\).

Therefore Lemma 5.1 is valid, including the often-omitted directedness/generation part.

## 2. Pairwise commutation

Let \(P,Q\) both have positive complements. If \(x\ge0\) lies in \(\operatorname{ran}P\), then

\[
Qx\ge0,\qquad x-Qx=(I-Q)x\ge0.
\]

Hence \(0\le Qx\le x\), so the order-ideal property puts \(Qx\) back in \(\operatorname{ran}P\). Generation extends this invariance to the whole range. Applying the same argument to \(\operatorname{ran}(I-P)\) shows that \(Q\) preserves both summands in

\[
V=\operatorname{ran}P\oplus\operatorname{ran}(I-P).
\]

Thus \(Q\) is block diagonal relative to \(P\), and

\[
PQ=QP.
\]

This proof uses only scalar positivity. Complete positivity is needed for the quantum operational interpretation and composition of physical branches, not for commutation itself.

Attempts to construct noncommuting examples necessarily dropped at least one binding condition:

- a positive idempotent whose complement is not positive can fail to commute with another;
- two arbitrary CP idempotents can fail to commute;
- incompatible partial effectus operations can lack a common composition.

None is an operational split projection in the paper’s sense.

## 3. Physical Boolean operations

For commuting split projections \(P,Q\), the algebraic formulas

\[
P\wedge Q=PQ,\qquad
P^\perp=I-P,\qquad
P\vee Q=P+Q-PQ
\]

are correct.

More importantly, the paper supplies operational implementations.

Sequentially running the two complete binary instruments produces the four branches

\[
PQ,\quad P(I-Q),\quad (I-P)Q,\quad (I-P)(I-Q).
\]

The meet is the first branch, while its complement is the coarse-graining of the other three. The join is the coarse-graining of every branch except \((I-P)(I-Q)\). Thus neither subtraction in \(I-PQ\) nor subtraction in \(P+Q-PQ\) is being treated as a physical primitive.

The common refinement is therefore constructed from:

1. complete retained flags;
2. sequential composition of those complete instruments; and
3. classical coarse-graining of their joint flag.

This is properly typed under the declared admission closure.

## 4. Finiteness, atomicity, and the finest core

Every idempotent has minimal polynomial dividing \(t(t-1)\), so it is diagonalizable. A commuting family of diagonalizable endomorphisms on a finite-dimensional complex vector space is simultaneously diagonalizable, including an initially infinite family.

In a common eigenbasis, every split projection has only zeros and ones on its diagonal. There are consequently only finitely many distinct split projections. Closure under meet, join, and complement makes them a finite Boolean algebra.

Its nonzero atoms \(A_i\) satisfy

\[
A_iA_j=\delta_{ij}A_i,
\qquad
\sum_iA_i=I.
\]

The same sequential-instrument construction produces their joint flagged instrument. Thus the atoms are not merely abstract Boolean elements; they are admitted CP branches.

For any other complete exclusive repeatable instrument \(\{m_r\}\):

- \(m_r\) is idempotent and CP;
- \(I-m_r=\sum_{s\ne r}m_s\) is an admitted CP coarse-graining;
- hence \(m_r\) belongs to the Boolean algebra;
- therefore \(m_r\) is the sum of exactly the atoms below it.

Every other instrument is a deterministic partition of the atomic instrument. The finest object is unique up to permutation.

No pair of incomparable finest instruments survives the declared closure assumptions.

## 5. Sharp postprocessing

For

\[
n_s=\sum_i\kappa(s\mid i)A_i,
\]

orthogonality gives

\[
n_s^2=\sum_i\kappa(s\mid i)^2A_i.
\]

Equality \(n_s^2=n_s\) and linear independence of the nonzero atoms imply

\[
\kappa(s\mid i)^2=\kappa(s\mid i).
\]

Thus each coefficient is \(0\) or \(1\), and stochastic normalization assigns each atom to exactly one output.

Corollary 5.5 is therefore correct: stochastic processing cannot create a sharper repeatable object than deterministic partition.

\[

## 8. Admission closure

The closure hypothesis is strong but legitimate and candidate-independent.

It does not assert that a desired common refinement exists merely because its algebraic formula is useful. It asserts that if two complete typed instruments are physically admitted, the law also permits their sequential experiment with both flags retained and permits ordinary classical postprocessing of those flags.

That is ordinary compositional closure for this declared process category.

A restricted laboratory grammar may admit two instruments separately while refusing their sequential composition. In that case two incomparable maximal instruments can remain, exactly as Section 9.2 states. Such a law fails the positive theorem’s scope; it does not refute the theorem.

For \(M_d\),

\[
0\le J(P)\le J(I)=|\Omega_d\rangle\langle\Omega_d|.
\]

Rank-one domination gives \(J(P)=\lambda J(I)\), hence \(P=\lambda I\). Idempotence makes \(\lambda\in\{0,1\}\). The core is therefore trivial.

Idempotence reduces each scalar to \(0\) or \(1\). When the block instruments are admitted, the operational atoms are exactly the minimal central blocks.

The proof equates coefficients of the atomic maps without explicitly proving their linear independence.

The missing sentence is immediate: if

\[

choose a nonzero vector in \(\operatorname{ran}A_j\) and apply the equality to it; orthogonality leaves \(c_jx=0\), hence \(c_j=0\).

### Scope clarification S1 — closure means complete flagged-instrument closure

Theorem 5.4’s phrase “closed under sequential composition” must be read as closure of the complete retained-flag instruments, not merely closure of their unflagged branch maps.

The pin, Definitions 4.1 and 4.3, Proposition 5.3, and Sections 9 and 12 make that intended meaning explicit. Under that reading, physical admission of the Boolean refinements is proved. Without it, the core can be nonunique and the positive rung is unavailable.

The commutation theorem applies only to projections \(P\) for which both \(P\) and \(I-P\) are positive, with physical split admission added operationally. Arbitrary CP idempotents are outside the theorem.
