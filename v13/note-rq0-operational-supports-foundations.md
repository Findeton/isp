## Abstract

A stable record does not come with a physical region attached. Pairing a W3 witness with a separately supplied list of operations therefore does not discover localization; it supplies it. We prove this as a non-identifiability theorem. We then replace external support by an internal construction. Every preparation, alternative projector, record projector, write, preserving continuation, eraser, no-write control and final probe in a W3 witness is required to be an arrow or effect of one finite amplitude instrument. The smallest composition-closed operational subinstrument containing that diagram exists and is unique for the fixed diagram. It is natural under presentation isomorphisms and covariant under the composition-compatible boundary gauge.

Different internal W3 diagrams can nevertheless generate different minimal envelopes. Exact automorphisms can exchange them, and no gauge-natural rule can select one without an invariant discriminator. The correct general object is therefore an action groupoid of pointed quantum charts, not a preferred set of regions.

Physical overlap is defined separately as a categorical pullback of already constructed instrument embeddings. Shared handles, equal laws and planted atom intersections have no standing. A contravariant record assignment is defined only on record-compatible chart maps and only from the W3-certified projectors. We also give an exact direct-sum counterexample: two amplitude instruments have identical realized amplitudes and record laws but different counterfactual completions and different intrinsic chart groupoids. Localization is consequently nomological at this scope; realized history alone does not determine it.

The result is a pre-spatial foundation. It identifies intrinsic quantum process charts and the obstruction to making them a pullback-closed physical cover. It does not identify those charts with places in spacetime.

## 2. Finite amplitude instruments

### Definition 2.1 — exact operational presentation

A **finite exact amplitude instrument** is

$$
\mathfrak D=
(\widetilde{\mathcal A}_{D},\rho_D,\mathsf{Prep}_D,
\mathsf{Eff}_D,\mathsf{Proj}_D,\approx_D,G_D).
$$

The components are:

1. a finite typed category $\widetilde{\mathcal A}_D$ with a distinguished unit boundary $\mathbf 1$;
2. an exact linear representation
   $$
   \rho_D:\widetilde{\mathcal A}_D\longrightarrow\mathbf{FinHilb}_{\mathbb C};
   $$
3. admitted preparations $\eta:\mathbf 1\to x$, effects or probes $f:x\to\mathbf 1$, and orthogonal projectors $P:x\to x$;
4. a typed composition congruence $\approx_D$ representing operational equivalence in every declared amplitude context;
5. a finite composition-compatible boundary-gauge group $G_D$ acting by natural unitary changes of boundary presentation.

The exact finite scope is deliberate. Hom-sets, preparation/effect families, projector families, the quotient by $\approx_D$ and the gauge group are all finite. The congruence condition means

$$
a\approx_D a',\quad b\approx_D b'
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
ba\approx_D b'a'
$$

whenever the composites are typed. It is also required to preserve every zero/nonzero closed-amplitude question used below. Without that requirement the W3 predicates would not be well defined on operational classes.

The category contains the complete declared counterfactual process law, not only the arrow used in one realized run. No coordinate, tensor-factor name, causal relation or support label is part of Definition 2.1.

### Definition 2.2 — strong instrument map

A **strong map** $F:\mathfrak A\to\mathfrak B$ consists of:

1. a typed functor $F:\widetilde{\mathcal A}_A\to\widetilde{\mathcal A}_B$;
2. an isometry $J_x:\rho_A(x)\to\rho_B(Fx)$ for every boundary;
3. the exact intertwining equations
   $$
   J_y\rho_A(a)=\rho_B(Fa)J_x
   $$
   for every $a:x\to y$;
4. preservation of admitted preparations, effects, projectors, identities and compositions;
5. reflection of $\approx$ on its image;
6. an equivariant map of the declared boundary-gauge actions.

For a projector $P$ carried by the map, the induced effect restriction is

$$
P^A=J_x^\dagger P^B J_x.
$$

A **strong embedding** is a strong map injective on boundary objects and operational arrow classes. An isomorphism has a strong inverse. These objects and maps form the category

$$
\mathbf{QIns}^{\mathrm{str}}_{\mathrm{fin}}.
$$

Composition is ordinary functor composition with composed boundary isometries. The intertwining, projector and gauge equations compose directly, so this is a category rather than a vocabulary attached to matrices.

### Remark 2.3 — what has been postulated

The analytical result below is conditional on that input and tests what follows from it. This is stronger than a realized-history rule and weaker than a derivation of the complete operational law.

## 3. An entirely internal W3 diagram

### Definition 3.1 — typed diagram

An **internal W3 diagram** $W$ in $\mathfrak D$ begins with three boundaries $x_0,x_1,x_2$, a candidate write $U$, a candidate no-write anchor $N$, and two admitted projector resolutions. Its preparation and probe families are the complete admitted families at the selected boundaries; they are not subsets chosen after inspecting the W3 result:

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\eta_j:\mathbf 1\to x_0 &&\text{preparations},\\
&U:x_0\to x_1 &&\text{write},\\
&N:x_0\to x_1 &&\text{no-write control},\\
&T:x_1\to x_2 &&\text{every admitted continuation of this type},\\
&f_i:x_2\to\mathbf 1 &&\text{availability probes}.
\end{aligned}
$$

At the cut $x_1$ it contains an admitted fine alternative resolution $\{Q_k\}_{k\in K}$ and a nontrivial admitted record resolution $\{P_r\}_{r\in R}$ with a declared refinement map $s:K\to R$ such that

$$
P_r=\sum_{s(k)=r}Q_k,
\qquad
Q_kQ_\ell=\delta_{k\ell}Q_k,
\qquad
\sum_kQ_k=\sum_rP_r=I_{x_1}.
$$

The projectors and every composite appearing below must themselves be meaningful internal arrows. The level-set map $s$ is determined by the exact projector refinement; it is not an operation-support label. The preserve and erase families will be derived by testing every admitted $T:x_1\to x_2$ rather than supplied as roles.

### Definition 3.2 — occurrence and availability

The write is perfectly correlated with the record when

$$
Q_kU\eta_j\ne0,\quad Q_\ell U\eta_j\ne0,\quad k\ne\ell
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
s(k)\ne s(\ell)
$$

for every admitted preparation $\eta_j$. This is $H_{\mathrm{corr}}(U,W)$.

A continuation $V:x_1\to x_2$ preserves availability when

$$
f_iVQ_k\ne0,\quad f_iVQ_\ell\ne0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
s(k)=s(\ell)
$$

for every admitted availability probe $f_i$. This is $H_{\mathrm{avail}}(V,W)$.

Historical occurrence and continuation-relative availability are therefore

$$
\mathrm{Occ}(W)=H_{\mathrm{corr}}(U,W),
\qquad
\mathrm{Avail}(W;V)=H_{\mathrm{avail}}(V,W).
$$

They are not the same predicate.

### Definition 3.3 — derived preserve, erase and no-write classes

For the fixed boundaries, preparations, probes, write and projector resolutions, define

$$
\mathsf{Pres}_D(W)=
\{T:x_1\to x_2:H_{\mathrm{avail}}(T,W)=1\},
$$

and let $\mathsf{Erase}_D(W)$ contain every admitted $T:x_1\to x_2$ that fails availability and has at least one nonzero cross-sector term

   $$
   C^{T}_{ijk\ell}
   =
   (f_iTQ_kU\eta_j)
   \overline{(f_iTQ_\ell U\eta_j)}\ne0,
   \qquad s(k)\ne s(\ell);
   $$

The remaining admitted continuations form

$$
\mathsf{Other}_D(W)=
\operatorname{Hom}_D(x_1,x_2)
\setminus
(\mathsf{Pres}_D(W)\cup\mathsf{Erase}_D(W)).
$$

They are retained rather than silently dropped; they are simply not certified as preserving or as witnessed coherent erasers by this diagram.

The diagram is a **W3-positive internal diagram** when:

1. $H_{\mathrm{corr}}(U,W)$ holds;
2. $\mathsf{Pres}_D(W)$ and $\mathsf{Erase}_D(W)$ are both nonempty;
3. the candidate no-write anchor is internal and fails correlation:
   $$
   H_{\mathrm{corr}}(N,W)=0.
   $$

Thus the same internal process family contains a record-writing seam, tests every admitted continuation of the selected type, and contains a coherent route that can reveal the phase relation again. A witness cannot become positive by omitting an inconvenient continuation from a hand-picked preserve family. A separately attached memory witness does not satisfy this definition.

### Lemma 3.4 — presentation and gauge invariance

The predicates in Definitions 3.2 and 3.3 are invariant under strong instrument isomorphism and covariant under composition-compatible boundary gauge.

*Proof.* A strong isomorphism sends every displayed composite to the corresponding composite and is injective, so zero and nonzero are preserved. Boundary gauge inserts a unitary and its inverse on each internal cut. They cancel in every closed scalar $fVU\eta$ and conjugate every projector resolution as a whole. Orthogonality, refinement, zero support and the existence of a nonzero cross-sector scalar are unchanged. $\square$

### Definition 3.5 — internal record algebra

For a W3-positive diagram, its persistent factual interface is

$$
\operatorname{Rec}(W)
=
\operatorname{Bool}\{P_r:r\in R\}.
$$

This definition is made from the same projectors used by $H_{\mathrm{corr}}$ and $H_{\mathrm{avail}}$. There is no second ambient projector resolution. Erasure changes availability but does not retroactively remove $\mathrm{Occ}(W)$.

## 4. The support-smuggling theorem

### Definition 4.1 — externally supported schema

An externally supported W3 schema is a triple

$$
(\mathfrak D,W,\sigma),
$$

where $(\mathfrak D,W)$ is an internal amplitude instrument and W3 diagram, while

$$
\sigma(W)\subseteq\operatorname{Arr}(\widetilde{\mathcal A}_D)
$$

is supplied independently as the operations said to support the record. Let

$$
\pi(\mathfrak D,W,\sigma)=(\mathfrak D,W)
$$

forget that label.

A localization rule is **intrinsic to the stated physical input** only if it factors through $\pi$ up to isomorphism. Equivalently, it must be constant, up to the declared physical isomorphisms, on every fibre of $\pi$.

### Theorem 4.2 — support smuggling

Let $L$ be a rule on externally supported W3 schemas. Suppose there are two labels $\sigma_0,\sigma_1$ on the same pair $(\mathfrak D,W)$ for which

$$
L(\mathfrak D,W,\sigma_0)
\not\simeq
L(\mathfrak D,W,\sigma_1).
$$

Then $L$ is not determined by the amplitude instrument and W3 diagram. In particular, no intrinsic rule $\bar L$ can satisfy

$$
L=\bar L\circ\pi.
$$

If $\sigma_0$ and $\sigma_1$ are exchanged by an automorphism of $(\mathfrak D,W)$, selecting either one also violates gauge-naturalness.

*Proof.* Both externally supported schemas have exactly the same image under $\pi$. If $L=\bar L\circ\pi$, then both outputs equal $\bar L(\mathfrak D,W)$ up to the declared isomorphism, contradicting the hypothesis. For the last statement, naturality under an automorphism $g$ would require the selected output to be fixed by $g$, whereas $g$ exchanges the two candidates. $\square$

### Corollary 4.3 — what the theorem does and does not forbid

An `access_operations` field can describe a region already established by independent physics. It cannot be evidence that the W3 witness generated that region. The theorem is not a no-go result against localization from internal process structure; it is a no-go result against claiming that an independently supplied support label was reconstructed.

### Q8 application

In the opened public `Q8` construction, the two-level W3 witness is built separately from the quaternion operation family. Copies of that witness are paired with the four lists labelled `core`, `i`, `j` and `k` through `access_operations`. Changing one such list changes the claimed record scope without changing the witness's write, preserve, erase, no-write, projectors, preparations or probes.

Theorem 4.2 therefore applies directly. The three cyclic order-four subgroups and their central order-two intersection are genuine algebraic objects, but the attachment of a given W3 witness to one of them is supplied rather than derived. The current `Q8` construction is a support-smuggling negative for intrinsic localization, not a negative against its own finite group arithmetic.

## 5. Minimal internal operational envelopes

### Definition 5.1 — closed subinstrument

Work first in one exact presentation of $\mathfrak D$, with arrow classes saturated under $\approx_D$. A **closed subinstrument** $\mathfrak A\hookrightarrow\mathfrak D$ consists of selected boundary objects, arrow classes, preparations, effects and projectors such that:

1. every selected arrow has selected source and target boundaries;
2. every selected boundary has its identity;
3. every composite in $\mathfrak D$ of selected composable arrows is selected;
4. operationally equivalent representatives are not separated;
5. every selected preparation, effect and projector retains its declared type and every relation among selected data;
6. the inclusion is a strong embedding.

Gauge does not mean that every representative moved by $G_D$ must be inserted into one chart. That union would turn symmetry-related alternatives into one larger process and could erase the very ambiguity being measured. Instead, the family of closed presentations is required to be $G_D$-equivariant, and the physical object retains the resulting gauge orbit or action groupoid.

### Definition 5.2 — generated envelope

Let $\operatorname{Gen}(W)$ be the finite typed family containing every selected boundary, every admitted preparation and probe at those boundaries, every alternative and record projector, the write and no-write arrows, and every admitted continuation $T:x_1\to x_2$ tested by the internal W3 diagram—including the preserve, witnessed-erasure and `Other` classes.

The **minimal internal operational envelope** is

$$
\operatorname{Env}_D(W)
=
\bigcap\{\mathfrak A\hookrightarrow\mathfrak D:
\mathfrak A\text{ is closed and }\operatorname{Gen}(W)\subseteq\mathfrak A\}.
$$

No operation-support label occurs in this definition.

### Theorem 5.3 — existence and uniqueness for a fixed diagram

For every internal W3 diagram $W$ in a finite exact amplitude instrument $\mathfrak D$, $\operatorname{Env}_D(W)$ exists and is the unique smallest closed subinstrument containing $W$.

*Proof.* The ambient instrument is one closed subinstrument containing $W$, so the family being intersected is nonempty. Intersections preserve typed boundaries, identities, composition closure, congruence saturation and the distinguished relations because each requirement is universal: if all members contain the required datum, so does their intersection. The intersection is therefore closed and contains $W$. It lies in every other such subinstrument by construction, proving minimality and uniqueness as an embedded subobject. Because the operational universe is finite, the same object is reached by iteratively adjoining missing identities, composites and congruence partners until no rule fires. $\square$

### Theorem 5.4 — presentation invariance and gauge covariance

Let $F:\mathfrak D\to\mathfrak D'$ be a strong instrument isomorphism. Then

$$
F\bigl(\operatorname{Env}_D(W)\bigr)
=
\operatorname{Env}_{D'}\bigl(FW\bigr).
$$

In particular, for $g\in G_D$,

$$
g\operatorname{Env}_D(W)=\operatorname{Env}_D(gW).
$$

*Proof.* The image under $F$ of a closed subinstrument is closed, and it contains $FW$ whenever the source contains $W$. Hence the right-hand side is contained in the left-hand side. Applying the same argument to $F^{-1}$ gives the reverse containment. Boundary gauge is a special strong presentation isomorphism. $\square$

### Corollary 5.5 — record-handle invariance

Renaming a record handle while preserving the internal arrows, projector resolution and their typed relations leaves the envelope isomorphic. The construction never reads the handle.

### 5.1 Where uniqueness stops

Theorem 5.3 proves uniqueness for one fixed internal diagram. It does not prove that an ambient instrument has one preferred W3 diagram or one preferred envelope.

Let $\mathsf{W3}(D)$ be the finite set of all W3-positive internal diagrams of $\mathfrak D$. Distinct elements can:

- use different record resolutions;
- use the same resolution with different preserve/erase families;
- generate the same envelope;
- generate incomparable envelopes;
- generate only the whole ambient instrument.

Supplying only the Boolean algebra $\operatorname{Rec}(W)$ therefore need not determine the operational envelope. The dynamics in the internal diagram are load-bearing.

### Definition 5.6 — pointed chart groupoid

The **pointed intrinsic chart groupoid** is the action groupoid

$$
\mathsf{Chart}_{W3}(D)
=
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak D)\ltimes
\{(W,\operatorname{Env}_D(W)\hookrightarrow\mathfrak D):
W\in\mathsf{W3}(D)\}.
$$

An arrow carries one internal diagram and its envelope to another by an ambient strong automorphism. Repeated presentations of the same physical diagram are therefore isomorphic rather than counted as new places.

A chart is **proper** when its envelope is neither the trivial identity instrument nor all of $\mathfrak D$. It is **operationally nontrivial** when it contains at least one write/preserve distinction and its eraser control is internal.

The construction always returns the full groupoid when $\mathsf{W3}(D)$ is nonempty. It returns no proper chart when every W3 envelope equals $\mathfrak D$. It never chooses one orbit representative by convention.

## 6. Three notions of a process chart

The generated closed envelope is the weakest of three candidate notions. The alternatives are useful but strictly stronger in different directions.

### 6.1 Closed subinstrument

The closed-subinstrument notion asks only that the complete internal W3 experiment and all of its required composites remain executable. Theorem 5.3 gives it a canonical generated object for each diagram.

Its limitation is physical: closure alone does not assert that the chart can be independently prepared, discarded or isolated from the complement. It is an operational fragment, not yet an independently controllable subsystem.

### 6.2 Split operational retract

A **split operational retract** is a pair of admissible maps

$$
i:\mathfrak A\longrightarrow\mathfrak D,
\qquad
p:\mathfrak D\longrightarrow\mathfrak A,
\qquad
pi=\operatorname{id}_{\mathfrak A}.
$$

The map $p$ supplies physical structure absent from a mere inclusion: a way to forget, reset or condition away the complement while preserving the declared process equations.

Splitness is not automatic. In the operator-algebraic analogue, the unital inclusion

$$
\mathbb C I\hookrightarrow M_2(\mathbb C)
$$

has no unital $*$-homomorphic retraction. If $p:M_2(\mathbb C)\to\mathbb C$ were such a map, its kernel would be a two-sided ideal. Any nonzero ideal of $M_2(\mathbb C)$ contains a nonzero matrix entry and, after multiplication by matrix units on both sides, every matrix unit; hence it is the whole algebra. Unitality rules out the whole kernel, so the kernel would be zero, which would inject a four-dimensional algebra into a one-dimensional one. A trace or conditional expectation exists as a channel, but it is not multiplicative. Thus the answer depends on which physical maps the theory admits.

Intersections of split retracts need not inherit compatible retractions. Consequently, split charts do not form a closure system without an additional theorem.

### 6.3 Operator-algebraic subsystem

For a boundary $x$, the represented endomorphism arrows of a closed envelope generate a finite operator algebra

$$
\mathcal O_W(x)
=
\operatorname{alg}\{\rho_D(a):a\in\operatorname{End}(x)\cap
\operatorname{Env}_D(W)\}.
$$

This algebra retains noncommutative internal dynamics, while $\operatorname{Rec}(W)$ is a derived commutative subalgebra generated by the record projectors.

An abstract $*$-subalgebra is not by itself an operational chart. It may lack admitted preparations, effects, interventions and a channel implementing its restriction. It can also be moved by an ambient unitary automorphism while all displayed algebraic dimensions remain unchanged. An operator-algebraic candidate becomes a process chart only when it is tied to an executable strong embedding or retract and contains an internal W3 diagram.

### 6.4 Comparison

| criterion | closed envelope | split retract | operator algebra |
|---|---|---|---|
| generated from one internal W3 diagram | yes | not automatically | only after operational generation |
| existence at the declared finite scope | always | may fail | generated algebra exists |
| unique for fixed diagram | yes | splitting may be nonunique | representation may be nonunique |
| presentation/gauge covariance | Theorem 5.4 | only if the split maps transform | only with represented operational maps |
| independently discardable complement | not claimed | explicitly supplied | requires a channel/expectation |
| pullback stability | for fixed-ambient closed embeddings | not general | algebra intersections exist, operational realization may fail |
| dormant-completion sensitivity | new diagrams can appear | retractions can appear/disappear | full algebra can change |

The analytical baseline is therefore the generated closed envelope. Split and operator-algebraic structures are retained as additional certificates, not silently built into the word chart.

## 7. Physical overlap as a pullback

### Definition 7.1 — physical pullback

Let

$$
\mathfrak A\xrightarrow{i_A}\mathfrak D
\xleftarrow{i_B}\mathfrak B
$$

be a cospan of strong embeddings in $\mathbf{QIns}^{\mathrm{str}}_{\mathrm{fin}}$. A **physical overlap** is a pullback object $\mathfrak P$ with strong maps

$$
\mathfrak P\xrightarrow{p_A}\mathfrak A,
\qquad
\mathfrak P\xrightarrow{p_B}\mathfrak B
$$

such that

$$
i_Ap_A=i_Bp_B,
$$

and for every admissible instrument $\mathfrak X$ and compatible cone $a:\mathfrak X\to\mathfrak A$, $b:\mathfrak X\to\mathfrak B$ satisfying $i_Aa=i_Bb$, there is a unique strong map $u:\mathfrak X\to\mathfrak P$ with

$$
p_Au=a,
\qquad
p_Bu=b.
$$

The quantifier ranges over the independently specified map category, not over a claimant-supplied list of test objects.

### Lemma 7.2 — uniqueness and presentation independence

If a physical pullback exists, it is unique up to unique strong isomorphism. Replacing any object of the cospan by an isomorphic presentation produces an isomorphic pullback.

*Proof.* The standard two universal arrows between two pullbacks are inverse by uniqueness. Transporting the cospan along isomorphisms transports its cones and therefore its representing object. $\square$

### Theorem 7.3 — fixed-ambient closed-subinstrument pullbacks

Fix $\mathfrak D$ and consider the poset category $\mathsf{Sub}_{\mathrm{cl}}(D)$ of its closed subinstruments and strong inclusion maps. For any two objects $\mathfrak A,\mathfrak B$, their common closed substructure

$$
\mathfrak A\wedge_D\mathfrak B
$$

is their categorical pullback over $\mathfrak D$.

*Proof.* The common substructure is closed because identities, compositions, congruence saturation and typed distinguished relations are each preserved under intersection. Its inclusions into $\mathfrak A$ and $\mathfrak B$ commute in $\mathfrak D$. In a poset, any compatible cone is precisely a closed subinstrument contained in both, hence contained in their greatest lower bound. The mediator is unique. $\square$

The concrete intersection is licensed only after the strong embeddings into one ambient instrument have been established. For independently presented charts, one first constructs the cospan maps and then transports to their images. Equality of source handles cannot replace those maps.

### 7.1 Limits of the theorem

Theorem 7.3 proves process-level pullbacks in one closure category. It does not prove that:

- the pullback contains a W3-positive diagram;
- two pulled-back record resolutions agree;
- a family of charts jointly generates the ambient instrument;
- split retracts or conditional expectations descend;
- every cospan in the larger category of strong maps has a pullback.

If the pullback exists only as a process with no stable shared record, the result is `PROCESS-OVERLAP-ONLY`. If the chosen category lacks a pullback, the result is `NO-PHYSICAL-PULLBACK`. Neither may be rewritten as fact descent.

## 8. Record descent only after charts and maps

### Definition 8.1 — record-compatible chart map

Let $(\mathfrak A,W_A)$ and $(\mathfrak B,W_B)$ be pointed charts. A strong map $F:\mathfrak A\to\mathfrak B$ is **record-compatible** when:

1. it maps the write, preserve, erase, no-write, preparation and probe diagrams with the required typed intertwiners;
2. its boundary isometry $J$ reduces every target record projector;
3. the exact pullbacks
   $$
   P_r^A=J^\dagger P_r^B J
   $$
   form the W3-certified record resolution of $W_A$;
4. value semantics, when present, are carried by an independently declared Boolean-algebra map rather than inferred from equal probabilities.

The reducing condition prevents compression of orthogonal target projectors from becoming a nonprojective POVM by accident.

Let $\mathbf{WChart}^{\mathrm{rec}}(D)$ be the category of pointed charts and record-compatible maps.

### Theorem 8.2 — the derived record functor

There is a contravariant functor

$$
\operatorname{Rec}:
\mathbf{WChart}^{\mathrm{rec}}(D)^{\mathrm{op}}
\longrightarrow
\mathbf{BoolAlg}_{\mathrm{fin}}
$$

defined on objects by

$$
\operatorname{Rec}(\mathfrak A,W_A)
=
\operatorname{Bool}\{P_r^A\}
$$

and on a map $F$ by projector pullback.

*Proof.* Record compatibility makes the pullback of the target resolution exactly the source resolution, so Boolean complements, meets and joins are preserved. For the identity isometry, $I^\dagger PI=P$. For composable maps with boundary isometries $J_F,J_G$,

$$
(J_GJ_F)^\dagger P(J_GJ_F)
=
J_F^\dagger(J_G^\dagger PJ_G)J_F,
$$

which is the direct-versus-composite law. Gauge covariance follows from conjugating $P$ and $J$ together. Since handles never occur, handle renaming produces a naturally isomorphic functor. $\square$

### Corollary 8.3 — fact descent is not token descent

When the three pullback paths of record-compatible chart maps agree, the Boolean propositions descend. Nothing in Theorem 8.2 identifies the physical occurrence tokens that carry them. Exact token automorphisms can therefore leave `Rec` set-valued while token descent remains groupoid-valued.

### 8.1 Why `Rec` is conditional

Not every strong chart map is record-compatible, and not every process pullback carries a common W3 resolution. The honest structure can therefore be a partial record fibration or a pseudofunctor over only the record-compatible subcategory. Extending `Rec` to every process map would require a new theorem, not a default convention.

## 9. Exact symmetry and the impossibility of canonical selection

### Theorem 9.1 — automorphism no-selection

Let a group $G\le\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak D)$ act transitively on a finite family $\mathcal C$ of candidate pointed charts. If $|\mathcal C|>1$, there is no $G$-natural rule selecting one element of $\mathcal C$.

*Proof.* A selection from the unchanged ambient object must be fixed by every automorphism: $g\,s(\mathfrak D)=s(\mathfrak D)$ for all $g\in G$. A transitive action on more than one element has no globally fixed element. Hence no natural selection exists. $\square$

### Corollary 9.2 — the honest output

When the permitted data contain no invariant discriminator, the output is the action groupoid

$$
G\ltimes\mathcal C,
$$

or the corresponding full orbit inside $\mathsf{Chart}_{W3}(D)$. Naming one chart is gauge fixing. It is not a physical localization result.

An invariant discriminator can split the orbit—for example, a genuinely different accessible continuation law—but only if that discriminator is internal to the amplitude instrument and survives the referent and no-smuggling gates. A coordinate, tensor-factor name or supplied region label does not qualify.

### Remark 9.3 — family invariance is not member selection

The set of all symmetry-related charts can be invariant even though no member is. Thus the theorem does not force the answer “nothing exists.” It forces the type of the answer to retain the symmetry. A fully symmetric model can yield either a nontrivial chart groupoid or, if no proper internal envelope exists at all, no proper localization.

## 10. Counterfactual completion and the grounding of localization

### 10.1 An exact W3 seed

Take the four-configuration branch-memory seed on

$$
H=\mathbb C^2\otimes\mathbb C^2
$$

with

$$
U=\operatorname{CNOT}(H_2\otimes I),
\qquad
V=H_2\otimes I,
$$

$$
E=(H_2\otimes I)\operatorname{CNOT},
\qquad
N=H_2\otimes I,
$$

where $H_2$ is the exact Hadamard matrix. Use all basis preparations and probes, the rank-one configuration alternatives, and the two record sectors of the memory bit.

This is an internal W3 diagram:

- $U$ passes correlation;
- $V$ preserves availability;
- $E$ fails availability and restores cross-sector coherence;
- $N$ fails correlation.

Let $\mathfrak A$ be the finite closed instrument generated by this diagram. By construction,

$$
\operatorname{Env}_A(W)=\mathfrak A.
$$

### 10.2 Two completions of one realized amplitude process

Put two copies of each boundary space side by side:

$$
\widehat H=H\oplus H.
$$

For every seed arrow $a$, define the synchronized arrow

$$
\Delta(a)=a\oplus a.
$$

For a seed preparation $\eta$ and effect $f$, define the normalized synchronized preparation and effect

$$
\widehat\eta=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}(\eta\oplus\eta),
\qquad

Then every realized amplitude agrees exactly with the seed:

$$
\widehat f\,\Delta(a)\,\widehat\eta=f a\eta.
$$

The synchronized record resolution is $\Delta(P_r)=P_r\oplus P_r$, so the realized W3 decisions and record probabilities also agree exactly.

Now define two complete counterfactual instruments.

1. **Synchronized completion $\mathfrak D_{\Delta}$.** It admits only the synchronized process arrows, synchronized preparations/effects and synchronized projector resolutions generated by $\Delta(W)$. Block injections, block projectors and independently chosen block operations are absent.
2. **Independently addressable completion $\mathfrak D_{\times}$.** It contains the same synchronized subinstrument and also the two block embeddings of $\mathfrak A$, their block preparations/effects/projectors, and independently selectable arrows $a\oplus I$ and $I\oplus a$. Their composites contain $a\oplus b$.

Let $\mathfrak R$ be the full subinstrument generated by the synchronized arrows, the actual synchronized preparations/effects and the synchronized record resolution. Define the **realized restriction** of either completion to be its restriction along the common embedding of $\mathfrak R$. These restrictions are strongly isomorphic. The realized experiment therefore cannot distinguish the two completions.

### Theorem 10.1 — localization-grounding counterexample

There exist two finite exact amplitude instruments with identical realized preparation-to-readout amplitudes and identical realized W3 record laws but non-equivalent intrinsic chart structures.

*Proof.* Use $\mathfrak D_{\Delta}$ and $\mathfrak D_{\times}$ above. Their realized restrictions are both $\mathfrak R$. Equality of every realized closed amplitude follows from

$$
\widehat f\,\Delta(a)\,\widehat\eta=f a\eta
$$

and from the common projector resolution $P_r\oplus P_r$.

In $\mathfrak D_{\Delta}$, the internal synchronized W3 diagram generates every admitted nontrivial arrow by the assumption that $W$ generates $\mathfrak A$. Its envelope is the whole synchronized instrument. There is no admitted block injection, block preparation, block effect or block projector from which a proper copy could be generated.

In $\mathfrak D_{\times}$, each block contains an internal copy $W_1,W_2$ of the seed, with strong embeddings into the ambient direct sum. Their envelopes are two proper subinstruments. The block-swap automorphism exchanges them, so they occur as two objects of a nontrivial chart groupoid.

The two chart groupoids are not equivalent as embedded-chart structures: one has no proper W3 envelope, while the other has two proper envelopes. Yet the realized amplitudes and records are identical. $\square$

### Corollary 10.2 — localization is nomological at this scope

No localization rule that reads only realized amplitudes and record laws can reproduce intrinsic chart structure for both completions. At least some counterfactual preparation, intervention, composition, probe or projector structure is load-bearing.

This result does not say that every dormant matrix completion is physical. It says that the theory must specify which counterfactual operations are physically admissible or accept underdetermination. Permanently inaccessible raw columns cannot be promoted merely because they occur in a matrix representation.

### 10.3 The grounding trilemma

The counterexample leaves three coherent positions.

1. **Nomological grounding:** the full operational amplitude law individuates charts, including unused but admissible interventions.
2. **Additional provenance:** some new physical structure selects which counterfactual operations belong together.
3. **Groupoid or underdetermination:** where the admitted law has exact symmetry, all compatible chart structures remain.

A realized-record-only unique localization rule is not a fourth option.
