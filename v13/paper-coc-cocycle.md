## Abstract

The transport geometry measured so far is **two-chart**: every loop runs
between a pair of frames. Real atlases have many charts glued pairwise, and the
next structural floor is **triple** consistency. For charts $A$, $B$, $C$ with
admitted pairwise identifications the composite

$$\delta \;=\; g_{CA}\circ g_{BC}\circ g_{AB}$$

is the **cocycle defect**. The question with content beyond the two-chart
measurements is whether every triangle defect is generated by the bigon
holonomies — the connection is the whole story — or whether some triangle
carries structure **outside** the bigon-generated group.

Base G carries two frames per setting, so a third chart must be built from the
base's own admitted material. The chart rule is: **the orbit of the two
committed frames under a declared admitted group, with the identification
search running over that same group.** Base G declares **two** such groups, and
the rule is applied to both.

The unit's central result is the criterion that decides what each atlas does:

> **The cocycle closes at an atlas if and only if the chart-generating group
> centralises the level-1 holonomy.**

Closure is therefore not a property of the connection. The defect **telescopes**
to a conjugate, by the *first* chart's own relabelling, of a product of
committed-frame maps; a generating group that centralises the level-1 holonomy
makes that conjugation trivial and nothing can leave, whatever the geometry
does; a generating group that does not makes an escaping conjugate available,
and whether an admissible triangle realises it is then a measurement. Both
halves are gated, at both atlases.

At the group the 162-element relabelling scope admits — order two, measured
abelian — the atlas has **4** charts and **1,824** admissible triangles, and
**0** of them escape. That zero is **analytically forced**, and it is reported
as a disclosure, not as a measurement.

At the group base G's declared 216-element extension admits — order eight,
measured **non-abelian**, exactly **4 of its 8** elements centralising the wing
exchange — the same chart rule, the same four-clause predicate, the same two
gluing rules, the same coordinates and the same pinned completion give **16**
charts and **333,312** admissible triangles, of which **32,256** carry a defect
**outside** the bigon-generated holonomy group: **16,128** at each of the two
geometry-bearing settings, at three of the four checkpoints, 5,376 per cell. The
escaping element is named: the wing exchange composed with **both pointer
transpositions**, of order 2 with 9 fixed configurations, inside base G's
declared 216-element extension, outside its 162-element relabelling scope, and
outside the Klein four-group the two-chart loops generate.

What is forced and what is free is separated. At the four settings whose
connection is measured **flat** the admitted set is measured **not** contained
in the trivial group, so those **576** triangles are the ones a naive reading
would call free. They are not: the orbit construction telescopes the defect to a
product of two committed-frame maps on one unordered pair, and at multiplicity
one those two maps coincide, so the identity is forced there by the pair table
alone. The free measurement of the abelian atlas is the **pair table** — which
coordinates carry multiplicity — and the membership zero is its arithmetic
consequence. What is free at the level of the unit's question is the extension
atlas's escape count, which had to be realised by admissible triangles and is
measured to be.

**Unit verdict:** `COC-HIGHER-OBSTRUCTION-AT-THE-EXTENSION-ORBIT-ATLAS`, at the
committed finite scope, at base G's declared 216-element extension admission
scope, at the atlas the declared chart rule generates from the group that scope
admits, at the pinned completion, per coordinate — with the atlas the narrower
162-element scope generates measured to close, that closure analytically forced.

## 1. The question, and what decides it

Two chart-level facts are inherited. Lawful transport between declared contexts
is path-dependent; and the group the loops generate at a symmetric setting is
the Klein four-group, with one generator the base's own wing exchange and the
other manufactured by the declared completion.

Both are readings on an atlas with **two** charts. In a two-chart atlas every
loop is a bigon, and the cocycle condition — that the identifications of three
charts compose to the identity on their common overlap — cannot even be stated.
It is the first condition that is not a restatement of the connection: a
triangle defect that no bigon holonomy generates would be structure at a level
the connection does not reach, which is what the pin calls the higher
obstruction.

The answer turns out not to depend on the connection at all. It depends on the
group that generates the charts. Section 5 states and gates the criterion;
section 6 proves the three identities behind it; sections 7 and 8 are the two
censuses that instantiate it.

Everything the two atlases need — the chart rule, an admission rule for each
pair, a coordinate at which the three identifications are all read, and the
verdict's decision rule — is declared in section 2 before any fixture value
appears. The instrument's own gate order is the proof of that ordering, and it
is gated: every declaration gate is measured to sit strictly before the first
fixture-truth gate, with the defect counter measured at zero when the freeze is
recorded.

> **Criterion.** The cocycle closes at an atlas if and only if the

**Gated at both atlases:**

|---|---|---|

## 6. The mechanism: three identities

All three hold for every input. They are stated here as theorems and verified on
this run's own data; the verification is entered as a **disclosure**, not as a
must-pass gate.

### 6.1 Equivariance

> **Lemma.** For $\sigma,\tau$ in the atlas's declared admitted group and
> committed frames $X, Y$,
> $$\mathrm{Adm}(\sigma.X,\ \tau.Y)\;=\;\tau\cdot\mathrm{Adm}(X,Y)\cdot\sigma^{-1}$$
> at every coordinate and under every rule.

*Proof.* Each clause is stated on conjugated legs and pushed-forward laws, and
$M\mapsto\tau M\tau^{-1}$ is a bijection that preserves Born-key equality and
multiset equality; every element of the admitted group fixes $j_0$, so clause 1
transports too. Uniqueness therefore transports as well. $\square$

**Measured** as an equality of sets of admitted candidates: **576 of 576**
(ordered pair) × (rule) × (coordinate) cases at the abelian atlas and
**11,520 of 11,520** at the extension atlas, **0 violations** at either.

### 6.2 The defect telescopes

> **Theorem.** For a triangle $A=\sigma.X$, $B=\tau.Y$, $C=\upsilon.Z$ with
> rules $r_1,r_2,r_3$,
> $$\delta \;=\; g_{CA}g_{BC}g_{AB}\;=\;\sigma\,\bigl(\rho^{r_3}_{ZX}\rho^{r_2}_{YZ}\rho^{r_1}_{XY}\bigr)\,\sigma^{-1}$$
> — every relabelling except the first cancels identically.

Both atlases have **two seeds**, so any three pairwise-distinct charts repeat a
seed; on the repeated pair the seed-level map is the self-map, measured uniquely
the identity (§9.1), so

$$\delta \;=\; \sigma\,\bigl(\rho^{r_3}_{XZ}\bigr)^{-1}\rho^{r_2}_{XZ}\,\sigma^{-1},$$

a conjugate by $\sigma$ of a level-1 object: the identity or the multiplicity
bigon at that coordinate.

**Corollary (the abelian atlas).** $S=\{1,W\}$ is abelian of exponent two, so
the conjugation is trivial and
$\delta \in \{\mathbf{1}\} \cup \{\text{the multiplicity bigon at that
coordinate}\}$ **for every input, at every setting, at every completion,
whatever the pair table says** — and every multiplicity bigon is by definition a
closed loop of the committed two-chart graph, hence an element of its holonomy
group. `COC-MEMBERSHIP` at that atlas *cannot* fail on any admissible input.

**Corollary (the extension atlas).** $\sigma W \sigma^{-1} \notin \{1,W,D,WD\}$
for exactly the four non-centralising $\sigma$, and every one of those four
conjugates is measured to be the **same** permutation.

**Measured** on the censuses themselves: $\sigma_A^{-1}\delta\sigma_A$ lies in
the seed-level admitted set for **1,824 of 1,824** and **333,312 of 333,312**
triangles, 0 violations.

### 6.3 The pair table is a coboundary, per rule

Write $\varepsilon_r(X,Y)\in\mathbb{Z}/2$ for the drawn map at a coordinate
($0$ = the identity, $1$ = the wing exchange).

> **Theorem.** $\varepsilon_r(X,Y) = f_r(X) + f_r(Y) \bmod 2$ at every
> coordinate and under each rule, with
> $f_{\mathrm{FULL}}(\sigma.F_i) = [\sigma = W]$; $f_{\mathrm{REAL}} =
> f_{\mathrm{FULL}}$ at the four flat settings and
> $f_{\mathrm{REAL}} = f_{\mathrm{FULL}} + [i = 2]$ at the two symmetric ones,
> each up to the potential's global flip.

**Measured:** a potential exists at **48 of 48** (coordinate, rule) cells, 0
exceptions, and the closed form reproduces it at all 48. Equivalently, in closed
form,

$$\delta \;=\; \prod_{\text{edges assigned REALIZED}} w_X\,w_Y, \qquad
w_X = W \ \text{iff the chart's frame is } F_2,$$

which agrees with the census on **1,824 of 1,824** triangles. Every headline of
the abelian atlas falls out of this in one line: at a flat setting the two
potentials coincide, the sum telescopes and the defect is identically the
identity; at a symmetric setting they differ by $[\,\text{frame} = F_2\,]$, and
$\delta$ is the parity of $\sum_{\text{REAL edges}}$, non-zero for exactly 96 of
192 per cell; and the single-rule triangles are all identity because each rule's
table is separately pure gauge.

### 6.4 Rule-inertness

A coordinate is **rule-inert** when no ordered pair there carries two distinct
maps, so no rule assignment can change any edge. **18 of 24** coordinates of the
abelian atlas are rule-inert — the sixteen flat-setting coordinates plus GP-E
and GP-F at $t=2$ — and **576 of 576** flat-setting triangles sit at one.
