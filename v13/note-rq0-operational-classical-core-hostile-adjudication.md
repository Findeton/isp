## 4. Second earned rung: operational classical core

### 4.1 Physical primitive

\[

### 4.2 Split projections and order ideals

Every branch of such an instrument is a split projection:

\[
P^2=P,
\qquad
P\text{ CP},
\qquad
I_S-P\text{ CP}.
\]

At scalar order, the ranges of \(P\) and \(I-P\) are complementary generated
order ideals. If \(Q\) is another split projection and
\(x\geq0\) lies in \(\operatorname{ran}P\), then

\[
0\leq Qx\leq x.
\]

The order-ideal property forces \(Qx\) back into \(\operatorname{ran}P\).
The same holds for the complementary range, so \(Q\) is block diagonal with
respect to \(P\). Therefore

\[
PQ=QP.
\]

The reviewers find no finite ordered-space, operator-system or effectus
counterexample satisfying all these hypotheses. Arbitrary CP idempotents are
not covered: complement positivity is essential.

### 4.3 Physical Boolean closure

The law must admit sequential composition of complete retained-flag
instruments and finite classical coarse-graining of their joint flags. Under
that explicit physical closure,

\[
P\wedge Q=PQ,
\qquad
P^\perp=I-P,
\qquad
P\vee Q=P+Q-PQ
\]

are admitted split projections.

These are not formal subtraction tricks. The sequential two-instrument
experiment has four physical branches

\[

\quad P(I-Q),

\quad (I-P)(I-Q),

If a restricted laboratory law admits two instruments but refuses their

instruments.

### 4.4 Finiteness and unique finest instrument

All split projections commute and are diagonalizable. At finite dimension,
they can be simultaneously diagonalized, with only zero and one in every
common diagonal coordinate. Hence there are finitely many and they form a

Its nonzero atoms \(A_i\) obey

\[
A_iA_j=\delta_{ij}A_i,
\qquad
\sum_iA_i=I_S.
\]

The admitted closure constructs them as the branches of one complete flagged
instrument. Every branch of every other admitted exact nondisturbing

\[
\boxed{

=
\{A_i\}_i
}
\]

is the unique finest instrument up to permutation of outcomes.

A one-atom core is a valid positive classification. It says the minimal
boundary contains no nontrivial exactly readable classical split under the

## 6. Adjudication of review findings

### 6.1 Minor wording issue: repeated flags

The operator-system and instrument reviewers independently flag the sentence

> Forgetting either flag leaves the state of every admitted effect unchanged.

Its exact admissible reading is:

\[
\sum_s\rho\circ m_rm_s=\rho\circ m_r,
\qquad
\sum_r\rho\circ m_rm_s=\rho\circ m_s.
\]

Forgetting one of two repeated flags leaves the other one-run marginal
unchanged. Forgetting both flags recovers \(\rho\). A state conditioned on the
remaining flag is not generally the original state.

This is a wording defect, not a failure of Definition 4.1, Proposition 4.2's
probability formula, or either registered rung. The immutable paper is not
edited; this adjudicated interpretation is binding.

### 6.2 Minor proof detail: atomic-map independence

linear independence. If

choose a nonzero \(x\in\operatorname{ran}A_j\). Applying the equality to
\(x\) gives \(c_jx=0\), hence \(c_j=0\). Thus the omission is bounded and
does not alter the result.
