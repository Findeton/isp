### Theorem 7.2 — RQ0-L4 class impossibility `[FIN]`

*Let the declared data be the committed configuration: the five-configuration
carrier, the committed state, the declared preparation the whole carrier, and
any committed law whose admitted-isomorphism group is the full 24. Let $f$ be
any statistic of the declared patch data — including one evaluated at
counterfactual admitted preparations or at generation profiles, since those are
themselves functions of the patch — such that*

- *(i) $f$ is admitted-isomorphism-covariant, and*
- *(ii) $f$ is not a function of the boundary's resolution profile.*

*Then no such $f$ exists.*

**Proof.** By (i), $f$ is constant on the orbits of the admitted-isomorphism
group acting on declared boundaries. By Theorem 7.1 the orbit invariant is the
shape profile, so $f$ factors through it. The shape profile is the boundary's
resolution data — the sizes of the declared atoms, refined by the state's level
classes — contradicting (ii). $\square$

**Corollary 7.3.** Every covariant statistic that separates the committed pairs
separates them **by shape alone**, and assigns identical verdicts to
declarations of identical shape and opposite provenance — witnessed by the
committed collision (`A14`).

**Measured on the declared family** (`L4-COV4`): no declared candidate is both
covariant and non-resolution at DET. The intersection is empty, exactly as the
theorem requires. The corridor is empty at the committed configuration.

### 7.4 The covariance analysis the pin demanded

*Are the committed pairs' declared data related by any admitted isomorphism?*
**No.** The three coarse boundaries lie in three distinct orbits, of sizes 6, 3
and 1, and no admitted isomorphism carries one to another — their block-size
profiles differ, so no relabelling whatever could (`L4-COV2`). Covariance does
**not** force them equal and does **not** block separation. Their shapes are

$$\{0123\mid4\}\mapsto\{(4,0),(0,1)\},\qquad
\{01\mid23\mid4\}\mapsto\{(2,0),(2,0),(0,1)\},\qquad
\{01\mid2\mid3\mid4\}\mapsto\{(2,0),(1,0),(1,0),(0,1)\}.$$

*Then what do the resisting invariants share?* This is the sharp answer, and it
is the opposite of the expected one. **The pairs resist not because they look
alike, but because the only thing that distinguishes them covariantly is how
much they resolve.** The committed configuration's own symmetry collapses the
52 declared boundaries to 12 distinguishable ones, and the surviving invariant
is the block-size profile. A covariant statistic has nothing else to read; the
declaration's history is not in the quotient.

### 7.5 The scope of the theorem, stated against itself

Theorem 7.2 is silent where the declared data has no symmetry. At the
counter-law the group is trivial, the quotient is the whole 52-record lattice,
and covariance imposes nothing — which is precisely why C1 passes G2b there and
nowhere else. The theorem is therefore not "no fingerprint exists"; it is
**"at a symmetric configuration, covariance and non-resolution are
incompatible"**, and the asymmetric case is settled instead by Theorem 6.1,
which is what kills C1. The two theorems together cover the declared family at
every committed law, and neither is claimed beyond it.
