## 1. Scope of the inherited state theorems

The inherited minimal-sufficiency theorem concerns parameterized normal states on a von Neumann algebra, with equivalence generated by normal CP or Schwarz coarse-grainings. It supplies a minimal sufficient experiment unique up to normal algebra isomorphism; it does not supply an embedded process boundary or identify physical projections in a comb. This is the scope of [Kuramochi’s minimal-sufficiency theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.03394) and the CP/Schwarz sufficiency framework of [Jenčová–Petz](https://arxiv.org/abs/math-ph/0412093).

For a finite matrix experiment, after restricting to the joint support of the family, the Koashi–Imoto form is

\[
H\simeq\bigoplus_r H_r^Q\otimes H_r^N,
\qquad
\rho_\theta\simeq
\bigoplus_r p(r\mid\theta)\,
\rho_{\theta,r}^Q\otimes\omega_r^N,
\]

and the corresponding minimal algebra is

\[
M_{\min}\simeq
\bigoplus_r B(H_r^Q)\otimes I_{H_r^N},
\qquad
Z(M_{\min})\simeq
\bigoplus_r\mathbb C I_r .
\]

This classical/quantum/redundant decomposition is consistent with the original [Koashi–Imoto theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0101144) and with the operator-algebraic definition of the classical part in [Kuramochi’s nondisturbing-access theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.01599).

The paper should have stated the joint-support reduction explicitly. A common null sector is not part of the minimal experiment. This is a scope clarification, not a counterexample to the controls actually used.

Minimal uniqueness is only abstract uniqueness. It does not identify two isomorphic centers with the same embedded physical record projectors. In particular, different \(2+2\) decompositions of \(M_4\) can all yield an abstract algebra \(M_2\oplus M_2\) and center \(\mathbb C^2\) while representing inequivalent PVMs in the laboratory process.

## 2. Proposition 5.1 survives

The proposition

\[
\text{restriction to }Z(M_{\min})\text{ is sufficient}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
M_{\min}\text{ is abelian}
\]

is correct.

The clean proof uses minimal rigidity rather than the paper’s abbreviated uniqueness argument. Suppose the center restriction has an admitted recovery. Composing restriction and recovery gives an endomorphism of the minimal experiment that factors through \(Z(M_{\min})\) and fixes every state. Minimality forces this composite to be the identity. Its range is central, hence

\[
M_{\min}=Z(M_{\min}),
\]

so \(M_{\min}\) is abelian. The converse is immediate.

Thus a nontrivial center is not the same thing as center-only sufficiency. The paper’s \(M_2\oplus\mathbb C\) Koashi–Imoto control correctly retains quantum parameter dependence in the first central sector. The within-sector pair

\[
|0\rangle\langle0|\oplus0,
\qquad
|+\rangle\langle+|\oplus0
\]

has identical center statistics but trace distance \(\sqrt2\), so every center-only recovery has worst-case trace error at least \(\sqrt2/2\).

The proposition is earned; its printed proof has only a minor omitted step.

## 3. Proposition 3.1 and the minimum-rank theorem

For finite \(\Theta\), the identity

\[
\max_\theta\|z_\theta\|
=
\max_{\sum_\theta\|g_\theta\|_*\leq1}
\sum_\theta g_\theta(z_\theta)
\]

is the standard duality between a finite \(\ell^\infty\)-sum and its \(\ell^1\)-sum dual. The superchannel hom-set and the dual ball are compact convex sets, and the objective is continuous affine in each variable. Finite-dimensional minimax therefore gives the displayed formula in Proposition 3.1. Compactness also turns deficiency zero into an attained exact simulator.

The witness \(g\) is a signed decision functional. It is a literal admitted decision test only when the dual ball is generated by the declared signed tester evaluations, a qualification the paper substantially includes.

The minimum-rank argument also survives:

1. The identity is a stabilizing idempotent, so stabilizing idempotents exist.
2. Their ranks are integers, so a minimum-rank stabilizing idempotent \(e\) can be chosen.
3. For a stabilizing endomorphism \(f=efe\) of \((X,e)\), every \(f^n\) remains in the compact hom-set and is power bounded.
4. The Cesàro averages converge in finite dimension to the projection \(p\) onto \(\operatorname{Fix}(f)\).
5. Closedness and convexity make \(p\) admitted, and \(p=epe\) fixes every experiment point.
6. If \(f\neq e\), then \(f\) is not the identity on \(\operatorname{ran}e\); hence \(\operatorname{Fix}(f)\) is proper and
   \[
   \operatorname{rank}p<\operatorname{rank}e,
   \]
   contradicting minimality.

The retract calculation \(eR\Gamma e=e\) and the rigidity-based uniqueness proof are correct. Reversible presentation maps conjugate stabilizers and preserve rank because they are linear isomorphisms.

No ordinary-comb splitting theorem is proved. The result belongs to

\[
\operatorname{Kar}(\mathsf{Proc}_D),
\]

exactly as the paper ultimately says. The closedness, convexity, kernel-preservation, and norm-contraction assumptions are essential; the theorem is not asserted for an arbitrary nonclosed laboratory grammar.

## 4. Fatal defect in Proposition 6.1

The Choi calculation itself is correct. With

\[
\operatorname{Tr}_B J(\Phi)=I_A
\]

and \(W\) swapping the two Choi factors,

\[
J(\Phi_\rho)=I_A\otimes\rho_B
\quad\longmapsto\quad
\rho_A\otimes I_B,
\]

so

\[
\operatorname{Tr}_B(\rho_A\otimes I_B)=2\rho_A,
\]

which violates channel normalization unless \(\rho=I/2\). Therefore the particular ambient state map

\[
J\longmapsto WJW
\]

is not a deterministic superchannel on all qubit channels.

That does not prove Proposition 6.1 as stated:

> “Exact CP equivalence of a family of normalized Choi states does not imply exact process equivalence by one admitted deterministic superchannel.”

The displayed family is the singleton identity-channel state, which the swap fixes. But the identity superchannel also fixes that family. Hence the family is process-equivalent despite the swap map itself being nonphysical. Showing that one state-level witness fails the superchannel constraints does not show that no other superchannel realizes the same finite-family transformation.

Accordingly:

- **Proposition 6.1 must be withdrawn as an established theorem.**
- The abstract’s claim of “two exact counterexamples” must be downgraded.
- The narrower normalization warning survives:

> An arbitrary CP map used to witness normalized-Choi state equivalence cannot itself be promoted to a deterministic superchannel unless it preserves the full comb normalization constraints.

This is fatal to Proposition 6.1 but major-and-bounded for the paper. The center rung still fails because the positive burden of constructing a multiplication and admitted readout is unmet, independently of this proposition.

## 5. The operator-system counterexample is valid and stronger than printed

Let

\[
S=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb C}
\{(I,I),(Z,Z),(X,2X+Z)\}
\subset M_2\oplus M_2.
\]

Writing these generators as \(s_0,s_1,s_2\),

\[
s_2^2=(I,5I),
\]

so

\[
z_1=\frac{5s_0-s_2^2}{4}=(I,0),
\qquad
z_2=\frac{s_2^2-s_0}{4}=(0,I)
\]

belong to \(C^*(S)\). Multiplication by \(z_1,z_2\) recovers \(X,Z\) separately in both summands, proving

\[
C^*(S)=M_2\oplus M_2.
\]

Neither \(z_1\) nor \(z_2\) lies in \(S\). Comparison in the first block of

\[
\alpha s_0+\beta s_1+\gamma s_2=(I,0)
\]

forces \(\alpha=1,\beta=\gamma=0\), leaving \(I\) in the second block.

The two scalar norm witnesses are sufficient to identify the \(C^*\)-envelope. Since \(M_2\) is simple, the only nonzero proper ideals delete one of the two full blocks.

- Deleting the second block lowers
  \[
  \|s_2\|=\sqrt5
  \quad\text{to}\quad1.
  \]

- Deleting the first block lowers the norm of
  \[
  -3s_1+s_2=(X-3Z,\,2X-2Z)
  \]
  from
  \[
  \sqrt{10}
  \quad\text{to}\quad\sqrt8.
  \]

A norm loss already at matrix level one rules out complete isometry. Neither proper ideal is a boundary ideal, so the Shilov ideal is zero and

\[
C_e^*(S)=M_2\oplus M_2.
\]

There is also an explicit operational proof that no intrinsic matrix-order manipulation of \(S\) can recover the block probabilities. Let \(\operatorname{tr}_2\) be normalized trace and define states

\[
\varphi_\lambda(a,b)
=
\lambda\operatorname{tr}_2(a)
+
(1-\lambda)\operatorname{tr}_2(b),
\qquad 0\leq\lambda\leq1.
\]

Every \(\varphi_\lambda\) has the same restriction to \(S\):

\[
\varphi_\lambda(s_0)=1,
\qquad
\varphi_\lambda(s_1)=0,
\qquad
\varphi_\lambda(s_2)=0.
\]

But

\[
\varphi_\lambda(z_1)=\lambda.
\]

Thus operational states on \(S\) do not determine the central block weight. No affine readout—and therefore no scalar effect or complete instrument defined only from \(S\)—can return the envelope-center statistic. Matrix amplification cannot repair two states that are already identical on every element of \(S\).

The envelope center is canonical as an abstract algebra under complete-order isomorphism. It is not thereby an embedded physical record interface.

## 6. No omitted Choi–Effros theorem rescues the paper

The Choi–Effros theorem gives a \(C^*\)-algebra product

\[
x\circ y=P(xy)
\]

on the range of a contractive completely positive projection \(P\) on a specified \(C^*\)-algebra; see [Choi–Effros, “Injectivity and operator spaces”](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-1236(77)90052-0).

Those hypotheses are absent here. A Karoubi object \((X,e)\) supplies:

- a finite process vector space or tester quotient;
- a compact convex deterministic base; and
- an idempotent deterministic superchannel on that process object.

It does not supply:

- an ambient \(C^*\)-algebra of observables;
- an Archimedean matrix order;
- a completely positive contractive projection on that full algebra;
- idempotence on off-normalization Choi directions; or
- an admitted instrument reading the central projections.

Calling \(e\) a superchannel does not fill these gaps. The paper’s theorem is intentionally formulated only on the span of deterministic processes after tester quotienting. Even if a particular physical realization admitted a UCP projection and hence a Choi–Effros product, that would be additional structure beyond Theorem 4.2.

Nor does the center of the composition algebra of endomorphisms solve the problem. Its elements are higher-order transformations, not observable effects, and its center is not the Koashi–Imoto classical part.

Therefore no canonical admitted central instrument can be constructed from only the paper’s process-retract data.

The minimum extra structure sufficient to reopen the center rung would be:

1. a presentation-covariant operator-system or finite \(C^*\)-algebra realization of the minimal retract;
2. a specified physical UCP idempotent or conditional expectation whose range product is functorial under reversible presentations;
3. admitted central effects \(z_r\) and a complete nondemolition instrument \(\{\mathcal I_r\}\) satisfying
   \[
   \mathcal I_r^\dagger(1)=z_r,
   \qquad
   \sum_r\mathcal I_r
   \text{ deterministic},
   \]
   whose total action fixes the minimal experiment; and
4. an independently fixed task law showing that this instrument was not selected from the desired PVM.

An abstract center without item 3 remains algebraic metadata, not a W3 record.

## 7. The task-smuggling theorem survives, with a scope clarification

For a finite PVM \(P=\{P_r\}\), dephasing has range

\[
A_P=\bigoplus_r P_rB(H)P_r.
\]

Choose finitely many faithful states whose affine hull is the trace-one self-adjoint affine space of \(A_P\). Any CP/Schwarz endomorphism fixing all those states fixes their affine hull, hence all of \(A_P\). The experiment on \(A_P\) is therefore minimal, with center

\[
Z(A_P)=C^*(P_1,\ldots,P_m).
\]

The paper’s wording “affine span is the self-adjoint state space” should read “affine hull is the trace-one self-adjoint affine space.” That is a minor defect.

No center is silently assumed in the inference: the desired PVM is explicitly inserted into the task through \(\mathcal D_P\). That is precisely why the theorem detects circularity. Indeed, one can strengthen the construction by fixing a faithful tomographically complete family on \(B(H)\) independently of \(P\); its image under any later chosen \(\mathcal D_P\) affine-spans \(A_P\).

The theorem does not say that task choice alone manufactures every PVM center for every fixed nonseparating past family. A separating family is required. The branch-memory paper acknowledges this when it invokes a tomographic past extension.

Thus Theorem 7.1 is correct as an adversarial planted-answer theorem and establishes a downstream `TASK-SELECTION` hazard. It is not the first block because the generic process center is already undefined.

### Classical and Koashi–Imoto controls

The classical likelihood vectors for

\[

\qquad
p_1=(1/6,1/6,1/3,1/3)

identify exactly the blocks \(\{a,b\}\) and \(\{c,d\}\). The minimum is \(\mathbb C^2\).

In the displayed Koashi–Imoto family, the two nonorthogonal states \(|0\rangle\) and \(|+\rangle\) generate an irreducible qubit block, the second direct summand supplies a distinct classical sector, and \(I_N/2\) is a redundant tensor factor. The minimum is

\[
M_2\oplus\mathbb C,
\qquad
Z=\mathbb C^2.
\]

Two distinct nonorthogonal pure qubit states likewise have minimum \(M_2\) and scalar center. These controls are correct.

### Qubit W3 control

The preserving \(Z\)-dephasing task gives

\[
p_0=(3/4,1/4),
\qquad
p_1=(1/4,3/4),
\]

a minimal classical experiment \(\mathbb C^2\). The no-write reset to \(|+\rangle\) gives the same \((1/2,1/2)\) statistics for both settings.

No classical-bit preparation channel can reconstruct both original pure states: each recovered state would be a convex combination with both coefficients positive, and a convex combination can be pure only if every positive-weight component equals that pure state. The two target pure states differ, so exact recovery is impossible. Compactness yields strictly positive deficiency.

The printed coherence calculation is correct:

\[
\left\|
|\psi_i\rangle\langle\psi_i|
-
\mathcal D_Z(|\psi_i\rangle\langle\psi_i|)
\right\|_1
=
\frac{\sqrt3}{2}.
\]

This validates the nonemptiness of the proposed W3 discriminator at its one-step classical-output scope. It does not establish a generic process-center lift.

## 9. Fatal defect in the branch-memory minima

Section 9 explicitly retains each \(1/4\)-weighted source branch through the deterministic success/sink embedding

\[
\widehat x_j
=
\frac14|1\rangle\langle1|\otimes\bar x_j
+
\frac34|0\rangle\langle0|\otimes\omega_{\rm sink}.
\]

The later minimal-algebra calculations then silently discard the sink.

For the eraser terminal task, the actual classical distributions are

\[
q_j(\bot)=\frac34,
\qquad
q_j(k)=\frac14\delta_{jk},
\]

on the five-point alphabet

\[
\{\bot,0,1,2,3\}.
\]

Their likelihood vectors are

\[
(3/4,3/4,3/4,3/4)
\]

for \(\bot\), and \((1/4)e_k\) for the four successful labels. No two are proportional. The ordinary minimal sufficient statistic therefore has five atoms:

\[
M_{\rm eraser}=\mathbb C^5,
\]

not \(\mathbb C^4\).

Equivalently, erasure has reduced pairwise \(\ell^1\) distance from \(2\) to \(1/2\). No stochastic recovery can convert the common failure outcome into the unknown label \(j\). Conditioning on success would recover the four-point experiment, but that renormalizes away the \(1/4\) weight and violates the governing pin.

Consequences include:

- The preserving minimum remains trivial because all four complete distributions are equal.
- The eraser and tagged-bundle minima contain four label atoms plus one uninformative failure atom, hence \(\mathbb C^5\).
- Retaining only the four orthogonal written states together with the sink also gives \(\mathbb C^5\), not \(\mathbb C^4\).
- With a tomographically separating success family, the minimum is
  \[
  M_4\oplus\mathbb C,
  \]
  not \(M_4\); its center is \(\mathbb C^2\), not scalar.
- A candidate-matched \(2+1+1\) dephasing gives
  \[
  (M_2\oplus\mathbb C\oplus\mathbb C)\oplus\mathbb C
  \]
  and center \(\mathbb C^4\), not \(\mathbb C^3\).
- A candidate-matched \(2+2\) dephasing gives
  \[
  (M_2\oplus M_2)\oplus\mathbb C
  \]
  and center \(\mathbb C^3\), not \(\mathbb C^2\).

The common success/failure marginal contains no information about \(j\), but it cannot be removed: failure masks the parameter-dependent state rather than tensoring on an independent spectator.

Thus Theorem 9.1 and the abstract’s \(\mathbb C\to\mathbb C^4\) or \(M_4\) reclassification are not correct as printed. The qualitative negative conclusion survives: none of the nine inherited coarse seams or the complex seam is selected, and the success/failure center fails the write-correlation requirement.

This is fatal to the exact branch-memory theorem but major-and-bounded for the registered ladder.

## 10. Common hostile-review questions

1. **Is the future experiment genuinely typed?**  
   Yes for the explicit common-profile controls. The construction retains weights, branch flags, disturbances, and outputs without a free \(\theta\)-label. The general use of \(A_\theta\) requires a predeclared common alphabet or neutral padding; a direct sum tagged by \(\theta\) would be forbidden task leakage.

2. **Does simulation use one parameter-independent superchannel?**  
   Yes. Every exact simulator and recovery in Sections 3–4 is one morphism acting on the entire family.

3. **Is deficiency attained and is Proposition 3.1 correct?**  
   Yes under the printed finite-dimensional compactness and tester-norm assumptions.

4. **Does every nonidentity stabilizer produce a lower-rank admitted projection?**  
   Yes. Power boundedness, Cesàro convergence, closed convex admission, and strict reduction of the fixed space are sufficient.

5. **Does Theorem 4.2 prove all claimed properties?**  
   It proves existence, rigidity, retract minimality, presentation covariance, and uniqueness in the Karoubi completion. It does not prove an ordinary comb splitting or autonomous boundary, and it does not claim one.

6. **Is the process theorem stronger than normalized-Choi state sufficiency but weaker than autonomy?**  
   Yes. Its transformations are admitted process superchannels rather than arbitrary CP state maps, while its output is only an idempotent-split process object.

7. **Do the Choi swap and operator-system examples prove two independent center-lift failures?**  
   No. The operator-system example is a genuine center/readout counterexample. The Choi swap proves only that one ambient state map is not a superchannel; Proposition 6.1’s family-level non-equivalence is unproved.

8. **Is the task-smuggling theorem correct?**  
   Yes at affine-separating state scope. It deliberately inputs the desired PVM through the dephasing task and thereby proves circular manufacture. It is not a theorem about every fixed nonseparating past family.

9. **Are the qubit and branch-memory controls correct?**  
   The qubit control is correct. The branch-memory transition amplitudes, uniform preserving law, and coherent eraser action are correct, but the minimal algebras are wrong because the required sink is discarded.

10. **Is center lift the first registered obstruction?**  
    Yes. The common-profile future experiment and abstract Karoubi minimum survive. No canonical multiplication or admitted central instrument follows. The task-selection and branch-memory defects occur later.
