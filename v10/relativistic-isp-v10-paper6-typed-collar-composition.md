## 4. Exact separator encoder

Let a region have interior variables `I` and retained boundary variables `B`.
Its unnormalized effective boundary message is

$$
M_{I\to B}(b)
=
\sum_{i\in\{-1,+1\}^{I}}
\prod_{f\in H_{I\cup B}}
\phi_f((i,b)|_{S_f}).
$$

The encoder reads only the factors assigned to the region. It does not inspect
outside completions. The message nevertheless depends on the supplied factor
values and on the premise that the supplied factor cover is complete.

### Theorem 4.1 — exact supplied factor-partition composition

Suppose two finite regions have a supplied partition of unique factor IDs and
meet on a declared separator `S`. Their effective messages compose by
contraction:

$$
M_{A\cup_S B}(a,b)
=
\sum_s M_A(a,s)M_B(s,b).
$$

Direct elimination equals both three-piece parenthesizations and leaf-to-root
contraction on the audited branching tree. Contraction is associative, and
the elimination schedule is invariant for one fixed factor multiset. Joint
relabeling of variables and tables preserves the result. This is not a
physical construction-order gauge theorem for different record-birth
histories.

#### Proof

Expand the right side. Every full assignment receives the product of every
factor exactly once because identities are allocated exactly once. Every eliminated
variable is summed over a finite set. Finite addition and multiplication of
rationals are associative and distributive, so the order of multiplication
and finite summation may be changed without changing the result. Joint
relabeling is a bijection of the finite assignment set. ∎

The theorem is the familiar finite sum-product/variable-elimination identity.
Its use is constructive but conditional, not a claim of mathematical novelty.
The SHARD-specific result is the exact placement of its premises: complete
factor cover, identity ownership, factor scope, and factor value are the data earlier
investigations left open.

The receipt's three-factor chain gives the same boundary table under direct,
left-nested, right-nested, and reverse elimination:

```text
(29, 25, 31, 35).
```

The branching-tree leaf messages also equal direct elimination. If one ID
appears on both sides of a composition, the operation refuses it. A separator
factor may be allocated to the left or right with the same result when it is
included exactly once, and nested provenance union refuses its reintroduction.
Opaque IDs do not detect semantic duplication under fresh IDs and do not
derive physical collar ownership or interface types.

## 5. Factorization-relative predictive sufficiency

Take an already supplied candidate variable `z` and proposal factors `F_z` whose
scopes lie in `{z} union B`. Its weight is

$$
W(z)=\sum_bM_B(b)\prod_{f\in F_z}\phi_f(z,b).
$$

Normalizing over `z` gives its conditional state distribution. There is no
null/no-birth alternative, proposal measure, eligibility selector, evidence
increment, seal test, or normalization over inequivalent record extensions.

### Theorem 5.1 — the boundary positive ray is sufficient

If two interiors induce proportional positive messages

$$
M'_B=cM_B,\qquad c>0,
$$

then every common external proposal-factor family gives the same normalized
prediction.

#### Proof

Every candidate weight is multiplied by the common factor `c`, which cancels
under normalization. ∎

### Theorem 5.2 — minimality against arbitrary positive full-separator tests

If two normalized positive boundary messages differ, some strictly positive
external full-separator factor distinguishes them. Hence the coarsest
universally predictive message for that unrestricted test class is the
positive ray `[M_B]`.

#### Proof

Let normalized messages `p` and `q` differ at boundary assignment `b_0`.
Choose a positive test `g(b)=1+1[b=b_0]`. Then `E_p[g]` and `E_q[g]` differ.
Implement a binary candidate-state test with factor `phi(z=-1,b)=1` and
`phi(z=+1,b)=g(b)`. Its normalized `z` probability is a strictly monotone
function of that expectation, so the predictions differ. ∎

The exact receipt exhibits both directions. A unary history with message
`(2,4)` and a different hidden-variable history with the same message both
give

```text
P(Z=-1,+1) = (5/12, 7/12).
```

The reverse message `(4,2)` on the identical unmarked boundary gives

```text
(7/12, 5/12).
```

An unmarked uniform boundary gives `(1/2,1/2)`. Retaining the accumulated
message can therefore make the recorded state sufficient even when the
unmarked visible state is non-Markovian.

This does not make the primitive full-history measure Markovian. The message
is a function of the relevant history. It says that the history's predictive
effect is represented by the retained separator table under the supplied
factorization. A restricted physical proposal class may identify a coarser
quotient, so the ray is a universal diagnostic target rather than a derived
minimal physical record.

## 6. Why composition does not derive record birth or the click law

The positive theorem begins with supplied factor tokens. The decisive question is
whether covariance, composition, or unique-ID bookkeeping selects their scopes
and numerical values. It does not in the frozen arena.

### 6.1 Value nonselection

On one fixed `B-Z` scope, positive equal/unequal factors `(4,1)` and `(2,1)`

```text
(2/5, 3/5) versus (4/9, 5/9).
```

The algebra composes either law and selects neither.

### 6.2 Scope nonselection

On a symmetric formal separator `{A,B}`, freeze three possible candidate
scopes:

```text
{Z,A}, {Z,B}, {Z,A,B}.
```

The swap `A<->B` exchanges the first two and fixes the joint scope. Exactly
three nonempty swap-invariant eligibility families survive in this candidate
list:

1. both one-leg scopes;
2. the joint scope;
3. all three scopes.

A deterministic single-`A` choice fails covariance, but covariance does not
choose among the three invariant families. This is not a classification of
all covariant numerical proposal laws. It is the D2 interface-selection wall
expressed inside one frozen D5 arena.

### 6.3 Scope and incidence are not transport

The receipt separates four table relations. A listed two-variable scope can
contain a dummy leg, so its essential-variable set is smaller. A table can
depend essentially on both variables yet factor exactly into two unary tables,
so it has no irreducible pair interaction. A primitive pair token can be
irreducible. Eliminating a shared ancestor can create a derived correlated
two-variable message even though no primitive pair token existed.

Thus the following are different:

1. listed scope;
2. essential dependence scope;
3. irreducible statistical interaction;
4. primitive factor-hyperedge incidence;
5. a derived correlated separator message;
6. directed physical carrier/transport.

D5 executes distinctions 1–5. It has no orientation or transport law and
proves item 6 nowhere.

### 6.4 What remains of the click law

The factor construction supplies the third layer only after the first two are
given:

```text
proposed scope -> eligibility/unique-ID allocation -> numerical factor
               -> conditional candidate state.
```

D5 constructs the last arrow after a candidate already exists. It does not
derive the proposal/no-birth measure, possible scope, eligibility rule, factor
value, evidence increment, or seal event. The v7 survival law remains
downstream of an evidence input that D5 does not construct.

## 7. Channel rank is not participant count

For `n` primitive binary variables, define nonconstant parity characters

$$
Q_S(x)=\prod_{v\in S}x_v,
\qquad \varnothing\ne S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}.
$$

At the uniform full-support screen, these characters are orthogonal. Their
covariance/Gram matrix is the identity, so the complete ledger has exact
screen-relative rank

$$
r_n=2^n-1.
$$

The receipt verifies `1,3,7` for `n=1,2,3`. Repeating a linearly dependent
character adds a listed channel but not rank. Joint bijective relabeling of
the whole ledger and screen preserves rank; adding a translated observable can
increase it, as the one-coordinate versus two-coordinate swap control shows.

Conversely, the single character

$$
Q_{\{1,\ldots,n\}}=x_1\cdots x_n
$$

has rank one while involving all `n` variables. The receipt verifies this
through `n=10`; the proof is immediate for every finite `n`. Therefore

$$
\text{participants}\ne\text{listed channels}\ne\text{independent rank}.
$$

### 7.1 Multichannel Fisher capacity is a screen/model-relative matrix

For an exponential-family record with parameters `h_alpha`,

$$
J_{\alpha\beta}(h)
=
\frac{\partial^2\psi}{\partial h_\alpha\partial h_\beta}
=
\operatorname{Cov}_h(Q_\alpha,Q_\beta).
$$

The scalar one-channel crossing `C(eta)=J(eta)` is not automatically an
equation in this arena. `C(h)` is scalar while `J(h)` is a matrix. A direction,
eigenvalue, trace, determinant, norm, or resource allocation must be selected
before the comparison is typed. D5 derives none of those scalarizations as
canonical. The honest multichannel capacity object is a matrix or Pareto
frontier.

In the exact product control with each binary variable biased `3/4`, the
single-coordinate covariance is `3/4`. For `n` independent coordinates,

$$
\operatorname{rank}J=n,
\qquad
\operatorname{tr}J=\frac{3n}{4}.
$$

The receipt verifies this through six channels, and the product proof holds at
every finite `n`. The Fisher reading presumes the corresponding exponential
family and natural parameterization; trace is one chosen scalarization, not an
intrinsic content. The one-channel `W_*` cannot be silently promoted to a
uniform total-record bound when screen-relative rank is allowed to grow.

## 8. Bounded additive KL alone supplies no arity bound

The stronger obstruction does not require a high-rank parity ledger. It uses
arbitrarily weak independent channels.

Let

$$
P_i(+)=\frac12+\delta_i,
\qquad
P_i(-)=\frac12-\delta_i,
\qquad
\delta_i=2^{-(i+3)}.
$$

Every channel is nontrivial. Relative to the uniform binary law `U`,

$$
D(P_i\Vert U)
\le
\chi^2(P_i\Vert U)
=4\delta_i^2.
$$

KL divergence adds on independent products, hence for every finite `N`,

$$
D\!\left(\bigotimes_{i=0}^{N-1}P_i
\middle\Vert
\bigotimes_{i=0}^{N-1}U\right)
\le
\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}4\delta_i^2
<
\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}4\delta_i^2
=\frac1{12}.
$$

Thus arbitrarily many nonzero independent channels fit below one fixed finite
KL upper budget. More generally, multiplying all `delta_i` by a sufficiently
small positive constant fits the same construction below any prescribed
positive KL ceiling. A maximum participant or channel count follows only after an
additional theorem supplies a uniform positive cost `epsilon` per relevant
carrier and proves additive allocation:

$$
N\epsilon\le W_{\max}.
$$

Neither premise exists in the current corpus. The witness concerns additive
KL/evidence divergence from the chosen null. It does not pay for scope
descriptions, rational parameters, unique identities, carrier metadata, or
exact encoding, and therefore is not a theorem about every possible meaning
of finite record content.

## 9. Connected-domain closure and joining proposal tokens

Treat every factor scope as a hyperedge of the record incidence graph.

### Theorem 9.1 — connected-domain closure given a seed

If a supplied candidate has primitive factor legs only into one existing
connected component, then it joins that component and cannot merge it with
another. If the proposal domain enforces this rule and a supplied seed is
connected, every later accepted finite factor hypergraph remains connected.

The proof is the elementary component invariant: adding a vertex and edges to
one component cannot create a path to a different component.

### Theorem 9.2 — explicit multileg-token requirement for a join

A supplied candidate merges two old components only if its proposed factor
family has scopes meeting both components. Those multileg factors are the
joining token. Tensor contraction uses them but does not generate them or
prove they are directed physical carriers.

The receipt begins with components `{A,B}` and `{C,D}`. A `Z-A` proposal leaves
two components. A proposal with both `Z-A` and `Z-C` legs produces one. Among
all nonempty parent subsets of the four old records, six lie within one old
component and nine span components; the declared one-component domain accepts
the former and refuses the latter.

Shared ancestry must not be confused with primitive pair incidence or directed
transport. In the control,
`A` and `B` are correlated through hidden ancestor `H`, yet no factor scope
contains both `A` and `B`. Correlation, path connectivity, and direct incidence
are three different relations.

The component invariant is conditional on the chosen proposal domain and seed.
It shows that one connected sparse history is coherent without all-to-all
factor incidence. D5 does not derive the domain, seed, first join, direction,
or a physical universe realization.

## 10. Exact state costs

### 10.1 Separator width

An arbitrary binary message on boundary width `b` has

$$
2^b
$$

unnormalized entries and `2^b-1` normalized degrees of freedom. This is a
worst-case table count, not a claim that every physical message fills the full
space. Factorization, symmetry, or low treewidth may compress it.

The loop control shows why the joint table cannot generally be replaced by
one-site marginals. Its exact message is

```text
(20, 6, 6, 20),
```

while both one-site marginals are uniform. Their product erases the retained
correlation.

### 10.2 Numeric precision at fixed width

Boundary width is not the only resource. The receipt propagates the fixed
integer transition table

```text
[[1,1],[1,2]]
```

along a width-one chain. Writing its unnormalized ray as `(a_n,b_n)`,

$$
(a_{n+1},b_{n+1})=(a_n+b_n,a_n+2b_n),
\qquad (a_0,b_0)=(1,1).
$$

The recurrence preserves `gcd(a_n,b_n)=1`, because
`gcd(a+b,a+2b)=gcd(a,b)`, and `b_n` grows strictly. Thus the positive-ray
gauge cannot remove the growth. Through 32 steps the primitive integer
description grows from 2 to 45 bits and the normalized rational description
from 3 to 46 bits. The receipt gates the recurrence, gcd-one reduction, and
both bit counts.

This is exact rational table-description growth, not an optimal encoding
lower bound or physical evidential capacity. A short recurrence plus a depth
index is a different exact description. A physical implementation would have
to declare its encoding and may choose among:

- distributed storage;
- bounded-state factorization with continuing internal records;
- approximate compression;
- an unbounded or projective record alphabet;
- or refusal of exact screening at arbitrary depth.

D5 has not selected one.

### 10.3 Structural zeros

The base theorem uses positive factors. The control with deterministic
equality factors gives the exact joint separator

```text

```

and composes normally because its total support is nonempty. An all-zero table
has no conditional law and is refused. The repaired type system rejects
negative weights and separately verifies that equality and inequality support
tokens multiply to an impossible all-zero message. These are controls only. A
full zero-support theory would need fiberwise compatibility and conditional
versions in addition to unique IDs.

## 12. Relation to Barandes-style full-history dynamics

Nothing in Theorem 4.1 assumes that the primitive stochastic process is
Markovian in an unmarked present. The supplied factor history may remember
arbitrarily many earlier tokens. The claim is factorization-relative
augmented-state sufficiency:

$$
\text{full relevant recorded history}
\longmapsto
[M_B]
\longmapsto
\text{declared continuation-class prediction}.
$$

If the complete path measure supplies a complete factor cover whose external
dependence meets the eliminated region only through `B`, the message is a
sufficient statistic for the declared continuation class. If two histories
with the same permitted separator message require different futures, the
factorization premise fails; D5 may not silently repair it by attaching global
history.

Thus non-Markovianism and factorization-relative augmented sufficiency are
compatible. No claim is made that every Barandes-style indivisible process has
a finite sufficient separator.
