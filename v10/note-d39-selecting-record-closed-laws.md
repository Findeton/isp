## 2. The comparison map has two levels

The phrase “D37 and D38b are one object” is forbidden.  There are two distinct
comparison problems, and only the first is currently constructible.

### 2.1 Level A — architecture-level regional functor

Let `C_38^fin` be the finite category whose objects consist of

```text
authenticated D38b finite record region;
typed external parent/frontier references;
positive finite future cylinder;
record restriction and admitted append/update data.
```

Its morphisms are generated by identities, nested record restrictions and
admitted typed updates.  Until Section 4 succeeds, a D38b object retains its
complete authenticated finite history as admission metadata.  Carrying that
witness through the comparison is not a localized-certificate theorem.

Let `C_37^or` be the oriented regional-interface category underlying D37,
before choosing K1, K2, K3 or the joint birth/arbitration family.  An object has

```text
incoming boundary rows;
lateral/live-interface rows;
typed interior atoms;
a proper normalized kernel;
restriction/transport and update maps.
```

The comparison target is a structure-preserving functor

```text
Phi : C_38^fin -> C_37^or.
```

On a finite D38b region `D`, `Phi` is pinned to act as follows:

```text
retained authenticated source record       -> typed interior/source row;
omitted causal-parent source ID             -> incoming boundary row;
omitted current-frontier source ID          -> lateral/live-interface row;
typed future action/event                    -> typed regional atom;
D38b record restriction/update              -> oriented transport/update.
```

If `pi_D` is the typed atom map and `gamma_D` the D38b positive-cylinder
kernel, the target kernel is the pushforward

```text
bar_gamma_D = (pi_D)_* gamma_D.
```

The Level-A theorem target is finite and exact:

```text
Phi(id_D) = id_(Phi D);
Phi(r_2 o r_1) = Phi(r_2) o Phi(r_1);
bar_gamma_D is proper and normalized;
oriented restriction commutes with Phi;
admitted update commutes with Phi;
nested kernel composition survives pushforward.
```

The receipt must distinguish equality of maps, equality of pushed measures and
equality of typed serialization.  Pairwise overlap agreement is insufficient;
D37's triple-cover counterexample remains a negative gate.

Level A is not an equivalence theorem.  `Phi` need not be full, faithful,
essentially surjective or invertible.  It does not prove that D34b generates a
D37 conflict carrier, belongs to a K family or supplies a spatial DLR kernel.

### 2.2 Level B — generated conflict content

A contentful comparison would require the law itself to generate the D37
opportunity complex.  For a record history `H`, the required extractor would
have to produce

```text
Conf(H) = (generated proposal roles,
           participant/base-version incidence,
           incompatible-pair relation,
           mode and arbitration records).
```

Chosen D34b does not contain that ontology.  Its existing incidence persists;
topology grows only by a birth's parent-child edge.  It does not discover an
arbitrary peer, join previously disconnected components, emit D36 proposals
against a frozen base version, or generate prepare/grant/reject/decision rows.
Two possible next D34b events are alternatives in a stochastic generator, not
two durable proposals contending for the same participant base.

Therefore the registered D34b conflict extractor has no contested cell.  An
empty or conflict-free image cannot discriminate how a law resolves an actual
conflict.  K-family behavior on contended rows is consequently vacuous at this
level; no K1/K2/K3 membership result may be printed.

The contentful bridge remains a named construction problem:

```text
record-closed generated law
  emits PROPOSAL/PREPARE/GRANT-or-REJECT/DECISION/APPLY-or-RELEASE/ACK;

conflict edge
  iff two generated proposals demand incompatible use of one authenticated
  participant base version;

induced arbitration kernel
  = conditional law of generated selection records given the generated
  opportunity carrier.
```

Only after that object exists can one ask which K-family member, or which new
family, the generated law induces and whether its activity parameters are
functions of the generator's rates.  D39 pins that question but does not
promise its construction in Paper 28.

## 3. The positive locality target: an admission certificate

### 3.1 Baseline and prior

D38b proves finite existence by carrying a complete authenticated history as
an admission witness.  The witness derives every current head and incidence,
so a regional append cannot fork a head.  Paper 27 correctly says this is not
a locality theorem.

D39 first tests the prior that admission is per-touched-wire.  Every D34b event
touches at most two existing actor wires; no-fork is a per-wire safety
property.  D36 round one supplies the governing warning: an unsigned or
self-authored `tau` cannot assert a remote tip.  Legitimate tip information
must be authenticated, owner-issued and actor-local.

### 3.2 Candidate H0 — static signed-head certificate

For a proposed event `e`, the smallest candidate certificate is

```text
Cert_0(e) = (
  exact typed event body,
  for every touched existing wire w:
    HEAD_CLAIM(owner(w), w, current_head_id, row_digest, event_body_digest),
  authenticated incident edge/port credentials named by e
).
```

A birth has no prior child head.  Its parent's claim must bind the fresh child
ordinal, Ulam identity, new edge, two ports and child clock address.  An
interaction must carry both existing wire claims and the authenticated
incident port/edge typing.  Idle carries one existing wire claim.

Every head claim uses the corpus's ideal signature discipline.  It is not a
cryptographic or Byzantine theorem.

`Cert_0` is tested first and is not presumed sufficient.  A signed statement
that a head was current when issued may be stale when used.  The receipt must
include an interleaving in which a genuine later event advances a wire after a
claim is signed but before the certificate is presented.  If the stale claim
passes, H0 is rejected and the counterexample is retained as a result.

### 3.3 Candidate H1 — one-use attempt-bound per-wire grants

If H0 fails only through freshness or replay, the next candidate is not a
global witness.  It is a D36-style owner-local capability:

```text
attempt = carrier-derived typed event attempt;

HEAD_PREPARE(attempt, wire, expected_head, event_body_digest);
HEAD_GRANT(owner, attempt, wire, expected_head, row_digest);

decision accepted only after one authentic grant from every touched wire;
each owner grants at most one incompatible attempt for that head;
apply consumes the grant and advances that owner's head exactly once.
```

The attempt key is derived from the event carrier and touched-wire roles, not
from a global transaction ordinal.  Participant comparison is actor-local.
The decision is causally after the required grants; no physical global commit
order is introduced.  Rejection or incomplete grant collection releases the
attempt without a partial physical event.

H1 deliberately reuses D36's safety ontology.  It is an admission protocol,
not yet a stochastic generated-conflict law and not evidence for a K-family
arbitration kernel.

### 3.4 Certificate theorem target

For every registered finite D38b history and proposed birth/idle/interaction,
compare certificate admission with the complete-witness oracle.  A successful
certificate must have both directions:

```text
soundness:    certificate acceptance implies complete-witness acceptance;
completeness: every complete-witness-valid next event has an obtainable
              certificate that accepts;
state match:  both routes derive the same successor discrete rows, heads and
              simple-edge incidence.
```

The receipt must disclose which candidate succeeds:

```text
H0_SUFFICIENT_AT_DECLARED_INTERLEAVING_SCOPE;
or
H0_REJECTED / H1_SUFFICIENT;
or
PER_WIRE_CERTIFICATE_REJECTED_WITH_GLOBAL-DEPENDENCY WITNESS.
```

Failure is a finding.  The implementation may not recover a PASS by silently
reintroducing the complete history.

## 4. Selection conditions classify; they do not promise a point

### 4.1 Action/history compatibility

Paper 16's warning remains load-bearing: writing `exp(-I)` for a supplied
opportunity does not by itself produce or select a normalized history law.
D39 tests an increment family only after record closure and comparison.

For an admitted boundary `b` and typed event `e`, let `w(e|b)>0`.  The local
conditional is

```text
gamma(e|b) = w(e|b) / sum_(e' admitted at b) w(e'|b).
```

Calling `w=exp(-J)` action-derived is meaningful here only if the increment
family satisfies all declared constraints:

```text
typed locality          J reads only the declared regional boundary/event;
relabeling covariance   J is invariant under admitted oriented renamings;
restriction naturality  increments agree under the Level-A comparison;
commuting-update cocycle
  w(e1|b) w(e2|U_e1 b) = w(e2|b) w(e1|U_e2 b)
  whenever e1,e2 are declared independent;
history compatibility  finite path products descend to the admitted
                       construction-order quotient or record why they do not.
```

The receipt works with exact positive weights and product equations; symbolic
`J=-log w` may be printed, but floating logarithms carry no theorem.

The target is to solve and classify the finite constraint set.  Boundarywise
normalization gauges, action coboundaries or multiple inequivalent positive
weight packets must be reported rather than tuned away.  If D38b's chosen
kernel violates one of the added action conditions, the obstruction is the
result; the condition may not be weakened after seeing the output.

### 4.2 Finite all-transport unimodularity

Root-free balance is a separate condition.  For a probability law `mu` on
finite connected marked networks rooted at `o`, require

```text
E_mu sum_v F(G,o,v) = E_mu sum_v F(G,v,o)
```

for every nonnegative covariant transport `F` in the declared finite marked
state space.

On a finite registry, this is exhaustible.  D39 enumerates the isomorphism
orbits of doubly rooted marked networks.  Indicator functions of those orbits
form a complete finite transport basis.  Passing every basis row proves the
finite all-transport statement, not merely one illustrative transport.

The expected finite classification theorem is itself gated:

```text
conditional on each unrooted finite connected marked network,
an all-transport root law is uniform over vertices
(equivalently, rooted-isomorphism weights are proportional to orbit sizes).
```

Mixture weights between different unrooted networks may remain free.  Uniform
rerooting is an added root law; it does not show that the seeded D34b growth
process has an intrinsic root-free infinite completion.

The receipt must keep three statements separate:

```text
finite all-transport classification;
compatibility of that root law with a finite generated history distribution;
existence of an infinite root-free/unimodular completion.
```

The third remains open unless D39 actually constructs it.  Unimodularity alone
must not be advertised as selecting D34b's birth/interaction rates.

## 5. Observable classes are frozen before identification

It also excludes any auxiliary total serialization of incomparable actor

calibrated timed laboratory record.

the chosen construction-time coordinate.  Paper 21 and D34d forbid calling


together with the monitored system, dwell interval and units.  Only this class
can support an absolute physical rate statement.

### O-V — visibility/birth records

carriers, the coherent parent line, coupling `g`, monitored record count and

## 6. Identifiability target: ratios first, physical scale only through a bridge

On a finite positive registered state, parameterize typed event intensities by

```text
lambda_(a,m,t) = rho_a * beta_m * q_t(a,m),
```

where `a` is the initiating actor, `m` the mode, `t` an eligible target role,
`rho_a>0` an actor-clock factor, `beta_m>0` a mode weight and `q_t` the declared
target allocation.  D34b is one supplied point of this family.

An auxiliary embedded-generator row is

```text
P_emb(a,m,t | z) = lambda_(a,m,t)(z) / Lambda(z),
Lambda(z) = sum_(a,m,t) lambda_(a,m,t)(z).
```

That row is a computational representation, not an observable global winner
among incomparable events.  The O-U law `P_U` is obtained by pushing complete
embedded paths to typed causal DAGs and summing every gauge-equivalent
serialization.

The O-U theorem target, on a declared connected positive parameter family with
the complete typed event alphabet observed, is

```text
P_U(lambda) = P_U(lambda')
iff
lambda' = c lambda for one c>0 on the registered connected component.
```

This projective injectivity is a target, not an inherited fact.  It must be
proved from gauge-pushed typed DAG probabilities.  Paper 21's marked shared-
wire order cell is the mandatory relative-rate witness: packets `(1,2)` and
`(2,2)` give respective order masses `(1/48,1/24)` and `(1/32,1/32)` without
reading an incomparable global serialization.  If the target passes, O-U
identifies actor-rate ratios, mode-weight ratios and target-allocation ratios
when their factorization is itself identifiable.  If it fails, every extra
null direction is retained.  In either case the common intensity scale is not
identified.  The receipt must include:

```text
common-scaling null direction;
two relative-rate packets separated by an untimed order probability;
two mode-weight packets separated by typed mode counts;
a reduced O-L collision or a proof that none exists on the registered cell;
Jacobian/rank and exact nonlinear injectivity checks, kept distinct.
```

The timed survival law is

```text
P(T>t | z) = exp[-Lambda(z)t].
```

O-T identifies `Lambda` in construction-time units.  Under O-C the observed
physical hazard is `Lambda/s`.  If `s` is not fixed, the transformation

```text
(Lambda,s) -> (c Lambda,c s)
```

leaves every calibrated-time distribution unchanged.  A declared clock bridge
fixing `s` is therefore load-bearing for an absolute physical rate.

D26 visibility can constrain BORN incidence and `g` through
`(1-g)^(N/2)` only under O-V.  Event counts without a dwell-time bridge do not
become rates.

## 7. Operational width at pinned alphabets

Paper 27 proves that nonzero attenuation does not merge an exact infinite-
precision Branch-F quotient.  D39 asks a different, operational question:

> At O-P's declared preparation/query alphabet and rational tolerance
> `epsilon`, does a registered sealing/attenuation channel admit a finite
> predictive carrier with error at most `epsilon`?

For a candidate carrier `C`, define its error by the supremum total-variation
distance over every admitted preparation/query pair in O-P.  `W_op(O-P,
epsilon)` is the least disclosed carrier capacity achieving that bound on the
registered family.  Threshold closeness is used as an error criterion, not
silently promoted to an equivalence relation.

The receipt must gate all of the following:

1. at `epsilon=0`, every nonzero exact influence remains distinguishable;
2. at positive rational `epsilon`, a single attenuated path can acquire a
   finite cutoff;
3. per-record attenuation is insufficient when the number `N_r` of omitted
   records grows fast enough that the exact tail bound
   `sum_(r>R) N_r delta_r` does not fall below `epsilon`;
4. a summable-tail positive control does fall below `epsilon` at a printed
   finite `R`;
5. the D31B cancellation cell is evaluated from the complete operational
   channel, not inferred from transitive structural reachability; and
6. an exact-zero read cut is labeled a hypothetical interface rule unless a
   physical V6 record bridge is actually constructed.

The quarter-law/sealing language may enter only conditionally through its
declared alphabet and bridge.  D39 does not identify a toy attenuation factor
with a physical V6 seal, derive a sealing rate or erase `W_ont`.
