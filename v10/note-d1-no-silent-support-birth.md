# D1 — intrinsic no-silent center and support-birth campaign

**Status:** original preregistration frozen before the production receipt,
2026-07-11; O1 was a transparent pre-paper amendment after the first exact
execution; O2 below records hostile-round-1 openings before the repaired
receipt. No result is promoted outside its executed gate.

## 1. Question

Given a finite committed sealed history, can the existing boundary law force
the appearance of a new joint support without using emergent distance, a
global construction order, hidden labels, or a fitted future outcome?

The v6 proposal is the no-silent seam. If lower record data `X` and upper
record data `Z` meet through a visible screen `Y`, exact eventless composition
requires

$$
X\perp Z\mid Y.
$$

If this fails, a boundary-definable center may have to be printed. D1 tests
whether that necessity is also a unique support-birth law.

## 2. Frozen finite object

A D1 cell is a rational probability law

$$
p(x,z,b)
$$

on finite alphabets. Here:

- `X` is the retained record on the lower/left side of the candidate seam;
- `Z` is the retained record on the upper/right side;
- `B` is the complete boundary micro-record made available to this finite
  experiment;
- the visible screen `Y=y(B)` is a deterministic partition of `B`.

The complete boundary `B` is not assumed to be physically minimal. It is the
finite arena in which candidate centers are enumerated. A candidate center is
not an arbitrary hidden variable. It is a deterministic boundary partition

$$
C=c(B)
$$

that refines `Y`: every `C`-cell lies inside one `Y`-cell. Center labels are
gauge; only the partition of boundary atoms matters.

## 3. Exact seam criterion

For a partition `C`, exact conditional independence is tested without
logarithms. For every center cell `c` and every `x,z`,

$$
p(x,z,c)p(c)=p(x,c)p(z,c).
$$

All quantities are rational. This cross-product identity is the primary gate.
Conditional mutual information is a high-precision report, not the equality
oracle:

$$
I(X;Z\mid C)
=
\sum_{x,z,c}p(x,z,c)
\log\frac{p(x,z,c)p(c)}{p(x,c)p(z,c)}.
$$

Zero-probability terms use the standard support convention. Structural zeros
are disclosed because they can destroy intersection properties and create
multiple minimal separators.

## 4. Order on centers

For boundary partitions `C1,C2`, write

$$
C_1\preceq C_2
$$

when `C2` refines `C1`. Thus `Y` is the least admissible partition and the
singleton partition of `B` is the greatest.

A complete center satisfies

$$
X\perp Z\mid C.
$$

A minimal complete center is a complete center with no strictly coarser
complete refinement of `Y` below it.

In the unmarked D1A census, two centers are the same iff they induce the same
boundary partition; relabeling center values does not create a second center.
In D1B, a center algebra also carries the typed pair `(field name, stable
provenance)`. Distinct pairs are retained even when they induce one partition,
unless a future record-gauge quotient explicitly identifies them.

## 5. Support-birth reading

The proposed rule is deliberately conditional:

1. If `Y` is complete, no new center-supported joint event is licensed.
2. If `Y` is incomplete and there is a unique minimal nonreconstructive
   complete center `C*`, that center is **eligible for the supplied candidate
   seam**.
3. If several incomparable minimal centers exist, no unique center is
   identified even after the candidate seam has been supplied.
4. If only the full boundary lookup closes the seam, the current grain does
   not supply a nonreconstructive physical center.
5. If even the full boundary fails, no boundary-definable center exists in the
   cell; the boundary must expand or a new sector must be supplied.

Eligibility is not firing probability. D1 does not derive the positive
extension weight or outcome/transfer kernel.

## 6. Nonreconstruction

Let `|B|` be the number of boundary atoms with nonzero probability and `|C|`
the number of occupied center cells. A center is full lookup when

$$
|C|=|B|.
$$

The main D1 positive requires

$$
|C|<|B|.
$$

This finite inequality is only the first nonreconstruction gate. It does not
prove asymptotic subraw growth.

## 7. Frozen scenario suite

The production receipt must include all of the following.

### S0 — factorized statistical control

`X` and `Z` are independent at the visible constant screen. Boundary nuisance
data are independent. Expected: `Y` is already complete; no joint support.

### S1 — complete visible screen

`X` and `Z` share variation through `Y` but factor conditionally on it.
Expected: unconditional dependence may be positive, while `Y` is complete.

### S2 — unique nonlookup center

`Y` omits a boundary common-cause bit `H`; the complete boundary also contains
independent nuisance `N`. Expected: `(Y,H)` is the unique minimal complete
partition and forgets `N`.

### S3 — common-ancestor branch

Two descendant record ports share a recorded ancestor carried by the
boundary. Expected: the ancestor partition is a complete nonlookup center.

### S4 — three-record common-root law

A supplied three-variable common-root law carries a shared center plus
nuisance. Expected: center detection is invariant under boundary-atom and
center-label relabeling. This does not by itself prove an irreducible
three-record event.

### S5 — competing minimal centers

Search or construct an exact finite law with positive visible residual and at
least two incomparable minimal complete boundary partitions. Strict positivity
is preferred; structural zeros must be disclosed if used. This is a mandatory
adversary. If none is found in the frozen search class, report the search
boundary; do not infer uniqueness universally.

### S6 — lookup-only closure

The full boundary atom is the only complete refinement of `Y`. Expected: seam
residue is real, but nonreconstructive support birth is refused.

### S7 — no boundary-definable closure

Even conditioning on the full boundary leaves `X,Z` dependent. Expected:
boundary expansion/new sector required.

### S8 — target-marginalization and screen/grain adversaries

Classify separately (a) marginalizing a target port and recomputing the
center, and (b) changing which boundary field is declared visible. Neither is
to be called a record-history restriction without an explicit typed map.

## 8. Covariance gates

For every scenario:

1. permuting boundary atoms and transporting the screen produces the
   transported set of minimal partitions;
2. permuting `X` or `Z` outcome labels changes no center verdict;
3. D1A center-value labels never enter canonicalization;
4. D1B's typed field-name/provenance marks are preserved and are not confused
   with arbitrary integer center-value labels;
5. all D1A enumeration is over partitions, not integer labels or construction
   histories.

## 9. Typed operation and restriction gates

Three operations must not be conflated:

1. **target marginalization:** remove a variable from `X` or `Z` while holding
   the boundary alphabet and visible screen fixed;
2. **screen/grain change:** alter which boundary observable is declared
   visible;
3. **record-history restriction:** project a marked history's lineages, ports,
   parent map, boundary-field carriers, support marks, and joint law together.

Only the third operation supports a projectivity equation. For a typed record
restriction to retained lineages `K`, D1B uses the explicit intersection
projection `r_*S=S\cap K`, discarding supports with fewer than two retained
lineages. The target is

$$
r_*\operatorname{Eligible}(p)
=
\operatorname{Eligible}(r_*p).
$$

This equality is not assumed. D1 must classify:

- target-marginalization cells in which a center is retained or becomes
  unnecessary;
- screen changes that create or erase apparent eligibility;
- exact typed record restrictions, including direct-versus-successive paths;
- any genuine typed failure in which projected and recomputed eligible
  supports differ.

A marginalization or screen-change example cannot establish failure of typed
record projectivity. O4's parity/synergy cell is a genuine marked-history
restriction and therefore decides the strong eligibility equation negatively,
even though the underlying history-data restriction remains path-independent.

## 10. Numerical discipline

- Probability and partition gates: integers and `fractions.Fraction` only.
- Conditional-independence equality: exact cross products only.
- Entropy reports: Python standard-library `Decimal` with precision 120 or
  greater; no virtual environment or third-party package is required.
- Canonical output: deterministic ordering and byte-reproducible text.
- Random search, if used to discover an adversary, is not the final receipt;
  the discovered integer table is frozen and checked exhaustively.

## 12. Verdict map

### IDENTIFY-SCOPED

Allowed only if the tested physical class has a unique nonlookup minimal
center, factorized refusal, covariance, and a valid typed restriction rule.
Claim ceiling: finite center identification inside an already supplied seam,
not support birth or firing probability.

### FAMILY / NO-SELECTION

If incomparable minima survive with different support semantics, no-silent
closure is necessary but not a unique birth law.

### GAUGE-CANDIDATE

If incomparable minima give identical future marked predictions under every
tested extension, their difference may be center gauge. This requires a
separate quotient theorem; it is not assumed in D1.

### GRAIN-REFUSAL

If closure is lookup-only or absent, the finite boundary grain cannot supply a
physical center.

### PROJECTIVITY-REFUSAL

If typed support eligibility cannot be transported consistently under genuine
record restriction, the strong eligible-family rule is refused. The marked
history data may still form a path-independent restriction system.
