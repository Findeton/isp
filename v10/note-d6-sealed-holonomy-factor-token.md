**Status:** preregistered before the D6 executable was written or run,
2026-07-11; revised after hostile-review round 1. The original title asked
whether sealed holonomy generates a primitive token. Round 1 correctly forced
the executed arena down to supplied ordered-law RN reconstruction. Reviews ON.
All production code, data, papers, and reviews must

## 1. Question

D5 proved a conditional composition theorem. If a complete finite factor
partition is supplied, exact separator contraction composes it. But D5 did
not obtain the primitive factor token that starts that algebra.

D6 asks whether the older sealed-diamond machinery already generates such a
token. The desired object has at least these logically distinct fields:

```text
proposal opportunity
scope / participating records
orientation
factor value or whole-history likelihood
ownership / exactly-once identity
eligibility and null alternative
evidence increment
seal / division status
accepted record outcome
```

The executed arena does not itself construct a typed sealed diamond, closed
exchange loop, or physical holonomy measurement. Those are possible supplied
interpretations, not proved objects. The investigation must not identify description with generation. In
particular, an RN derivative can describe the change from one supplied law to
another without proposing either law.

## 2. Corpus commitments under audit

### 2.1 Sealed diamond and RN exchange action

For two mutually absolutely continuous ordered process laws on the same
finite supplied history space,

$$
A=\log\frac{dP_{AB}}{dP_{BA}}
$$

is canonical. Equivalently the positive likelihood factor is

$$
R=\frac{dP_{AB}}{dP_{BA}}.
$$

It obeys reversal, normalization, and independent-composition identities.
D6 will credit these as generic RN derivations conditional on both ordered
laws. Calling them physical sealed holonomy requires a further typed diamond,
screen, exchange-loop, and closure construction.

### 2.2 Complete closed-holonomy ledger

V6 Paper 4 proposed a complete set of nonconstant closed-history contrasts.
On a finite history space with `m` atoms it contains `m-1` independent
coefficients, exactly the dimension of the positive probability simplex.
Given those coefficients, the law is reconstructible.

D6 will distinguish:

```text
finite identifiability from a complete supplied cochain
```

from:

```text
physical selection of the cochain, its primitive units, and its orientation.
```

### 2.3 Conditional division-event commitment on a supplied normalized mode

V6 derives, conditional on additive RN/KL evidence `I`,

$$
S(I)=e^{-I}.
$$

On a supplied primitive oriented contrast it further proposes

$$
\nabla\psi(h)=e^{-h}.
$$

For one parity mode this is `tanh(h)=exp(-h)`. D6 will test and preserve this
conditional coefficient-selection result. It will separately test whether the
same rule selects the primitive mode, its sign, or its opportunity to arise.

### 2.4 Diamond center, positive shadow, and h-transform

V7 Paper 31 gives the finite transition form

$$
\Pr(P\mid C)\propto D(P,C)h(P)
$$

after a candidate set, diamond center, positive shadow, and effective weights
have been supplied or learned in the audited finite sector. Paper 31 itself
marks the center and weight as teacher-dependent and refuses universal
uniqueness. D6 will not treat normalization over a supplied candidate list as
generation of that list.

### 2.5 Profinite histories

A projectively compatible family of finite cylinder laws determines a measure
on the inverse-limit history space. D6 will test the narrower question:
whether projective compatibility selects one such family. Hosting a law and
selecting a law are different statements.

## 3. Frozen finite arenas

### 3.1 Binary ordered-law arena

All finite history atoms are binary tuples. Strictly positive rational laws
`P,Q` give the exact RN factor `R=P/Q`. Exact gates use `Fraction`; only
`log(R)` and KL reports use `Decimal`.

The conditional RN data extracted from supplied ordered laws is:

```text
coordinate dependence = presentation-relative essential coordinates of R
protocol orientation  = P relative to Q
numerical factor field = R
expected evidence      = D(P || Q)
comparison-neutral     = R identically 1
```

This extraction is not yet a D5 token: listed physical scope, unique ID,
ownership, primitive/derived type, and complete-cover allocation are absent.
It also leaves proposal, eligibility, realized evidence, physical seal, and
accepted birth unset. `AB/BA` are treated only as supplied protocol
orientations; no construction-order gauge theorem is proved.

### 3.2 Parity twins

For three binary variables and `theta` in `Q intersect (-1,1)`, define

$$
P_\theta(x)=2^{-3}(1+\theta\,x_1x_2x_3).
$$

The `+theta` and `-theta` laws have identical one- and two-coordinate
marginals but different triple history. This audits whether lower-order
diamond shadows determine a primitive high-order factor.

### 3.3 Hidden-history lifts

Two laws may have the same endpoint marginals and the same endpoint RN ratio
while retaining different conditional hidden histories. D6 will construct
this exactly and test a future readout that separates the lifts.

### 3.4 Commitment modes

The scalar commitment root will be solved at Decimal precision 110 for a
supplied parity contrast. The same construction will be repeated after
reversing the contrast and after changing its scope. These are not attacks on
the root; they are tests of the premise that the primitive oriented mode is
already known.

### 3.5 Proposal and profinite families

A symmetric two-candidate proposal arena includes an explicit null outcome.
Multiple null masses will be tested under the same symmetry and evidence
data. Separately, exact Bernoulli cylinder families with different rational
parameters will test projective nonselection.

## 4. Preregistered gates

### R — RN reconstruction

**R1.** Given positive normalized `P,Q`, `R=P/Q` exactly satisfies
`E_Q R=1`, `E_P R^{-1}=1`, reversal, and `P=QR`.

**R2.** Independent products multiply RN factors; joint relabeling covaries.

**R3.** `P=Q` gives exactly `R equiv 1` and zero KL: an RN-neutral
comparison. It does not imply an eventless history or a null proposal.

**R4.** Common support alone does not determine `R`. Equal scalar KL evidence
under a reversal-symmetric pair does not determine orientation or the RN
field.

**R5.** The essential coordinate scope of `R` is derivable only after `P,Q`
are supplied. It is not a proposal rule for which laws or records are offered.

### H — whole-history and complete-ledger gates

**H1.** Three-bit parity twins have exactly equal one/two shadows and unequal
triple history.

**H2.** A full binary character basis has `2^n-1` nonconstant directions,
equal to the simplex dimension. Its high-precision log-density transform
reconstructs a supplied positive law.

**H3.** Dropping the top parity channel leaves a nontrivial fiber of laws.

**H4.** Endpoint marginals plus their full RN action can remain identical
while hidden-history lifts and future readouts differ.

**H5.** Complete supplied Walsh/log-density-ledger reconstruction will be
reported as finite identifiability. A physical closed-holonomy theorem would
additionally need typed closed paths, cochain provenance, primitive contrast
units, orientations, and coefficient measurement without first receiving the
law. The v6 phrase “all future-relevant contrasts” is a consistency criterion,
not a first proposal generator, unless future response is independently known.

### C — commitment and survival/division-weight gates

**C1.** `S(I)=exp(-I)` obeys additive gluing in supplied evidence, has
`S(0)=1`, and gives complementary numerical survival/no-division weight `S`
and division/commitment weight `1-S` once actual accumulated evidence exists.

**C2.** Reversal-symmetric candidates with equal expected KL magnitude receive
the same scalar survival/division weights although their RN orientations are
opposite. Expected KL is not a realized evidence carrier, and neither weight
is an executed physical seal event.

**C3.** `tanh(h)=exp(-h)` has the audited positive root and selects a
coefficient for a supplied balanced `+/-1`, naturally parameterized, oriented
parity mode.

**C4.** Reversing the primitive orientation gives the mirror law; changing
the primitive parity scope gives another law satisfying the same scalar
commitment equation. The rule therefore does not select scope or orientation.

**C5.** Numerical survival/division weighting is not proposal or realized
sealing. A rule conditional on `I` does not define candidate opportunities,
their null mass, evidence carrier, random commitment outcome, or ownership.

### P — proposal, factor ownership, and h-transform gates

**P1.** Symmetry between two opposite candidates leaves a free null mass.
Two normalized proposal laws with different null mass will pass the same
candidate symmetry.

**P2.** A supplied h-transform normalizes supplied deletion multiplicities
and positive weights. Different admissible weights or a changed candidate
domain give different transitions.

**P3.** A factorized RN field may be represented by one composite supplied
token or multiple primitive supplied tokens. The joint law alone does not
determine exactly-once physical ownership.

**P4.** An irreducible RN field relative to a declared tensor split requires a
supplied nonproduct joint law. Independent tensor-sector laws generate only a
factorized RN field. This does not by itself establish disconnected physical
components or a cross carrier.

### F — profinite gate

**F1.** Three positive rational Bernoulli cylinder families are each exactly
compatible under end deletion. `p=1/3` and `p=2/3` are global-sign mirrors;
`p=1/2` lies outside that mirror pair.

**F2.** Their finite laws and inverse-limit measures differ. Profinite
compatibility hosts both and selects neither.
