## Abstract

SHARD histories are intended to be generated by record-local events while
their observable shadows retain memory. These requirements are compatible,
but not for the simple reason suggested by the statement “enlarge each local
state until the law is Markov.” Remembering the complete past always gives a
Markov representation, but that state is global and generally grows without
bound. For the chosen static-adjacency D34b birth/idle/interaction exemplar we
prove a sharper result: the complete Harris configuration is a time-
homogeneous strong Markov process, and its generator is a sum of actor-star-
local terms. Here local means bounded touched/write support and graph-star-
local rate data; the incident star can have unbounded degree. This does not
yield a bounded predictive state inside one record. In the actual D34b
grammar, B's incoming-interaction rate toward A changes exactly from `1/4` to
`1/8` when B acquires a second neighbor, although A's own tip and private clock
state are unchanged.

Observable memory is characterized through predictive sufficiency and
law-scoped lumpability. An exact three-state specimen has histories `10` and
`00` with the same present record but next-record probabilities `3/8` and
`7/20`. Its observer predictive beliefs continue to proliferate even though
the hidden realization has only three states. Exponential construction clocks
remove residual-age memory; a renewal replacement requires the complete age
vector. Common rate scaling is a change of construction-time units only when
the horizon is scaled with it, and relative actor rates remain physical model
data.

The quantum analogue is operational and instrument-specific. The D34c
diamond shows that durable classical records can omit predictive coherence. A
separate fixed three-slot process then passes the causal-break criterion for
quantum memory: two past instruments, followed by the same middle outcome and
repreparation, produce certain opposite future outcomes. Retaining the joint
system–boundary state Markovizes this finite total evolution; eliminating the
boundary produces a reduced multi-time memory process.

The resulting endpoint is conditional and exact: **the chosen static-
adjacency D34b exemplar has a global strong-Markov configuration generated by
actor-star-local event terms, while record projections can be non-Markov when
they omit the relevant boundary or belief state.** No bounded all-future local
predictive collar, physical proper time, timed quantum history law or unique
universe rule is derived.

## 1. The question and the false shortcut

The proposed reconciliation was:

> Every consistent SHARD history law is Markov on an appropriately enlarged
> local state, while every physically observable record process is generally
> non-Markovian.

This sentence combines four distinct claims:

1. a complete history can be used as a Markov state;
2. the state can be compressed without loss of future predictions;
3. that compression can be carried on a local record or collar;
4. the visible projection is non-Markov.

Only the first is automatic. The second is a predictive-sufficiency problem.
The third is a locality and capacity theorem. The fourth depends on the
projection and initial-law scope; identity and lumpable projections remain
Markov.

The corrected investigation therefore asks:

> What is the smallest operational state that screens the licensed future,
> where is that state carried, and what memory appears when an observer
> retains less than it?

## 2. Five state levels

The following objects must remain separate.

### 2.1 Complete past

For a history law `mu` with past filtration `F_t`, let

```text
X_t = complete marked history through t.
```

When regular conditional future laws exist, `X_t` is Markov by construction:
conditioning on `X_t` is conditioning on the whole past. Null histories still
require a declared conditional version, and a nonstationary law may require
the current parameter `t` inside the state. This representation is generally
global and unbounded.

### 2.2 Current generative configuration

For the chosen D34b actor law, `Z_t` contains the current information required
by the generator:

- active or sealed Ulam actors;
- private ring and birth ordinals;
- typed adjacency and eligible-neighbor sets;
- current wire tips and persistent event records;
- the modeled carrier fields.

This is the current configuration of the entire constructed universe. It is
distributed over actor and event records, but it is still global.

### 2.3 Predictive state

Two pasts are predictively equivalent when they give the same conditional law
for every licensed future experiment:

```text
h ~pred h'  iff  Law(future | h) = Law(future | h').
```

The equivalence class `[h]_pred` is the minimal behavioral predictive state at
the declared law, future-query and instrument scope. It need not have finite
width, bounded storage or a record-local realization.

### 2.4 Boundary or collar state

A regional state `B_A(h)` is a local predictive state only if

```text
Law(licensed future of A | complete past h)
    = Q_A(licensed future | B_A(h))
```

and `B_A` is actually carried by A, its incident records or a declared
boundary. Calling the complete configuration “distributed” does not prove
this factorization. A whole past-closed stem is a representative before
predictive quotienting; an active collar is only a candidate physical carrier
of the quotient. Predictive equivalence is not stem equivalence. A future
investigation may ask whether a law-relative predictive quotient factors
through marked stem data or an active collar, but neither factorization nor a
map to the v9 profinite stem spectrum is established here.

### 2.5 Observable record

An observer retains a projection

```text
R_t = pi(predictive or generative state).
```

The record process is Markov only if this projection retains enough state to
close its own future law at the declared initial-law and instrument scope.

## 3. Global strong Markov, local generator

### 3.1 The chosen D34b law

Every active unsealed actor `y` has a rate-one Poisson ring process. At a ring
it selects:

```text
birth                         probability 1/4,
interaction with x in E_y    probability 1/(4 |E_y|) each,
idle                          probability 1/2,
```

where `E_y` is the current set of eligible unsealed neighbors. Birth creates a
fresh Ulam child. Interaction creates one shared event on initiator and
receiver wires; the receiver's private ring does not advance. Idle creates an
event only on the initiator wire. Sealed actors neither initiate nor receive
these events in the reconstructed grammar.

### 3.2 Generator

For a bounded cylinder function `f` on the complete configuration,

```text
(L f)(z) = sum_y [
    1/4 (f(B_y z)-f(z))
  + sum_(x in E_y) 1/(4 |E_y|) (f(I_yx z)-f(z))
  + 1/2 (f(N_y z)-f(z)) ].
```

Every actor row has total intensity one. Every term reads the initiator's
current local eligibility data and touches only the initiator plus a fresh
child or declared target. There is no universe-wide opportunity denominator.
“Local” here means bounded touched/write support and graph-star-local rate
data. The incident eligible star is graph-radius one but may have unbounded
degree, so no uniformly bounded read support or memory width is proved.

### 3.3 Strong-Markov theorem

**Theorem 1.** *The ideal chosen D34b configuration process `Z_t` is a
time-homogeneous strong Markov pure-jump process at stopping times of its
completed natural construction-time filtration: the filtration generated by
revealed actor events and source coordinates through `t`, completed in the
usual probabilistic sense.*

**Proof.** Preassign independent rate-one Poisson point processes and
independent preparation-independent mark streams to all possible Ulam actor
addresses. Activate an address at its birth. On every finite construction-time
interval, the inherited D34b Yule bound gives finitely many live actors and
events almost surely, so the total jump intensity is finite. Every jump is a
measurable function of the current configuration and the fresh local mark.
Independent Poisson increments after a stopping time, together with the
independent unused marks, have the original product law and are independent of
the stopped past. Applying the same current-state update map therefore gives
the strong-Markov transition law. ∎

This theorem attaches to the ideal probability source. The high-precision
Decimal/keyed-stream actor program is a reference implementation, not a proof
that deterministic pseudorandom tapes are iid.

### 3.4 What “local” has been proved

The generator is support-local in its update terms. Disconnected actor
components factor, and changes in a disconnected component do not alter A's
local generator row. This is stronger than a global sequential lottery, but
weaker than a bounded predictive-state theorem for one record.

“Distributed” means that the configuration and generator source terms are
factored across actor and event records. It does not claim literal operating-
system processes or hardware threads, and it does not remove the central
atomic evaluator used by the reference simulator to commit shared events.

## 4. The exact obstruction to one-record sufficiency

Let A and B be adjacent active actors. When B has only A as an eligible
neighbor,

```text
rate(B interacts into A)
  = actor rate x interaction mass x target share
  = 1 x 1/4 x 1
  = 1/4.
```

Let B birth an additional unsealed child C. A's own tip, ring ordinal and
private clock state are unchanged, but

```text
rate(B interacts into A)
  = 1 x 1/4 x 1/2
  = 1/8.
```

**Proposition 2.** *A's own tip/private actor state is not a sufficient
predictive state for A's next wire event in the chosen D34b law.*

The counterexample is exact and internal to the actual actor grammar. It does
not show nonlocal dynamics: the missing datum is B's locally stored eligible
degree. It shows that locality of the update rule does not imply that one
actor stores every datum needed to predict its future.

Passive reception sharpens the point. An incoming `i(B,A)` appends a physical
event and predecessor to A's wire but advances B's private ring, not A's.
After one such event:

```text
A own-ring count   = 0,
A wire-event count = 1,
global event count = 1.
```

Thus even “local click count” has two meanings.

No fixed bounded all-future collar is proved here. At reviewed strength the
possibilities are:

- a growing record-carried boundary state;
- a belief over unobserved neighboring configuration;
- the whole connected component for sufficiently rich future questions;
- an additional screening theorem not yet found.

## 5. Observable memory and lumpability

### 5.1 The exact classical witness

Use hidden states `A,B,C`, visible records `A,B -> 0`, `C -> 1`, uniform
initial law, and transition rows

```text
A -> (A,B,C) = (1/2, 0,   1/2),
B -> (A,B,C) = (0,   3/4, 1/4),
C -> (A,B,C) = (1/2, 1/2, 0).
```

The histories `10` and `00` both end at visible record zero. Their exact
posterior hidden states are

```text
belief(10) = (1/2,1/2,0),
belief(00) = (2/5,3/5,0),
```

and

```text
P(next record 1 | 10) = 3/8,
P(next record 1 | 00) = 7/20.
```

Therefore this visible process is not first-order Markov for the declared
initial law.

### 5.2 Small hidden state does not mean small predictive state

The three pure hidden states are future-distinct, but the observer's
predictive state is its posterior belief. Exact enumeration of positive
observed histories gives

```text
depth                    1  2  3  4  ... 12
reachable belief classes 2  3  4  5  ... 13.
```

At visible zero, every reachable belief has form `(p,1-p,0)` and

```text
P(next record 1) = 1/4 + p/4.
```

Distinct `p` values are therefore already predictively distinct in one step.
The belief state has a compact one-parameter representation here, but its
reachable values do not collapse to the three hidden labels.

### 5.3 Three lumpability scopes

Strong lumpability means that every hidden state in one visible block has the
same transition mass into every visible block. It makes the visible process
Markov for every initial law.

That condition is not necessary for one chosen initial law. A second exact
control is not strongly lumpable because hidden A and B have different block
rows, but from initial `delta_A` the offending B state is unreachable. The
visible process is then Markov on the closed reachable A/C sector.

The correct statement is therefore:

> a record projection is Markov exactly when the filtered predictive state
> closes through that projection at the explicitly declared initial-law
> scope; strong lumpability is the corresponding all-initial-laws criterion
> for the finite homogeneous chain.

“Observable processes are generally non-Markov” must mean that arbitrary
record projections are not guaranteed to be sufficient, not that every
record projection has memory.

## 6. What the construction clock does—and does not do

### 6.1 Four time objects

SHARD currently contains four different temporal notions:

1. actor-own ring ordinal;
2. causal/wire partial order;
3. D34b real-valued construction time;
4. an eventual emergent proper-time ruler.

Auxiliary heap serialization is gauge. None of these facts identifies
construction time with measured seconds or emergent proper time.

### 6.2 Why exponentials are Markov-friendly

For exponential survival `S(t)=exp(-lambda t)`,

```text
S(a+s)/S(a) = S(s).
```

Residual clock age can be omitted from `Z_t`. This is a property of the chosen
waiting law, not a derivation of that law from sealed records.

For a uniform renewal time on `[0,2]`, the probability of ringing in the next
half unit is

```text
age 0: 1/4,
age 1: 1/2.
```

The age-free configuration is insufficient. The complete graph-plus-age
vector is a piecewise-deterministic Markov state: residual survival is
`S(a+s)/S(a)`; surviving ages advance; the initiator resets after its ring; a
newborn starts at zero; a passive receiver does not reset. For actors at ages
`(0,1)`, the exact next-winner law is `(1/4,3/4)`.

An observer that does not know neighboring ages may still require a posterior
belief over them.

### 6.3 Rate and coordinate scopes

The exact transformation table is:

```text
Law_(c lambda)(Z_T) = Law_lambda(Z_(cT)).
```

Pathwise, divide every preassigned exponential wait by `c`, retain all marks

```text
(lambda_A,lambda_B)=(2,2): (1/32,1/32).
```

Relative rates have not been derived; they are model data.

## 7. Quantum predictive state and process memory

### 7.1 The D34c diamond

For fine histories `h=(s,p,o)`, with durable source/receiver value `s`,
unrecorded path `p` and durable output `o`, D34c has

```text
D(h,h') = (1/8) delta(s,s') delta(o,o')
          (-1)^[p(1+s+o)+p'(1+s'+o')].
```

It is a rank-four strongly positive functional. Coarse-graining the unrecorded
path at amplitude level gives

```text
(P_00,P_01,P_10,P_11) = (0,1/2,1/2,0).
```

Adding an orthogonal path receiver constructs a distinct recorded-path
functional

```text
D_rec(h,h') = delta(p,p') D(h,h') = I_8/8,
```

whose durable law is four `1/4`s.

For fixed `s`, the coherent path carrier and its dephased version have the same
computational-basis diagonal. The later H/output instrument is deterministic
`o=1-s` in the coherent context and uniform in the recorded context. Thus
durable `s` is operationally insufficient across the declared past instrument
contexts. Because those are distinct past instruments, this comparison alone
is not yet a one-process non-Markov test.

### 7.2 Fixed-process causal-break witness

Use one fixed process with system P and retained boundary/environment E.
Initially,

```text
rho_PE = 1/2 (|00><00| + |11><11|).
```

At the past slot choose `I_P` or `X_P`. At the middle slot apply the same
causal break: select outcome `P=0`, discard P, and reprepare `P=0`. The outcome
has probability `1/2` for either past choice, and the reduced middle system
state is exactly `P0` in both cases. The conditional joint states are

```text
past I: |00><00|,
past X: |01><01|.
```

Apply the same future `CNOT(E->P)` and read P:

```text
past I -> future P0 with certainty,
past X -> future P1 with certainty.
```

**Proposition 3.** *The declared instrument sequence witnesses operational
non-Markovianity of this fixed finite process: future statistics retain past-
instrument dependence after the same nonzero causal break and
repreparation.*

### 7.3 Two equivalent architectures at different widths

If E is retained, the joint `P,E` density operator is different in the two
middle histories and evolves by an ordinary fixed unitary. The total finite
evolution is Markov on that enlarged joint boundary state.

If E is eliminated, the reduced P state is identical at the middle cut but the
future differs. The reduced description therefore requires multi-time process
memory.

These are alternative representations, not contradictory ontologies. The
price of Markovization is retaining the state that carries the correlation.

For the D34c real carrier sector, `P0,P1,P+` form a tomographic effect set for
one real-symmetric two-level density operator. They do not reconstruct a
complex qubit's Y component or a multi-time process tensor. A non-Markov
quantum process cannot be assigned a universal finite Markov order for every
possible instrument merely from this finite exhibit.

### 10.1 Passive and operational scopes are different inputs

history measure `mu`, its completed natural filtration, a declared region `A`,



Stopping scope is part of the quotient. D34e must preregister separate algebras
