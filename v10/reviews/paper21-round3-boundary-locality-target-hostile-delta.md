## MAJOR findings

### M1 — predictive screening is not a “predictive form of no silent erasure”

Section 10.2 says that deep sealed records may be eliminated after every
influence on licensed futures is exported into the message and calls this the
predictive form of NSE. This conflates two different conditions.

Predictive screening is **query-relative**. It permits two pasts to be merged
whenever every `q in Q_A` has the same conditional future law. NSE is a
stronger physical claim about preservation of distinguishability/evidence in
the total ontology. Information can be irrelevant to the declared `Q_A` and
still be protected by NSE. Exporting only its influence on `Q_A` does not prove
that all sealed evidence has been routed isometrically or remains physically
recoverable.

There are two licensed operations that must be named separately:

```text
omit sealed data from the predictive boundary message
    — allowed after a screening proof; the sealed record still exists,

physically erase/coarse-grain the sealed record
    — requires the independent NSE/isometric-distinguishability theorem.
```

**Required repair:** replace the NSE sentence with this two-ledger rule. Add an
NSE gate to D34e only if physical deletion/compression is proposed: prove that
the total record/carrier map preserves all protected distinguishability, not
merely the selected future probabilities. Otherwise state that D34e compresses
the **predictive representation** while every sealed record persists in the
global history.

### M2 — recursive closure and the finite campaign use the wrong/insufficient
time update scope

The recursive rule in §10.3 updates after an event from old boundary plus
touched/created records. That is incomplete for continuous-time and renewal
laws. A predictive state at the next construction-time stop may also depend on

- the elapsed construction time `Delta t`;
- survival/no-ring information accumulated between events;
- deterministic age flow for renewal clocks;
- passive incoming receptions that change A's wire without advancing A's own
  ring;
- boundary crossings whose initiator lies outside A.

The rule must therefore be either explicitly embedded-chain-only or have the
form

```text
B_(tau') = U(B_tau, Delta t, no-event/survival data,
             event e, touched/created boundary records).
```

Section 10.6 then begins with an “embedded finite-event-horizon” campaign.
Global event count is the exact stopping rule D34b rejected as a locality
criterion: disconnected census changes its probabilities. It may be used as
an auxiliary enumeration truncation, but it cannot define the regional
predictive quotient or certify local screening.

**Required repair:** preregister separate algebras for fixed construction time,
A-own-ring count and A-wire-event count (including passive receptions). Keep
fixed global event depth as the named negative/control only. Every reported
predictive class and carrier witness must print which stopping algebra it uses.
For continuous time, gate the flow/survival update; for an embedded chain,
state exactly which time information was intentionally quotiented away.

Construction covariance must be tested at the same scope. Incomparable event
presentations may commute as typed DAG updates while different elapsed/no-event
information still changes a time-conditioned boundary message.

### M3 — the D34e “decision protocol” has no frozen outcome classes

The protocol lists computations but does not define mutually exclusive
verdicts. As written, a finite-radius candidate can pass several horizons,
width can grow, composition can be conditional, and the campaign has no rule
for whether the result is a theorem, a growing-boundary result, an obstruction,
or merely finite-horizon survival.

**Required repair:** freeze a decision table before code. At minimum:

1. **ALL-FUTURE BOUNDED-CARRIER PASS** — one declared record-native carrier
   has screening, recursive time/event closure, construction covariance,
   composition, capacity bound and a proof for all licensed futures.
2. **ALL-FUTURE GROWING-CARRIER PASS** — exact recursive/compositional carrier
   exists but physical width is unbounded or population/horizon dependent;
   no finite-memory headline.
3. **FINITE-HORIZON ONLY** — exact sufficiency survives every enumerated cell
   but no stabilization/all-future theorem is proved.
4. **CANDIDATE-CLASS OBSTRUCTION** — an exact same-carrier/different-future
   witness kills each tested carrier family at its declared scope; this does
   not prove no other carrier exists.
5. **WHOLE-COMPONENT/GLOBAL ONLY** — a proof shows the licensed quotient needs
   whole-component/global state, with the exact law/query scope stated.
6. **NO RECORD-CARRIED EXACT REALIZATION** — earned only by a theorem over the
   declared carrier class, not failure of a finite search.
7. **REFUSAL/UNDEFINED** — conditional version, interface typing, factor cover
   or stopping algebra is missing, so no quotient comparison is licensed.

The classes must say separately whether covariance, composition, NSE and
capacity passed. A partial pass cannot be averaged into a bounded-collar noun.

## MINOR findings

### m1 — the committed-prefix tower is auxiliary, not a physical boundary

The labeled global committed-prefix tower is useful for source-level online
conditioning, but its serialization of incomparable events is auxiliary and
fixed global depth is census-dependent. Call it an **auxiliary construction
presentation**. The physical regional candidate must descend to timed
past-closed restrictions or typed-DAG/down-set data and pass the construction-
covariance gate. Do not call the global prefix tower the natural physical
carrier.

### m2 — D5 composition is conditional supplied-factor algebra

D5 proves exact contraction/composition after a complete factor cover, scopes,
values, interface embeddings and exactly-once ownership are supplied. It does
not derive a record-native carrier, direction/transport law, factor scope or
cover completeness.

Section 10.3/10.6 should therefore say “D5-style conditional typed-factor
message candidate,” then require D34e to construct or explicitly assume and
audit the physical factor cover. A D5 contraction pass alone cannot earn the
record-carried or causal-boundary clauses.

### m3 — fixed graph radius is not bounded capacity when degree is unbounded

The protocol correctly tests fixed-radius candidates but must not classify a
fixed radius as a bounded collar. In D34b an actor's incident degree is
unbounded, so even radius one can contain arbitrarily many ports, actors and
factor legs. Print at least:

```text
graph radius,
actor/record count,
open-port count,
classical state count or continuous dimension,
quantum/operator rank,
identifier/precision cost.
```

Only a uniform bound on the relevant physical resource ledger licenses
“bounded.”

### m4 — “causal boundary” is record-DAG language, not a spacetime claim

Earlier paper text makes this distinction, but the new section's title should
carry it locally: the candidate is a boundary in the record/wire causal DAG.
No light-cone, Lorentz-covariance, finite propagation speed or proper-time
meaning is assumed. “Construction covariance” here means invariance under
allowed incomparable-event presentations, not general coordinate covariance.

### m5 — intervention notation should expose the controlled input and output

`Law_mu(q|h)` is compact but can blur an intervention with its random readout.
For classical controls use, schematically,

```text
Law_mu(output sequence | do(intervention sequence q), h),
```

and for quantum instruments compare the complete licensed outcome
distributions/process-functional pairings. This also makes clear that the
quotient changes when `Q_A` changes.
