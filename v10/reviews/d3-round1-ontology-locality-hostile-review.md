## Major findings

### M1 — A common-future extension is not yet an interaction or bridge record

The executable proves the following exact incidence statement:

> add a new maximal causet element whose past is a down-set meeting two or more
> comparability components.

Then the new element is above selected old elements and makes the **undirected
comparability graph** connected through itself. It inserts no old-old order
relation. This is correctly called a `bridge shadow` in Definition 2.2.

Nothing in the executable says:

- that the selected old elements jointly influence the new outcome;
- that information or a signal passes between them;
- that the event has multiple typed input ports;
- that it contains one joint RN/likelihood block;
- that a collar, holonomy, seal, or outcome couples them;
- or that the two old components were physically eligible to meet.

A common future may be a joint interaction, a common readout, a later
comparison, or merely one event causally above two independent causes. The
unmarked order cannot distinguish these ontologies.

The body acknowledges this, but the abstract, executable verdict, note, and
conclusion repeatedly say **“bridge birth,” “new joint incidence,”** or
“variable-history bridge birth is coherent.” Those phrases promote the weak
shadow into the missing interacting law.

**Required revision:** use `common-future extension` or `bridge-shadow
extension` for every D3 theorem and receipt verdict. Reserve `interaction`,
`joint record`, `bridge event`, and `carrier birth` for a marked law that makes
ports, a joint proposition/outcome, and evidence/transport load-bearing.

The strongest positive statement is:

$$
\text{old-past preservation does not forbid new common-future incidence.}
$$

It is not yet:

$$
\text{an interacting bridge record can be born consistently.}
$$

### M2 — Temporal prefix consistency is not all-subset/projective locality

B2 proves consistency only for the inverse sequence

$$
\Omega_{n+1}\xrightarrow{\text{delete top label}}\Omega_n.
$$

This is **end-deletion cylinder consistency**. It is sufficient to construct a
measure on the labeled sequential-prefix inverse limit.

It is not any of the following:

- consistency under restriction to every retained record subset;
- D1's marked-history restriction functor;
- D2's intersection/shadow restriction;
- D2's contained-event restriction;
- subsystem marginal consistency;
- causal locality or Bell causality;
- or covtree/stem-observable refinement consistency.

This distinction is load-bearing. Restricting a common-future extension to a
subset can erase a parent, turn a bridge shadow into a nonbridge, or leave a
lower-arity shadow depending on the support ontology. D1 already proves that
eligible-support formation can fail naturality under genuine record
restriction.

Paper 4 often says simply “projectivity,” including Theorem 4.1's headline
that “covariance/projectivity do not select bridge weight.” The receipt has
not shown that either witness law passes arbitrary-subset record projectivity.

**Required revision/opening:**

1. replace unqualified `projective/projectivity` for B2 by
   `end-deletion prefix consistency`;
2. define both arbitrary-subset restrictions of `C[D]` that correspond to the
   D2 intersection and contained ontologies;
3. test whether the candidate-extension and bridge-shadow classifications
   commute with those restrictions;
4. if they fail, state the failure rather than importing prefix consistency as
   subsystem locality.

Until this is done, the nonselection result is a theorem on the temporal
prefix tower, not on every finite restriction of a record history.

**Required opening:** for both kernels:

1. push every naturally labeled state to its canonical unlabeled order at
   each audited level;
2. sum all fiber masses exactly;
3. exhibit at least one unequal canonical event probability;
4. distinguish this physical pushforward from unequal raw natural-label path
   weights;
5. state what, if any, consistency map exists between the unlabeled finite

Without this, B4 proves inequivalence of two labeled sequential measures, not
yet inequivalence of the claimed physical quotient laws.

### M4 — A covtree/Stone measure is not automatically a full-history law

The distinction between the labeled prefix tower and covtree refinement is a
major strength of D3. The exact rank-two witness correctly proves that one
covtree node can contain multiple nonisomorphic stems and therefore is not one
two-click prefix.

The paper then overreaches in the opposite direction when it says a covtree
measure may serve as a primitive **full-history law** or defines a measure on
“completed histories.” V9 Paper 7 identifies the Stone spectrum `X` with the
space of stem-observable theories. The evaluation map from completed causets
to `X` has rogue fibers: distinct histories can have identical stem data.

A Radon measure on `X` is therefore a law on the covariant stem-observable
quotient. It does not by itself provide:

- a unique probability measure on completed causets;
- a lift through each rogue fiber;
- a marked history;
- or conditional probabilities for the next sealed record.

Surjectivity of the evaluation map eliminates ghost spectrum points; it does
not make the lift unique.

**Required revision:** call a covtree law a primitive
`stem-observable/spectrum measure`. To obtain a full-history law, require an
explicit lift through the evaluation map and state its nonuniqueness on rogue
fibers. To obtain a next-record law, additionally require a committed marked
filtration or extension kernel. Do not call covtree rank a click count.

### M5 — D2 composes marked support skeletons, not completed diamonds

D3's abstract says “Diamonds compose the resulting marked interfaces
downstream,” and Section 7 says Paper 3's pushout “can compose compatible
diamonds.” The final hostile result of D2 is narrower:

> supplied typed spans have canonical pushouts in a finite marked
> support-skeleton category.

D2 explicitly excludes probability, screen, collar, source, transport, and
holonomy gluing. Therefore D3 may hand a marked support skeleton downstream to
D2, but it cannot claim that D2 composes full sealed diamonds.

**Required revision:** replace those sentences by:

> once a typed marked support skeleton is supplied, D2 composes its incidence;
> a full sealed-diamond gluing theorem remains open.

The same caution applies to D1: its center receipts model finite boundary
logic; they do not manufacture a sealed holonomy event.

### M6 — The global component-weight witness is deliberately a universe-ledger law

The witness kernel must know:

- the entire finite prefix `H_n`;
- every legal down-set;
- the comparability-component decomposition of the whole prefix;
- and one globally normalized choice of the next maximal event.

It is therefore not a local interacting click law. It is a global sequential
growth law on a presentation tower. The paper acknowledges this unusually
clearly and says the witness is used only for nonselection.

That disclosure must remain in the theorem boundary. The witness proves:

> broad full-prefix covariance and end-deletion consistency do not select a
> numerical kernel.

It does not prove:

> two record-readable local bridge laws survive all locality gates.

A global measure need not require a literal centralized simulator, but D3 has
not supplied a local specification, commuting local update family, bounded
record neighborhood, or decentralized realization. “A global measure might
admit one” is a live possibility, not a result.

**Required revision:** state the theorem as a global-prefix nonselection
witness and explicitly exclude locality. The next campaign must either derive
a record-readable finite-boundary statistic or classify nonlocal bridge-sector
laws honestly.

## Barandes and full-history conditioning

This section is mostly well scoped and should survive with one clarification.

D3 correctly distinguishes:

- **indivisibility:** a supplied interval stochastic law need not factor into
  coarse intermediate propagators;
- **non-Markov conditioning:** a conditional may depend on the entire
  committed path rather than one present state;
- **disintegration:** conditional one-extension probabilities can be extracted
  from an already supplied path measure;
- **derivation:** none of the preceding facts chooses that measure.

Thus D3 does not infer a law from Barandes's correspondence and does not call
full-history conditioning Markov divisibility.

The remaining clarification is that Barandes indivisibility does not itself
supply D3's committed-history filtration, candidate extension domain, or
regular conditional version. The primitive-measure program must posit those
objects as part of its rulebook. Conditional kernels are determined only on
positive-mass prefixes; the paper's zero-mass caveat is correct.

## Covtree dynamics: neither understated nor a click law

After M4's wording repair, the covtree section is sound.

- Covtree transition probabilities are genuine dynamics on covariant
  stem-observable resolution; D3 should not call them “mere topology.”
- The topology alone supplies no numerical probabilities; a random-walk law
  is extra input.
- A covtree step refines a completed history's observable stem theory and is
  not automatically a one-record birth.
- A physical identification between observable rank and click number would be
  an additional postulate, not a consequence of the inverse limit.

This is the correct relationship to v9 Paper 7.

## Profinite and marked scope

The labeled prefix inverse limit theorem is valid:

- every level is finite;
- end-deletion is the specified bonding map;
- compatible finite probabilities extend uniquely to a Borel probability on
  the compact metrizable inverse limit;
- the measure is Radon;
- positive-cylinder ratios recover the prefix kernel.

The paper also correctly refuses a marked profinite theorem for unrestricted
real/rational marks. Finite observable partitions or an explicit compact
zero-dimensional mark topology would be required.

The phrase “profinite consistency” must nevertheless identify **which** tower
is meant. D3 proves it for the labeled end-deletion tower, not automatically
for covtree, marked histories, or arbitrary record restrictions.

## Final recommendation

D3 contains an important correction to D2: immutable old records do not
forbid a newly born event from having a multi-component past. That result is
solid and worth keeping.

The paper should be revised around the phrase **common-future bridge shadow**,
not “interacting bridge birth.” Its measure theorem should be labeled temporal
prefix consistency, not general record projectivity. Its covtree discussion
must stop at the stem-observable quotient unless a lift is supplied. Its two
witness laws must be pushed through the physical construction quotient before
they certify physical nonselection.

With those revisions, Paper 4 can become a strong scoped result: variable
history evades the fixed-vertex incidence no-go, but neither profinite arena,
full-history access, nor past immutability selects the new-event law.
