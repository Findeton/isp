My overall view: D31 is genuine progress; D32A is useful calibration, but it weakens the "single fractional dimension" story; D32B should not yet be treated as a search for the physical click law.

D31: the strongest part

The graph-blind no-go is clean and important:
If the rule only knows how many unfinished records exist, while birth remains possible forever and construction order is treated as gauge, then interactions are forced to zero.
That means a universe-producing rule must inspect relational structure: connections, degrees, motifs, age, components or something richer.
The important qualification is that this is conditional on construction-order covariance being a physical requirement. The mathematics does not prove that nature must quotient different accretion orders. If the order in which operations occur is itself recorded physical history, the constraint may not apply. D31 acknowledges this, but it is the deepest remaining fork.

D31B also closes an important confusion correctly:
The wire DAG is the structural causal order.
The port-dependency construction independently produces the same order.
Operational influence is a different, alphabet-dependent graph that can lose edges through cancellation.
So D30 measured a legitimate structural order—not actual influence and not yet a spacetime metric.

D31C is less complete than its title suggests. It excludes several simple grading schemes, but the most promising classes—degree-sensitive, motif-sensitive and distance-degree rules—remain open. The promised systematic exact search was not completed for that family. That is precisely where a physically admissible interacting rule might still live.

D32A: good instruments, but an important conceptual change

The repaired spatial estimator now behaves convincingly on the test geometries:
d=3: correlation about 0.814
d=4: correlation about 0.787
The conventions are now sensible: canonical Spearman ranking, invariant spacelike distance, larger samples, and explicit disclosure of the older readings.
But it currently validates distance ordering, not a calibrated spatial metric. It can say "this pair is generally farther apart than that pair"; it does not yet recover an absolute distance or prove the correct scaling law. So "the spatial vacancy is filled" should mean:
An order-only spatial ranking instrument now exists.
It should not yet mean that SHARD has reconstructed physical spatial distance.

There is also a statistical weakness in the repaired certification: the 120 pairs come from only three independent sprinkled universes. The bootstrap resamples individual pairs as though all 120 were independent. They are clustered within three shared causal sets. The correlations look genuinely strong—the three individual readings are all healthy—but P(< bar) = 0.000 is too confident. A referee-grade validation should use many independent sprinklings and bootstrap by sprinkling, not merely by pair.

The 1.76 finding has changed meaning

The anchor reproduces exactly: d_MM = 1.756 ± 0.101. But the midpoint estimator gives d_mid = 1.630 ± 0.127. Their difference is 0.126, and the review finds that difference is 2.66 standard errors from zero. The known small-interval bias explains only about 0.017 of it.
Therefore these are not really two agreeing measurements of one fractional dimension. They pass the pre-registered tolerance, but statistically they disagree.
The better interpretation is:
The collar order has a robust non-manifold signature: MM sees approximately 1.76, midpoint scaling sees approximately 1.63, and neither is compatible with M².
That is arguably more interesting than "a 1.76-dimensional object." It suggests the web may not possess one dimension that every manifold estimator must recover. Different scale-sensitive questions see different effective exponents.
Consequently I would retire "self-consistent fractional dimension" and carry the ordered pair (d_MM, d_mid) ≈ (1.76, 1.63) as part of the object's signature.

The central problem with D32B

The proposed grid is called an "admissible dimension map," but the note explicitly says every grid kernel is stationary and path-covariance violating.
That is acceptable if D32B asks: How do radius and interaction density affect the geometry of these toy growth models?
It is not sufficient if D32B asks: Which of these is nature's click law?
D31 has just shown that covariance is highly restrictive, while D32B proposes spending hundreds of runs on rules already known to violate it. Recording the violation in a column is honest, but it does not make those rules candidate physical laws.
I would choose between two clearly named routes:
Phenomenological D32B: run the grid explicitly as a geometry-mechanism experiment. Learn what raises or lowers the effective order signatures, without presenting any cell as the final law.
Physical-law route: first finish D31C's degree/distance/motif-sensitive exact search and find a stationary covariant interacting family. Then map the dimensions of that surviving family.
For the program's stated goal—finding the real interactive click law—I would prioritize the second.
