| Cut system | Signed affine dimension through size 3 | Positive control |

The paper defines its interval family rather than silently treating

Most importantly, the paper does **not** counterfeit collar-complete or typed
direct-carrier restrictions as vertex subsets. It says an unmarked poset has
no screen, collar, projected provenance, or carrier type and leaves those cuts
for a marked-diamond construction. That is the correct boundary.

### 2. No-silent trichotomy — pass

The revised paper cleanly separates:

1. **autonomous** cuts, with a proved kernel on the retained state;
2. **boundary-conditional** cuts, with an explicit retained carrier and
   decoder/update law;
3. **refused** cuts, which are not complete physical laboratories because no
   permitted sufficient retained state has been supplied.

The all-subset theorem is confined to the first, unmarked sector. The
completion quotient only diagnoses the second. Collar/direct-carrier cuts
remain refused until typed marks and gluing maps exist. This correctly
implements D1's no-silent fork instead of assuming every subset must be an
autonomous universe.

### 4. Capacity premise and loopholes — pass

The fixed-token theorem now states its additional operational assumptions:

- one pre-sampling token;
- deterministic context encoding;
- one uniform finite alphabet at all depths;
- fixed-law and retained-structure side information only;
- no depth/global-clock side channel;
- one token rather than an expanding boundary;
- exact zero-error prediction.

The paper explicitly says this premise is **not** derived from finite
evidence/KL weight, Shannon entropy, or the v6 binary-event capacity. Thus it
does not infer a finite alphabet from “finite evidence.”

The receipt and paper formalize four ways to escape the theorem:

1. a binary stochastic encoder with depth-dependent mixing `q_n`;
2. six distributed bits for depths 1–64, growing thereafter;
3. the unbounded integer mark `n`, finite at each finite depth;
4. a one-percent approximate tail bin beyond depth 99.

They correctly say these controls relocate global dependence, grow total
capacity, remove a uniform alphabet, or relax exactness. None is promoted to
a local compositional law. The stochastic control is best understood as a
global sampler rather than an observed predictive boundary state; the paper's
explicit refusal to count it as a local encoder prevents an ontology error.
