## 2. No-time and operational-diamond core — pass at the D35 scope

Sections 3 and 4 correctly distinguish three notions:

```text
A-wire succession                 physical causal order;
mailbox/linear-extension order    construction gauge;
elapsed duration or rate          absent from D35.
```

Within the supplied grammar, A1 is fixed lower boundary data, A is instructed
to initiate, and the terminating nested call creates the first new A-wire
event A2. The actor-owned integer is used as a causal successor identity and
does not supply seconds, a rate, simultaneity or proper-time length. Thus D35
is a valid existential demonstration that one conditional next-A law can be
defined without a numerical clock.

Section 4.1 also states the geometric ceiling correctly. Its call diamond is
the finite operational region consisting of A1, the selected adjacent
query/return records and A2. It is not a Lorentzian interval, a light cone or
a metric ball. Section 12 maintains that distinction and makes no cone,
dimension or unit inference.

The D intervention likewise supports the paper's narrow operational statement:
the bit changes the durable A2 output on the six queried call trees and on no
unqueried tree, with exact reach mass `1/16` in Q1 and `3/40` in Q2. This is a
result about one bounded classical datum under one supplied transfer grammar,
not a theorem that ancestry, correlation or entanglement is generally a
signal.

## 3. MAJOR M1 — the completed call diamond is not a proved predictive boundary

Section 9.3 says that D35 “supplies a selected finite call diamond,” that “the
predictive boundary is therefore the completed call boundary,” and that this
validates the proposed below-A-click causal boundary. The first clause is
correct; the inference is not.

D35 distinguishes at least three objects:

```text
conditioning data    the complete typed current Network state plus Q and the
                     instruction that A initiates;
sampled region        the finite query/return call diamond;
observable output     A2 and its durable fields.
```

The completed call diamond, including A2, is produced after sampling. It is
therefore not the pre-A2 information from which its own probability is
computed. D35's Markov statement in section 9.2 uses the complete simulator
state. The law can inspect the owned-port structure of actors reached during
the recursive call; no theorem in D35 identifies a smaller boundary carrier,
proves sufficiency of a completed-call boundary presentation, or proves
minimality by predictive equivalence.

Paper 23 proved a genuinely different statement: under D34b and its complete
unlimited-horizon Branch F, the current rooted marked component gauge class is
the minimal exact predictive quotient. D35 uses a different law and asks for
one A-conditioned next-record outcome. That is enough to remove any
contradiction, but it does not establish a smaller predictive quotient. The
phrase “adding a return-limiting causal protocol” also risks sounding as if
D35 repaired D34b; D35 replaces the D34b dynamics and query with a different
supplied grammar.

**Required repair:** replace the predictive-boundary conclusion in section
9.3 with the narrower result:

> D35 realizes a finite **acquisition/stopping region** for one sampled A2.
> Only records on the selected issued-call tree can enter that A2. The
> pre-click predictive state in the implemented law remains the complete typed
> D35 state; its minimal exact quotient was not characterized.

State that Paper 23 and D35 differ in both law and observable, so no comparison
of minimal predictive-boundary sizes has been proved. “Return-limiting” may be
used only as a description of the D35 call grammar, not as a derived physical
limiter added to D34b. The abstract and conclusion should call the below-A
object an operational acquisition region unless a separate predictive-
sufficiency/minimality theorem is supplied.

## 4. MAJOR M2 — the overlap specification is a candidate, not yet a precise object

The abstract calls the missing object precise and says the laminar D35 model
is its first solved special case. Section 11.3 proposes

```text
gamma_D(interior marked history | boundary b)
```

and asks for restriction, overlap and completion. This is a promising research
direction, but the displayed object and seven prose conditions are not yet a
mathematical local specification.

### 4.1 The conditioning boundary is unoriented

For a generative causal law, incoming/past boundary records are conditioning
data, while upper seals and outgoing records are generated outputs. A kernel
conditioned on an undifferentiated “boundary b” may condition on A2 or another
future upper seal. That would define a bridge/smoothing distribution, not the
next-click rule whose absence motivates the paper. The region must specify its
incoming, outgoing and lateral/collar interfaces and which marks are inputs.

### 4.2 The stated restriction equation is not generally correct

If `D` is inside `E`, the boundary of D normally contains variables sampled in
the interior of E. Forgetting a sample from `gamma_E` down to D therefore
produces a mixture over the induced D-boundary values. It is not, without that
disintegration, equal to `gamma_D` at one fixed boundary b.

For a DLR-style specification the consistency statement is a kernel-
composition identity with boundary transport, conventionally of the form

```text
gamma_E gamma_D = gamma_E       for D inside E,
```

with the domains and conditioning variables made explicit. For a causal
instrument it may instead be a directed composition/comb identity. “Sample a
larger region and forget” is insufficient until the induced smaller boundary
and its conditional law are defined.

### 4.3 Pairwise overlap agreement need not give a global law

Equality of two marginals on a pairwise intersection is necessary but, in a
general marginal/contextuality problem, not sufficient for a joint extension
over three or more overlapping regions. The proposal needs an indexed region
category or hypergraph, compatible joint-extension/sheaf condition on every
finite cover, and a positivity/normalization condition on the common algebra.
For variable support it must also say how actors born in one region are
identified or excluded on another region's boundary.

### 4.4 The completion domain is not yet identified

“All finite causal diamonds” is not automatically a directed or cofinal family
of past-closed stems. The paper has not defined whether regions are order-
convex intervals, finite down-sets, operational call closures or something
else, nor the measurable global history space on which an extension should
live. Existence, uniqueness and extremal mixtures are separate questions.

**Required repair:** change “the missing object is now precise” to “a candidate
architecture is now identified.” Make the next investigation begin by defining:

1. the category of finite causal regions/stems and admissible variable-support
   embeddings;
2. incoming conditioning interfaces versus generated upper/outgoing records;
3. restriction/boundary-transport maps and the exact kernel-composition law;
4. joint extension on arbitrary finite overlapping covers, not only pairwise
   marginal equality; and
5. the global measurable history space plus existence and uniqueness criteria.

Only after those definitions should D35 be embedded and proved to satisfy the
laminar subcase. Until then “first motivating laminar example” is supported;
“first solved special case of the specification” is not.

## 5. MINOR m1 — the headline identifies an event with its region and weakens the root qualifier

The manuscript itself defines A2 as the **upper seal** of a call diamond. A2 is
one event; the diamond also contains A1 and its query/return interior. Thus the
title “The next click is a causal diamond” and the ordinary-language phrase
“diamonds that could become A's next update” are literal category errors.

The subtitle's “executable rooted universe” also exceeds the terminal noun,
and section 13's unqualified “proper time is not logically required to define
the probabilities of A's next record” omits the condition that A is externally
chosen to initiate under the supplied rooted grammar.

**Required repair:** use, for example,

```text
A's next click is the upper seal of a causal call diamond, not the winner of a clock race
A time-free rooted family, an executable logical-actor sampler and the missing overlap principle
```

Qualify the abstract and terminal statement with “in the supplied A-rooted
grammar.” Replace diamonds “becoming” an update with histories or call diamonds
**whose upper seal is** the update.

## 6. MINOR m2 — generic completed histories need an A2-existence convention

Section 3.1 allows infinite or terminal completed histories, while sections
3.2--3.4 define A2 and the reach event as though every extension contains an
immediate next A-wire successor. Terminal histories, and a future root-free law
in which A never updates again, need not contain A2.

This does not affect the implemented D35 measure: the external iteration starts
another terminating A call, so the next A successor exists with probability
one. It does affect the paper's general formula for an arbitrary completed-
history law `mu`.

**Required repair:** define the reach conditional on the event that A2 exists,
or add a cemetery outcome `A2 = no-successor` and include it in the normalized
next-A kernel. State explicitly that D35 assigns the cemetery outcome zero.

## 7. MINOR m3 — the call-diamond product formula must use the effective actor menu

Section 4.4 writes a fixed

```text
q = (q_idle,q_birth,q_visit,q_fork)
```

and then multiplies `q(action at v)` divided by the “number of selected port
sets.” Literally this is wrong at leaves and degree-one actors. The executable
folds unavailable visit/fork mass into idle. For example, at a leaf the idle
factor is `q_idle + q_visit + q_fork`, not `q_idle`.

**Required repair:** define the actor-state-dependent effective option law
`q_v^eff(o | H) = local_options(v,H)` and write

```text
P(D | H,A1) = product_v q_v^eff(the exact action-and-port option realized at v).
```

Equivalently, spell out `q_visit/deg(v)` and
`q_fork/binomial(deg(v),2)` when available, with unavailable mass added to
idle. Replace “number of selected port sets” with “number of eligible local
port sets.”

## 8. MINOR m4 — actor locality is stated too broadly at one point

Sections 1 and 8.3 correctly say that the code runs in one Python process and
uses one shared exact carrier vector and event-DAG collector. The local
stochastic menu and licensed ports are actor-indexed. However, section 8.3
then says the scientific test is that “local transitions consult only their
declared actor/collar data.” The transition evaluator also reads and mutates
the shared joint carrier and ancestry representation; birth checks a parent
marginal and total norm in that joint object.

This is not a nonlocal opportunity normalizer and does not invalidate the
accepted logical actor protocol. It does make the quoted sentence stronger
than the implementation.

**Required repair:** say that **menu selection, capability admissibility and
the factors acted upon** are determined by declared actor/collar data, while
the resulting local maps are evaluated on a shared joint carrier and recorded
in a shared event DAG. Replace “one exact local actor realization” in section
13 with “one exact logical-actor realization with a shared joint evaluator.”

## 9. MINOR m5 — `output_sources` is a positive-source set, not complete route provenance

Section 7.1 calls the returned field “the set of source identities,” and
section 7.2 says the D source identity follows the bit rule. In the executable,
`output_sources` contains D when D's carried bit is one and is empty when it is
zero. A queried zero-valued D is instead evidenced by the capability route and
event provenance. The round-3 quantum review made this distinction explicit.

The operational intervention result remains valid, but the current wording
can be read as though the source set alone records every queried source.

**Required repair:** call the field the “positive-source identity set.” Add
that the authenticated structural route records a queried zero-valued source,
so absence from the positive-source set is not interpreted as absence of the
query.

## 10. MINOR m6 — the global-next-event paragraph presents a false dichotomy

Section 11.1 says that selecting exactly one next actor requires either a
ready-set normalization or a race of clocks. Those are common constructions,
but they are not exhaustive. A global priority field, a supplied discrete
ordering, a deterministic selector or another global random mechanism can
also choose one actor. Such devices still add global selection structure, but
they need not be expressed as the two mechanisms named in the paper.

**Required repair:** replace the dichotomy with the invariant point:

> Requiring exactly one globally next actor introduces an additional global
> selection law. Ready-set normalization and clock races are two examples.
> D35 instead assigns a law to an A-conditioned partial-order region and makes
> the machine linear extension gauge.

This retains the motivation without asserting an exhaustive implementation
theorem.
