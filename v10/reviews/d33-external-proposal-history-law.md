After rereading the repaired Paper 20, I would change the priority order for the theory. Paper 20 ranks the fixed-budget aligned-direction experiment first. That is the best next mechanism experiment, but not the most pressing theoretical accomplishment.

The most pressing objective: **Construct—or prove impossible—the first unbounded, local, projectively consistent history law with persistent births and interactions.** In other words, stop treating the next-opportunity kernel K as the primitive object. Define the probability law of complete record histories first, then derive K by conditioning it. That is exactly the lesson of "the rulebook is the history law."

Paper 20 has shown that the present sequential approach is trapped: stationary interacting kernels violate construction-order covariance; covariant interacting kernels require a finite size cap; K_flat is covariant but knows the final horizon; static interactions compress the web toward a chain; suppressing interactions returns it toward a branching tree; attaching directions does not cure this unless interaction placement and the underlying geometry change. This suggests that the problem is not another missing value of r, λ, g, or κ. It is the mathematical architecture of the growth law.

What the history should be: a typed causal partial order — events are clicks; record wires connect clicks locally; seals and channel information mark the events; order along a shared record is physical; two causally unrelated clicks have no physical global ordering. A simulator may process those unrelated clicks one after another, but that total sequence is bookkeeping. Different linearizations of the same causal history should represent the same universe. This gives a likely resolution of O7 that is subtler than "all construction order is gauge" versus "all construction order is physical": **causal order is recorded physics; arbitrary ordering between incomparable events is gauge.** That is the version compatible with proper time and relativity. The flags record local clicks, not a universal master-clock sequence.

The mathematical target: compatible laws μ_n on finite marked histories with (restriction)_*μ_{n+1} = μ_n; the finite laws define one measure μ on complete histories — the appropriate use of the profinite history space. The effective click rule K_μ(o|H) = μ(H+o|H) is no longer independently guessed; it may depend on the full causal past (Barandes-style non-Markovianism) while remaining local in the sense that remote unrelated records cannot alter a local conditional.

Success conditions: (1) relabelling and linear-extension invariance; (2) consistency under restriction; (3) positive birth and interaction densities as the history grows without bound; (4) no final-horizon parameter or encoded universe-size cap; (5) no silent erasure and preservation of sealed records; (6) local dependence on causal ancestry, not a census of the whole current universe; (7) a proper quantum version — decoherence functional if an ordinary probability measure is insufficient. If no such law exists, that is also decisive: at least one of stationarity, covariance, the existing birth/interact grammar, unbounded growth or persistent interaction must be abandoned.

Constructive idea: autonomous local record clocks rather than one global opportunity lottery. Each unsealed record carries a local activity budget determined by its own evidence and causal past. A local click decides among: creating a child; interacting with a locally available record; remaining idle. The birth-versus-interaction hazard is local. Directional or channel information redistributes a fixed interaction budget among possible partners. Adding a remote record does not dilute every existing interaction probability merely by enlarging a universe-wide denominator. Successful interactions must also update the relational substrate — closing diamonds or modifying future adjacency — not merely add another operation on an otherwise frozen birth tree. Paper 20 strongly suggests that metric-inert interactions are the wrong family. A candidate finite-history weight might schematically contain local contributions from record birth, diamond completion, sealed holonomy consistency, channel compatibility, and penalties for chain saturation and uncontrolled branching. But Paper 16's nonselection theorem must be respected: a local action alone does not uniquely determine the history measure — boundary state, measure/contour and any selection principle must be included explicitly rather than smuggled into normalization.

Where "many clocks, few factors" enters: only after such a history law exists should the Paper 11 bridge become the main test. The profinite space is the branching carrier of complete histories; S² ≅ CP¹ would be a local channel-state fibre attached to records; the history law determines how those local fibres affect birth, interaction and causal structure. Then: do many clock readings compress to four latent factors; do those factors generate the structural cone; does operational influence approach the same cone; do d_MM, d_mid and scaling measures converge toward 4; does it survive larger webs without importing a sphere or coordinates.

Priority: (1) history-law/O7 theorem; (2) existence or no-go for an unbounded interacting projective measure; (3) dynamic relational kernel; (4) clocks-to-factors bridge. The fixed-budget aligned-direction experiment remains valuable as a controlled diagnostic. The theory's real bottleneck is now the absence of a lawful infinite interacting history measure from which the click kernel follows.
