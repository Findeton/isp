I've read Paper 20. It is the clearest synthesis of D28–D32 so far, and its main conclusion is stronger—and more negative—than the earlier "find the right dimension dial" framing.

Its actual message is:

1. **Simple covariant growth laws cannot both grow forever and interact.** Count-only, degree, distance-degree, motif and component-size versions all fail within their stated scopes. Either the law needs substantially richer structure, or construction order is physical history rather than gauge.

2. **The grown web does not have one real dimension.** Its honest fingerprint is (d_MM, d_mid), not one d. The collar anchor gives approximately (1.76, 1.63), and fresh runs show substantial variation. This confirms our previous conclusion: these are effective exponents, not yet "many clocks, four factors."

3. **Ordinary interaction compresses the order.** Increasing interaction range or strength makes more events causally comparable. The web approaches a chain, whose effective dimension is near one. It does not become a higher-dimensional spacetime.

4. **Pure birth produces the opposite pathology.** MM can report approximately five, but midpoint scaling remains near one. That is a branching-tree signature—not a five-dimensional universe.

5. **The first S² test did not lift dimension.** It initially appeared to do so, but only because anti-aligned weighting suppressed almost all interactions. Starving interactions returns the web toward the high-MM birth-tree regime. Once interaction frequency is matched, the directional effect is actually slightly compressive.

That is excellent scientific correction: it explains why the apparent lift was false rather than merely reporting that it disappeared.

My main reservations are about presentation and what the S² experiment actually tested:

- "Compression law" is acceptable because it is labelled measured and scoped, but it is not a universal law. It is an empirical regularity of static birth-tree-metric collar kernels.
- "The S² test: a designed negative" is slightly misleading. It was designed as a positive mechanism test whose apparent success was later diagnosed as confounded.
- The test did **not** yet test the full Paper 11 bridge. It attached icosahedral direction labels and a compatibility weight to local opportunities. It did not show that arbitrarily many clock readings compress into four latent factors, nor that those four factors generate the structural and influence cones.
- The planned aligned variant must hold the total interaction rate fixed by construction. Otherwise it risks repeating the starvation confound with the opposite sign. Ideally, directional weights should redistribute a fixed interaction budget among candidates, with shuffled-direction and direction-blind controls.
- The opening abstract says "count-blind"; the established class is more precisely **graph-blind or u-only**.

The central discovery of Paper 20 is therefore:

> Within the present static growth grammar, neither more interaction nor the first directional mechanism generates spacetime. Interaction makes the order too chain-like; removing interaction makes it too tree-like. The missing mechanism must reorganize interaction placement while preserving a healthy interaction rate—or change what interactions do to the underlying relational geometry.

Paper 20 is still marked **DRAFT (pre-round)**. Its underlying D31/D32 results are heavily reviewed, but the synthesis itself has not yet had its paper-level hostile review. Conceptually, though, I think its claim boundary is mostly excellent.
