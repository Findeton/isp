## 1. The answer in ordinary language

There are three different questions hiding inside “what is the law?”

```text
architecture   can local regions exchange compatible boundary records?
admissibility  do action products and root-free transports agree?
identification can the declared observations distinguish the couplings?
```

Papers 26 and 27 answered complementary parts of the first question.  Paper
26 showed how regional conditional laws can agree when the opportunity complex
and couplings are supplied.  Paper 27 showed how one chosen generator can
write and authenticate the records used by its own next finite cylinder.  The
two constructions did not yet inhabit one proved interface, and neither chose
the rates.

The shared-interface construction in this paper is the smallest honest bridge.
It says that both record restrictions and supplied opportunity regions can be
represented by the same four boundary roles:

```text
internal    durable rows inside the region;
incoming    typed causal data entering the region;
lateral     exterior peers exposed by restriction;
generated   rows written by the regional law.
```

That common grammar is useful, but it is not an equivalence.  In particular,
chosen D34b still does not generate contending proposals against one base
version.  Without conflict, an “induced arbitration kernel” contains no
information.

The selection result is also mixed by design.  Action compatibility has
solutions, so it is not an impossibility theorem.  Chosen projected D38b is
not one of those solutions on the registered star-boundary complex, so the
criterion is not decorative.  Finite root balance has a unique conditional
answer—uniform rooting—but leaves the distribution of unrooted networks free.
Typed untimed records distinguish the finite ratio grid, but a common clock
scale is absent.

So the best available answer is not one formula.  It is a set of filters and a
map of their residual freedoms.

## 4. Local admission as typed causal certification

### 4.1 Why H0 fails

For event e, H0 signs each touched wire's current head, row and event-body
digest.  It correctly admits a fresh one-wire event.  But after A performs a
birth, the old signed A-head claim remains authentic while no longer current.
The complete oracle rejects the old event.  Signatures establish who issued a
statement; they do not make the statement permanently current.

### 4.2 H1 record grammar

H1 binds the attempt to the typed event carrier:

```text
attempt = H(event-id, event-body, touched-wire roles).
```

For every touched existing wire w, the protocol writes

```text
HEAD_PREPARE(attempt,w,expected-head,event-body)
HEAD_GRANT(owner(w),attempt,w,expected-head,row,event-body).
```

The grant parents both the prepare and the certified head.  An interaction
also carries an edge credential parenting the authenticated incidence source.
A commit or abort decision parents the complete grant set and edge credential.
Each owner then writes APPLY or RELEASE, followed by an acknowledgement.

The validator checks unique record identities, ideal signatures, causal parent
closure, acyclicity, exact parent-kind schemas, common attempt/body/event
payloads, present head and row equality, incident-edge source equality and
one-use consumption.

## 5. Selection conditions are filters

### 5.1 Action/history compatibility

Let z be a local boundary state and e an admitted action.  A positive increment
weight w obeys the commuting-square cocycle when

```text
w(e1|z) w(e2|e1 z) = w(e2|z) w(e1|e2 z)              (1)
```

whenever the two paths are admitted and reach the same declared target
boundary.  In logarithmic coordinates this is a homogeneous linear equation.
Normalization is a separate condition; an unnormalized solution must not be
called a stochastic law.

### 5.2 Root-free mass transport

For a finite marked network G rooted at o, the mass-transport principle is

```text
E sum_v F(G,o,v) = E sum_v F(G,v,o)                    (3)
```

for every nonnegative covariant transport F.  Covariance means that F depends
on a doubly rooted isomorphism class, not arbitrary vertex labels.

### Theorem 4 — finite conditional root law

Conditional on one finite unrooted marked network G, every covariant root law
satisfying (3) is uniform over vertices.  Equivalently, if O_i are the vertex
orbits of Aut(G), then

```text
P(root in O_i | G) = |O_i|/|V(G)|.                    (4)
```

### Proof

For two vertex orbits O_i and O_j, use the covariant transport that sends one
unit from every vertex in O_i to every vertex in O_j.  Its expected outgoing
mass is `p_i |O_j|`; its expected incoming mass is `p_j |O_i|`.  Equation (3)
gives

```text
p_i/|O_i| = p_j/|O_j|.
```

The equality for every pair of orbits and normalization imply (4).  ∎

The D39b regression exhausts 18 doubly rooted orbits on three marked cells.
The reduced transport matrix has rank `number of root orbits minus one` in all
three.  Uniform rooting passes, degree bias fails, and noncovariant label
vectors are excluded before mass transport is tested.

### 5.3 What remains unselected

Theorem 4 fixes the conditional root inside each finite unrooted network.  It
does not fix mixture weights among different networks.  Theorem 3 classifies
an unnormalized finite increment variety, its one-dimensional algebraic
quotient and a normalized kernel on a separate initial depth-three registry.
It does not normalize the full action complex or construct a stationary
infinite kernel.  No theorem proves that one joint law satisfies record
closure, action compatibility, infinite root-free balance and D34b's selected
coefficients.
