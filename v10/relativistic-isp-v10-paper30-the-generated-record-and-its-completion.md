### 1.2 The grammar [POSITED]

Carriers are wires: participants and version objects.  The event poset is
the carrier-wise wire closure; causal order is physical, incomparable order
is gauge.  All weights are exact rational numbers (Python `Fraction`s in
the receipts).  The event alphabet is typed, with the initiator in the
type — paper 28's lesson that type data is load-bearing:

- `('g', v0)` — genesis: version `v0` held by all participants.  This is
  the declared supplied boundary.
- `('p', a, b, x)` — participant `a` proposes payload `x` against base
  version `b`.  Carriers `{a}` alone: a proposal is a local record event
  on the proposer's wire referencing a held copy of `b`.  Version wires
  are touched only by arbitration events; a shared version carrier would
  chain same-base proposals into comparability and make conflict
  grammatically impossible.
- `('r', a, C, w)` — arbitration: initiator `a`, a member of conflict
  component `C`'s proposer set, resolves `C` selecting a feasible winner
  set `w`, a maximal independent set of `C`'s conflict graph.  The
  arbitration event *is* acceptance: it creates the successor version
  `v' = (base, value(w), authors(w), initiator)`.  Carriers: `C`'s
  proposers plus `v'` only; the base enters as event data, never as a
  carrier wire.
- `('d', s, r, v)` — delivery: sender `s` transmits version `v` to
  receiver `r`.  Carriers `{s, r}`: a delivery is a join of knowledge,
  the one two-wire event class beside arbitrations.  Re-delivery is
  admissible and physical (holdings are idempotent; the join of wires is
  the point).  After delivery, `r` holds `v` and `r`'s past contains
  `s`'s chain up to the delivery — supersessions travel with the join,
  not as payload.
- `('m', a, pkey, w)` — merge: `a` locally reconciles two versions it
  holds.  Carriers `{a, v_m}`: merge is a single-participant event — the
  holder of both reconciles, exactly as in distributed version control.
  The merged version supersedes both members of its pair; the
  supersession is pair-scoped, so unmerged third forks remain mergeable
  with `v_m` and reconciliation recurses.
- `('n', a)` — recorded idle, the budget absorber.

**Holdings** propagate only through participation or delivery: `a` holds
the genesis version, every version created by an arbitration or merge `a`
participated in, and every version delivered to `a`.

**Admission** is past-relative throughout — paper 28's H1 causal
certificate, now the grammar's working law.  A proposal `('p', a, b, x)`
is admissible at a record point iff the event creating `b` is in its past,
no supersession of `b` is in its past, and no *unresolved* prior proposal
by `a` on `b` is in its past (a prior resolved by an arbitration in the
past does not block; supersession then blocks re-proposal on the old base
on its own).  An arbitration is admissible iff all proposals of `C` are in
its past, `C` is maximal there, the base is unsuperseded there, and no
prior arbitration on that base is in its past.  A singleton live proposal
is a (trivial) component, so its proposer may self-arbitrate: this is the
uncontested-acceptance path, without which no version is ever created
conflict-free.  A merge is admissible iff both versions are held, both
were created on the same base, their creating events are incomparable in
the merger's past, and neither is superseded there.

**Conflict [POSITED]:** two proposals conflict iff they share a base,
their payloads differ, and the two events are incomparable — neither in
the other's past.  Same-payload proposals are compatible (co-authorship).
Conflict is thus born from generated concurrency and exhibited as
conditioning; there is no separate conflict lottery.

**The local weight law [POSITED, the fixed-budget standard]:** at each
participant local step, conditioned on causal past only: a propose-total
of `1/4` split equally over enabled `(base, payload)` options in the
initiator's view; an arbitrate-and-merge total of `1/4` split equally over
admissible arbitration components (computed at the join's own past — the
component first exists as a record at the join) plus enabled merge pairs,
times the kernel law's winner distribution; a deliver-total of `1/4` split
equally over enabled `(receiver, version)` pairs in the sender's view; and
idle absorbing every unavailable total — redistribution, never dilution.
Baseline idle at genesis is exactly `1/2` (propose and deliver open,
arbitration closed); with all three sectors open it is `1/4`.  Randomness
enters only as recorded click outcomes.

**Kernel laws, both computed exactly on the same enumerated alphabet
[POSITED as alternatives, inherited from paper 25 §10]:** K1 = a uniform
recorded order-click over the `|C|!` component orders followed by greedy
acceptance; K2 = uniform over maximal independent sets.  On the
three-proposal path `P–Q–R` they give `{P,R}` weight `2/3` versus `1/2`
[EXACT], the discriminating instance of paper 25 §10 — here *generated*,
not supplied.  The merge of a value-conflicted pair is a recorded binary
click at uniform `1/2` (K1 and K2 agree on a pair); the equal-value merge

## 5. The decided trilemma

### 5.1 The completion problem, posed exactly

A **placement completion** is strictly positive data `Z` with the
transfer

```text
q'(e|h) = q(e|h) . Z(h+e) / Z(h),
```

required to be (a) per-cut normalized (`sum_e q'(e|h) = 1`) and
(b) foliation-invariant: the completed conditional is a function of the
record alone (class-constant) — equivalently, chain products agree
across the linear extensions of every history.  For class-constant `Z`
the gradient form makes this automatic (§5.5's telescoping theorem).
The existence of a globally consistent `Z` is the
**discrete Tomonaga–Schwinger integrability condition** for the record
functional: per-diamond the two-path constraint is solvable and

two-actor family — 1,191 histories, 427 canonical classes, 202 canonical

### 5.3 Gradient completions exist at every finite depth [EXACT]
