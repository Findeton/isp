## 2. Four different objects that must not be merged

The word “event” has carried too much work. Four stages are distinct.

### 2.1 Proposal

A proposal is an opportunity for a particular extension to occur. It must
specify a domain of possible new marked histories and include a null or
no-proposal alternative. Before a proposal exists there is no candidate whose
evidence can be evaluated.

### 2.2 Ordered comparison

Given two possible ordered processes on one supplied atom space, the RN field
compares them. `AB/BA` here denote supplied physical protocol orientations,
not two construction presentations of one gauge history. If they were merely
construction presentations, they would first require a canonical physical
pushforward. D6 proves no construction-order gauge theorem.

### 2.3 Commitment weights and a possible later seal

The pointwise action `log R(omega)`, its expectation `D(P||Q)`, actual
accumulated record evidence, numerical survival weight `S`, complementary
division weight `1-S`, a sampled commitment decision, and a durable seal are
different objects. D6 computes the expectation and weights. It constructs no
evidence carrier, random decision, or sealed record.

### 2.4 Outcome and placement

An accepted outcome must enter the record history with a scope, orientation,
and exactly-once physical ownership. This is the input required by D5's
composition algebra.

## 3. Conditional ordered-law RN field theorem

Let `Omega` be a supplied finite history atom space, and let `P,Q` be strictly
positive normalized laws on it. Define

$$
R(\omega)=\frac{P(\omega)}{Q(\omega)}.
$$

Then exactly:

$$
\mathbb E_Q[R]=1,
\qquad
\mathbb E_P[R^{-1}]=1,
\qquad
P=QR.
$$

Reversing the ordered comparison sends `R` to `R^{-1}`. Independent products
send `(R_1,R_2)` to `R_1R_2`, so the logarithmic action adds. Joint relabeling
simply relabels the field. These are not fitted properties; they follow from
the definition of the RN derivative.

For the exact binary witness

$$
P=(-:1/4,+:3/4),
\qquad
Q=(-:3/4,+:1/4),
$$

the RN field is

$$
R=(1/3,3),
$$

and the directed KL is

$$
D(P\Vert Q)
=0.5493061443340548456976226184612628523\ldots.
$$

Because this witness is a swapped pair,

$$
D(P\Vert Q)=D(Q\Vert P),
$$

while the RN fields are inverse. Thus even equal scalar evidence does not
select a physical protocol orientation.

### Theorem 3.1 — conditional RN-data extraction

Given a supplied ordered pair `(P,Q)`, one may canonically extract:

```text
numerical field        R=P/Q
protocol orientation  P relative to Q
coordinate dependence presentation-relative essential coordinates of R
expected evidence      D(P||Q)
comparison-neutral     R identically 1
```

The theorem is conditional on the ordered pair and common atom space. It does
not construct either member of the pair, decide when the comparison is
available, assign physical participating records, or allocate a primitive D5
factor identity. `R identically 1` means only `P=Q`; it is neither an
eventless-history statement nor a null/no-proposal outcome.

#### Proof

The factor and normalization identities are pointwise rational arithmetic.
The presentation-relative essential dependence set is the unique smallest
listed coordinate set outside which `R` is constant. None of these
operations maps one existing record history to a distribution over new
history spaces. Therefore they are reconstruction, not extension proposal.
∎

## 5. What the complete supplied log-density ledger really proves

$$

For a supplied positive law `P` and uniform count reference `mu`, the complete

$$
\log\frac{P}{\mu}
=c_\varnothing+\sum_{S\ne\varnothing}c_S\chi_S.
