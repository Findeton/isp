## 13. The successor register

Registered, not claimed.

**S-1 — the connection is the whole question, and it is a price rather than a
task.** The Born menu's blindness to everything but the count residue is what
makes the absolute polarity representative-relative — and that blindness is
forced by a DERIVED item: paper-20's connection group is Z_3 *because the
arena is over F_3*, and every register of the form `w^n` over Z_3 is a
function of `n mod 3` by construction. So the absolute question is not merely
unanswered at this arena; it is unposable without converting `F5` from
DERIVED to DECLARED, or changing the field. A metric-consuming coin is not a
free route to it either: the GDL adjudication rules that raising the
consumption strength cannot rescue the blind functional AT ANY strength, and
names the live route as breaking block-diagonality — which re-gates
paper-20's transport. Any successor here inherits that ruling and that route,
not a fresh start.

**S-2 — the emission reading needs a derivation, not a declaration.** The two
readings give opposite polarity signs at every coin and every arena measured
here. Nothing in the corpus derives one of them; paper-20 declared both and

menus are `q(l|x)` transports of the same law — one takes the post-coin Born
weight, the other the division count — so it is a LAW-TRANSPORT question at

one-parameter family interpolating the two weights, run at A4 to horizon 5,
asking whether the sign crosses once or is discontinuous.

**S-3 — the coupling, not the rate.** The transportable statement is the one
that survives both currencies: a round's deposit on a cell is coupled by the
partition to eight it does not choose while an emission's is coupled to
nothing, so the grammar cannot concentrate and cover at once. A successor
should ask whether that obligation persists at larger budgets, whether any
grammar-side schedule loosens it, and — separately — whether this corpus has
any *derived* exchange rate between a round and a step. If it has, the
