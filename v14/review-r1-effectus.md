## 3. K1 — THE COPYING QUESTION (primary)

### 3.1 (a) The theorem, stated and proved

> **THEOREM R2-B (disjoint-copy invariance).**  Let X be a finite CW
> complex and let
> $$N_B \;=\; \Big(\coprod_{k=1}^{B} X_k\Big) \sqcup \{p\},\qquad X_k\cong X,$$
> with p a 0-cell carrying no incident 1- or 2-cells.  Let Q be any function
> on finite complexes that is (i) **additive under disjoint union**,
> Q(Y ⊔ Z) = Q(Y) + Q(Z), and (ii) **zero on a point**.  Then
> Q(N_B) = B·Q(X).  Consequently, for any two such Q₁, Q₂ with Q₂(X) ≠ 0,
> $$\frac{Q_1(N_B)}{Q_2(N_B)} \;=\; \frac{Q_1(X)}{Q_2(X)}$$
> is **independent of B**.

*Proof.*  (i) gives Q(N_B) = Σₖ Q(X_k) + Q(p); (ii) kills Q(p); X_k ≅ X
gives Q(X_k) = Q(X).  The ratio's B cancels. ∎

The counts |E|, |F| and the Betti numbers b_i (i ≥ 1) over any field are all
such Q (b_i of a point vanishes for i ≥ 1; |E| and |F| of a point vanish).
Hence, **given only** that the blocks are pairwise isomorphic and the
basepoint is isolated,

- N_coh density = |F(N_coh)|/|E(N)| = f/e is constant in B;
- b₂ density = b₂(N_coh)/|F(N_coh)| = β₂/f is constant in B.

The statement the protocol asked me to adjudicate is therefore **PROVED, not
refuted**, with no hypothesis beyond the two named.

> **COROLLARY R2-B′ (the converse half — why the other three move).**  A
> quantity of the form A(N_B)/n with A additive and n = |V(N_B)| =
> B·|V(X)| + 1 equals (B·A(X) + a₀)/(B·|V(X)| + 1), where a₀ = A(p).  This
> is constant in B **iff** A(X) = a₀·|V(X)|.  Generically it is not, and
> then its whole variation is O(1/B), caused entirely by the single
> basepoint.

Measured against the delivery:

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| DIMENSION_PROFILE at 6 | 7 | 0 | 7 | 7 = 0 — false | moves ✔ |

φ is not of that form (its denominator is quadratic in B) and moves for a

I also prove the theorem that governs the *drawn* relation, because it
forces most of the rest of the census:

> **THEOREM R2-A (the admission rule).**  Let ⟨τ⟩ be cyclic of order N
> acting on a set S, and admit (a,b), a ≠ b, iff **exactly one** π ∈ ⟨τ⟩ has
> π(a) = b.  Then {k ∈ ℤ_N : τᵏa = b} is empty (b ∉ orbit(a)) or a coset of
> Stab(a) of size N/|orbit(a)|.  Hence (a,b) is drawn **iff b ∈ orbit(a) and
> |orbit(a)| = N**.
> **Corollary:** the drawn relation at a cell is the disjoint union of
> **complete** graphs on the *regular* orbits; non-regular orbits draw
> nothing.  The overlap graph is therefore always a **union of cliques**.

*Verification:* exhaustive over every cyclic action of order N ≤ 12 on

## 8. THE R2-GATEWAY QUESTION

**Is A₁ the right gateway per the pin?**  It is **pin-legal**: the pin says
any Aₖ with φₖ < 1 unlocks the manifold rung, and the unit computed the

**Is it meaningful?  No — on four measured grounds.**

1. **The criterion cannot fail.**
   > **THEOREM R2-C.**  The basepoint 0 is Σ-fixed at every declared member,
   > and every coordinate cell's support lies in a block or in its
   > ⟨Σ⟩-saturation — both inside L∖{0}.  So 0 lies in **no** cell's support,

   > pairs are undrawn.  Hence φ ≤ (n−2)/n < 1, **always**.

   Verified at 5/5 members, with b₀ = blocks+1 confirming the basepoint's
   isolation at each.  "The first member with φ < 1" therefore degenerates
   to "the first member".  G23's non-vacuity check ("φ's declared range
   admits 1") is satisfied by an **unreachable** endpoint; G23's only
   falsifier is `gateway-lax`, a mutant that hard-codes a different answer —
   nothing tests that the criterion can fail.  Per #208 the gateway clause
   is a disclosure.

2. **A₁ uniquely attains the forced maximum.**  φ(A₁) = 7/9 = (9−2)/9 —
   A₁ is the **only** member at the bound (A₂ 1/4 vs 7/8; A₃ 6/43 vs 41/43;
   A₄ 3/25 vs 24/25; A₅ 2/19 vs 55/57).  Its overlap graph is K₈ ⊔ {pt}:
   the *most* complete overlap graph the atlas permits, with φ < 1 caused by
   **one isolated, structureless label**.  Handing that forward as "the
   arena where locality exists" is backwards.

3. **No member has a non-trivial overlap pattern.**  By THEOREM R2-A the
   overlap graph is always a union of cliques, and I measured componentwise
   completeness = **1 at 5 of 5 members and both probes**.  The simplicial
   nerve at every member is a disjoint union of simplices plus a point —
   I3's degenerate case (K₃₆ ⟹ contractible nerve) repeated m times.  The
   I4/TOP successor requirement (i), *a non-complete overlap graph so that
   there is simplicial topology at this scale*, is met in **letter** and
   defeated in **purpose**: φ < 1 is achieved by disconnection only.
   (What non-trivial topology this unit *does* see lives in the
   **coordinate resolution** — edges per cell, rules per edge — not in the
   overlap pattern.  That distinction must reach R2 or it will measure the
   wrong object.)

4. **A₁ is in the heterogeneous head.**  It is built by a rule shared with

3. **If a member must be named anyway, name A₃, not A₁** — the first
