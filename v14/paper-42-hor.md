## The question

Does the theory need a size? Every unit of this programme so far has declared
one — a lattice period, a record scale — and then measured inside it. A
declared size is a piece of furniture: it is not something any measurement
found, and if a local result depends on it, the result is about the furniture.
The pin asks whether the theory can be written so that no law mentions a size
at all, with every statement quantified over horizons instead.

Two things had to be true for that to be more than a wish. Influence has to
travel at a finite speed, so that an observable of finite depth has a horizon
at all; and the physics inside a horizon has to be indifferent to what the
arena does far away. Both are measured here, and so is the part that is not
indifferent — because the answer is not that everything survives. Some of what
the corpus has stated at a closed arena has no counterpart on the open patch
this unit declares, and this unit says which.

## The arena, declared as data

Two arenas, one set of generators. The **closed** arena is R5's: the square
lattice with opposite edges identified, its links, its plaquettes, its coin
family and its single-occupation carrier. The **open patch** is the same square
of places with no identification anywhere: a link exists exactly when both of
its endpoints do, a plaquette exactly when all four of its corners do, and a
path that walks off the edge simply has nowhere to go. Nothing else differs —
not the field, not the alphabet, not the coin family, not the link operator.

The link operator is the parent's, quoted from its bytes and used here as the
definition of what the tick is built from:

> acts as $U$ on the two-dimensional span of the link's own domino and as the identity on every other site

Both ladders are declared before the run: closed sizes and open sizes, each
swept entire, with the odd closed sizes included precisely because they are
where the parent's own stratification is expected to misbehave.

## The emergent speed

The pin asks for

> the exact maximal propagation speed of influence per tick, measured (not assumed) on the committed dynamics

and the measurement is taken twice over, by two routes that share only the
closure rule under test. The structural route propagates a set through the
parity classes' partner relation and touches no amplitude at all; it is the
cone proper, the places influence *can* reach. The amplitude route evolves an
exact state and reports the places influence *does* reach at a particular coin.

First, how far one tick can carry anything. Unitarity decides it, coin by coin,
and it leaves no room: the diagonal entries of an admissible coin vanish
together and the off-diagonal ones vanish together, so there are three
vanishing patterns and no others.

Unitarity leaves exactly 3 vanishing patterns over the 640 coins, so the reach of a tick is zero at 64 of them and one site at 576, and it exceeds one site at 0.

Then the cone, censused to the declared depth rather than bounded. Its
sum-norm radius is exactly the tick number at every depth — not at most, but
exactly — and its shape is a product of a range in each direction, so it is a
box and not a diamond. The licence for calling the speed a maximum sits at two
depths and both are published: the structural cone reaches the radius at every
censused depth, and the dynamics reaches it at every depth of the shorter
attainment sweep.

In the sum norm the emergent speed is 1 site per tick; the structural cone attains it at 16 of 16 censused depths and the dynamics attains it at 8 of 8 attainment depths, so it is a maximum, not a bound.

Over whole sweeps the max-norm speed is 1/2 site per tick, one value at every sweep.

## The shape is a declaration; the speed is not

A schedule has to say which parity goes first, and that choice is visible: the
cone reaches further forward than backward, by exactly one place. Reversing the
parity order inside the schedule reflects the whole shape and moves no radius
in either norm — the instrument is self-tested under the declaration's own
action, and the result is a clean separation. The asymmetry is arena-relative
and is reported as such; the speed is not.

The cone is a product of an x-range and a y-range at every one of the 16 depths, its x-range is asymmetric at 16 of them, and reversing the parity order inside the schedule reflects it at 16 while moving no radius in either norm.

## Where a cone has content, and where it stops having any

On a closed arena a cone is a wasting asset. It fills the arena at a measured
depth and says nothing afterwards, and before that it starts to double back on
itself. Both depths are measured here for every closed size, and both are the
reason a cone censused on a closed arena stops carrying information past a
measured depth — which is the mechanism R4's own wrap sentence names:

> The bound is not saturated by all of them, because the torus wraps

On an open patch the cone has content for as long as the patch has room. That
is the whole reason this unit built the open arena before measuring a speed.

## The speed measured here is not the speed the parent measured

R4b measured a maximal group speed on this lattice and reported, in its own

That object is a phase advance on the dual torus. It exists because the arena is
closed, and this unit neither re-runs it nor revises it nor contradicts it. The

## The open patch and its gauge group

The constant twists an arena realises are measured by propagation over its own
links rather than read off a formula, so both closures answer the same
question. The answer is the sharpest closure result here. A twist has to close
around a cycle, and an open patch has no cycle to close around:

> A constant link twist is realisable by a gauge transformation exactly at the even values, because the phase must close after $L$ steps.

The open patch realises all 8 constant twists at every one of its 7 sizes, so its merging index is 1, which a closed arena reaches only where the phase order divides its size.

The consequence is that the open patch sits exactly where a closed arena sits
when the phase order divides its size — the case the parents singled out:

> at every L divisible by eight it is 1, and there the price is not reduced, no orbit pair is identified and no observable is pinned

and

> What does not survive is the class merging: 0 merges at L = 8 against 72 here

So, under this unit's declared flush, the identified pairs that carry the
parents' price, their prohibition and their pinned observable are bought by an
identification whose length the phase order does not divide; an arena with no
identification never has them at any of the seven open sizes swept, which is
where a closed arena already stands at the size the twist parent sealed and the
potential parent ran. The class count under the full stencil image, by

## The closure-artifact census

Every sealed law this unit uses goes through one classifier that sees only the
law's residual on the open arena and its residual on the closed one. A law
whose two residuals agree survives the flush verbatim; one that differs with a
stated proviso survives scoped; one that differs without a proviso, or that has
no residual on the open arena at all because the object it speaks of is not
there, differs across the pair.

**The pair is one pair, and every row prints it.** The open side is the patch
at the largest size on the open ladder and the closed side is the torus at the
largest size on the closed one — the size the potential parent itself ran its
boundary lattice at — and one row, the stratification, compares ladder sums
instead and says so in its own arena column. Nothing here is decided by taking
the two sides of a comparison at different sizes. Beyond the pair, every
witness in the table is measured again at each of the nine sizes on which every
declared shape fits, and those ladders are published in the receipt beside the
row they belong to.

**Two columns, not one.** The verdict says what the flush did to the law. The
basis says what kind of witness carried the verdict: a witness drawn from each
arena's own objects is **measured**, and a witness that no arena can move —
a defect count the construction forbids, or a quantity computed with no arena
argument at all, such as the coin family handed to both sides of the comparison
or the class count under the full stencil image — is **structural**, and
survives the flush trivially rather than informatively. The label is not taken
on trust: a structural label the nine-size sweep can move stops the run.

## The undetermined-size form

The formulation the pin asks for can now be written, because every ingredient
in it has been measured. A **HOR-law** is a statement of the form: *for every
finite open patch and every observable whose support and whose depth-cone both
lie inside it, the following holds.* No symbol in a HOR-law denotes a size, no
quantifier ranges over a completed totality, and every instance is a statement
about one finite patch. The patches are directed under inclusion and the
inclusions carry values unchanged on exactly the observables a HOR-law
quantifies over; that property is what is measured, and it is not a
convergence, not a limit and not an object. The basis is disclosed rather than
summed over: the reference is the smallest size in the system, and the
comparisons it makes with itself are counted separately from the ones that
cross a proper inclusion.

Across 5 open sizes directed under inclusion the inclusions carry 400 values and fail to carry 0, and of those comparisons 80 are the reference size against itself and 320 cross a proper inclusion.

The standing prohibition is the parent's and it is not weakened here. R1 says
of its own two stabilised values:

> The two constant values are not a limit and not a convergence.

and, of what it does not claim:

> Nothing is claimed about arenas outside that list or about a limit of the family in any topology.

Neither sentence is contradicted by anything above. A HOR-law is a schema over
finite patches; the directed system is a way of saying which statements are
schema-shaped, and no member of it, and no object beyond it, is asserted to
exist. This unit makes no actual-infinity claim of any kind.

## The gate on the formulation

Stating the form is cheap. The pin therefore asks for a gate: every sealed law
this unit uses must be quantifiable over horizons, or the exception must be
named. It is run on every row of the census, and the word it returns says what
it tested. The test is the **closure-witness test** — the law's witness on the
open arena is its witness on the closed one, or differs from it with a stated
proviso — and a law that passes it is horizon-statable **by that test**. That is
not the same property as carrying no symbol for a size, and this unit does not
claim to have measured the second one.

12 of the 18 laws are horizon-statable by the closure-witness test and 6 are not, and every one of those carries the name of the object it needs.

The exceptions are not failures of the formulation; they are its content. They
are the winding family, the winding classes a loop can carry, the
translation-covariant generator family and the momentum route that needs it,
and the two completeness properties of the link and plaquette sets that hold
only when there is no edge. Every one of them is a statement about a closure.
None of them is a statement about an observable inside a horizon. Whether any
horizon-statable law depends on one of them is not measured here, and is handed
on rather than assumed.

The arena is two-dimensional and finite at every size swept; the field, the

## Verdict

The theory does not need a size. Inside any horizon, the physics this corpus
has built is indifferent to how much arena there is beyond it, and the
formulation that never declares a size is available, stated and gated. What
needs a size is exactly the part that was about the closure all along, and that
part is now listed by name.
