## 1. The question, and what would have counted as an answer

The weld put a grammar record inside I7's admissible class. This unit asks
the summit question on that record: run a quantum dynamics whose
interactions emit division events **through the confirmed law**, updating
the very counts the dynamics propagates on, and ask whether the quantum side
*requires* that update.

The pin makes the answer a measurement rather than a target. Three gates:
the coupled step must be well defined; its observables must move against a
**mandatory frozen-stage control**; and a closure condition internal to the
quantum dynamics must fail on the frozen stage and pass on the coupled one.
Five outcomes were pre-registered, including `COUPLING-INERT`,
`COUPLING-INCONSISTENT`, and `COUPLING-BLOCKED-AT-THE-LAW-TRANSPORT` as a
first-class finding rather than a failure.

The requirement gate was well posed rather than rigged, and the reason is
CR-A's: its static-geometry theorem is what makes a demand for motion a real
demand, so a coupling that produces motion had something to fail at. Two of
the three gates pass in the strong direction. The third returns a negative,
and the negative is sharp: **no closure internal to the quantum side that is
not the update rule restated fails on the frozen stage and passes on the
coupled one**. What replaces the missing theorem is a theorem of the
opposite kind — §8's staleness-blindness result, which says why the witness
is not there and what a successor would have to change to find one — and a
threshold nobody asked for, §9's admissibility exit.
