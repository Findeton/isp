## 9. Verdicts and stopping rules

Allowed verdicts:

```text
ACCEPT

REVISE
REJECT
INTEGRITY-BLOCK
```

A **semantic counterexample** changes the scientific verdict. Examples:

- an induced map is not positive/CP as claimed;
- causal factorization does not imply the printed schedule result;
- no-signalling compares the wrong marginal;
- the positive history measure fails normalization/measurability;
- the quotient is not a congruence;
- one model-specific theorem is used to establish another model's result; or
- the rung promotes global predictive bookkeeping to local ontology.

A source typo, missing citation qualifier, or sentence whose correction does
not alter a definition, theorem, product coordinate, or physical
interpretation is a bounded fix.

At the first decisive semantic counterexample, a seat may stop additional
promotion science after recording enough controls to locate independent
surviving coordinates. It must not repair the candidate.
