## 0. Disposition

The next foundational advance may come not from adding a new mechanism but
from finding a structure that physics has treated as necessary when it was
only sufficient, representational, effective, or convenient.

The Barandes lesson is the model case. Markovian stochastic processes imply
stochastic processes, but stochastic processes need not be Markovian. A
one-step transition law need not determine a complete multi-time process, and
mathematical enlargement into a Markov state need not identify a physical
memory carrier. The important move is not the vague instruction to question
assumptions. It is the exact audit

$$
\boxed{
A\Longrightarrow B\ \text{under stated premises};
\qquad
B\stackrel{?}{\Longrightarrow}A.}
\tag{1}
$$

This programme will search for similarly illicit converses, but also for
four neighboring errors:

1. identifying a mathematical representation with its physical referent;
2. identifying an observational conditional with a causal intervention;
3. promoting an effective laboratory regularity to a universal ontology; and
4. treating existence of a completion or dilation as physical selection of
   that completion or dilation.

It is not a license to remove every premise at once. Each investigation must
delete one precisely typed assumption, retain the rest, recover the relevant
known physics, charge the replacement resources, and end in a theorem,
counterexample, empirical discriminator, or named underdetermination.

## 1. Historical precision and the real Barandes lesson

Non-Markovian stochastic processes were not absent from probability theory.
The sharper foundational contribution is to refuse the tacit identification

$$
\text{ordinary-positive stochastic description}
\stackrel{?}{=}
\text{divisible instantaneous-state dynamics}.
\tag{2}
$$

Barandes explicitly formulates quantum systems as indivisible stochastic
processes, with Hilbert space and the wavefunction potentially secondary
representations. Independent process-tensor work likewise shows that a
multi-time quantum process is not exhausted by a sequence of state-to-state
maps and must be defined relative to a physical intervention grammar.

The responsible historical claim is therefore not that all physicists
believed every stochastic process was Markovian. It is that important
quantum-foundational arguments and ontological no-go results often restrict
their model class using Markov, instantaneous-state, noncontextual,
factorization, or fixed-intervention premises whose physical necessity must
be audited separately.

The programme must retain two distinctions:

$$
\text{non-Markovian}
\ne
\text{Barandes-indivisible},
\qquad
\text{mathematically Markovizable}
\ne
\text{physically carried by a Markov state}.
\tag{3}
$$

An ever-growing history can formally Markovize almost any process. That fact
does not show that nature contains an ever-growing memory object, nor that the
resulting state is local, finite, measurable by available instruments, or
independent of the target process used to construct it.

## 2. The mirror audit: Barandes is a guide, not an exemption

The same method must be applied symmetrically to Barandes's current
formulation. At the source scope already reconstructed in v17, the displayed
models begin after several structures have been supplied:

1. a configuration space $\mathcal C$;
2. time labels and conditioning or division times;
3. a contingent probability distribution;
4. first-order conditional laws $\Gamma(t\leftarrow t_0)$;
5. a division grammar;
6. configuration trajectories or an actuality claim at declared scope;
7. Cartesian-product kinematics for some composite examples; and
8. spatial and light-cone premises for causal-locality applications.

Those inputs may be physically correct. They have not thereby been derived.
The stochastic--quantum correspondence shows what follows once a suitable
stochastic law is given; it does not yet supply one target-blind physical
principle that generates the correct configuration domain, law,
interventions, readers, composition, chronology, or gravitational coupling
for held-out experiments.

The mirror questions are therefore:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{physical possibility} &\stackrel{?}{=} \text{configuration at a time},\\
\text{actuality} &\stackrel{?}{=} \text{one trajectory through }\mathcal C,\\
\text{law} &\stackrel{?}{=} \Gamma(t\leftarrow t_0)\text{ plus contingent state},\\
\text{composition} &\stackrel{?}{=} \text{Cartesian product of configurations},\\
\text{time} &\stackrel{?}{=} \text{a supplied real parameter}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
$$

No negative answer is assumed. A positive answer must be earned from physical
interfaces and held-out consequences rather than inherited from the
mathematical language used to state the proposal.

## 3. Six recurrent hidden-assumption errors

Every candidate assumption must be classified before mathematics begins.

### 3.1 Illicit converse

One proves $A\Rightarrow B$ and silently uses $B\Rightarrow A$.

Example: a tensor factorization defines subsystems; it does not follow that
every physical subsystem is fundamentally a tensor factor.

### 3.2 Representation--referent collapse

A predictive representation exists and is treated as the material ontology.

Example: a Hilbert dilation of a positive law does not by itself make the
dilating environment or complex amplitude physically actual.

### 3.3 Conditional--intervention collapse

An observed conditional distribution is treated as a causal transformation.

Example: inference from a correlated system--environment preparation is not
the same map as an independently implementable re-preparation intervention.

### 3.4 Effective--fundamental promotion

A structure that works in isolated laboratory regimes is assumed at all
scales and for the universe as a whole.

Examples include exact subsystem factorization, universal purification,
external time, and a fixed smooth manifold.

### 3.5 Existence--selection promotion

A mathematical completion, purification, embedding, or stochastic realizer
exists and is treated as the one nature uses.

### 3.6 Syntax--physics promotion

An order, label, coordinate, graph edge, tensor slot, or program sequence in
the formalism is treated as physical chronology, identity, locality, or
geometry without an operational descent map.

These six errors are related but not interchangeable. An audit that merely
finds the wrong category of mistake has not removed the assumption.

## 7. The deepest common assumption: sufficient physical boundaries

Temporal state sufficiency and spatial subsystem factorization may be two
orientations of one deeper separability assumption.

Let a complete experiment be decomposed across a candidate interface
$\Sigma$. The usual formalisms assume that there exists a boundary object
$B_\Sigma$ and a composition rule such that all influence between the two
sides is mediated through $B_\Sigma$:

$$
\mathcal L(E)
=
\mathcal L(E_-)
\star_{B_\Sigma}
\mathcal L(E_+).
\tag{6}
$$

Different familiar assumptions are specializations:

- a temporal $\Sigma$ and ordinary state $B_\Sigma$ give an
  instantaneous-state or Markov picture;
- a regional $\Sigma$ and tensor-factor state give an ordinary subsystem
  picture;
- an operator algebra or process boundary gives a noncommutative interface;
- boundary charges or edge modes give a nonfactorizing gauge interface;
- $B_\Sigma$ equal to the entire past gives formal history Markovization;
- absence of any proper sufficient $B_\Sigma$ gives a genuinely indivisible
  regional or whole-process law.

The first high-leverage necessity question is therefore:

> Does nature supply independently identifiable, compositionally sufficient
> physical boundaries, and if so, what is their minimal physical type?

The word *independently* is essential. $B_\Sigma$ may not be manufactured by
encoding the complete held-out target law. The word *physical* is equally
essential. A mathematical sufficient statistic is not automatically a
carrier in the world.

Allowed outcomes include:

1. a compact ordinary-positive boundary state;
2. a boundary algebra or other noncommutative interface;
3. a boundary object whose size grows with history or region;
4. a scale- or state-dependent interface that becomes tensor/Markov only in
   laboratory regimes;
5. no proper sufficient boundary within the tested class; or
6. underdetermination because the registered instruments cannot distinguish
   the alternatives.

This question does not preselect Barandes, Hilbert ontology, an all-at-once
law, discreteness, continuity, spacetime, or gravity.

## 8. Discovery protocol

### Step 1 — state the alleged equivalence

Write both directions. Identify which direction is a theorem and which is the
candidate hidden assumption.

### Step 2 — classify its status

The assumption must be labelled as a definition, theorem, empirical
regularity, modelling convenience, or ontology postulate. Changing labels
mid-argument is a semantic failure.

### Step 3 — reconstruct the strongest theorem domain

List system class, topology, regularity, intervention grammar, independence,
composition, causal, and measurability premises. No no-go theorem may be
promoted beyond its domain.

### Step 4 — delete exactly one premise

Construct the smallest countermodel or enlarged class that retains every
other premise. Simultaneously abandoning locality, probability, causality,
composition, and spacetime is not informative.

### Step 5 — recover known physics on the same interface

At the relevant stage, an alternative must recover:

1. the terminal R1 fixed-background source-to-record baseline;
2. the B0 complete massive-matter interface once source review is valid;
3. operational no-signalling, contextual composition, and stable records;
4. the appropriate relativistic/QFT and classical limits; and
5. eventually the reciprocal GR limit.

A logical counterexample that cannot model the experiment is a control, not a
candidate law.

### Step 6 — charge replacement structure

The ledger must expose target-process input, future-setting access, unbounded
history, exponential context, infinite precision, preferred foliation,
nonuniform per-experiment advice, boundary postselection, and fitted geometry.

### Step 7 — require a reduction or consequence

Removing an assumption merely enlarges the model class. It earns physical
progress only if it does at least one of the following:

1. derives a previously supplied structure;
2. removes a declared input without importing equivalent information;
3. explains two previously separate phenomena with one parent law;
4. forces a measurable difference;
5. proves a representation-independent resource cost; or
6. establishes a no-go or exact underdetermination under a named class.

### Step 8 — hostile converse audit

Before promotion, an independent reviewer must try to reintroduce the deleted
assumption under another name: memory, reference, gauge fixing, environment,
boundary state, latent code, program order, or calibration.

laboratory validity is exact, approximate, or emergent under the boundary

dilation theorem.

### Priority C — causal order and physical time

Audit N3--N5 only after the intervention interface is explicit. A process
matrix demonstrates logical room beyond fixed global order; it does not by
itself derive a physical chronology or show that every admissible matrix is
realized in nature.

### Priority D — gravity source and mediator inference

Audit N20 and N21 on the eventual common B0/MG0 experiment. The Aziz--Howl
construction is a class-relative counterexample to an overbroad mediator
inference, not evidence that gravity is classical. The target is the weakest
complete premise set under which a gravity-sensitive record distinguishes
fully specified laws.

### Priority E — Barandes source completion

Return the surviving structures to the native question: can one uniform
ordinary-positive indivisible law generate the complete held-out process
without receiving the wavefunction, process matrix, action, phase, holonomy,
configuration domain, or reader answer as input? Failure of that construction
would be recorded without promoting Hilbert ontology by default.
