## 1. The finite hybrid repair is exact

For a finite retained record set $R_D$, the proposed observable object

$$
\mathcal O_D
=\ell^\infty(R_D)\otimes\mathcal A_D
\cong\bigoplus_{r\in R_D}\mathcal A_D
$$

is the standard classical--quantum algebra needed at the boundary.  Its
center contains the record events, while each block retains the quantum
observables available under that record.

Let $\{\mathcal J_s\}_{s\in S}$ be CP branch maps satisfying

$$
\sum_s\mathcal J_s(1)=1.
$$

Then

$$
\widehat{\mathcal J}((A_s)_s)
=\sum_s\mathcal J_s(A_s)
$$

is CP because the positive matrix amplifications are sums of positive matrix
amplifications.  It is unital precisely by instrument normalization.  Thus it
is one complete normalized arrow.  The central injection into summand $s$
recovers the branch, while the diagonal map $A\mapsto(A)_s$ recovers the
nonselective operation.  These are two restrictions of the complete arrow,
not rival definitions of it.

The v2 counterexample now composes.  For a retained binary $Z$ result followed
by $I$ for $s=0$ and $X$ for $s=1$, define

$$
\widehat T(A)=(A,XAX).
$$

Then

$$
(\widehat{\mathcal J}\circ\widehat T)(A)
=\mathcal J_0(A)+\mathcal J_1(XAX),
$$

which is exactly the branchwise probability calculation already accepted in
v2.  No branch probability, POVM effect, state update, or physical parameter
has changed.  The repair adds the missing codomain and composition law.

The same reasoning extends over an already retained finite record by using
the corresponding block matrix/decomposable maps.  A guarded future is a map
whose block at $r$ is the registered operation for $r$; a discard is the
inclusion of record-independent observables; and a deterministic
coarse-graining is the classical pullback, tensored or composed with the
appropriate quantum map.  Identity and explicit skip remain different
physical arrows because their boundary targets differ even when a quantum
component is an identity channel.

Disposition: Sections 3.1, 4, 5, and 6 of the pin are internally coherent and
sufficient for a finite construction.

## 2. Complete instruments, records, and conditioning

The pin correctly separates four operations that v2 had conflated at the
Heisenberg-target level:

1. the **complete instrument** is the normalized arrow into the hybrid
   record-bearing target;
2. a **branch** is a generally nonunital restriction selected by a central
   event;
3. the **nonselective channel** is the diagonal restriction that forgets the
   result; and
4. an **adaptive guard** reads the retained central coordinate and applies a
   block-dependent continuation.

For a finite positive-support event $e$, a normal input state $\rho$ gives

$$
p(e)=\rho(\widehat{\mathcal J}(e)),
$$

and conditional normalization is permitted only when $p(e)>0$.  At zero
support there is no normalized posterior.  In the standard-Borel regime,
$[\chi_\Delta]$ is a central projection only modulo the declared null ideal;
a null singleton is the zero projection and cannot define a point posterior.

This is prediction conditional on a record.  It neither actualizes an outcome
nor promotes the record boundary to a division.  The future-sufficiency test
remains independently required.

## 3. Standard-Borel/W*-normal scope

The pin does not claim that an arbitrary CP-valued measure has a normal
hybrid extension.  It admits only a represented sigma-finite $W^*$ packet
with separable predual, a supplied technical domination class, and an
instrument carrying both:

$$
\Phi_{\mathcal I}:
\mathcal A_E\,\overline\otimes L^\infty(S,\mu)
\longrightarrow\mathcal A_D,
$$

as a normal unital CP map, and a strongly measurable posterior/state-record
kernel for `Ev`.

That is a legitimate conditional theorem.  The normal extension property is
not automatic.  Okamura--Ozawa prove a correspondence between NEP
instruments and measuring processes and relate NEP, under their stated
hypotheses, to strongly measurable posterior families; they also exhibit CP
instruments without NEP.  Approximation by NEP instruments therefore cannot
be silently promoted to an exact extension.  The pin prints all of these
distinctions.

The construction must nevertheless verify the following separately for each
admitted continuous instrument:

- normality, CP, unitality, and the exact domain/codomain of
  $\Phi_{\mathcal I}$;
- the NEP or an explicitly equivalent normal extension, not only scalar
  countable additivity;
- a strongly measurable `Ev` kernel into the declared standard-Borel
  state/record space;
- normal-state closure under the registered operation and positive-support
  conditioning; and
- equality of `Ev` and `Heis` expectations for every normal boundary state
  and every complete reader, not merely a generating scalar sample unless a
  valid monotone-class/normality proof extends it.

If any item fails, that instrument is outside the exact continuous theorem.
The finite retained coarse-graining or terminal-reader fallback remains
available, but it is a different, explicitly weaker scope.

## 4. Reference measure classes

The reference measure is technical typing data, not a physical prior.  For a
fixed normal instrument and faithful normal reference state $\varphi$, its
reference outcome measure

$$
\nu(\Delta)=\varphi(\mathcal I(1,\Delta))
$$

dominates every normal-state outcome law: if $\nu(\Delta)=0$, positivity and
faithfulness force $\mathcal I(1,\Delta)=0$, hence
$\rho(\mathcal I(1,\Delta))=0$ for every normal $\rho$.  Equivalent choices
of $\nu$ yield the same $L^\infty$ null ideal and the canonical identity
isomorphism on equivalence classes.  A singular replacement changes the
object and is not a gauge change.

Two qualifications are binding.

First, a single boundary object used by several incoming mechanisms needs one
measure class dominating **all** registered normal outcome laws reaching that
exact schema.  Faithfulness for one instrument does not prove simultaneous
domination for an arbitrary family.  For example, the uncountable family of
Dirac laws $\{\delta_x:x\in[0,1]\}$ has no sigma-finite common dominating
measure: such a measure would need a positive atom at every $x$, while a
sigma-finite measure has at most countably many positive atoms.  Such a
packet must restrict the family, provide and prove a common class, or refine
the boundary schemas.  It is not admitted merely by calling each individual
law normal relative to its own measure.

Second, a measurable record map is not by itself enough to define a pullback
on $L^\infty$ equivalence classes.  For

$$
f:(R,\nu_R)\longrightarrow(T,\nu_T),
$$

the map $[g]\mapsto[g\circ f]$ is well defined only if

$$
f_*\nu_R\ll\nu_T,
$$

equivalently every $\nu_T$-null set has $\nu_R$-null preimage.  With
$R=T=[0,1]$ carrying Lebesgue measure and $f(r)=0$, the target functions
$0$ and $\chi_{\{0\}}$ are equal in $L^\infty(T)$, but their pullbacks are
$0$ and $1$.  The naive pullback depends on the representative.

This is not a blocker to the frozen pin because Section 4.2 admits only maps
preserving the declared state-class interface and refuses arbitrary
representative choice.  It is, however, an exact construction obligation:
“preserving” must be realized as the relevant measure-class nonsingularity
(or a stronger proved condition), and closure under composition must be
shown.  Mere Borel measurability does not pass C6.

## 5. Ev/Heis duality and process memory

The pin's displayed duality equation is coherent if its left side is made
literal.  If the state/record kernel for a path is

$$
K_p(d\rho_E,dr\mid\rho_D),
$$

and a final hybrid reader is represented by the measurable field $F(r)$,
the required scalar is

$$
\int \rho_E(F(r))\,
K_p(d\rho_E,dr\mid\rho_D).
$$

Construction must print this evaluation map (or an exactly equivalent one),
prove measurability/integrability, and then prove equality with

$$
\rho_D(\operatorname{Heis}_\Xi(p)(F)).
$$

Writing $\int F\,d\operatorname{Ev}$ without the state evaluation is only
notation; it is not itself the proof.

The memory clause is essential.  Suppose two predictive boundary values have
the same normal state on $\mathcal O_D$ but different hidden memory values,
and a licensed future reads that memory to choose $I$ or $X$.  They then agree
on every present hybrid observable but disagree on a future reader.  No map
out of $\mathcal O_D$ can represent that future, so duality and operational
congruence fail.  Therefore every future-relevant quantum memory degree of
freedom must be:

- included in $\mathcal A_D$ and acted on by the full primitive CP maps; or
- included in one explicitly typed predictive extension with a printed
  evaluation and update map sufficient for every licensed future.

A name such as “process memory” or an `Ev`-side cache is not exposure.  A
later primitive may not consume information absent from the boundary object
on which `Heis` acts.

## 6. Localized system--probe composition

The pin preserves rather than modifies the v2 localized measurement formula.
The Fewster--Verch comparator supports induced CP operations from a localized
system--probe coupling, postselected instruments, and composition under a
named causal-factorization premise.  It does not make arbitrary instruments
commute and does not remove the physical source premise.

For v3, each localized reader with retained output must be packaged as the
complete hybrid arrow, not as a single selected CP map.  A certificate for
two disjoint probes must compare the full arrows and full state/record
kernels, including:

- the permutation matching the two output record coordinates;
- the same exposed product-probe preparation on both orders;
- all quantum and classical target schemas; and
- any memory/source lineage consumed downstream.

Operation-level no-signalling concerns the complete nonselective remote
operation.  A selected positive-support conditional may steer and incurs a
record.  Neither commutation alone nor equality on one state proves the
complete statement.  Correlated probes are a joint source mechanism, not a
product-source certificate with the correlation hidden.

## 7. Certified concurrency quantifiers

The repaired quantifiers are sufficient.

One exchange certificate generates equality only for the adjacent exchange
it proves and for consequences under category congruence.  Paths connected by
finitely many certified adjacent swaps have equal `Ev` and `Heis` semantics.
An arbitrary pair of linear extensions is equal only when every co-enabled
incomparable pair reachable in the protocol is certified (with all schemas,
records, and sources included).

This avoids both errors:

- it does not infer global schedule independence from one commuting square;
- it does not interpret an exposed, uncertified procedural order as a hidden
  fundamental clock.

If an incomparable pair is uncertified, its two orders remain distinct
procedures unless the packet refines them into an ordered or joint mechanism.
The construction must quantify certification over every reachable context,
not only the initial frontier or one chosen topological sort.

## 10. Fresh quantum-probability attacks

These attacks are additional to the pin's mandatory 48.  Each is a binding
test for the future construction.

### Q1 — Representative-dependent measurable pullback

Use the constant-map Lebesgue example of Section 4.  **Required response:**
reject the map unless the declared source/target classes make it nonsingular.
Measurability alone fails.

### Q2 — No common dominator across one boundary schema

Send an uncountable mutually singular family $\{\delta_x\}$ into one alleged
standard-Borel target object.  **Required response:** restrict the registered
family, supply a proved common class, or split the schema.  Per-mechanism
reference measures do not define one object.

### Q3 — Same current state, different hidden process memory

Choose two `Ev` values with the same state on $\mathcal O_D$ but hidden bits
$m=0,1$, then let the next slot apply $I$ or $X$ according to $m$.
**Required response:** expose $m$ quantumly/classically in the boundary or
forbid the future.  A hidden cache invalidates duality.

### Q4 — Quantum-valued integral erased to a scalar outcome measure

Give two posterior ensembles with the same scalar record distribution but
different $\int\rho_E(F(r)),dK$.  **Required response:** `Ev` must retain the
posterior state kernel and use the printed evaluation map; the outcome
measure alone is insufficient.

### Q5 — Instrument-specific normality passed off as boundary normality

Let each mechanism have a posterior normal relative to its own singular
measure class, but provide no common boundary class.  **Required response:**
fail packet admission.  “Normal” is meaningful only after the represented
boundary object is fixed.

### Q6 — NEP only on a coarse sigma-algebra

Supply a normal extension for a finite partition of $S$ but not for the raw
standard-Borel record.  **Required response:** earn only the finite retained
coarse-graining theorem.  Do not infer the continuous retained-record result.

### Q7 — Adaptive guard after record erasure

Discard or erase $r$, then use a later operation whose block depends on its
old value.  **Required response:** the composite is untyped or the memory was
not actually discarded.  Marginal equality before the guard cannot repair
the lost interface.

### Q8 — Future-result guard encoded by block labels

Make a current control map block diagonal in an outcome produced only at a
later slot.  **Required response:** reject it at the source boundary.  A
formal direct sum must not become retrocausal memory.

### Q9 — One faithful state treated as an actual ensemble

Change the technical faithful reference state while preserving its measure
class and all registered laws.  **Required response:** canonical equivalence
and unchanged predictions.  Any empirical change reveals that the reference
device was illicitly used as a physical prior.

### Q10 — Approximate NEP promoted by a limit without topology control

Use NEP instruments $\mathcal I_n$ approaching a non-NEP instrument only in
the approximation sense available from injectivity, then take an unproved
limit of their hybrid maps/posteriors.  **Required response:** approximate
comparator only unless the required normal extension and measurable kernel
are independently established at the limit.

### Q11 — Pairwise initial certificates miss a reachable context

Certify exchanges for every incomparable pair at the empty frontier, then
let a retained result enable a new pair whose maps fail to commute.
**Required response:** the protocol is not fully certified; all-linearization
equality is unavailable.

### Q12 — Output-order mismatch hidden by isomorphism

Two disjoint instruments write records in orders $(r,s)$ and $(s,r)$ and the
certificate compares their raw direct sums without the permutation.
**Required response:** fail the certificate until the exact central
permutation and occurrence identities are included.

### Q13 — Product probe replaced by correlated probe

Keep the same reduced probe marginals but correlate their source, producing a
different joint operation.  **Required response:** type a joint source and do
not reuse the product-source exchange/no-signalling proof.

### Q14 — Equality on tomography-incomplete readers

Match `Ev` and `Heis` on a finite reader family that does not separate the
hybrid algebra, while disagreeing on another central-quantum observable.
**Required response:** no duality theorem unless equality is proved for all
complete readers or the chosen family is proved separating with a valid
normal extension argument.

All fourteen attacks have a pin-consistent refusal or construction
obligation.  None forces a change to a v2 probability.
