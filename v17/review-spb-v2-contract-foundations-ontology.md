## 2. Independent answer to the circularity question

For the properly typed compact candidates, `D,S,C,I,X_g` test real empirical
distinctions rather than define sufficiency true by fiat.

1. `D` can fail while a target-sized predictive table succeeds, because descent
   requires independently identified hardware and source facts before the
   connected target opens (contract lines 288–318 and 348–353).
2. `I` can fail while a predictive coordinate fits, because an implemented
   preparation/instrument/reader with retained outcome and backaction is
   required (lines 355–359).
3. `S` can pass, fail, or remain underdetermined on held-out intention-to-treat
   laws after nontrivial history separation and independent boundary matching
   (lines 427–442 and 446–529).
4. `C` can disagree with `S`: independently trained kernels must predict one
   directly executed composite with a seam intervention, complete failure law,
   and sub-target resource use (lines 533–615).
5. `X_g` concerns a distinct regime and names the transferred duty, so primary
   composition can pass while `X_C` fails (lines 364–388).

Candidate choice, supplied apparatus factorization, and operational coordinates
remain inputs. The contract does not derive them. That is not circularity so
long as the output is only a task-, policy-, apparatus-, margin-, and
regime-relative sufficiency result. Two frozen outcome routes nevertheless
cross this boundary: Findings O2-01 and O2-02 are disposition-controlling.
