## 1. The exact physical question

> Can the externally parameterized laboratory predictions accepted in Paper
> 03 be recovered from physical, imperfect, interacting clock subsystems, and
> can two independently constructed clocks be shown to be reductions of one
> clock-neutral relational process, so that held-out relational predictions no
> longer consume an external evolution parameter?

This question has three logically distinct levels:

1. **Clock correlation:** a subsystem has readable phases correlated with an
   external laboratory parameter.
2. **Clock-relative adequacy:** conditioning on its physical record reproduces
   the registered laboratory probabilities on a declared window.
3. **External-parameter elimination:** both the clock-relative and laboratory
   descriptions are reductions of one parameter-free or gauge-invariant parent
   law, and held-out predictions can be computed without the external
   parameter, program position, or hidden schedule counter.

Level 1 does not imply level 2. Level 2 does not imply level 3. A construction
must return the product of these answers rather than one scalar verdict.

## 3. Frozen terminology

### 3.1 External parameter

An external parameter is a real or discrete label `s` used by the comparator
to index evolution, switch an interaction, order a laboratory schedule, or
define a family of states or maps. It is not counted as observed unless an
explicit physical reader returns it.

A relettering `s -> f(s)` is bookkeeping only when every preparation,
operation, clock calibration, reader, and joint probability transforms
covariantly. Replacing one Hamiltonian or one instrument by another is not a
reparameterization merely because both use a variable called time.

### 3.2 Physical clock

A registered physical clock is the packet

$$
\mathfrak C=
(\mathcal B_C,\mathfrak S_C,\sigma_C,\mathcal L_C,
 E_C,\mathcal J_C,R_C,W_C,\mathcal R_C).
$$

Its fields are:

1. a physical subsystem algebra or exact stochastic factor
   $\mathcal B_C$ inside the complete Paper-03 boundary;
2. an admitted clock state class $\mathfrak S_C$ and preparation $\sigma_C$;
3. its autonomous law or generator $\mathcal L_C$, including all couplings
   relevant on the declared experiment;
4. a normalized finite or standard-Borel clock-reading POVM/instrument $E_C$;
5. the complete readout operation $\mathcal J_C$, including disturbance;
6. a retained record channel $R_C$ exposed to all licensed futures;
7. a declared calibration window $W_C$;
8. its resource ledger: dimension, energy support, coherence, resolution,
   running interval, and preparation/readout cost to the extent used; and
9. complete clock readers $\mathcal R_C$.

The clock is not licensed merely because one variable is monotone in a source
file. At least two distinguishable readings with positive operational support,
a physical preparation, dynamics, and readout are required.

### 3.3 Independently constructed clocks

Clocks `A` and `B` are independent constructions only if they have distinct
physical subsystem identities and at least one independently variable
preparation, generator, or readout. The following do not count:

- two names for the same readout;
- a deterministic classical copy made after one clock is read;
- two functions of one hidden counter;
- a clock and a postprocessed unit change of that clock; or
- two clocks whose calibration parameters were jointly selected on the test
  data.

Independently constructed does not mean probabilistically independent. The
clocks may interact or be entangled, but those correlations must be retained.

### 3.4 Clock reading, clock record, and clock-relative prediction

A reading is a measurement outcome. A record is a physical variable carrying
that outcome into later licensed operations. For a clock outcome set
$\Delta$ and another registered output $Y$, the finite or positive-event
clock-relative law is

$$
P_C(Y\in A\mid T_C\in\Delta,u)
=
\frac{P(Y\in A,T_C\in\Delta\mid u)}
     {P(T_C\in\Delta\mid u)}.
$$

Here `u` is the complete exposed preparation/control context. Zero-support
events have no normalized conditional state.

For a diffuse clock outcome, a regular conditional kernel

$$
K_C(\tau,A\mid u)
=P(Y\in A\mid T_C=\tau,u)
$$

is claimed only almost everywhere with respect to the clock marginal and only
under the standard-Borel/common-instrument hypotheses inherited from Paper
03. A chosen null-point posterior is not physics.

### 3.5 Clock-neutral parent

A clock-neutral parent $\mathfrak N$ is one complete physical object from
which the descriptions relative to clocks `A`, `B`, and the laboratory
comparator are derived. It must include:

1. one state/law/history object, not separately fitted clock-conditioned
   tables;
2. the physical clock subsystems and their interactions;
3. a declared gauge, constraint, or parameter-forgetting structure;
4. relational observables or conditional readers;
5. reduction maps for every claimed temporal frame;
6. a physical normalization or inner product adequate for probabilities; and
7. complete source, intervention, record, and reader lineage.

Two parameterized models with matching output tables are not a clock-neutral
parent. A formal constraint written after the tables are fixed is also not a
derivation.

The parent may be expressed as a constrained Hilbert-space model, a positive
stochastic/history law with a genuine reparameterization quotient, or another
exact representation. Hilbert space is not presumed fundamental. Any route
must construct the same operational objects and pass the same controls.

### 4.5 Parametrization is not explanation

An arbitrary externally parameterized dynamics can often be embedded in a
larger constrained or history-state construction by adding a clock degree of
freedom. Such an embedding may establish a formal clock-neutral
representation. It does not by itself establish that:

- the parent law is independently physical;
- the clock factorization is selected;
- the original evolution parameter was merely gauge in nature;
- one actual temporal frame is selected; or

The construction must therefore state how the parent was fixed. If its

## 5. Two-clock comparison

### 5.1 Common experiment

The two clocks must be carried through the same physical procedure, with one
joint distribution

$$
P(d\tau_A,d\tau_B,dy,dr\mid u).
$$

Separate runs with separately fitted system laws cannot establish clock-frame
equivalence. The joint law must retain correlations, clock-system
backreaction, records, and all adaptive choices.

### 5.2 Same-path affine calibration

On one declared comparator path and a preregistered good-clock window, units
and origins are calibrated by an orientation-preserving affine map

$$
\tau_B=a\tau_A+b,\qquad a>0.
$$

For nonideal clocks, this means a declared conditional calibration kernel and
error criterion centered on the affine relation, not exact equality of noisy
readouts. The fit uses a training subset. Agreement is evaluated on held-out
readings, preparations, and system readers.

Clocks transported on different spacetime paths are not required to agree
affinely. Forcing them to do so would erase the proper-time effects that a
later gravity test must retain.

### 5.3 Change of temporal frame

Where both clock readings have positive support, a physical change-of-clock
map must be derived from the common parent and joint law. It must transform
complete states, observables, records, and readers—not only the scalar label.

In an ideal reversible overlap, the round trip is identity on the reachable
operational quotient. For imperfect clocks the map may be a stochastic or
UCP channel and need not be invertible; information loss must be reported,
not hidden.

For every complete reader `R`, the two reductions must satisfy the same
joint-law comparison. One convenient form, when a conditional kernel exists,
is

$$
P_A(R\mid\tau_A,u)
=
\int P_B(R\mid\tau_B,u)
       M_{B\mid A}(d\tau_B\mid\tau_A,u),
$$

with exact support and almost-everywhere qualifications. This equation is a
theorem target, not an admission rule.

### 5.4 Multiple-choice outcome

If different valid clock choices yield inequivalent reduced dynamics on a
complete operational context, the result is
`P04-CLOCK-CHOICE-DEPENDENT-DYNAMICS`, not a failed implementation. The
construction must locate whether inequivalence comes from finite resolution,
backreaction, interaction, periodicity, support, or a genuinely different
deparametrization.

## 6. Good, finite, periodic, stopped, and recurrent clocks

### 6.1 Good-clock window

A clock is good only relative to a declared window, preparation class,
resolution, and task. The construction must quantify at least:

- distinguishability of registered readings;
- coverage and positive support;
- calibration bias and dispersion;
- recurrence or wraparound probability;
- disturbance from readout;
- clock-system interaction; and
- retained-record fidelity.

No universal perfect clock is presumed.

### 6.2 Finite and periodic clocks

A finite clock generally recurs. Within one certified cycle it may define a
local temporal frame. Outside that window, the same reading may correspond to
multiple physical histories. A winding number may disambiguate them only if
it is stored by an additional physical subsystem in the complete boundary.
The path index cannot serve as an unrecorded winding number.

### 6.3 Stopped clock

If the clock reading ceases to change while registered system readers still
distinguish later histories, that clock is not future sufficient. The result
is a stopped-clock domain boundary. The system is not declared frozen.

### 6.4 Recurrent clock

If the same clock reading recurs with different conditional system laws, the
reading alone is not a global time coordinate. The construction must either
restrict to a certified injective window, expose a physical cycle record, or
return clock ambiguity.

### 6.5 Nonmonotone readings

An arbitrary nonmonotone map of readings is not a gauge
reparameterization. A clock may run backward or fluctuate in a model, but the
resulting multi-valued relation and its probabilities must remain explicit.

## 7. Backreaction and finite resources

At least one positive construction must include nonzero clock-system
interaction or clock-readout disturbance. A proof using only

$$
H_{\mathrm{tot}}=H_C\otimes 1+1\otimes H_S
$$

may establish an ideal reference theorem but cannot establish the physical
clock result by itself.

The common law must determine how the clock changes the system and how the
system changes the clock. Interaction may yield a time-nonlocal conditional
equation or a noisy temporal reference. Such behavior is an output, not a
defect to be removed.

A finite-resource clock must not be assigned infinite accuracy, unlimited
running time, or a Hamiltonian unbounded below without explicit idealization.
Energy, dimension, coherence, and readout costs are reported at the level
used by the theorem. Paper 04 does not attempt a universal optimal-clock
bound.

## 18. Success and halt rule

External-parameter elimination is earned only if all of the following hold:

1. two genuine physical clocks are constructed;
2. their common joint law is derived from one complete packet;
3. sequential and adaptive clock-relative predictions reproduce the
   comparator on held-out contexts;
4. same-path two-clock agreement passes without post-hoc calibration;
5. a single normalized or physically inner-product-complete clock-neutral
   parent is constructed;
6. laboratory and both clock descriptions are reductions of that parent;
7. reparameterization covariance and hidden-time attacks pass;
8. finite-clock limits, recurrence, stoppage, and backreaction are retained;
9. multiple-choice dependence is either absent by theorem or printed as an

10. every ontology, causal-order, spacetime, and gravity wall remains closed.

If items 1--4 hold but 5--7 fail, the exact result is clock-relative
operational adequacy, not time elimination. If the two clocks disagree, that
is a positive multiple-choice result about the limitations of the parent.
