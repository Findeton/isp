## 5. Independent defect: readout-time laundering

A time-independent Hamiltonian or fixed channel does not by itself eliminate
external runtime. Consider a device whose autonomous state evolves until an
external pulse, issued at a prescribed laboratory duration, opens a detector or
writes the retained record. If the pulse time changes the endpoint law, then the
pulse and its timing resource are part of the mechanism. Relabeling them as a
supplied `readout protocol` launders the scalar being claimed redundant.

A supplied passive interrogation is admissible only if it reads an already
available record and does not create, enable, select, or stabilize the clock
outcome. Operationally, the record law must remain valid throughout a
preregistered nonzero interrogation domain, up to frozen error bounds, or the
endpoint must be triggered by a represented physical completion mechanism. If a
second clock chooses the endpoint, that clock and its resources belong in `P`;
the result is clock comparison, not elimination of external timing.

T5, C8--C9, T14, and the stopped/recurrent-window language express the intended
firewall, but the pin simultaneously retains supplied readout context and gives
no literal positive decision rule for this boundary. Because the exact physical
question expressly excludes an external runtime parameter, the distinction must
be frozen before model comparison rather than inferred favorably afterward.
