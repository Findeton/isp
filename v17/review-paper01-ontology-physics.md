## 2. Executive assessment

The candidate proves something correct but deliberately modest. Given the
complete quantum process, experiment, and contingent preparation, it compiles
one reversible model of the whole laboratory and pushes the Born measure to
actual setting/outcome records. That supplies ordinary positive probabilities
on records and preserves the complete operational interface. It does not derive
the quantum process from stochastic microdynamics.

The construction is consequently not a hidden-variable completion, a selected
configuration ontology, or a microscopic stochastic trajectory theory. Its
history space contains records. The phase-complete quantum process or dilation
class remains in the contingent/program data that generate those record laws.
The paper says all of this. Calling the result a representation with ontology
debt is therefore accurate; calling it a complete Barandes-style ontology would
be false.

Three distinctions are load-bearing:

1. A probability measure on complete **record strings** is not a probability
   measure on complete microscopic configurations at every proposed cut.
2. A primitive assertion that one record string occurs is coherent without a
   microscopic realizer, but it is not a derivation of actuality and does not
   answer what occurs at unrecorded cuts.
3. Laboratory program order can organize interventions without being an
   emergent direction of time.

The paper maintains all three distinctions. I therefore accept the ontology
claims with the already printed scope and reject every stronger reading.

## 4. Reconstructed probability lineage

### 4.1 Preparation to physical reader

Take a qubit in

$$
|+\rangle=\frac{|0\rangle+|1\rangle}{\sqrt2}
$$

and first apply the computational-basis instrument. For the Lüders version, a
recording dilation has the relevant action

$$
|+\rangle|0\rangle_R
\longmapsto
\frac{|0\rangle|0\rangle_R+|1\rangle|1\rangle_R}{\sqrt2}.
$$

A later $Z$ reader gives the positive fine and pushed-forward law

$$
p_{\rm L}(a,b)=\frac12\delta_{ab}.
$$

For the reset instrument, the same outcome is recorded but the outgoing qubit
is reset to $|0\rangle$, giving

$$
p_{\rm R}(a,b)=\frac12\delta_{b0}.
$$

Both have immediate marginal $p(a)=1/2$, while their common continuation
separates them. The lineage is therefore genuinely

```text
preparation/process -> complete instrument -> outcome-recording dilation
-> modulus-squared fine law -> environment/Kraus pushforward
-> physical (a,b) record -> reader probability
```

and not an effect-only terminal table.

### 4.2 Whole-program composition

For two Hadamards, compiling the whole program gives $H^2=I$, hence a
computational-basis input is returned with probability one. Multiplying the
endpoint kernel

$$
B=|H|^2=\frac12
\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}
$$

would instead give $B^2=B$. The correct stochastic image therefore wires the
two laboratory operations before evaluating the complete record law. This is
associative at the retained program/dilation level, while the proposed
intermediate two-state cut is not a division.

### 4.3 First-order laws to inequivalent realizers

With $X_0=0$ fixed and each of $X_1,X_2$ individually fair, define

$$
R_+(00)=R_+(11)=\frac12,
$$

and

$$
R_-(01)=R_-(10)=\frac12.
$$

Both reproduce every licensed first-order law from $X_0$. They disagree on the
higher-order statistic:

$$
R_+(X_1=X_2)=1,
\qquad
R_-(X_1=X_2)=0.
$$

Thus the minimalist first-order law identifies an equivalence class of
Kolmogorov realizers. It does not secretly put a uniform distribution on an
absent realizer label.

### 4.4 Actuality and gauge classification

For one fixed physical program:

- the setting and outcome values in $h$ are candidate physical records;
- the fine dilation label $\lambda$ is summed and is not a beable;
- the process functional and phase-complete continuation data remain
  contingent/program inputs to the fixed rule;
- one $h$ may be asserted actual as an explicit primitive;
- no microscopic $X_t$ at an unrecorded cut and no actualization selector are
  supplied;
- incompatible settings do not receive one simultaneous counterfactual value
  table.

This classification is internally coherent. It is not ontologically complete.

## 6. Physical-versus-representational gauge

The paper's gauge distinction is substantially correct:

1. A Kraus rotation confined to an inaccessible dilation fiber is
   representational gauge only after all fine labels are pushed forward.
2. A simultaneous unitary change of coordinates on state, operations,
   projectors, ports, and readers is a passive full-packet representation
   change.
3. Rotating only a state, apparatus, or proposed beable algebra is a physical
   mutation unless an independent ontological isomorphism is supplied.
4. Equal statistics under one terminal reader are insufficient for gauge; the
   complete continuation family is required.

The construction establishes empirical/representational gauge. It does not
establish that every operationally equivalent microscopic realization has the
same ontology. That stronger claim is explicitly withheld by the failure of
$JK$ to be ontological identity.

One wording correction is advisable but nonsemantic: Section 3.3 says that
`Exp_dil` is quotiented by the complete gauge “in Section 10”; the gauge packet
is in Section 11.

## 7. State, law, setting, and actuality

### 7.1 State versus law

The universal instruction “wire the supplied process and experiment, dilate,
square, and push forward” is fixed. $W$, $\rho$, preparation labels, instrument
choices, and control settings are arguments. This is a valid operational
state/law separation.

It is not yet a fundamental nomology. The rule consumes the quantum process it
is meant to represent. The paper states this, so it earns a representation
theorem rather than a derivation of quantum physics.

### 7.2 Context is apparatus, not metadata

For Bell and Peres--Mermin experiments, settings select actual CP operations and
are recorded by physical control systems. Removing the context from the
physical program would change the experiment. Conversely, a post hoc reader
relabel does not change the measure already defined on records. The candidate
therefore avoids both context erasure and invisible context lookup.

### 7.3 Actual record versus selection

There is no mathematical contradiction in taking one element of a probability
space to occur without adding a further random-selection mechanism. That is a
primitive stochastic actuality assertion. It remains an interpretive postulate,
not a theorem from normalization.

Here the postulate ranges only over $\mathsf{Hist}_{\rm rec}(\mathcal P)$. It
does not settle which microscopic configuration, if any, existed between
records. The paper's split result
`ONE-ACTUAL-RECORD-HISTORY-POSTULATED` plus
`MICROSCOPIC-ACTUALITY-UNCONSTRUCTED` is the correct classification.

## 8. Relation to Barandes, exactly scoped

I checked the two cited primary papers rather than treating the candidate's
summary as authority.

Barandes's stochastic--quantum theorem states that every finite-configuration
indivisible stochastic process can be regarded as a subsystem of a
unistochastic process. Its logical input is the stochastic process and its
output is a unitary Hilbert representation. The same paper separately points
to the converse physical claim, developed in the companion work, that a
comprehensive quantum system including devices can be modeled as an
indivisible stochastic process.

The companion paper treats configuration space and stochastic law as the
ontological/nomological core, regards the system as taking one trajectory, and
describes a minimalist law as compatible with many non-Markovian realizers. It
also places measurement devices inside the overall system. Its Bell-locality
analysis is explicitly deferred to future work rather than proved by the
stochastic correspondence.

Primary sources checked:

- Jacob A. Barandes, [The Stochastic-Quantum Theorem](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.03085v2), especially Sections 4.1--4.2;
- Jacob A. Barandes, [Quantum Systems as Indivisible Stochastic Processes](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.21192v1), especially Sections 2, 3, and 5.

Paper 01 agrees with those points but does not reproduce a Barandes
configuration trajectory. It constructs positive laws only for physical
records and retains the quantum process as operational input. Its novel scope
claim is therefore a compositional record-level audit over instruments and
causally ordered process tensors, not completion of Barandes's ontology.

One source-scope clarification would improve Section 13 without changing any
theorem: it should say explicitly that Barandes publishes the converse as part
of the broader stochastic--quantum correspondence, while the named theorem's
formal direction is stochastic-to-unistochastic. The candidate currently
implies this distinction but does not state it as sharply as the primary
sources do.
