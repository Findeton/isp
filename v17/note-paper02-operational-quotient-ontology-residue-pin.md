## 4. Class of adequate representations

### 4.1 Adequate packet

An adequate representation `R` is a packet

$$
R=(\mathcal S_R,J_R,K_R,\mathsf{Hist}_R,\Gamma_R,
   \mathsf{Read}_R,\mathsf{Gauge}_R,\mathsf{Act}_R)
$$

with the following properties.

1. `S_R` is a typed symmetric-monoidal operational structure supporting every
   Paper 01 preparation, instrument, continuation, control, discard, and
   record.
2. `J_R:Q -> S_R` preserves typed sequential/tensor composition up to a
   declared complete operational equivalence.
3. `K_R` is defined on the reachable image/quotient and satisfies
   `K_R J_R` naturally operationally equivalent to identity on `Q`.
4. `Gamma_R` is one uniform rule assigning ordinary normalized measures to
   complete candidate histories from typed contingent state and experiment
   data.
5. Every registered reader probability equals its quantum comparator.
6. Physical mixtures are affine, and ignored slots, ancillas, discard,
   conditioning, and complete continuations are preserved.
7. Every retained microscopic field, memory, order, or law parameter is
   declared and typed. No evaluator cache is exempt from ontology.
8. Gauge acts on the complete packet and preserves all registered
   continuation profiles.

The class includes Paper 01's record representation but is not restricted to
Hilbert dilations. A proposed non-Hilbert representation must construct, not
assume, every property above.

### 4.2 Reachable boundary objects

For each typed laboratory boundary `A`, let `B_R(A)` be the reachable
candidate boundary-state objects after admitted prefixes. These may contain
records, memory, microscopic variables, or other declared data.

Two such objects are operationally equivalent when every compatible physical
continuation and reader gives the same complete distribution:

$$
x\sim_R y
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\Pr_R(o\mid C,x)=\Pr_R(o\mid C,y)
$$

for every typed ancillary extension, continuation `C`, and complete record
outcome `o`.

One terminal reader is insufficient. Equality is exact, not finite-precision
or limited-tomography equality.

### 4.3 Operational quotient and fibers

Define

$$
\mathcal O_R(A)=B_R(A)/\!\sim_R.
$$

For an operational class `q`, define its representation fiber

$$
F_R(q)=\{x\in B_R(A):[x]_R=q\}.
$$

Paper 02 must distinguish:

- coordinate changes within one physical object;
- dilation/Kraus presentation gauge;
- redundant empirically idle fiber variables;
- contingent but currently unobserved physical variables;
- alternative microscopic dynamics with the same operational quotient; and
- physical differences exposed by an extended experiment.

These are not synonyms.

### 4.4 Morphisms between representations

A representation morphism `T:R -> R'` is a typed measurable map/functor on
reachable packets that:

1. commutes with the embeddings and operational decoders;
2. preserves every registered history pushforward and reader probability;
3. respects sequential and tensor wiring;
4. maps records and physical controls to their typed counterparts; and
5. declares whether it forgets, refines, or merely relabels microscopic data.

An alleged universal ontology invariant must be natural under every admitted
representation isomorphism and robust under explicitly registered fiber
refinements/coarse-grainings. Invariance under one chosen presentation is not
