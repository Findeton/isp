### 4.1 Induced instruments

For a probe effect $B$ and system observable $A$, the intended Heisenberg
instrument has the form

$$
\mathcal J_{\sigma}(B)(A)
=
(\operatorname{id}\otimes\sigma)
\bigl(\Theta(A\otimes B)\bigr),
$$

with exact domain/codomain supplied by the chosen measurement scheme. The
outcome effect is $\mathcal J_\sigma(B)(1)$ and

$$
p(B\mid\omega)
=\omega\bigl(\mathcal J_\sigma(B)(1)\bigr).
$$

For positive support, the posterior is

$$
\omega_B(A)
=
\frac{\omega(\mathcal J_\sigma(B)(A))}
     {\omega(\mathcal J_\sigma(B)(1))}.
$$

At zero support the branch is the zero functional and no normalized state is
introduced. Summing a complete probe instrument gives the nonselective map;
it need not be the identity on the causal future of the coupling.

The construction must derive these equations from one uniform measurement
scheme. It may not insert a table of terminal probabilities.

### 4.2 Causal factorization

For registered coupling regions, the scattering maps must satisfy the exact
causal factorization theorem appropriate to their support ordering. In
particular, causally disjoint couplings must give one composite instrument
independent of which linear serialization is used:

$$
\mathcal J_i\circ\mathcal J_j
=
\mathcal J_j\circ\mathcal J_i
$$

on the full admitted observable/state domain when $K_i$ and $K_j$ are
causally disjoint. For causally ordered couplings, only the physically allowed
order is asserted.

Program-loop commutativity or equality on one initial state is insufficient.

## 6. Exact theorem targets

Construction must prove or refute each target independently.

### T1 — comparator typing

Every algebra, state, operation, effect, region, embedding, and reader in the
registered packet has an exact type. Abstract $C^*$-algebra statements are not
silently mixed with representation-dependent von Neumann statements. The
state sigma algebra and every update/reader map are part of the type; standard
Borel regular conditioning is claimed only when its hypotheses hold.

### T2 — locally covariant naturality

For every admitted `Loc` embedding $\psi:M\to N$, observables, probe theories,
coupling regions, operations, and readers are transported covariantly, while
states are compared by the contravariant pullback

$$
\psi^*\nu=\nu\circ\mathcal A(\psi).
$$

Evaluation must commute with those two directions. An arbitrary state on
$M$ is not presumed to have a canonical extension to $N$. Forward state
transport is used only for comparator isomorphisms or when a separately typed
extension map is part of the packet. Naturality, not coordinate cancellation
in a final scalar, is required.

### T2b — state-class covariance without state selection

The admitted state class must be stable under the covariant pullbacks and
registered instruments actually used. For free-field curved-spacetime
examples, Hadamard/microlocal-spectrum assumptions must be stated explicitly.
Local covariance does not supply a preferred natural state on every
spacetime; under the hypotheses of the no-natural-state theorem, claiming one
is a contradiction rather than a construction.

### T3 — localized measurement construction

The induced effect and instrument are positive/CP, normalized on complete
probe outcomes, localized in the causal hull allowed by the scheme, and
compose with later measurements.

### T4 — causal factorization

Composite scattering and instrument maps factor according to physical causal
support. Causally disjoint schemes commute as complete operations; timelike
schemes retain order.

### T5 — nonselective no-signalling

For a complete local instrument in region $O_A$ and every observable/reader
localized in a causally disjoint $O_B$, the nonselective remote marginal is
unchanged. This must be proved for the declared operation class, not inferred
from commutators alone.

### T6 — steering/signalling separation

Selective conditioning may alter a remote conditional state. The outcome is
uncontrolled and requires a classical record to compare subensembles; it does
not enable superluminal signalling. The exact unconditional identity and
conditional difference must both be exhibited.

### T7 — spacelike schedule independence

Every linear execution order of one causally disjoint finite family yields the
same complete joint record law and final operational class. A hidden loop
index or foliation that changes the law kills this target.

### T8 — Bell compatibility

The packet supports commuting local observables and states violating CHSH
while preserving T5. The proof must distinguish Einstein causality,
operational no-signalling, parameter independence, outcome independence,
measurement independence, and Bell factorization.

### T9 — positive predictive-history representation

Using algebraic predictive states/functionals rather than density matrices
when necessary, construct one positive history representation of every
registered finite measurement program. Direct states use point measures,
trusted mixtures use mixtures, instruments use outcome-weighted state-update
kernels, and causal factorization supplies prefix coherence.

This is an existence control. It may be global, contextual, and
memory-bearing. It is not a selected relativistic microontology.

### T10 — procedure contextuality and idle fibers

Extend $\mathcal P\to\mathcal Q$ factorization, contextuality, idle-fiber
inflation, and admitted-projection nonselection without using spacetime labels
as procedure-name tags. A latent preferred foliation ignored by all registered
readers is an idle possibility, not evidence that it exists or that covariance
is fundamental.

### T11 — type-III/tensor refusal

No proof may assume that every bounded region has a density matrix, trace,
finite Kraus family, or Hilbert tensor factor. Where local algebras are type
III, states are positive normal functionals and operations are normal CP maps.
Type III is asserted only for comparator models/theorems that establish it.

### T12 — split-property conditional independence

For suitably separated inclusions, a split property may provide a type-I
intermediate factor and strong operational independence. It must remain
conditional on its nuclearity/split hypotheses and geometric separation.
Touching regions, arbitrary gauge theories, and the continuum as a whole do
not inherit tensor factorization.

### T13 — Reeh--Schlieder control

Local operators may generate a dense set from the vacuum without creating a
controllable superluminal preparation. The required operation may be
unbounded in norm or postselected with vanishing/small success. The theorem
must not be misreported as a signal or as local tensor independence.

### T14 — gauge and superselection firewall

Observable algebras, charged field algebras, Wilson/flux operators,
superselection sectors, and edge/center data are separate types. Gauss-law
nonfactorization may block a naive subsystem tensor product. No gauge group,
charge spectrum, or sector statistics is derived from operational covariance
alone.

### T15 — identical-particle/field firewall

Field/observable algebras and superselection sectors replace labeled-particle
tensor factors. Particle number, Fock representation, asymptotic particle
interpretation, and spin/statistics require their own hypotheses and are not
fundamental outputs of this unit.

### T16 — continuum and UV scope

The result must state whether it is abstract-net, free-field, perturbative,
cutoff, or finite-region. A lattice or mode cutoff is a control, not physical
discreteness. Entanglement entropy, local density matrices, and UV limits may
be undefined without extra structure.

### T17 — operational preferred-frame test

All registered probabilities and complete process laws must be invariant under
the comparator's covariance maps, and causally disjoint schedule choices must
agree. This earns at most **no operationally visible preferred frame in the
registered domain**. It does not exclude empirically idle preferred
microstructure.

### T18 — ontology and actuality boundary

referent, complete indivisible law, contingent state, and one actual

No collapse hypersurface, actualization rule, or microscopic locality may be
