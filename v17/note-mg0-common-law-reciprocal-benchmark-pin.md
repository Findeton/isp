## 6. Genuine distinctness

At least two complete laws must also be physically distinct. A second entrant
is not created by:

1. gauge fixing;
2. coordinate, basis, notation, or presentation change;
3. relabelling configurations or records;
4. selecting a different value of a free parameter inside one law;
5. a merely mathematical unravelling with no independently physical
   referent;
6. coarse-graining or refining the same law without a changed physical claim;
7. duplicating one predictive table behind two ontological names; or
8. changing only the comparison decoder.

An objective stochastic unravelling may count when its actual referent,
occurrence law, source functional, and reciprocal coupling are frozen as
physical nomology. Two laws may count as genuinely distinct even if the
registered benchmark later finds them empirically equivalent. Such agreement
is a nonselection result, not evidence that they were the same law.

Distinctness must be certified before outputs are unblinded. It may not be
declared retrospectively because two numerical predictions differ.
