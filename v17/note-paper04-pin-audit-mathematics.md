## 2. Independent reconstruction of the mathematical boundary

### 2.1 Positive-event and diffuse conditioning

For a finite or positive-support event $\Delta$, the pin uses the ordinary
joint-law conditional

$$
P(Y\in A\mid T_C\in\Delta,u)
=
\frac{P(Y\in A,T_C\in\Delta\mid u)}
     {P(T_C\in\Delta\mid u)}.
$$

This is normalized exactly and is undefined when the denominator vanishes.
For standard-Borel diffuse outcomes, disintegration gives a kernel only
almost everywhere relative to the clock marginal. A change on a marginal-null
set is not a new prediction. These rules are compatible with the normal
integrated semantics accepted in Paper 03.

Composition at a clock cut must mean conditioning and continuation inside
the complete joint history/instrument law. It does not license a Markov
factorization through the current reading. The pin's memory firewall is
therefore load bearing.

### 2.2 Route O

Route O is an ordinary externally scheduled laboratory model containing
physical clock subsystems, preparations, dynamics, POVMs, complete readout
instruments, retained records, resources, and readers. It can establish
clock-relative adequacy and finite-clock performance. It cannot by itself
remove the parameter that schedules its dynamics or switches its couplings.

This is a clean control route and prevents a calibrated oscillator from being
misreported as a clock-neutral law.

### 2.3 Route P

A valid constrained route requires:

$$
(\mathcal H_{\rm kin},\widehat C,\mathcal H_{\rm phys},
\eta_{\rm phys},E_A,E_B,\mathcal O_{\rm rel},
\mathcal R_A,\mathcal R_B).
$$

The physical solution space and physical inner product or rigging map must be
specified. Kinematic normalization is not enough. Clock POVMs and reduction
maps must be defined on their actual domains, with superselection sectors and
null vectors handled before probabilities are formed.

The Page--Wootters conditional state, relational Dirac observables, and
relational Heisenberg dynamics may agree under the exact trinity hypotheses.
That agreement is a representation theorem. It neither selects a
factorization nor proves that the parent was independently physical.

Sequential measurements require instruments, apparatus memory, retained
records, and adaptive controls inside the constrained description. Supplying
their switching times externally would leave the parameter in the predictor.

### 2.4 Route S

A positive stochastic parent must be one normalized measure or compatible
measure family on complete relational histories, together with physical
clock variables, a genuine gauge/parameter-forgetting structure, and exact
reduction maps.

A time-indexed transition family cannot become clock neutral by replacing
its index with the name of a random variable. If a gauge quotient is used,
probabilities on relational events must be well defined despite orbit volume,
stabilizers, recurrences, or nonfree action. If a history law is indivisible,
conditioning at a clock record cannot silently introduce a forbidden
intermediate kernel.

### 2.5 Two-clock reduction

One joint law

$$
P(d\tau_A,d\tau_B,dy,dr\mid u)
$$

is sufficient to define the regular conditionals that compare the clocks.
It is not, by itself, sufficient to prove

$$
P_A(R\mid\tau_A,u)
=
\int P_B(R\mid\tau_B,u)
M_{B\mid A}(d\tau_B\mid\tau_A,u).
$$

The right side replaces
$P(R\mid\tau_A,\tau_B,u)$ by $P(R\mid\tau_B,u)$. That step needs a proved
conditional-sufficiency relation, such as

$$
R\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\perp}T_A\mid(T_B,u),
$$

or a complete state-valued reduction map from which the equality follows.
The pin correctly calls the equation a theorem target rather than an
admission rule. Construction must retain that distinction.

For noisy or coarse clocks, a clock-frame map can lose information. Then no
inverse is promised, and the exact deficiency must appear in the product.

### 2.6 Reparameterization

A legitimate reading-coordinate change pushes forward:

- the clock POVM or instrument;
- the clock marginal and its null class;
- the retained record;
- calibration units and error model;
- conditional kernels at their almost-everywhere level; and
- all dependent readers.

Changing only the label is incomplete. A noninjective map is coarse-graining,
not a gauge coordinate change. An orientation reversal is not admitted as
proof of a physical reversal because the pin does not derive a time arrow.

### 2.7 Periodic and imperfect clocks

A periodic reading distinguishes a local phase, not a unique global history.
The same phase can have distinct future profiles on different cycles. A
cycle counter repairs this only when it is a genuine additional physical,
gauge-valid record generated by the common parent and included in the
complete boundary. A path index or appended integer is not such a record.

A stopped clock fails future sufficiency whenever the system continues to
have distinguishable futures. Nonmonotone and recurrent clocks may still be
physical devices, but their readings define multivalued or local relational
descriptions rather than a global time coordinate.

### 2.8 Formal parametrization versus explanation

Given a target propagator, one may enlarge the system with a clock and impose
a constraint engineered to reproduce that propagator. This establishes, at
most, a formal clock-neutral representation.

Operational redundancy requires a stricter chronology:

1. freeze parent variables, constraint/law, factorization, clock instruments,
   calibration family, and training/test split;
2. calibrate only on the training domain;
3. remove the external parameter from the held-out prediction interface; and
4. compute complete held-out reader probabilities without selecting or
   retuning the parent from those target probabilities.

Even this result would not decide whether time is fundamentally absent.

## 3. Binding scope required before construction

### B1 — measurable reparameterizations

Every registered reading change must be a Borel order isomorphism, or a
stronger explicitly named regularity class, between declared good-clock
windows. Null classes transform by pushforward. Conditional-kernel equality
is only at the corresponding almost-everywhere level.

### B2 — nonvacuous covariance family

Registering only the identity map cannot earn reparameterization covariance.
The construction must freeze a nontrivial separating family before testing,
including at least one nonlinear monotone coordinate change where the clock
window permits it. Otherwise T16 and product coordinate 16 remain untested.

### B3 — clock-change sufficiency

The displayed $M_{B\mid A}$ formula is awarded only after conditional
sufficiency or an equivalent complete reduction theorem is proved. Otherwise
the correct comparison retains
$P(R\mid\tau_A,\tau_B,u)$ and reports clock-choice dependence or information
loss.

### B4 — physical inner product

Route P must distinguish the kinematic inner product, constraint solution
space, rigging/group-averaging map, physical null quotient, and physical
inner product. All conditional probabilities and reduction maps must be
well-defined on the physical quotient, not merely on convenient kinematic
vectors.

### B5 — gauge parameters versus external inputs

A dummy integration variable used in group averaging is not an externally
supplied clock reading. The hidden-time test forbids a value of an evolution
parameter, schedule counter, or switched coupling from entering the
prediction functional. It does not forbid internal mathematical integration
over gauge orbits.

### B6 — complete sequential reduction

Every claimed sequential Page--Wootters or stochastic reduction must include
physical interventions, their controls, disturbance, apparatus memory,
records, and adaptive continuation. One-time conditional states do not pass.

### B7 — periodic cycle information

A winding or cycle label is admissible only when it is a physical,
gauge-valid variable of the parent with its own resource and disturbance
ledger. A periodic clock alone does not generate a gauge-invariant winding
observable.

### B8 — interacting generator type

For an interacting clock, the symbol $\mathcal L_C$ must state whether it is
a reduced clock generator, a total generator, or a component of the parent
constraint. All clock--system and readout interactions belong to the complete
parent. An autonomous marginal generator may not silently omit them.

### B9 — frozen empirical chronology

The exact calibration window, error rule, parent family, fitting variables,
training contexts, and held-out contexts freeze before construction-level
selection or fitting. “Independently specified” is evidenced by this
chronology and dependency graph, not by prose.

### B10 — same-path scope

Affine agreement concerns calibrated readings on one declared physical path
and local window. Raw nonlinear detector response may be calibrated before
this test. Different-path clocks, nonlinear drift outside the window, and
proper-time differences are not forced into the affine relation.

### B11 — route-specific reduction maps

Reduction maps must have explicit domains, codomains, support sectors, and
normalization properties. They need not all be CPTP maps: a constrained
partial isometry, a conditional kernel, and a stochastic coarse-graining are
different typed objects. A round trip is claimed only where both maps are
defined.

### B12 — formal-wrapper classification

An exact parent reverse-engineered from the full target dynamics remains
P04-FORMAL-PARAMETRIZATION-REPRESENTATION even if its final notation contains
no external parameter. Both independent pre-test specification and
held-out parameter-free prediction are necessary for operational redundancy.

These are scope constraints on the frozen experiment. They add no clock,
Hamiltonian, state, parameter, outcome, or preferred ontology.

## 7. Fresh Seat-M semantic countermodels

### M1 — nonmeasurable reading relabeling

Take a standard-Borel clock window and an arbitrary set-theoretic bijection
whose inverse is nonmeasurable. The pushed-forward POVM or conditional kernel
is not a standard-Borel physical clock packet. B1 excludes this object.

### M2 — null-class mismatch after coordinate change

Let two kernel versions differ on a clock-marginal null set $N$. Push the
label but keep the old control measure. The image $f(N)$ need not be null for
the incorrectly retained measure, so a fake physical difference appears.
Pushing forward the entire marginal/null class removes it.

### M3 — vacuous identity covariance

Register only $f(\tau)=\tau$. Every model passes T16, including one that
hard-codes the numerical coordinate. B2 requires a nontrivial frozen family
or leaves the coordinate untested.

### M4 — kinematic zero-norm contamination

Let two kinematic solutions differ by a physical-null vector. A kinematic
conditional reader distinguishes them, while every physical observable must
identify them. Unless reduction descends through the physical null quotient,
the claimed probability is representative dependent. B4 rejects it.

### M5 — divergent group average

For a noncompact constraint group, the formal average
$\int d\lambda\,e^{i\lambda\widehat C}$ may diverge. Writing this expression
does not create $\eta_{\rm phys}$ or a normalized state. The parent remains
unconstructed until a rigging/domain theorem is supplied.

### M6 — dummy gauge parameter falsely prohibited

A valid group average uses $\lambda$ as a bound integration variable but
requires no supplied value of $\lambda$. A purely lexical hidden-time test
would reject it. B5 correctly tests functional dependence on an input value,
not the presence of a dummy symbol.

### M7 — tower-property fallacy in clock change

Let $T_A,T_B$ be independent fair bits and set $R=T_A$. Then
$P(R=1\mid T_A=1)=1$, while
$\sum_b P(R=1\mid T_B=b)P(T_B=b\mid T_A=1)=1/2$.
Conditioning additionally on $T_A$ matters. A joint law alone does not prove
the displayed $M_{B\mid A}$ formula. B3 requires sufficiency or the full
conditional.

### M8 — two outputs of one hidden counter

Set $T_A=n\bmod 3$ and $T_B=n\bmod 5$ for one inaccessible counter $n$.
The readings are distinct and neither copies the other, yet they are not two
independently constructed physical clocks. Definition 3.3 plus the hidden
input audit refuses the pair. Exposing $n$ diagnoses one clock with two
readouts; it does not make the readouts independent clocks. A passing pair
needs distinct physical subsystems and the independently variable structure
required by Definition 3.3.

### M9 — periodic phase plus appended winding integer

Let $\theta=\lambda\bmod 2\pi$ and append $w=\lfloor\lambda/2\pi\rfloor$
after solving the model. The pair $(\theta,w)$ is injective but $w$ is an
external path label, not a relational observable. B7 rejects it. A genuine
cycle memory must be generated and recorded by the parent.

### M10 — periodic relational observable outside one cycle

For a nonperiodic system observable and periodic clock, the proposed complete
observable is invariant only transiently over one cycle. Reusing it globally
is not gauge invariant. The pin must return local-cycle scope or clock
ambiguity, consistent with B7.

### M11 — externally switched sequential apparatus

The state is constrained and the first one-time Page--Wootters conditional is
correct, but an external schedule turns the second instrument on. Sequential
statistics then still consume external time. B6 and T17 prevent promotion.

### M12 — engineered history-state wrapper

Given the full target unitary family $U(s)$, define a clock-history state
$\int ds\,|s\rangle U(s)|\psi\rangle$ and its annihilating constraint. The
reduction is exact, yet every target value was encoded before testing. B12
classifies it as formal parametrization rather than explanation.

### M13 — nonfree gauge action and Gribov multiplicity

A candidate clock surface intersects some gauge orbits twice and misses
others. Conditional reduction double-counts the first and loses the second.
A global clock-frame map does not exist; the result must be local,
sector-dependent, or multiple-choice.

### M14 — stopped reading with distinct futures

Two complete histories have the same final clock value after the clock
stops, but one later system reader differs. The clock value is not sufficient.
The complete history remains valid while the clock domain ends.

### M15 — hidden Markovization at a clock event

Two histories share one clock reading and present system density operator but
carry different process memory, leading to different adaptive probabilities.
Replacing both with one conditional state violates Paper-03 completeness and
does not create a division.

### M16 — nonlinear drift hidden by local affine fit

Let $\tau_B=a\tau_A+b+\epsilon\tau_A^2$. A narrow training window makes the
quadratic term invisible while a held-out window reveals it. B9/B10 require
the error model and held-out domain to freeze before fitting and permit the
agreement coordinate to fail.

### M17 — one-way backreaction

An interaction changes the system conditional dynamics while the clock state
is manually reset after every step. This is not a bidirectional autonomous
clock; the reset is either a physical operation with cost or a hidden external
controller.

### M18 — positive law with renamed target-time index

Let $\Gamma(y,t\mid x,t_0)$ be a normalized stochastic transition law and set
$T_C=t$. No clock subsystem, readout instrument, gauge quotient, or parent
reduction has been constructed. Route S and attack 57 correctly refuse it.

### M19 — singular temporal-frame support

Clock A assigns positive probability to a region on which clock B has zero
support. No regular change-of-clock channel exists there. The transformation
is partial and its domain loss must be printed; a global round trip is false.
