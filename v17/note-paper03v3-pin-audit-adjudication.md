## 4. Physical interpretation

The distinction is not an inconvenience to hide. It is standard continuous
probability and is physically informative.

### 4.1 Three different objects

The corrected construction must keep separate:

1. a **sampled record value** $r$ in one stochastic history;
2. a **posterior field** $r\mapsto\rho_r$, defined only almost everywhere
   without additional regularity; and
3. a **normal joint predictive state** on the hybrid algebra.

The first is sample-space data. The second and third are predictive
structures. None of them selects which outcome is actual.

### 4.2 Record is not division

A finite or atomic positive-support record may define an exact restartable
conditional boundary. A generic nonatomic point record does not thereby
define a normal restart state or a complete stochastic division. Continuous
adaptive feedback can still be represented inside the complete integrated
program through measurable almost-everywhere fields.

This sharpens rather than weakens the programme's existing principle:

> retaining a record and possessing a complete lawful division are distinct
> physical properties.

An exact continuous point becomes restartable only if extra structure earns
it—for example an atom, a positive-measure coarse-graining, or a separately
proved canonical pointwise posterior/regularity theorem. No such structure is
generic on a standard-Borel space.

### 4.3 Why finite-only is not chosen

Restricting all retained controls to finite records would be mathematically
clean but scientifically narrower than necessary. Continuous monitoring and
continuous feedback are genuine parts of quantum measurement theory. The
integrated theorem handles them without pretending that conditional
probability at a null singleton is canonical.

### 4.4 Why a point-separating algebra is not chosen

and destroys the represented sigma-finite $W^*$ theorem. It exchanges one
false universal claim for another. The corrected pin must not take that
