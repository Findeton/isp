# Reality-first relational quantum gravity

## Programme charter for ISP v17

Date: 2026-08-22

Status: **ERA CHARTER — ontology hypotheses and research gates, not a physical
result**

## Abstract

Quantum theory and general relativity describe one universe, yet their usual
formulations assign incompatible foundational roles to time, state, locality,
and geometry. This programme investigates whether both are regimes of one
relational, history-based stochastic theory. It takes seriously Jacob
Barandes's proposal that a quantum system can be understood as an indivisible
stochastic process in an ordinary configuration space, with Hilbert-space
objects serving as secondary representations. It does not assume that his
present framework already supplies quantum gravity, a law selector, a
background-free configuration space, or a complete account of Bell locality.

The v16 programme developed a rigorous language for typed experiments,
conditional history laws, nondivision, stable records, erasure, point-free
quotients, and hostile scope control. Its strongest accepted law is a finite
binary reference construction, not a candidate selected by observations or
first principles. Attempts to extract chronology, dimension, and gravity from
that construction accumulated declared structure faster than they removed it.
v17 therefore reverses the direction of work. It begins with the complete
empirical and operational content that any quantum ontology must reproduce,
then asks what configuration, law, state, and actuality structures are
necessary. Only after this quantum foundation is coherent does the programme
remove external time and couple matter to dynamical geometry.

The central hypothesis is deliberately defeasible: one actual relational
history may be governed by a generally covariant indivisible stochastic law,
while complex amplitudes, Hilbert space, classical spacetime, and particles
are faithful representations or effective regimes. The programme also admits
the outcomes that Hilbert structure is irreducible, that spacetime is
fundamental, that the stochastic ontology requires preferred structure, or
that no single proposed ontology accommodates both theories.

## 1. The physical question

The question is not whether two mature mathematical theories can be embedded
in a larger formalism. Almost any finite data set can be embedded in one. The
question is:

> What must the world be like for the experimentally established structures of
> quantum physics and gravitation to arise together without contradiction?

This decomposes into four questions that must not be collapsed:

1. **Ontology:** what can be actual?
2. **Nomology:** what fixed rule relates possible histories or experimental
   contexts?
3. **Contingency:** what state, boundary condition, or cosmological history is
   realized without being part of the universal law?
4. **Representation:** which calculational structures encode the same physical
   probabilities, and which of their elements correspond to reality?

The v17 programme refuses to call an answer to any one of these an answer to
the others.

## 2. Epistemic labels

Every load-bearing statement is assigned one label.

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `OBSERVED` | a reproducible empirical regularity within a stated precision and domain |
| `STANDARD-THEORY` | an established mathematical model of observations, not automatically ontology |
| `DECLARED` | an input chosen for a candidate or control |
| `HYPOTHESIS` | a physical conjecture exposed to discriminators |
| `DERIVED` | a theorem from declared inputs |
| `CALIBRATED` | inferred from an independent training protocol, then tested held out |
| `REPRESENTATION` | a mathematically faithful encoding with no ontic promotion by itself |
| `ACTUALITY-POSTULATE` | an explicit rule or primitive identifying the actual history |
| `UNCONSTRUCTED` | not supplied; never silently treated as zero, uniform, or impossible |

An absent variable is underdetermined, not uniformly distributed. A
conditional law does not select the state on which it is conditioned. A
representation theorem does not select an ontology.

## 3. What is taken from reality

The initial empirical target is standard laboratory quantum physics in finite
dimensions, extended to explicit multi-time instruments. The target includes:

- continuous phase-sensitive interference;
- arbitrary finite-dimensional preparations, transformations, and
  measurements at the declared accuracy;
- tensor composition and entanglement;
- Bell-inequality violation together with operational no-signalling;
- contextuality;
- sequential disturbance and adaptive instruments;
- non-Markovian multi-time processes and memory;
- decoherence, stable records, quantum erasure, and reversible uncomputation;
- tomography sufficient to distinguish candidate process laws.

These are not claimed to be fundamental descriptions. They are the body of
phenomena a proposed replacement must recover.

The later gravitational target includes:

- local Lorentz invariance;
- universal free fall and the equivalence principle;
- causal propagation and clock comparison;
- Newtonian and post-Newtonian limits;
- dynamical metric response to independently specified matter;
- covariant conservation and constraint closure;
- gravitational radiation or the corresponding low-energy propagating
  sector where applicable;
- semiclassical consistency and quantum matter in weak curvature;
- the empirical dimensionality and signature of observed spacetime.

No current v17 object is credited with any of these gravitational properties.

## 4. The ontology under investigation

The broad candidate is a relational stochastic-history ontology. Its neutral
mathematical packet is

\[
\mathfrak T=
(\mathsf{Cfg},\mathsf{Exp},\Omega,\boldsymbol\Gamma,
 \mathsf{Obs},\mathsf{Act}).
\]

The entries are typed as follows.

1. `Cfg` is a measurable groupoid or stack-like configuration domain. Its
   objects may be finite, countable, continuous, field-valued, or hybrid.
   Discreteness is not assumed.
2. `Exp` is a typed operational category of preparations, interventions,
   compositions, controls, and readers. An arrow is not a physical clock tick
   merely because it is written in sequence.
3. `Omega` is contingent state or boundary information. It may include an
   initial distribution, cosmological condition, or relational clock state.
   It is not part of the law unless an independent principle proves that it
   must be.
4. `Gamma-bold` is a compatible family of conditional laws over complete
   histories for all admitted experiments and contexts. A single transition
   matrix is not enough.
5. `Obs` maps histories to actual records and operational outcomes. Readers
   do not define the underlying physical quotient.
6. `Act` is the actuality status. It may be a primitive assertion that one
   history occurs, a further law, or an emergent notion. Until earned it stays
   external.

The word “relational” means invariance under changes of nonphysical
presentation and dependence only on physically available relations. It does
not mean “finite graph.” The word “stochastic” means ordinary nonnegative
probabilities on possible actual histories at the ontic level. It does not
prejudge whether complex amplitudes are fundamental, emergent, or an
indispensable representation. The word “indivisible” means that conditioning
or factorization is licensed only at complete sufficient boundaries; it does
not mean that every intermediate object is unreal.

## 5. The Barandes inheritance, exactly scoped

Barandes distinguishes a fixed configuration space, a contingent standalone
probability distribution, and fixed transition laws. His
stochastic–quantum correspondence motivates the hypothesis that Hilbert-space
objects can be secondary while quantum predictions are reproduced by an
indivisible stochastic process. This is a valuable ontological opening.

It does not by itself supply:

- the configuration space of the universe;
- a background-independent replacement for the time parameter used in the
  present stochastic formulation;
- a state or boundary condition for the universe;
- an actualization selector beyond one actual stochastic trajectory;
- a derivation of locality compatible with Bell correlations;
- a dynamical spacetime or Einstein equation;
- a unique extension from laboratory quantum systems to quantum gravity.

v17 treats the correspondence as a theorem and research clue to be audited at
the complete process level, not as permission to invent a small `Gamma` and
call it nature.

## 6. The core hypothesis and its alternatives

### H-RSH — relational stochastic history

There exists a complete packet `T` such that:

1. exactly one relational history is actual in each physical instantiation;
2. `Gamma-bold`, together with contingent `Omega`, gives ordinary
   probabilities for possible histories;
3. the operational quotient is equivalent to quantum theory in its tested
   domain;
4. the Hilbert/process representation is secondary rather than ontic;
5. classical spacetime and local fields arise as a controlled regime of the
   same matter–geometry history law.

The following alternatives remain live:

- `A-HILBERT`: complex Hilbert structure or an equivalent noncommutative
  amplitude object is ontically irreducible;
- `A-SPACETIME`: spacetime geometry is fundamental rather than emergent;
- `A-DUAL`: stochastic histories and Hilbert processes are empirically
  equivalent but ontologically underdetermined;
- `A-PREFERRED`: a stochastic completion reproduces quantum data only with a
  preferred foliation, measurement dependence, or another observable cost;
- `A-INCOMPLETE`: no tested packet supplies all quantum and gravitational
  phenomena without changing the empirical theory;
- `A-NOVEL`: a new, independently motivated law predicts a controlled
  departure from quantum theory or GR and survives experiment.

No outcome is ranked by elegance in place of evidence.

## 7. Why v16 is not the foundation of v17

v16 Paper 13D constructed a point-free typed finite conditional law with
nondivision, stable records, erasure, complete divisions, finite-set
covariance, and response. That is an important mathematical existence proof.
Its numerical primitive, binary carriers, execution syntax, size law, and
fusion grammar were declared. No experiment or selection principle identifies
them as nature's law.

Paper 14 retained a structural locally finite bundle poset and unit count but
lost its point-free probability descent and intrinsic weight. Paper 15 built a
powerful conditional `1+1` spacetime diagnostic inside a declared two-order,
Poisson, decoder, and smooth metric family. Paper 16 built a declared `3+1`
Einstein–scalar control. These are useful test rigs. They are not a derivation
from Paper 13D.

Papers 17–23 clarified why the route stalled: conditional dynamics does not
select process occurrence, scalar weights do not create omitted variables,
current sector classes do not form the fusion algebra required by the proposed
bootstrap, and the orientation consumed by the conditional spacetime decoder
is not determined by the accepted finite law. The last purported universal
orientation no-go was withdrawn because it confused an absent latent variable
with a weighted one. That correction is foundational for v17.

The right conclusion is neither “relational ontology fails” nor “one selector
is missing.” The right conclusion is that the candidate law was chosen before
the complete quantum and relativistic target was specified.

## 8. Research method

### 8.1 Inference direction

Each unit proceeds in this order:

1. state the observed or standard-theory target;
2. declare the operational interface that makes the target testable;
3. classify all extra assumptions;
4. prove representation or obstruction theorems;
5. identify ontology only where operational structure and independent
   physical principles support it;
6. construct discriminating experiments or an exact equivalence theorem;
7. implement only after mathematical acceptance.

No unit begins by choosing a pleasing finite dynamics and asking what it can
be made to resemble.

### 8.2 Theory versus data

A model can play one of four roles:

- `REFERENCE`: a known theory that defines the target probabilities;
- `CONTROL`: a deliberately declared family used to test estimators;
- `CANDIDATE`: a proposed law with independently motivated inputs;
- `PHYSICAL`: a candidate that has survived the applicable empirical and
  ontological discriminators.

The same object cannot change roles without an explicit gate.

### 8.3 The declared-input balance

Every unit updates `DECLARED_INPUTS.md`. A positive result reports:

\[
\text{derivation balance}
=\#(\text{independently derived physical structures})
-\#(\text{new load-bearing declarations}).
\]

The number is diagnostic, not a scientific score. More importantly, the table
shows whether a purportedly emergent object was supplied under another name.

### 8.4 Mathematical and empirical equivalence

For two descriptions `A` and `B`:

- operational equivalence means equal outcome distributions for every
  experiment in a frozen separating class;
- representational equivalence additionally supplies natural translations
  respecting composition, coarse-graining, and conditioning;
- ontological equivalence requires an isomorphism of complete beables and
  probability measures, not merely the same laboratory numbers.

Only the first is needed to reproduce empirical quantum theory. The third is
needed to claim that two descriptions say the same thing exists.

## 9. Ordered programme

### Phase Q — quantum-process adequacy

Construct or refute a complete relational stochastic representation of
finite-dimensional quantum processes. Required axes include arbitrary
phases, dimensions, instruments, composites, entanglement, Bell,
contextuality, temporal memory, decoherence, erasure, and tomography.

The phase may end with:

- a compositional equivalence theorem and an explicit ontic map;
- empirical equivalence with ontological underdetermination;
- a preferred-structure or contextuality price;
- a specific obstruction;
- or a controlled deviation from quantum theory.

### Phase T — clocks and external-time removal

Laboratory time in Phase Q is an operational clock record. Phase T asks
whether the theory can be written with parametrized dynamics and internal
clock subsystems while preserving all relational predictions. It tests clock
dependence, multiple-clock agreement, reparametrization, temporal Bell-like
structures, and indefinite-order controls. “The code ran first” is never a
time orientation.

### Phase G0 — common matter–geometry configuration

Enlarge the configuration ontology so gravitational and matter degrees of
freedom are variables of one law. Candidate arenas may be continuous,
discrete, or hybrid. Minisuperspace, `1+1` dilaton systems, parametrized field
theory, or lattice controls may be used as diagnostics, never silently
promoted to the universe.

### Phase G1 — low-energy gravitational adequacy

Demand independent recovery of relativistic propagation, universal coupling,
constraint closure, conservation, Newtonian/post-Newtonian behavior, and a
propagating gravitational sector where the target requires it. Fit-once and
held-out comparisons are mandatory. Matter response and geometry response
must be computed from one common law, not copied from Einstein's equation.

### Phase S — spacetime status

Only after Q, T, G0, and G1 may the programme ask whether spacetime is:

- fundamental;
- a decohered relational observable;
- an effective state-space geometry;
- a causal/order-volume reconstruction;
- or one member of an empirically equivalent dual description.

Order plus count is never called a metric without dimension, signature,
scale, manifoldlikeness, clock/radar agreement, and dynamics.

### Phase P — predictions

Identify parameters or regimes where the accepted theory differs from
standard quantum theory, QFT on curved spacetime, or GR. Register the test
before fitting. If no difference exists in the declared domain, publish the
equivalence and the remaining ontology underdetermination.

## 10. Paper 01 question

Paper 01 asks:

> Does there exist one typed relational stochastic-history architecture whose
> operational quotient is naturally equivalent to the complete
> finite-dimensional quantum process interface over the registered domain,
> and if so, what ontology, contextuality, locality, state, and gauge costs are
> unavoidable?

This is not a request to reconstruct complex amplitudes from the Paper 13D
matrix. Standard quantum theory supplies the target. Barandes's
stochastic–quantum correspondence supplies a candidate representation route.
Quantum combs and process tensors supply the compositional and multi-time
completeness tests. Bell and contextuality supply ontology discriminators.
Decoherence and quantum erasure supply the record test.

Paper 01 is mathematics-only until its theorem package survives review. It
contains no spacetime, dimension, gravity, occurrence selector, or numerical
implementation.

## 12. Evidence hierarchy

From weakest to strongest:

1. consistent example;
2. exact theorem inside a declared family;
3. representation theorem over the registered operational domain;
4. independent empirical adequacy on held-out experiments;
5. discriminatory prediction against rival theories;
6. replicated observation favoring the candidate;
7. coherent extension across quantum and gravitational regimes.

The programme currently has v16 examples and scoped theorems. It does not yet
have levels 4–7 for a unified candidate.

## 14. Nonclaims

This charter does not claim:

- that quantum theory or GR has been derived;
- that Barandes's ontology is correct;
- that complex amplitudes are unreal;
- that the universe is discrete;
- that histories form a causal set;
- that records are spacetime atoms;
- that a selector law is required to choose a law or an actual state;
- that time, dimension, signature, or metric is emergent;
- that Paper 13D is fundamental;
- that a unified theory must be unique;
- that interpretational clarity substitutes for new empirical content.

## 15. Success and failure

v17 succeeds scientifically if it sharply narrows what can describe the real
world, even if its motivating ontology fails. A negative result must identify
the exact assumption responsible. A positive result must name every declared
input and every empirical domain not tested.

The programme fails methodologically if it produces an elegant architecture
whose output was encoded in its inputs, or if it spends more effort certifying
software than challenging the physical hypothesis. Code protects a frozen
theorem; it does not decide which theorem nature uses.
