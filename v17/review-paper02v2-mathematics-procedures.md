## 3. M1 — physical-procedure referent

### 3.1 Reconstructed referent

A declaration is a finite colored incidence structure. Vertex colors include
the calibrated preparation, channel, instrument, measurement, randomizer,
record-copy, control, and discard types. Edges carry compatible physical
port types, and the external legs are the laboratory boundary. Literal
procedure identity retains the vertex colors, incidence, settings,
calibration, landmarks, record status, and randomizer contract.

The presentation groupoid acts only by incidence isomorphisms that preserve
all those colors and external data. Passing to its orbits therefore removes a
description of a protocol, not the protocol's reproducible physical
instructions. A run token is not an arrow: it is a realized occurrence of an
arrow and belongs, if modeled, to a history.

| Case | Correct classification | Reconstruction |
|---|---|---|
| unlandmarked factor-index rename | presentation gauge | the full typed incidence and calibration move together; no external landmark or record value changes |
| physical factor exchange | symmetry arrow of $\mathcal P$ | wires and attached systems are physically routed through an exchange; it is an executable map, not a rename |
| two calibrated devices with one quantum image | distinct arrows, possibly one $q$ class | their device-generator colors or complete procedure declarations differ while all registered contexts may agree |
| changed setting, landmark, retained record, incidence, or correlation | new physical procedure/type | every such change is expressly forbidden in $\mathsf{Pres}$ |

This boundary is coherent. It does assume a declared reproducibility
criterion for device types; it does not derive apparatus identity from
quantum predictions. That is appropriate because $\mathcal P$ is the
laboratory input domain, not emergent ontology.

### 3.2 Token/type and syntax attacks

Two executions of one calibrated instruction are two history tokens but one
procedure type. Conversely, two source strings that compile to the same
colored incidence object are one presentation, while two physically
different randomizers or device protocols can remain different arrows even
if serialized identically. No evaluator flag, branch name, memory address,
or loop index enters the referent.

**M1 result:** P02V2-PHYSICAL-PROCEDURE-CATEGORY-CONSTRUCTED, with the
declared laboratory-type scope retained.

## 4. M2 — category laws and presentation transport

Let $[D]$ denote the presentation orbit of a declaration $D$. If
$D\simeq D'$ and $E\simeq E'$ by typed incidence isomorphisms and their
boundaries match, functorial gluing gives

$$
E\circ D\simeq E'\circ D'.
$$

Disjoint union gives the corresponding tensor statement. Composition and
tensor are therefore well defined on orbits.

| Law | Reconstruction |
|---|---|
| associativity | the two parenthesizations glue the same finite incidence data; bound-name/redrawing isomorphism makes their orbits equal |
| unit | the empty typed wire glues no new vertex and returns the original orbit; it is not counted as a physical event |
| symmetry | the calibrated exchange wiring is a morphism; two exchanges compose to the identity orbit |
| interchange | gluing on disjoint typed components commutes, so $(f_2\otimes g_2)(f_1\otimes g_1)=(f_2f_1)\otimes(g_2g_1)$ |
| adaptation | a retained finite record selects a compatible continuation; substitution/gluing is branchwise and preserves the output type |
| discard | termination of a declared port is ordinary typed postcomposition |
| randomizer transport | an incidence isomorphism carries weights, branch arrows, calibration, and record status together |

An actual exchange is never generated by the presentation groupoid. A
landmarked factor cannot be renamed away. A presentation move also cannot
change a source-to-randomizer edge, so it cannot manufacture independence.

For strict symmetric-monoidal wording, $\mathsf B$ is the small
constructor-closed set of finite registered boundary lists, with the empty
boundary as unit. If one instead chooses a skeleton, the same construction
is symmetric monoidal up to the displayed canonical incidence isomorphisms.
This is a standard strictification/scope choice, not a new physical
identification.

**M2 result:** Theorem 1 reconstructs.

## 5. M3 — trusted randomization

$\operatorname{Mix}_r((\alpha_i,p_i)_i)$ is a colored incidence constructor,
not the formal convex sum of arrows in $\mathcal P$. The two affine equations
live at the decoded and applicable system-assignment levels. Thus

$$
\operatorname{Mix}_r((\alpha_i,p_i)_i)
\ne
\operatorname{Mix}_{r'}((\alpha_i,p_i)_i)
$$

may hold in $\mathcal P$ even though their $q$ images agree.

| Test | Exact disposition |
|---|---|
| nested forgotten mixtures | decoding flattens by $\alpha_i\beta_{j\mid i}$, but the nested and flattened laboratory procedures need not be equal arrows |
| retained outer and inner results | the output type contains the typed record pair or its declared dependent record tree; no flattening erases an intermediate record |
| branch permutation | harmless only for a forgotten bound branch variable when weights, branches, and calibration move together; a permutation of exposed record values is physical unless the entire calibrated external relabeling is included |

The assumptions that $r$ is calibrated, initially source/system independent,

## 6. M4 — complete operational congruence

For same-typed arrows define $p\sim_{\rm op}p'$ by equality in every frozen
one-hole complete context. If $D[-]$ is any allowed larger context after a
constructor is applied, absorbing that constructor into the hole produces an
allowed context for $p$ and $p'$. This gives:

| Closure | Reconstructed context |
|---|---|

| precomposition by $g$ | $C[-\circ g]$ |

| coarse-graining | classical postcomposition |
| retained record | comparison only inside the equally record-typed hom-set |

Reflexivity, symmetry, and transitivity are inherited from equality of all

## 7. M5 — quotient and universal property

Define $e:\mathcal Q\to\operatorname{im}(\mathsf{Sem})$ by
$e([p])=\mathsf{Sem}(p)$.

- Well-definedness: $p\sim_{\rm op}p'$ makes every separating quantum tester
  agree, hence their finite-dimensional operational objects agree.
- Faithfulness: equality of the operational objects gives equality under
  every compatible quantum context, hence the same class.
- Fullness: the codomain is the accepted **reachable** image, so each
  admitted operational arrow has a declared laboratory representative.
- Essential surjectivity: likewise on that reachable image, not on arbitrary
  native objects of some later representation.
- Smallness: finite incidence declarations over a declared small generator
  schema form a set; quotient hom-classes are sets.

If $F:\mathcal P\to\mathcal C$ preserves the registered constructors and is
constant on $\sim_{\rm op}$, then

$$
\bar F([p])=F(p)
$$

is well defined. Surjectivity of $q$ on classes makes $\bar F$ unique. This is
the ordinary congruence universal property. It supplies neither a microscopic
equivalence nor an inverse to $q$ on extra native states.

### Measurability boundary

At categorical scope no sigma algebra on the set of classes is needed. When
a packet calls $K_R$, a profile, or a fiber projection measurable, it must
equip the relevant reachable spaces with declared measurable structures. In
the positive model, density operators and finite combs are finite-dimensional
compact/closed Borel sets and the Born/link-product maps are Borel. In an
arbitrary standard-Borel presentation, a quotient equivalence relation can
have a non-standard or non-countably-generated quotient; the candidate does
not promote Theorem 3 to such a general measurable theorem.

**M5 result:** Theorem 3 reconstructs at reachable-image, categorical scope;
the measurable qualifier remains packet-specific.

## 9. M7 — idle fibers, admitted naturality, and context erasure

For a finite procedure tree, repeated integration of the normalized
standard-Borel kernels gives

$$
\int \nu_p(dz_0\mid h_0)
\prod_{k=1}^n L_{p,k}(dz_k\mid z_{k-1},h_{\le k})=1
$$

for each base history. Hence marginalizing $\Gamma_R^Z$ over the fiber returns
$\Gamma_R$. Literal reuse of the same prefix kernels gives causal-prefix
coherence. Trusted mixtures act on full measures, and independent tensor
sources use the declared product kernel; a correlated source is a different
typed input.

The projection $\pi_Z(h,z_\bullet)=h$ is measurable, positive, affine,
probability preserving, and operation-intertwining under precisely these
hypotheses. A $Z$-reader breaks the reader-intertwining equation and therefore
removes $\pi_Z$ from $\mathbf{Rep}_{\rm adm}$ rather than contradicting the
old quotient.

For a natural invariant,

$$
I_{R^Z}(h,z_\bullet)
=I_R(\pi_Z(h,z_\bullet))
=I_R(h).
$$

This proves fiber blindness only for the admitted projection. It does not
invent a map $R\to\mathcal Q$, erase contextuality, or assign priors to
representations.

### 9.1 Barycenter-erasure reconstruction

If one Markov kernel $D$ fixes every direct preparation,
$D_*\delta_\rho=\delta_\rho$, then for every physical decomposition

$$
D_*\left(\sum_i\alpha_i\delta_{\rho_i}\right)
=
\sum_i\alpha_iD_*\delta_{\rho_i}
=
\sum_i\alpha_i\delta_{\rho_i}.
$$

Except for a trivial decomposition this is not
$\delta_{\sum_i\alpha_i\rho_i}$. Context erasure is therefore nonlinear on
preparation measures and is not an admitted positive affine reduction.

### 9.2 Uncountable-base nonisomorphism challenge

An uncountable standard-Borel space $X$ can be Borel-isomorphic to
$X\times\{0,1\}$ as a **bare** measurable space. That does not defeat
Theorem 9. An isomorphism over the predictive projection would obey

$$
\pi\circ T=\pi.
$$

For each $x$, it would restrict to a bijection
$\{x\}\times\{0,1\}\to\{x\}$, which is impossible. Decoder preservation gives
the same obstruction because complete future effects separate predictive
objects. Thus the nonisomorphism is a slice/packet statement, not a cardinal
claim about total standard-Borel spaces.

**M7 result:** Theorems 7--9 reconstruct at their admitted-morphism and
over-projection scopes.
