## 1. Central decision

The next foundational question is not whether an arbitrary quantum experiment
can be assigned an ordinary positive probability measure on its completed
records. Paper 01 already establishes that at finite process scope.

The live question is:

> Does there exist one uniform, intervention-compatible, compositionally
> stable indivisible stochastic law that generates a scalable quantum-process
> family from independently specified physical primitives and contingent
> states, without receiving the target quantum process, the future experiment,
> or a context-by-context answer table as uncounted input?

This question has four honest outcomes:

1. **Generative positive result.** A law with ordinary-positive actual histories
   generates the registered scalable process family with controlled inputs and
   resources. Hilbert-space objects may remain useful representations but are
   not independent physical inputs.
2. **Representation-only result.** A positive history construction exists, but
   its state or law contains a naturally equivalent copy of the quantum process
   object. The ontology may be coherent, but no explanatory compression or
   physical selection has been earned.
3. **Noncommutative-residue result.** Complete intervention and composition
   force a phase-complete noncommutative boundary object, even if that object is
   not interpreted as a wavefunction. Ordinary positive record histories remain
   possible, but the predictive structure is irreducibly quantum-like.
4. **Premise-changing result.** The model uses measurement dependence,
   retrocausal or global constraints, preferred structure, uncontrolled
   continuous precision, or an empirical deviation. That branch is not banned,
   but the changed physics must be explicit and independently testable.

No outcome here derives time, spacetime, or gravity.

Thus finite operational representability is not the bottleneck. Uniform

### 2.2 What the cut theorems add

The private finite and continuous cut candidates isolate one precise cost. For
the Boolean Hidden Matching / partial-matching family, an ordinary-positive
future-sufficient cut variable obeys, under the printed independence,
screening, and fixed-error premises,

$$
I(X:\Lambda\mid S)
=
\Omega\!\left(\sqrt{n/\alpha}\right),
$$

whereas the matched quantum carrier uses (O(\log n)) qubits for fixed

This is not a no-go for positive histories. It says that one of the following
must happen:

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&\text{a large positive future-sufficient carrier,}\quad\text{or}\\
&\text{failure of positive cut sufficiency,}\quad\text{or}\\
&\text{a charged change in independence, intervention, or accuracy.}
\end{aligned}}

The second branch is the indivisible-law branch studied here.

### 2.3 What Barandes presently supplies

Barandes's native finite indivisible process contains:

- a configuration set;

  \(\Gamma(t\leftarrow t_0)\) from admitted division events \(t_0\) to target

That is an important exact structure. It is not yet, by itself, a complete

Barandes's present ontology also uses a fixed configuration set and an external

## 3. Four distinctions that may not be collapsed

### 3.1 Positive records versus positive cut state

A completed experiment can have an ordinary positive outcome law even when no
positive state on a proposed intermediate configuration carrier is sufficient
for every future intervention.

### 3.2 Nondivision versus non-Markovian memory

Failure of

$$
\Gamma(t_2\leftarrow t_0)
=
K(t_2\leftarrow t_1)\Gamma(t_1\leftarrow t_0)
$$

on one carrier is not equivalent to operational memory. A two-Hadamard process
is nondivisible on the basis-population carrier while having no environment
memory. Conversely, subsystem memory may disappear after enlarging the
physical carrier.

### 3.3 Whole-law dependence versus future-setting dependence

A law may assign a measure only after a complete physical program is wired.
That does not automatically mean a later free setting was available to the
earlier preparation. Prefix consistency and intervention autonomy must decide
the issue. If later settings enter earlier physical distributions, the model is
retrocausal, measurement-dependent, or globally constrained and must be
classified accordingly.

### 3.4 Hilbert-secondary ontology versus Hilbert-free nomology

Showing that a wavefunction need not be a beable is weaker than deriving all
phase-complete predictive structure without Hilbert-equivalent nomological
input. A theory may have actual configurations and still use a unitary,
Hamiltonian, process tensor, or naturally isomorphic object as its law. That is
a coherent ontology, but it is not an elimination or derivation of quantum
structure.

## 5. Candidate law packet

A candidate uniform indivisible theory is a typed packet

$$
\mathfrak U
=
(\mathsf{Sig},\mathsf{Sys},\mathsf{Cfg},\mathsf{Exp},
 \Omega,\mathcal L,\boldsymbol\Gamma,
 \mathsf{Read},\mathsf{Gauge},\mathsf{Act}).
$$

The fields have the following obligations.

### 5.1 Physical signature \(\mathsf{Sig}\)

This lists the primitive physical system, coupling, control, randomizer, and
reader types from which registered programs are generated. A finite signature
is not mandatory, but any continuous family and its calibration data must be
declared. The signature may not contain one primitive named after every target
process or every held-out answer.

### 5.2 Systems \(\mathsf{Sys}\)

These are typed physical systems and composites. Cartesian product,
disjoint union, tensor-like composition, or another rule must be stated rather
than inferred from notation. System labels are not automatically spatial
regions.

### 5.3 Configurations \(\mathsf{Cfg}\)

For each system type, this gives the possible actual configurations or states
of affairs asserted by the ontology. The carrier may be finite, continuous,
field-valued, relational, or hybrid. Discreteness is not presumed.

If configurations change with the experiment context, the transport and
physical meaning across contexts must be printed. A context-indexed carrier is
not rejected, but it is not experiment-independent ontology.

### 5.4 Experiments \(\mathsf{Exp}\)

This is the typed category or operad of physical preparations, interventions,
adaptive controls, compositions, randomizations, readers, failures, and
resource meters. Composition is physical wiring, not merely multiplication of
probability kernels.

### 5.5 Contingent data \(\Omega\)

This contains preparations, boundary conditions, cosmological state, and other
run- or system-specific inputs. It may not be relabeled as universal law.

### 5.6 Fixed law \(\mathcal L\)

This is one immutable rule schema applying across the scalable family. Any
coupling constants, spectra, interaction graphs, Hamiltonians, actions, or
other law parameters are typed here or in a clearly separated contingent
sector.

### 5.7 Complete history law \(\boldsymbol\Gamma\)

For every admitted physical specification and experiment,

$$
\boldsymbol\Gamma^{\mathcal L}_{e}(dh\mid\omega)
$$

is an ordinary normalized probability measure on possible complete physical
histories. It need not factor through an intermediate positive kernel unless
that boundary is a licensed division.

### 5.8 Readers \(\mathsf{Read}\)

Readers are calibrated physical maps from history records to registered
outputs. They do not define the underlying history retrospectively.

### 5.9 Gauge \(\mathsf{Gauge}\)

Gauge transformations preserve the complete experiment profile, including
post-intervention states, records, and resources. Kraus, dilation, basis, or
phase changes are not ontological differences merely because their formulas
differ.

### 5.10 Actuality \(\mathsf{Act}\)

The assertion that one history occurs is separate from normalization. If no
further selector is proposed, actuality is a postulate and its maximum earned
meaning must be stated.

## 6. The generativity and anti-table contract

The law must define one map schematically of the form

$$
(\mathcal L,\theta,\omega,e)
\longmapsto
\boldsymbol\Gamma^{\mathcal L,\theta}_{e}(\cdot\mid\omega),
$$

where \(\theta\) denotes declared system/coupling data and \(e\) is a physical
program constructed from \(\mathsf{Sig}\).

### G1 — one schema across scale

The form of \(\mathcal L\) is fixed independently of dimension, slot number,
reader choice, and held-out instance. Parameters may scale only through a
preregistered physical construction.

### G2 — no target-process input

An arbitrary quantum state, channel, comb, process tensor, or table of all
continuation probabilities may be supplied only if the result is explicitly
classified as a representation theorem.

If there is a translation

$$
J:W\longmapsto(\theta_W,\omega_W)
$$

from every target quantum process \(W\) into candidate data, the gate must
measure which structure in \(W\) is retained by \(J\), what is derived, and
whether \(J(W)\) is naturally invertible back to \(W\). An invertible recoding
is not explanatory compression.

### G3 — local substitution without refitting

Replacing one physical operation in a program changes only the corresponding
typed primitive and its physical inputs. It may not trigger the selection of a
new whole-program table or a new law parameter.

### G4 — held-out continuation

After \(\mathcal L\), \(\theta\), calibration data, and error tolerances freeze,
the same law predicts registered continuations, adaptive branches, circuit
depths, and contexts not used in construction.

### G5 — law-description accounting

The mathematical reduction may treat evaluation as computationally
unbounded, but the law description, nonuniform advice, compilation map,
continuous precision, and context bandwidth must be recorded separately. A
lookup table does not become a law because an evaluator can read it.

### G6 — no elegance selector

Shorter code or notation does not select ontology. Generativity earns derived-
input or scalable-structure gain; empirical selection requires a separating
experiment or independently physical principle.

### G7 — admissible uniformity witness

A future pin must choose, before construction, at least one literal witness of
uniformity:

1. a single finite evaluator specification that takes encoded physical
   primitives, contingent data, a registered program, a cylinder event, and an
   accuracy request, and returns or samples the corresponding probability with
   stated approximation and resource bounds; or
2. a finitely or recursively presented natural law whose existence and
   uniqueness theorem determines every registered history measure and whose
   size-dependent parameters are generated by printed recurrence, symmetry, or
   variational rules.

Writing one formula symbol \(F_n\) is not a uniformity witness if its definition
contains a new array or answer table for every \(n\). Noncomputable constants or
oracle data are not forbidden by definition, but their full physical status and
advice content must be charged; they cannot support a compression claim.

## 7. Complete operational laws

For each experiment \(e\), let \(\Phi_{\mathcal E}(\mathfrak U)(e)\) denote
the complete operational profile induced by \(\boldsymbol\Gamma_e\). It must
include immediate outcome laws, normalized post-event boundaries, adaptive
conditionals, null and failure outputs, stable records, and exposed resources.

Adequacy requires

$$
D_{\mathcal E}
\bigl(
\Phi_{\mathcal E}(\mathfrak U),
\Phi_{\mathcal E}(Q)
\bigr)
\le\varepsilon_{\rm phys}
$$

on the frozen target domain, with a complete-process metric and all compatible
continuations included.

Endpoint agreement is insufficient. Two models with the same

$$
p(z_{\rm final}\mid z_{\rm initial})
$$

may respond differently when an intervention is inserted between them.

### 7.1 Coarse-graining naturality

For a registered reader/coarse-graining \(c\),

$$
\boldsymbol\Gamma_{c\circ e}
=
c_*\boldsymbol\Gamma_e.
$$

The reader may not manufacture probability mass or redefine the history law.

### 7.2 Physical randomization affinity

If a trusted independent recorded coin chooses \(e_1\) with probability \(q\)
and \(e_2\) otherwise, the complete law is the corresponding physical mixture.
This statement does not identify two distinct preparation procedures merely
because their operational averages agree.

### 7.3 Composition without forced division

For a context \(C[-]\), \(\boldsymbol\Gamma_{C[e]}\) is generated by applying
the same \(\mathcal L\) to the physically wired program. It need not equal a
product of stochastic kernels at an unlicensed cut.

### 7.4 Independent parallel composition

Product factorization is required only for independently prepared, dynamically
noninteracting parents with independently generated states. Entangled or
common-cause parents remain joint systems. Declaring all composites independent
would exclude the target by assumption.

## 8. Intervention autonomy and temporal premise ledger

### I1 — input independence

For a freely randomized later setting \(Y\), any shared variable \(S\) and
earlier preparation variable \(X\) satisfy the registered independence
conditions unless a different branch is explicitly proposed.

### I2 — prefix consistency

Let \(p\) be a completed physical prefix and let \(f\), \(g\) be alternative
future extensions chosen only after that prefix. If \(\pi_p\) reads every
already retained prefix output, then

$$
(\pi_p)_*\boldsymbol\Gamma_{f\circ p}
=
(\pi_p)_*\boldsymbol\Gamma_{g\circ p}.
$$

This is an operational no-backwards-signalling condition relative to the
supplied laboratory order. It is not a derivation of a fundamental arrow of
time.

### I3 — physical setting entry

A setting enters the law through the physical setting generator, its record,
and its declared coupling. It may not appear as an evaluator-only index with no
physical carrier.

### I4 — adaptive consistency

Later settings may depend on earlier retained records through a registered
controller. The controller, its memory, and its resources are part of the
experiment, not free meta-level branching.

### I5 — retrocausal/global branch

A model may reject I1 or I2. It must then specify the physical joint boundary
condition or retrocausal law, show consistency under interventions, prevent
fine-tuned signalling where claimed, and expose at least one discriminator or
an exact nonselection theorem. It cannot be silently evaluated as the ordinary
forward-autonomous branch.

### I6 — causal-port locality

Whole-program mathematical access is not physical transport. For any registered
reader, replacing an operation outside its declared causal-ancestor port
component must leave that reader's marginal law unchanged, except through a
printed common parent or a physical system port that actually reaches the
reader.

In the one-way communication control, dependence of Bob's output on Alice's
preparation must be carried by the declared transmitted system. In a Bell
control, a joint reader may depend on the common source and both local records,
while each spacelike local marginal must remain insensitive to the remote
setting. This condition uses the supplied experiment wiring only; it does not
derive spacetime or chronology.

A global constraint model may reject causal-port locality, but then enters the
I5 branch. Merely saying that the complete evaluator knew the whole program is
not a causal explanation.

## 9. Genuine indivisibility, correctly typed

Indivisibility is always relative to a cut, physical carrier, experiment
family, and resource allowance.

### 9.1 Licensed positive division

At a cut \(D\), a positive division consists of a physical boundary variable
\(\Lambda_D\), preparation kernels \(\mu_p(d\lambda)\) determined by the
admitted past, and response kernels \(\xi_f\) for every licensed future, such
that

$$
P(o\mid p,f)
=
\int\mu_p(d\lambda)\,\xi_f(o\mid\lambda)
$$

with the required approximate error bound, and such that the same boundary
supports the registered interventions and post-event states.

The carrier may not depend on which future is later selected. A label of the
entire completed history is not a boundary state.

### 9.2 Relative nondivision

A declared configuration carrier is nondividing at \(D\) if no licensed
positive restart on that carrier reproduces every continuation. This is the
two-Hadamard kind of result.

### 9.3 Enlarged-carrier restoration

Adding a memory, wavefunction, process tensor, continuous exact variable, or
full preparation label may restore division. The result must then record:

- the new carrier's physical status;
- its preparation information;
- whether it is accessible or merely predictive;
- its context and future dependence;
- its scaling, precision, and composition cost; and
- whether it is naturally equivalent to the quantum predictive object.

Restoring division is not a defect. Hiding the restoration cost is.

### 9.4 Why absolute nondivision is usually vacuous

For a fixed finite experiment family, one can always encode enough of the past
or the complete response table into a sufficiently large positive variable.
Therefore the claim "no positive variable exists" is not meaningful without a
physical carrier and admissibility class. The scientific content lies in what
the proposed ontology contains, what resources cross the cut, and how the
whole law generates responses when no such carrier is admitted.

### 9.5 No free compression by deletion

A theory may not delete phase-complete or contextual information from the cut,
place it in an uncounted global evaluator, and claim compression. The resource
ledger must track displacement between:

$$
(R_{\rm cut},R_{\rm law},R_{\rm context},R_{\rm coordination},R_{\rm precision}).
$$

## 10. Resource and derived-input vector

For a scalable family, record at least

$$
\mathcal R_n
=
(R_{\rm state},R_{\rm cut},R_{\rm law},R_{\rm ctx},R_{\rm comm},
 R_{\rm comp},R_{\rm unif},R_{\rm prec},R_{\rm phys}).
$$

### 10.1 \(R_{\rm state}\)

Information or parameters required to specify the contingent physical state.
Idle ontic fibers are quotiented or reported separately.

### 10.2 \(R_{\rm cut}\)

Preparation information retained by a future-sufficient positive boundary.
The continuous cut theorem constrains this coordinate under its premises.

### 10.3 \(R_{\rm law}\)

Independent nomological information, including gate/process-specific tables,
couplings, nonuniform advice, and any target-to-model compilation map.

### 10.4 \(R_{\rm ctx}\)

Physical and evaluator context required to select the correct response law.
Unperformed future settings are never free context.

### 10.5 \(R_{\rm comm}\)

Physical coordination or communication across the matched operational cut.
This is distinct from the mathematical evaluator's access to a whole program.

### 10.6 \(R_{\rm comp}\)

Debt incurred when a model is recompiled for each composite rather than
generated by stable substitution laws.

### 10.7 \(R_{\rm unif}\)

Dependence of the rule schema on size, depth, dimension, or held-out instance.

### 10.8 \(R_{\rm prec}\)

Continuous precision and noise tolerance. One real coordinate is not one bit
of physical resource.

### 10.9 \(R_{\rm phys}\)

Energy, duration, volume, stability, preparation, and control cost. None of
the information-theoretic bounds alone determines this coordinate.

The vector is partially ordered; it is not collapsed into one aesthetic score.

## 11. Reference model classes

These are controls, not a menu from which the nicest winner may be chosen.

### C0 — terminal probability table

One table is supplied for each complete program. It can fit any finite data but
has no composition or held-out generativity. It fails G1--G5.

### C1 — Paper 01 whole-program representation

The quantum process and physical program are reversibly dilated; modulus-square
record probabilities are pushed forward. It is complete and positive but uses
the phase-complete quantum process as input. This is the accepted
representation-level baseline.

### C2 — Barandes endpoint family

A configuration carrier and first-order \(\Gamma(t\leftarrow t_0)\) are
supplied for division events. It demonstrates relative nondivision and admits a
unistochastic representation. Without a complete intervention extension, it
does not yet pass the target in Section 4.

### C3 — Hilbert-native generative dynamics

A fixed gate set, Hamiltonian family, action, or QFT law generates complete
processes and their probabilities. This is the standard-theory positive
control. It may pass uniformity while retaining noncommutative nomology.

### C4 — uniform positive indivisible generator

One configuration/history law generates all registered programs, denies a
compact positive division at selected cuts, and passes intervention,
composition, held-out, and scaling gates without target-process injection.
This is the desired constructive possibility, not a pre-awarded result.

### C5 — global constraint or retrocausal generator

A compact whole-boundary law generates histories but rejects forward prefix or
input independence. It is admissible only in the separately charged I5 branch.

### C6 — empirically modified law

A frozen principle forces a deviation from the quantum comparator. It enters an
empirical comparison only after its parameters and error law are independently
fixed.

## 12. Theorem-or-no-go programme

### Gate A — multi-time completeness

**Question.** Can the candidate's native stochastic objects determine every
registered adaptive intervention and post-event boundary, rather than only
unperturbed endpoint transitions?

**Positive product.** A natural translation from the native law to complete
finite process instruments, with inverse scope and gauge printed.

**Negative product.** Two processes with the same candidate native data but a
separating middle intervention.

### Gate B — uniform generativity

**Question.** Does one immutable law and declared primitive signature generate
the scalable family without \(W\)-injection, per-program tables, or post-holdout
changes?

**Positive product.** A single construction theorem, stable under physical
substitution, tensoring of independent parents, coarse-graining, and adaptive
control.

**Negative product.** A proof that the proposed input contains a recoverable
copy of the target process or requires nonuniform advice scaling with the
answer family.

### Gate C — information-displacement classification

**Question.** Where does the BHM response capacity live?

Possible certified branches are:

1. positive cut information \(\Omega(\sqrt n)\);
2. compact noncommutative boundary information \(O(\log n)\);
3. no future-sufficient cut, with capacity supplied by one uniform indivisible
   law and physically admissible temporal dependence;
4. measurement dependence, future-program compilation, or empirical failure.

This gate does not assume that branches 2 and 3 are ontologically different.
They may be naturally equivalent descriptions.

### Gate D — stochastic-native causal locality and Tsirelson test

**Question.** Does a configuration/transition-law principle, stated without
Hilbert tensor products, select the quantum CHSH bound rather than merely
re-express it?

The gate must:

1. define composite configuration spaces and local intervention ports;
2. distinguish no-signalling from absence of directed causal influence;
3. type the common preparation, free setting generators, and local records;
4. derive any factorization of local operations from the stochastic physical
   principle rather than postulate \(A_x\otimes B_y\) in Hilbert form;
5. admit the classical/Bell region and quantum/Tsirelson saturation controls;
6. reject or classify a PR-box control for an exact printed reason;
7. prove invariance under stochastic/Hilbert gauge changes; and
8. state whether the result is new physics, a representation theorem, or an
   independently motivated restriction on the law class.

If the proof requires the operator-norm Tsirelson theorem after translating to
Hilbert space, it may still be valid, but its maximum meaning is equivalence of
the proposed stochastic premises to quantum structure—not a stochastic-native
explanation.

### Gate E — scalable composition and limit

**Question.** Does the law remain one law as dimension, slot count, circuit
depth, and memory grow?

Required evidence includes explicit asymptotic resource bounds, stability under
neutral presentation changes, controlled approximation error, and refusal of a
finite-fixture conclusion.

### Gate F — reality-identification accounting

For every positive result, ask:

$$
\text{Did the admissible empirical class shrink, did an independent input
become derived, or was one representative merely rewritten?}
$$

Exact empirical equivalence earns ontology compatibility, not ontology truth.

Passing these is necessary, not sufficient, for a universal theory.
