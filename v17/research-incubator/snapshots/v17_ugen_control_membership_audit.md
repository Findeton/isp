## 4. Exact reconstruction of the fixed Hilbert record control

This is the most important neutrality test.

### 4.1 Finite positive history space

For a finite program $p$, let $\mathcal R_p$ be the finite set of all complete
retained bit/failure transcripts permitted by its syntax. Take

$$
\mathcal H_p=\mathcal R_p.
$$

This is a standard-Borel history space. It asserts actual completed records,
not an unmeasured microscopic configuration at every slot.

### 4.2 Complete transcript law

For transcript $r$, let $K_{p,r}$ be the product/controlled composition of the
fixed primitive Kraus operators on that branch. Define

$$
\Gamma_p(r)
=
\operatorname{Tr}
\bigl(K_{p,r}\rho_pK_{p,r}^{\dagger}\bigr).
$$

Completeness of every registered instrument gives

$$
\Gamma_p(r)\ge0,
\qquad
\sum_{r\in\mathcal R_p}\Gamma_p(r)=1.
$$

Because the fixed gate entries and primitive probabilities are computable
algebraic numbers, one finite evaluator approximates every $\Gamma_p(r)$ to
requested rational accuracy. Arbitrary runtime is allowed; no efficiency claim
is used.

### 4.3 Multi-time completeness

Every retained branch includes its normalized positive-support post-event
boundary. Appending a continuation multiplies/composes the appropriate fixed
instrument maps. Adaptive control is a physical controlled branch using an
earlier retained bit. Thus the same semantics determines immediate outputs,
post-event response, arbitrary registered continuations, discard, erasure, and
failure branches.

### 4.4 Prefix consistency

For $p\preceq q$, let

$$
\pi_{q,p}:\mathcal R_q\to\mathcal R_p
$$

truncate the later record fields. Summing all future instrument outcomes uses
trace preservation and gives

$$
(\pi_{q,p})_*\Gamma_q=\Gamma_p.
$$

This proves forward retained-record consistency. It does not assert a common
unmeasured trajectory across incompatible future contexts.

### 4.5 Structural descriptor

Take the descriptor to be the typed circuit/instrument expression itself.
Sequential, tensor, randomization, adaptive, and discard syntax compile by the
same fixed structural recursion. A local gate substitution changes one
subtree; it does not refit the law.

### 4.6 Classification

No arbitrary process tensor is supplied for each circuit, so the control is
uniform in the literal anti-advice sense. But the matrices for $H,T,T^\dagger$,
the tensor rule, complete-positive instrument calculus, and Born rule are fixed
nomological inputs.

Therefore:

## 5. Paper 01 placement

Paper 01 begins with an arbitrary finite causally ordered quantum process
$W$. Its whole-program dilation and Born pushforward construct a positive
record law with complete operational equivalence.

For each fixed Borel $W$, the result is a Borel-law packet. If $W$ has a finite
effective name, its probabilities may be evaluated relative to that name. But
the generic theorem ranges over arbitrary $W$ by receiving the phase-complete
process as independent input. It does not provide one fixed primitive law that

Consequently the generic Paper 01 result is correctly placed as:

```text
POSITIVE / COMPLETE / COMPOSITIONAL / TARGET-PROCESS-INJECTED.
```

Restricting $W$ to the output of the fixed gate grammar creates the Section 4
control. That is a useful specialization, not a retroactive derivation by
Paper 01.

## 6. Three distinct “table” mutants

The word “table” conceals three mathematically different cases.

### 6.1 Fresh table per program

For each program $p$, supply a new array $A_p$ as contingent data after $p$ is
known. This violates fixed-law and held-out stability. It is nonuniform advice.

### 6.2 One infinite real-coded table

Freeze a real $z$ whose digits encode every $A_p$ in the countable grammar.
This is one set-sized Borel law. It may even be extension-consistent. But the
entire answer family is independent nomological/oracle data. It is not an
effective finite compression and its physical access mechanism remains owed.

### 6.3 One finite algorithm

Freeze a finite program that computes $A_p$ from the typed program $p$. This
passes literal effective uniformity if it is total and satisfies the law
axioms. It cannot be rejected for resembling a calculator: a physical law is
also a compact rule that maps circumstances to probabilities.

The audit then asks which primitives the algorithm uses, whether it is
multi-time complete, whether its setting dependence is physical, whether it
preserves prefixes, and whether it predicts the quantum target. If those pass,
its explanatory and empirical status must be decided by input reduction and
selection—not taste.

This three-way split is essential. Without it, an anti-table rule either lets
in arbitrary advice or bans every compact mathematical law.

## 8. Global laws are not automatically retrocausal

Let one compact rule assign probabilities only after the complete physical
program is specified.

Two cases are operationally distinct:

1. for every independently chosen future extension, all already retained
   prefix-record marginals are unchanged; or
2. at least one future choice changes an earlier retained-record law.

Case 1 can pass forward record-prefix consistency even if its mathematics is
all-at-once. It may still owe a causal-port or physical implementation account,
but “global formula” does not prove backwards influence.

Case 2 fails the forward-autonomous stratum. It may remain a coherent
retrocausal, measurement-dependent, or two-boundary theory if the changed
premise and signalling constraints are explicit.

The class therefore tests operational dependence, not the order in which a
formula or computer program is evaluated.

## 9. Positive Markov and PR-box controls

### 9.1 Bell-local Markov control

A positive Markov model with free settings and Bell-local factorization can be
uniform, compositional, and prefix-consistent. It nevertheless fails the
registered quantum target on CHSH. The failed target is attributed to the
combined locality/free-setting premises, not to positivity alone.

Large contextual or nonlocal positive cut states are different classes and may
instead face Q-Cut information or coordination costs.

### 9.2 PR-box control

A PR box gives a finite uniform positive no-signalling law on the Bell
subgrammar. It exceeds the quantum/Tsirelson target, so it fails `Q`.

This proves that positivity and no-signalling are insufficient selectors. A
later causal-locality gate must establish, rather than assume, the additional
physical principle that excludes the PR box. Translating to Hilbert tensor
operators and invoking Tsirelson is valid at equivalence scope but does not by
itself show that positivity selected the bound.

## 10. Contextual histories and the repaired prefix rule

The first draft incorrectly required a Borel map

$$
\rho_{q,p}:\mathcal H_q\to\mathcal H_p
$$

for every future extension. That would force the same microscopic prefix
referent to exist across incompatible contexts. Paper 01 only guarantees
physical retained-record compatibility; a contextual ontology may use
different hidden fibers for different complete experiments.

The corrected class requires only

$$
\pi_{q,p}:\mathcal H_q\to\mathcal R_p
$$

and equality of retained-record marginals. A full $\rho_{q,p}$ is optional and
becomes a stronger `CONTEXT-STABLE-MICROHISTORY` coordinate.

This repair is scientifically necessary. It prevents the gate from selecting a
noncontextual ontology by definition while retaining an exact operational
no-backwards-signalling test.

## 11. Idle ontology is not always gauge

If a finite atomic history is tensored with an unobserved two-valued label, the
new history space generally has twice as many atoms and need not be Borel-
isomorphic in a measure-preserving way to the old one. The extra label can
therefore be a genuinely distinct ontology, not a presentation change.

It remains in the same registered empirical fiber when every reader ignores
it. No observation then selects its presence, and raw carrier size cannot count
as explanatory cost. The exact class now distinguishes:

- natural measure-preserving presentation/gauge equivalence; and
- empirically equivalent but ontologically different idle extensions.

fiber.

one finite total evaluator.

### D8 — malformed LaTeX control sequence

## 13. Remaining risks before a candidate pin

### R1 — exact adaptive-instrument category

The finite syntax is fixed at the generator level, but an official pin must
print the formal equality/congruence rules for adaptive control, failure, and
physical randomization, rather than rely on prose.

### R2 — Borel-code convention

A set-theory/probability reviewer must verify the chosen codes for Borel maps,
probability measures, and unique cylinder extension. Set-sizedness is secure in
outline; the exact presentation convention remains pin work.

### R3 — descriptor locality

The descriptor may faithfully contain the whole program. This is intentional:
U-Gen tests uniform law, not yet spacetime locality. A later causal-port gate
must not mistake syntactic composition for derived physical locality.

### R4 — derived-input predicate

“Independently derived” is not yet one Boolean set-membership condition. A
future pin needs a fixed dependency graph and admissible compositional
simulation preorder. Raw description length remains prohibited.

### R5 — closure theorem scope

The exact target is the finite gate grammar. Extension to arbitrary finite
instruments uses dilation and approximation results whose error accumulation
must be reconstructed. Continuous variables, indefinite causal order, and QFT
remain outside scope.

### R6 — no physical resource theorem

Information, code length, evaluator totality, energy, memory, duration, and
apparatus complexity are not interchangeable. Q-Cut supplies an information
coordinate only.

### R7 — no stochastic-native positive model

The class is nonempty because the Hilbert control passes. No complete
Barandes-native generator has been constructed. The desired branch remains a
question, not a result.

### R8 — no ontology discriminator

Even a compact positive generator could be exactly empirically equivalent to
quantum theory. Uniformity can earn explanatory organization or derived-input
gain; it cannot select actuality or ontology alone.
