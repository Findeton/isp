## 1. The four-level separation

### 1.1 Level R — physical record

A physical operational record is a value written into an exposed output factor by
a normalized instrument, retained at a typed boundary, and recoverable by later
registered readers. A stable record additionally survives every arrow in a frozen
nontrivial future subcategory. An objective record additionally has independent,
nondisturbing redundant readers under a declared tolerance.

A record answers:

> What value was physically retained?

It does not answer:

> How much time passed, in which direction, or along which worldline?

The private invariant-record theorem attacks exactly the failure that killed
Paper 04: a sector-controlled quantum pointer is not automatically a classical
record. It supplies a route to invariant commutative output without destroying
coherences inside one invariant fiber.

### 1.2 Level K — operational clock

An operational clock is a physical subsystem whose evolving phase can be read as
a sequence of records and calibrated against nontrivial changes in another
physical subsystem. It needs more than a record:

1. a clock carrier;
2. a family of distinguishable phase effects or states;
3. one fixed autonomous parent law transporting the phase;
4. a complete record instrument for the phase;
5. a nontrivial time-sensitive comparator;
6. a calibration protocol and held-out tests;
7. an explicit validity window, including stopping and recurrence;
8. resource, disturbance, and backreaction accounting; and
9. a proof that the predictor does not consume a hidden schedule parameter.

A clock answers, within its calibrated domain:

> Which phase of a physical process is this, and what changes correlate with that
> phase?

It does not by itself answer:

> Which intervention can influence which other intervention?

### 1.3 Level O — operational chronology

An operational chronology is a directed precedence relation derived from
interventions and complete readouts of one fixed law. It needs at least:

1. typed intervention opportunities;
2. complete separating readers;
3. nonzero signed response in the proposed forward direction;
4. an independent no-backwards or prefix-independence theorem;
5. direct-versus-mediated and common-cause controls;
6. co-onset bundling;
7. cycle/SCC classification; and
8. local-finiteness or continuum-order tests.

The order in which an experiment, proof, circuit, Hamiltonian simulation, or
category expression is written is never evidence for this relation.

A clock may parameterize an already supplied path while chronology remains wholly
supplied. Conversely, a partial operational precedence relation may exist before
any quantitative clock is calibrated.

### 1.4 Level M — proper time and metric

Proper time and metric reconstruction require still more structure:

1. multiple independently built clocks;
2. same-path cross-species calibration and held-out agreement;
3. signal propagation and radar protocols;
4. independently calibrated turnaround latency and limiting signal speed;
5. locality and a causal or operational order;
6. dimension and signature;
7. spatial as well as temporal interval reconstruction;
8. scale fixing and measure compatibility;
9. path dependence, redshift, and acceleration controls; and
10. agreement with independently reconstructed dynamics.

Even a successful two-clock comparison does not determine a spacetime metric.
Gravity additionally requires a dynamical geometry, universal matter coupling,
constraint closure, conservation, equivalence-principle tests, and held-out GR
limits.

## 2. Exact nonentailment results

### Proposition 2.1 — record does not entail clock

Fix any operational record instrument and every registered later reader. There
exist at least two physical completions with exactly the same record statistics:

1. a static completion in which the recorded carrier has no nontrivial phase
   transport; and
2. a completion tensored with an arbitrary independently evolving phase system.

Both reproduce the entire record experiment. They disagree on whether a clock
exists and, in the second case, on its rate and dynamics.

Therefore record formation, stability, and broadcasting do not select a clock.

### Proposition 2.2 — phase does not entail readable time

A subsystem may possess a covariant phase POVM or coherent orbit of phase states
without any valid retained instrument that writes its value. This is exactly the
kind of gap exposed by terminal Paper 04. Conversely, a frozen record may be
readable after its source phase has stopped evolving.

Hence phase transport and record formation are independent gates.

### Proposition 2.3 — finite periodic phase does not entail global order

Let a phase take values in a finite cycle Z_n and recur after n transports. Without
a separately retained winding variable, the same phase labels transports k and
k+n. No injective map from the entire trajectory into a linear time coordinate is
defined. Moreover an unlabeled cycle admits an orientation-reversing automorphism;
choosing one direction adds structure.

Thus a finite periodic clock supplies, at most, a local clock chart on a declared
validity window. It supplies neither a global clock nor an arrow of time.

### Proposition 2.4 — one clock does not fix a rate

For one clock record tau, every positive affine regraduation

$$
tau' = a tau + b, \qquad a>0,
$$

preserves its local ordering and can be absorbed into a changed calibration of
the comparator. A physical rate requires an independent standard or a frozen
dynamical law plus held-out comparison. An absolute unit remains unselected until
an operational or dynamical scale is supplied.

### Proposition 2.5 — correlation does not entail chronology

Consider a reversible cyclic carrier k and a comparator m with a symmetric
initial ensemble concentrated on m=k. The forward law k maps to k+1 and the
reversed law k maps to k-1 have the same un-oriented equal-phase correlations
after relabeling. Reading the correlation alone cannot choose which response
direction is physical.

Direction may be present in a law, preparation, entropy-producing apparatus, or
intervention response, but it is not created by the word "clock".

### Proposition 2.6 — two clocks do not entail metric

Two calibrated clock records along one or more supplied paths determine only the
registered clock relation. They supply no spatial distance, signal cone,
dimension, signature, or localization map. Infinitely many geometric models, and
nongeometric models, can reproduce the same finite clock table.

Therefore clock agreement is necessary evidence for proper-time reconstruction
but never sufficient evidence for a metric.

### Proposition 2.7 — operational clock does not select ontology

The same complete operational clock instrument can have inequivalent microscopic
dilations and native stochastic lifts. Stinespring equivalence at the channel
level and endpoint-law equivalence at the operational transcript level do not
select a configuration space, an actual trajectory, or a non-Markovian realizer.

The native and operational clock coordinates must remain separate.

## 4. Clock-construction gates

The following gates are conjunctive. Failure of one does not erase earlier
positive coordinates.

### K0 — accepted record type

Every clock outcome is written by a normalized instrument into a boundary whose
classical center or noncommutative memory type is explicit. The symmetry action
must preserve that type. Paper 04 failed here for clock A.

### K1 — nontrivial phase carrier

At least three distinguishable phase regions occur with positive support. A
binary state-change flag is not yet a clock; two points always permit an affine
fit and cannot test rate stability.

### K2 — fixed autonomous phase transport

One parent law transports the clock through the declared window without an
externally switched sequence of gates. Initial preparation and any program or
reference state are physical resources and remain exposed.

Autonomy is relative: a time-independent laboratory Hamiltonian can still use a
background parameter. Eliminating that parameter from operational predictions is
a later gate.

### K3 — time-sensitive comparator

The same parent contains another subsystem with at least one reader whose law
changes nontrivially across clock phase. A random pointer, copied program counter,
or inert spectator fails. The no-clock and mistuned-clock controls must separate
from the target.

### K4 — complete clock-relative law

Conditioning on the physical clock record yields complete comparator
probabilities, posteriors, retained memories, guards, and later readers. Scalar
reading tables or state vectors alone are insufficient.

### K5 — overidentified calibration

Freeze the calibration family before testing. Estimate its parameters on a
training subset and test at least two nontrivial held-out phase regions plus one
sequential task. If the family has p fitted parameters, the held-out observable
family must contain more than p independent constraints.

### K6 — external-parameter dependency audit

The parent predictor must not consume the laboratory schedule, circuit depth,
orbit index, or cached lookup table. A hidden-parameter model reproducing the
same printed table is an explicit attack. Passing this gate establishes only
operational redundancy on the registered domain, not metaphysical timelessness.

### K7 — finite-resource and failure classification

Print resolution, accuracy, recurrence, stopping probability, initialization,
energy/asymmetry/entropy cost, disturbance, and clock--system backreaction. A
finite clock may be approximate; an exact ideal-clock claim at finite cost is
refused.

### K8 — second independent clock

Construct a second physical clock from the same parent. It must not be a copied
record, relabeling, or fitted decoder. Compare complete instruments and dynamics,
not just scalar readings. Clock-choice-dependent dynamics and time-nonlocal
conditional evolution are allowed outcomes rather than automatic failures.

### K9 — frame change on complete physics

Where a change of clock is claimed, transform states, observables, instruments,
record labels, controllers, and null sets. State-level or reading-only equivalence
cannot earn complete frame equivalence.

### K10 — nonvacuous reparameterization covariance

Execute at least one nonidentity orientation-preserving reparameterization with
full measure, POVM, record, calibration, and reader pushforward. Identity-only
registration is vacuous. Orientation-reversing transformations are separate
chronology tests, not ordinary clock regraduations.

### K11 — native lift or explicit operational ceiling

Either construct one fixed native endpoint law whose complete transcript
pushforwards reproduce all clock arms, or state that the result is operational
quantum mechanics on a supplied comparator only. No Hilbert-space clock may be
called the Barandes clock without this lift.

### K12 — chronology firewall

Print the source of every ordering relation used. No clock result may earn an
operational-precedence edge unless the independent intervention-response and
no-backwards gates are executed.

## 6. Primary-source constraints

The following primary results constrain the future clock gate without selecting
ISP ontology.

1. [Höhn, Smith, and Lock](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.00033) establish an
   equivalence among relational-observable, Page--Wootters, and reduced
   descriptions under exact constraint, physical-inner-product, POVM, and clock
   domain hypotheses. This supports complete frame transformations; it does not
   select a clock or ontology.
2. [Smith and Ahmadi](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.00081) show that clock--system
   interactions can produce time-nonlocal conditional evolution. Hence
   interactions and source-dependent clock dynamics cannot be removed merely to
   make a scalar clock relation pass.
3. [Woods, Silva, and Oppenheim](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.04591) construct
   finite quasi-ideal clocks and autonomous control with explicit error and
   resource scaling. Finite clocks must carry validity and approximation ledgers.
4. [Erker et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.06704) derive an accuracy--resolution
   versus dissipated-heat tradeoff for their autonomous thermal-clock model.
   This motivates resource and entropy controls, not a universal thermodynamic
   arrow theorem.
5. [Brown and Kuchař](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9409001) show how physical dust
   can provide reference coordinates and a time variable in canonical gravity.
   The dust is added reference matter and introduces a privileged material frame;
   it is not a cost-free emergence of time.
6. [Dittrich](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0507106) develops complete observables
   relationally from constrained systems. This supports gauge-invariant
   correlations but does not select which physical degree of freedom nature uses
   as a clock.
7. [Barandes](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.21192v1) defines an indivisible process
   through configuration space, contingent distributions, and sparse first-order
   endpoint laws at admitted division events. His framework leaves many
   compatible non-Markovian realizers and uses target/conditioning time labels;
   it does not itself derive physical time from records.
