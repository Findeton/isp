# PRIVATE PHYSICS SELECTION — parent architecture for the operational-clock gate

Date: 2026-08-23

Status: **PRIVATE / OFF-TREE / NO MODEL PIN / NO UNIT OPENED**

Scientific result awarded: **none**

This note compares established physical clock architectures against the frozen
record-to-clock gates K0--K12. It does not choose parameters, dimension, hardware,
geometry, or a desired outcome. Its job is to identify the least assumptive parent
family capable of asking the Phase-T question without repeating Paper 04's type
error or encoding the answer in an external schedule.

## 1. Selection criteria

A viable parent architecture must support all of the following in principle.

1. A valid invariant record instrument, not a bare phase projector.
2. A nontrivial finite-resource phase carrier.
3. One fixed autonomous joint law.
4. A complete comparator law, including retained memories and later readers.
5. Interactions and backreaction rather than a decoupled ideal-clock fiction.
6. A stopped/recurrent/failure sector.
7. Overidentified calibration and held-out predictions.
8. At least one independent second clock for the strongest rung.
9. Complete frame changes, not scalar relabelings.
10. A dependency audit for hidden laboratory time.
11. A precise operational ceiling if no native stochastic lift is constructed.
12. No imported causal order, metric, or gravity disguised as a clock property.

The architecture is disfavored if its positive result follows by construction
from the same representation used to generate its comparator. A useful model
must expose at least one independent route or held-out observable capable of
failing.

## 2. Candidate architecture A — constrained relational quantum dynamics

### Core

Use a stationary constrained parent

$$
\widehat C|\Psi\rangle=0,
$$

with a physical clock subsystem, covariant clock POVM, physical inner product,
relational observables, and complete measurements. Conditional dynamics are
recovered from correlations with clock readings rather than a runtime label.

### Strengths

- Directly targets external-parameter redundancy.
- Supports clock-neutral, reduced, and Page--Wootters descriptions under exact
  hypotheses.
- Naturally exposes multiple-clock and frame-change questions.
- Can incorporate nonideal clocks and interactions.
- Is close to the mathematical structure needed later in constrained gravity.

### Risks

- A constraint plus a chosen factorization can encode the laboratory dynamics by
  construction.
- A covariant effect is not yet a retained record instrument.
- The clock and comparator may be co-designed, weakening evidential force.
- The constraint's gauge parameter can be mistaken for derived time.
- Different finite-clock measurement constructions can be operationally
  inequivalent.
- No native configuration or actual history is selected.

### Disposition

**Necessary kinematic route, insufficient as the only route.** It is the best
clock-neutral representation but needs an independently constructed autonomous
realization and complete transcript equivalence.

## 3. Candidate architecture B — autonomous finite quantum clock

### Core

Use a finite clock with a fixed time-independent Hamiltonian that autonomously
controls a system interaction. The clock is initialized once and subsequently
evolves, triggers the target interaction, and suffers calculable disturbance.

### Strengths

- Makes controller autonomy, finite resources, error, recurrence, and
  backreaction explicit.
- Can be tied to a laboratory implementation.
- Rejects external gate switching as the physical mechanism.
- Provides quantitative failure bounds rather than ideal-clock prose.

### Risks

- Its Hamiltonian evolution is still described against an external parameter.
- A program wavepacket can be mistaken for an emergent temporal order.
- The implemented task may be hard-coded into a potential.
- A single successful unitary does not establish general clock-relative law.
- It does not itself provide a constrained, clock-neutral description.

### Disposition

**Necessary dynamical route, insufficient as the only route.** It is the best
independent check that the relational model is not merely a static correlation
table.

## 4. Candidate architecture C — autonomous thermal tick clock

### Core

Use an out-of-equilibrium device powered by physical reservoirs. Its ticks are
records of an irreversible process, with accuracy and resolution tied to energy
and entropy production.

### Strengths

- Produces autonomous repeated records.
- Exposes thermodynamic cost and a physical direction.
- Has a clear laboratory interpretation.
- Supplies strong controls against a cost-free ideal clock.

### Risks

- It imports reservoirs, nonequilibrium boundary conditions, and a thermodynamic
  arrow rather than deriving chronology.
- The tick process is not automatically a quantum temporal reference frame.
- It is a poor test of reversible clock-frame covariance.
- Its direction may be contingent environmental asymmetry, not fundamental time.

### Disposition

**Mandatory control, not the base parent.** It tests how an operational arrow and
record cost enter when explicitly supplied.

## 5. Candidate architecture D — material reference fields in gravity

### Core

Use dust or scalar reference matter to deparametrize gravitational constraints and
define relational observables with respect to physical material coordinates.

### Strengths

- Directly couples reference systems to dynamical geometry.
- Makes reference-matter cost and backreaction physical.
- Is a genuine bridge to canonical gravity.

### Risks

- It imports GR, spacetime dimension, metric variables, and reference matter
  before Phase T has passed.
- The material frame can introduce a preferred foliation.
- It cannot test whether v17 derives the gravitational arena.
- It would confuse a later comparator with present ontology.

### Disposition

**Deferred gravity control.** It belongs after an operational clock and chronology
exist, not as the next parent.

## 6. Candidate architecture E — native indivisible stochastic clock

### Core

Choose a native configuration subsystem with a phase variable, first-order
endpoint laws at admitted division events, physical record maps, and complete
clock experiments. Require its pushforward to reproduce the operational quantum
clock law.

### Strengths

- Directly addresses the intended realist ontology.
- Separates actual configurations from Hilbert representation.
- Allows indivisible endpoint dynamics without an assumed Markov path.
- Could expose empirically distinct native extensions.

### Risks

- No v17 native configuration or endpoint law is currently selected.
- Barandes's existing formalism uses target and conditioning time labels; it does
  not derive them.
- Choosing a clock variable now could insert the desired time by hand.
- A unique path realizer is not part of the minimal theory.
- Operational equivalence alone cannot select the lift.

### Disposition

**Required eventual lift, not yet a selectable base.** A native clock construction
must follow, or be jointly frozen with, an operational parent only when the native
configuration and law are independently motivated.

## 7. Candidate architecture F — ordinary atomic/Ramsey clock experiment

### Core

Use an actual two-level or multilevel oscillator, Ramsey interrogation, retained
readout, and comparison against an independently calibrated reference.

### Strengths

- Strong empirical contact and mature error models.
- Direct tests of phase readability, stability, calibration, and disturbance.
- Can implement record and reference-resource controls.

### Risks

- Laboratory scheduling and spacetime are supplied.
- The external oscillator/reference already defines operational time.
- It cannot demonstrate background-free time or ontology.
- Standard agreement mainly validates quantum instrumentation.

### Disposition

**Empirical calibration layer.** It can test the record and finite-clock
subsystems, but not the clock-neutral claim alone.

## 8. Candidate architecture G — ideal continuous clock

### Core

Assume an exact self-adjoint time operator, perfectly distinguishable time states,
unbounded support, and exact translation.

### Disposition

**Refused as a parent.** It hides infinite resources, evades recurrence, and
cannot supply exact point records in a generic nonatomic operational algebra.
It may appear only as an approximation target with a frozen convergence theorem.

## 9. Candidate architecture H — bespoke finite cyclic clock

### Core

Choose a small cyclic group, tailor a constraint and comparator, and prove exact
finite correlations.

### Strengths

- Fully enumerable and useful for counterexamples.
- Can test typing, recurrence, and finite-resource logic.

### Risks

- Parameters and factorization are easily selected to fit the target.
- Periodicity obstructs global time.
- Finite algebra can be mistaken for discrete ontology.
- Paper 04 already showed that exact arithmetic does not guarantee a physical
  clock packet.

### Disposition

**Regression fixture only.** No new bespoke finite group should be the primary
physics evidence.

## 10. Selected architecture family — a dual-route operational parent

The least assumptive live route is not one of A or B alone. It is a paired
construction in which the same complete finite experiment is obtained by two
independently checkable routes:

1. a clock-neutral constrained relational representation; and
2. a finite autonomous laboratory dilation with one fixed Hamiltonian.

Call them

$$
\mathfrak P_{\rm con}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\mathfrak P_{\rm aut}.
$$

They must share a separately frozen physical interface but may not be derived by
copying one route's output table into the other. Their complete registered
transcript laws must satisfy

$$
(T_e)_*\,\mathbb P_{\rm con,e}
=
(T_e)_*\,\mathbb P_{\rm aut,e}
=
\mathbb P_{\rm lab,e}
$$

for every registered experiment e and every complete reader, within a frozen
finite-resource error bound. Equality of a few marginal clock tables is
insufficient.

The first equality tests whether the clock-neutral representation has an actual
autonomous mechanism. The second tests whether that mechanism reproduces the
ordinary laboratory comparator without consuming an external schedule as input.

This paired architecture is a selection of a question, not a selection of a
result. It can fail because:

- no valid invariant record instrument exists;
- nonideal measurement semantics disagree;
- the autonomous dilation cannot reproduce complete adaptive transcripts;
- interactions destroy the proposed clock domain;
- the second clock has no common valid sector;
- hidden external time remains in the predictor; or
- only a co-designed tautology can satisfy the equalities.

Any one is a useful Phase-T no-go.

## 11. Mandatory physical structure of the selected family

### 11.1 Shared interface frozen before models

Freeze only operational types before selecting model details:

- two candidate clock carriers C_A and C_B;
- one comparator S;
- invariant clock-readout memories R_A and R_B;
- controller memory and failure flags;
- complete preparations, interventions, and readers;
- calibration and held-out partitions;
- allowed error metrics;
- stopped and recurrent outcomes; and
- resource observables.

Do not freeze a desired clock relation, dimension, spectrum, or geometry.

### 11.2 Finite, nonideal clocks

Both clocks must have finite resources and explicitly nonorthogonal or finite-
resolution phase effects unless a finite exact model is physically justified.
Ideal continuous time states may be used only as a registered limiting
comparator.

### 11.3 Invariant records

The recorded output must be invariant under the declared joint redundancy or
relational to an explicit physical reference. Raw clock phase may remain a
quantum memory; its final classical record must satisfy the center-preservation
gate.

### 11.4 Physical measurements

The twirled-observable and purified-measurement constructions are not assumed
equivalent for nonideal clocks. The model must specify which physical apparatus
is implemented. The alternative is a control, not an algebraic rewrite.

### 11.5 Interactions retained

Clock--system and clock--clock couplings remain in the parent. If they induce
source-dependent or time-nonlocal dynamics, that is an outcome. A simple
Schrodinger form may not be restored by deleting the interaction.

### 11.6 Autonomous route

The autonomous dilation must use one fixed Hamiltonian or endpoint law after a
single exposed initialization. The program/reference state, energy, dimension,
entropy, and clock disturbance are recorded. Externally selected gate times are
not part of the mechanism.

### 11.7 Overidentified observations

At minimum:

1. train the calibration on a proper subset of clock phases;
2. test multiple independent held-out comparator effects;
3. execute one adaptive record-controlled continuation;
4. compare a second clock on the same physical path;
5. test a complete coherent reader beyond scalar clock statistics; and
6. run stopped, recurrent, no-clock, mistuned-clock, and interaction-deletion
   controls.

### 11.8 No native promotion

If no native endpoint packet is constructed, the maximum result is an operational
quantum clock architecture. The model does not become Barandes ontology through
the phrase "indivisible measurement."

## 12. Why this route is better than repairing Paper 04

Paper 04 selected a bespoke Z_7 model first and then tried to make both of its
phase coordinates into complete clocks. That invited three linked problems:

1. the clock packets were co-designed with the comparator;
2. one raw pointer required a sector-dependent action incompatible with its
   classical record type; and
3. exact finite arithmetic was mistaken for complete physical equivalence.

The selected family reverses the order:

1. freeze an invariant record interface and falsifiable transcript tasks;
2. select established finite-clock families by those tasks;
3. construct constrained and autonomous routes independently;
4. compare complete transcript laws; and
5. only then ask about external-parameter redundancy.

No fixed group, prime, polynomial, clock spectrum, or desired affine map is
chosen at this stage.

## 14. Next preparatory mathematics

Before any model pin, derive or refute these generic statements.

1. **Invariant-record dilation theorem.** Conditions for a finite covariant clock
   POVM to admit an energy-/constraint-compatible retained relational record.
2. **Dual-route equivalence criterion.** Necessary and sufficient conditions for
   a constrained relational instrument and autonomous Hamiltonian dilation to
   induce the same complete endpoint transcript law.
3. **Nonideal-measurement fork theorem.** Exact condition under which twirled and
   purified measurement routes agree; otherwise print their operational
   discriminator.
4. **Hidden-time nonidentifiability theorem.** Characterize when a clock-neutral
   transcript has an observationally equivalent hidden-schedule completion.
5. **Two-clock independence criterion.** Rule out copied records, relabelings, and
   common-controller pseudoindependence.
6. **Local-clock validity theorem.** Bound recurrence, stopping, resolution,
   disturbance, and calibration error on a declared window.
7. **Native-lift commuting square.** State the exact endpoint pushforward a future
   Barandes-compatible law must satisfy without assuming a path measure.

These are theorem/no-go questions. They should be settled before choosing a
finite model or implementation language.
