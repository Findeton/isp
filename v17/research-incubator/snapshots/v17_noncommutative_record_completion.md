# PRIVATE THEOREM SUPPLEMENT — noncommutative completion of a controlled record

Date: 2026-08-23

Status: **PRIVATE / RESULT-NEUTRAL / NO REPOSITORY UNIT**

This supplement develops the noncommutative/groupoid branch left open by the
invariant-record theorem package. It answers a physically important question:

> If a sector-controlled transformation fails to preserve a proposed classical
> record algebra, what is the smallest represented algebra that actually contains
> both descriptions, and what classical information survives in its center?

The answer is exact in the finite permutation domain. It does not select this
completion as fundamental ontology.

## 1. Setup

Let $S$ and $R$ be finite sets and

$$
\mathcal H=\mathbb C^S\otimes\mathbb C^R.
$$

Write $e_{ab}=|a\rangle\langle b|$ on the coherent controller and let

$$
\mathcal D_R=\operatorname{span}\{|r\rangle\langle r|:r\in R\}.
$$

The proposed hybrid algebra is

$$
\mathcal A=M_{|S|}(\mathbb C)\otimes\mathcal D_R.
$$

For each controller value $a$, let $V_a$ implement a permutation $\sigma_a$ of
$R$, and set

$$
W=\sum_{a\in S}e_{aa}\otimes V_a.
$$

When the permutations differ, $W\mathcal A W^*$ is generally not $\mathcal A$.
Define the minimal represented common algebra

$$
\mathcal B_{\rm rel}=C^*(\mathcal A,W\mathcal A W^*).
$$

This closure asks only for one algebra containing the declared observables before
and after the controlled transformation. It does not automatically promote $W$
itself to an observable.

Let

$$
K=\langle\sigma_a\sigma_b^{-1}:a,b\in S\rangle
\le\operatorname{Sym}(R)
$$

be the relative-permutation group.

## 2. Minimal-completion theorem

### Theorem 2.1

In the represented finite domain,

$$
\mathcal B_{\rm rel}
=M_{|S|}(\mathbb C)\otimes
C^*(\mathcal D_R,\{V_k:k\in K\}).
$$

### Proof

For every $a,b$,

$$
W(e_{ab}\otimes I)W^*
=e_{ab}\otimes V_aV_b^*.
$$

Multiplying by $e_{ba}\otimes I$ gives

$$
e_{bb}\otimes V_aV_b^*.
$$

The full matrix algebra on the controller transports this block to every diagonal
controller block, and summing those blocks yields

$$
I_S\otimes V_aV_b^*\in\mathcal B_{\rm rel}.
$$

Thus every relative permutation, the record diagonal algebra, and the full
controller matrix algebra lie in $\mathcal B_{\rm rel}$, proving inclusion of the
right-hand side.

Conversely, for $d\in\mathcal D_R$,

$$
W(e_{ab}\otimes d)W^*
=e_{ab}\otimes V_a dV_b^*
=e_{ab}\otimes(V_a dV_a^*)(V_aV_b^*).
$$

The first record factor is diagonal and the second lies in $K$. Therefore every
generator of $W\mathcal A W^*$ belongs to the right-hand side. QED.

### Corollary 2.2 — registered transformation family

For a finite family

$$
W_\lambda=\sum_a e_{aa}\otimes V_{\lambda,a},
$$

the same proof gives

$$
C^*(\mathcal A,\{W_\lambda\mathcal A W_\lambda^*\}_\lambda)
=M_{|S|}\otimes C^*(\mathcal D_R,V(K_{\rm all})),
$$

where $K_{\rm all}$ is generated by every relative permutation
$V_{\lambda,a}V_{\lambda,b}^*$. Thus a stable record claim must use the whole
licensed transformation family, not whichever generator leaves the preferred
pointer most classical.

## 3. Orbit-block theorem

Let $R/K$ denote the set of $K$-orbits in $R$.

### Theorem 3.1

The represented record completion is

$$
C^*(\mathcal D_R,V(K))
\cong
\bigoplus_{O\in R/K}M_{|O|}(\mathbb C).
$$

### Proof

The diagonal projections show that every orbit subspace $\ell^2(O)$ is reducing,
and no generated operator connects distinct orbits. If $r,s\in O$, choose
$k\in K$ with $k(s)=r$. Then

$$
|r\rangle\langle r|V_k|s\rangle\langle s|
=|r\rangle\langle s|.
$$

Hence all matrix units on $\ell^2(O)$ are generated. QED.

### Corollary 3.2 — surviving classical center

The center of the common algebra is

$$
Z(\mathcal B_{\rm rel})
=I_S\otimes
\operatorname{span}\{P_O:O\in R/K\},
$$

where $P_O$ projects onto an orbit. Thus the only pre-existing raw-record
information that remains classical in the minimal common algebra is the orbit
label. If $K$ acts transitively, the center is scalar and no nontrivial raw record
survives as classical.

## 4. Physical interpretation

The closure does not destroy information. It changes its type.

- Off-diagonal matrix units inside a $K$-orbit become necessary observables.
- A raw basis label within one orbit is therefore a quantum degree of freedom,
  not a central classical fact.
- Orbit labels remain central because no admitted relative permutation connects
  distinct orbits.
- A classical record may reappear only after a further instrument creates a new
  commutative output or after physical superselection/decoherence removes the
  offending coherences.

This is the precise sense in which a coherently controlled pointer is a quantum
memory rather than a classical record.

## 5. Do not confuse three closures

### 5.1 Common represented observable closure

The algebra $\mathcal B_{\rm rel}=C^*(\mathcal A,W\mathcal A W^*)$ uses only
relative permutations $V_aV_b^*$. It is the correct object for asking whether one
represented observable algebra accommodates both descriptions.

### 5.2 Algebra generated by the implementer

If one additionally declares $W$ itself an observable and forms

$$
C^*(\mathcal A,W),
$$

the record group is generally the larger

$$
L=\langle\sigma_a:a\in S\rangle.
$$

This can shrink the center further. A symmetry implementer or dynamical unitary is
not automatically an observable, so this stronger closure must never be inserted
silently.

### 5.3 Abstract action-groupoid algebra

The full transformation-groupoid/crossed-product algebra for $K\curvearrowright
R$ can retain stabilizer representations not present in the permutation
representation on $\ell^2(R)$. For a transitive orbit with stabilizer $H$, the
abstract finite groupoid algebra is Morita-equivalent to $C^*(H)$, whereas the
represented algebra above is only $M_{|O|}(\mathbb C)$.

Therefore an abstract groupoid is a useful bookkeeping and extension candidate,
but its stabilizer sectors are not physical degrees of freedom until a complete
representation, preparation, intervention, and reader interface makes them so.

## 6. Relation to an invariant output instrument

The invariant-record construction does something different from merely taking the
center of $\mathcal B_{\rm rel}$.

Let the full physical basis be $X$ and let $f:X\to Y$ be an invariant operational
question. Its projections $E_y$ may depend jointly on the coherent controller and
the former record variables. They need not be central in the input algebra.

A complete sharp instrument creates the output hybrid algebra

$$
\mathcal A_f^{\rm out}
=\bigoplus_{y\in Y}\mathcal B(E_y\mathcal H),
$$

whose center is generated by the new output labels:

$$
Z(\mathcal A_f^{\rm out})
=\operatorname{span}\{E_y:y\in Y\}.
$$

Within each block, every admitted coherence remains quantum. Between different
blocks, the nonselective classical output has removed coherence. Thus the
instrument creates a new operational classical record variable at a declared
coarse graining; it does not establish one actual outcome and does not discover
that the same raw coordinate was classical all along.

## 7. Application to the exposed Paper 04 record

The rejected A-pointer transformed, at fixed group element $g$, as

$$
\alpha\mapsto\alpha+(1+q)g
\quad(\bmod7).
$$

On the coherently admitted $q=0$ and $q=1$ sectors, the relative permutation is a
nonzero unit translation of $\mathbb Z_7$. Such a translation generates the full
cyclic action, which is transitive. Therefore

$$
C^*(\mathcal D_A,V(K))=M_7(\mathbb C)
$$

and the raw A-record center becomes scalar in the minimal common represented
algebra. The A label is a quantum memory coordinate, not a classical reading, as
long as the q coherence is retained.

For the joint raw pair $(\alpha,\beta)$, the q-relative transformation shifts
$\alpha$ while leaving $\beta$ fixed. Hence the relative orbits are the seven
$\alpha$ values at each fixed $\beta$, and

$$
C^*(\mathcal D_{A\times B},V(K))
\cong M_7(\mathbb C)\otimes\mathcal D_B.
$$

The B label remains central under the relative completion. This reproduces, at
the algebraic level, why the global B record survived while the controlled A
record failed.

The invariant

$$
y=2\alpha-(1+q)\beta
$$

is not a pre-existing central element of the original full-controller hybrid
algebra because it depends on q. It is instead a sharp joint observable that can
be converted by a complete instrument into a new classical output while
preserving q coherence inside each y fiber. This distinction is mandatory.

## 8. Four resolution features, now sharpened

1. **Invariant output:** construct $\mathcal A_f^{\rm out}$; preserve within-fiber
   coherence; accept that the operational question $f$ is extra and nonunique.
2. **Classicalized controller:** replace $M_{|S|}$ by a diagonal controller
   algebra; the joint $(a,r)$ skew-product action is classical, but the a
   coherence has been physically removed.
3. **Noncommutative completion:** retain coherence and use
   $\mathcal B_{\rm rel}$; only orbit labels are classical, and within-orbit raw
   values are quantum memory.
4. **Reference completion:** add an explicit asymmetry/reference system and record
   an invariant of the enlarged action; count the reference as a physical
   resource.

No theorem in this package selects one as the ontology of nature.
The four lines are not mutually exclusive. In particular, a reference completion
normally produces an invariant observable on the enlarged system, and a
noncommutative memory may precede a later invariant classical output.

## 10. Ontology and gravity boundary

The represented completion identifies the algebra needed for coherent operational
consistency. It does not tell us:

- which algebraic elements are beables;
- whether one configuration is actual;
- whether the group action is gauge, physical symmetry, or laboratory control;
- which invariant question nature records;
- whether a record is an admitted Barandes conditioning division or a strong
  screening boundary under a selected realizer;
- whether an action groupoid is fundamental;
- whether order, locality, spacetime, or gravity emerges.

It also does not imply that a within-orbit native configuration value is unreal.
The statement that a raw label is noncentral in this represented operational
algebra means only that it is not a classical output of the declared complete
boundary while the admitted coherence is retained. A Barandes-style definite
native variable would require a separately declared configuration (or optional
trajectory) function and a complete endpoint-experiment observation dictionary.
This package neither constructs nor refutes such a variable and does not select a
multi-time realizer.
