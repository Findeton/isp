## 1. Typed laboratory grammar

Use three types:

$$
I,\qquad Q,\qquad B,
$$

where $Q$ is one two-outcome laboratory system and $B$ is one retained
classical bit.

Primitive procedures are:

1. $\mathtt{prep}_j:I\to Q$ for $j\in\{0,1\}$;
2. $b:Q\to Q$;
3. $m:Q\to Q\otimes B$, a nondestructive configuration reader; and
4. $r:Q\to B$, a final destructive configuration reader.

The label $b$ means only “balanced positive endpoint.” The base packet does not
identify it with a Hilbert-space Hadamard operator; that identification belongs
to one possible completion and is exactly what the comparison tests.

A program is a finite typed word generated by these procedures. Each $m$
creates a retained physical record and a division boundary. A boundary between
two unmeasured $b$ operations is part of supplied laboratory syntax but is not
declared a physical record or division event.

Two programs are syntax-identical only under typed wire/record-preserving word
isomorphism. Equal outcome probabilities do not identify procedures.

## 3. Primitive endpoint laws

### 3.1 Unmeasured primitive

The isolated $b$ procedure has transition matrix

$$
G
=
\Gamma_b
=
\frac12
\begin{pmatrix}
1&1\\
1&1
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Thus, for either input configuration, a final configuration read immediately
after one $b$ is uniform.

### 3.2 Physical measurement/division

The nondestructive reader has kernel

$$
P_m(i',b\mid i)
=
\delta_{i'i}\delta_{bi}.
$$

The retained bit $b$ is stable for the remainder of the program grammar. The
event $m$ is a declared division: after conditioning on its positive-support
record, the next unmeasured block begins from the recorded configuration.

The final reader has

$$
P_r(b\mid i)=\delta_{bi}.
$$

### 3.3 What the base embedding omits

$\iota_G$ contains no value for:

- an unmeasured word $b^m$ with $m\ge2$;
- a stochastic kernel at an unrecorded intermediate $b$ boundary;
- a phase or Hilbert lift;
- a whole-program response table;
- a per-depth advice sequence; or
- an actuality law beyond the ordinary statement that a retained record occurs
  in a run.

Those omissions are underdetermination, not uniform distributions.

## 4. Complete extension of the base embedding

A complete extension supplies one fixed rule

$$
m\longmapsto\Gamma_m
$$

for every unmeasured block $b^m$, satisfying

$$
\Gamma_0=I_2,
\qquad
\Gamma_1=G,
$$

with every $\Gamma_m$ column stochastic.

For a program

$$
\mathtt{prep}_j;
b^{m_1};m;
b^{m_2};m;
\cdots;
b^{m_k};r,
$$

the complete retained-record law is generated by applying $\Gamma_{m_1}$,
recording/dividing at $m$, applying $\Gamma_{m_2}$ from the recorded
configuration, and so on. Blocks of length zero are allowed.

This rule supplies:

1. every complete record distribution;
2. every positive-support conditional after $m$;
3. every continuation in the grammar;
4. prefix consistency for already retained bits; and
5. explicit null branches.

It does not assign a joint trajectory distribution inside an unmeasured block.
Null transcript branches remain present with probability zero, but no
conditional continuation from a zero-support record is licensed or filled by
convention.

## 5. Uniformity contract

A completion is uniform only if one finite evaluator takes $m$ and returns the
entries of $\Gamma_m$ to arbitrary rational accuracy. The evaluator freezes
before any held-out block length.

Forbidden inputs are:

1. a fresh $\Gamma_m$ array for each $m$;
2. an infinite real whose digits are read as the answer sequence;
3. the standard quantum answer for the complete word supplied as contingent
   state; and
4. changing the rule after the $bb$ separator is inspected.

No runtime efficiency or short-code ontology claim is made.

## 7. Registered separators and controls

### S1 — unmeasured $bb$

Prepare $0$, apply $bb$, and read:

$$
\Gamma^Q_2=I_2,
\qquad
\Gamma^M_2=G,
\qquad
\Gamma^V_2=X.
$$

The $Q/M$ and $V/M$ total-variation distances are $1/2$ and the $Q/V$
distance is $1$.

### S2 — measured middle

Prepare $0$, apply $b;m;b$, and read. All three laws give a uniform final bit.
The retained middle bit is also uniform. This is the positive division control.

### S3 — longer held-out blocks

Freeze evaluation at the block lengths

$$
3,4,6,7,8,10
$$

without changing any rule. These lengths test that the result is generated by
one unitary-power/evaluator rule rather than a depth-two patch. At lengths
$6$ and $10$, $Q$ returns $I_2$ while $V$ returns $X$; at lengths $4$ and $8$
both return $I_2$.

### S4 — relabeling

Conjugate every configuration transition and reader by the same permutation of
$\{0,1\}$. All classification and separator magnitudes must remain unchanged.

### S5 — constant-reader mutant

A reader that always outputs $0$ hides S1. It is not measure determining and
cannot support an operational-equivalence claim.

## 8. Division classifications

At the unmeasured cut between the two $b$ operations,

$$
G^2=G.
$$

More generally, if that cut were a stochastic division through the printed
two-state carrier, some column-stochastic matrix $K$ would satisfy

$$
\Gamma_2=KG.
$$

But $\operatorname{rank}(KG)\le\operatorname{rank}(G)=1$, whereas both
$I_2$ and $X$ have rank $2$.

Therefore:

$$
\Gamma^M_2=G^2
$$

is divisible through the primitive carrier, while

$$
\Gamma^Q_2=I_2\ne G^2,
\qquad
\Gamma^V_2=X\ne G^2
$$

are both nondivisible through every stochastic continuation on that carrier.
The counterexample therefore does not rely on an empirically arbitrary
positive rule: it survives inside the
class of fixed-unitary coherent completions.

This is carrier-relative. A factorization through a larger, newly supplied
intermediate carrier is a different extension with additional ontology and is
not excluded by this lemma.

Thus the base endpoint law does not select composition, and the extra predicate
“nondivisible at $bb$” still does not select the quantum-coherent completion.
