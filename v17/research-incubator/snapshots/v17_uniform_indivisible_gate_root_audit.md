## 2. Reconstructed central claim

The blueprint does not claim that ordinary positive histories are impossible.
It distinguishes:

1. positivity of completed physical records;
2. existence and information cost of a positive future-sufficient cut state;
3. a whole-history law that intentionally denies such a division;
4. a noncommutative or Hilbert-equivalent predictive boundary;
5. a global, retrocausal, or measurement-dependent premise change; and
6. empirical deviation.

The intended test is whether a whole-history indivisible law is a uniform
physical generator or merely an evaluator containing the target process in
another place.

That is the right successor question to the continuous cut-information result.

## 3. Findings that survive hostile reconstruction

### F1 — the target is operationally complete

The gate requires instruments, post-event boundaries, adaptive controls,
ancillas, discard, memory, records, failures, and continuations. Endpoint
transition matrices cannot pass alone.

### F2 — no discrete web is assumed

Configuration carriers may be finite, continuous, field-valued, relational, or
hybrid. The law is constrained by operational behavior and scaling, not by a
lattice ontology.

### F3 — target-process injection is correctly separated

Supplying an arbitrary process tensor remains an accepted representation
control but cannot earn generative explanation. The proposed translation audit

$$
J:W\mapsto(\theta_W,\omega_W)
$$

asks whether the target remains recoverable and which independent inputs have
actually been removed.

### F4 — indivisibility is carrier relative

The blueprint refuses the absolute claim that no positive variable can exist.
A sufficiently large response table, full preparation label, or predictive
state can always restore sufficiency on finite domains. Physical content lies
in the admitted carrier, resource cost, intervention interface, and law.

### F5 — information cannot be deleted for free

The resource vector tracks displacement among boundary, law, context,
coordination, precision, and physical implementation. This directly blocks the
move "remove phase from the state and leave it in the evaluator."

### F6 — whole-program access is not confused with causal transport

The added causal-port condition requires dependence to follow declared system
lineage, interaction ports, or a common parent. This kills the trivial global
function

$$
P(o\mid x,y)
$$

when no physical carrier or admitted global-boundary mechanism connects the
relevant inputs to the output.

### F7 — temporal premises remain explicit

Prefix consistency, free-input independence, adaptive controllers, and the
retrocausal/global branch are separated. None is called fundamental chronology.

### F8 — Barandes is neither dismissed nor overcredited

The gate uses Barandes's exact insight—failure of positive division on a native
configuration carrier—while recording that his present framework retains a
fixed configuration set, external target time, and a first-order transition-law
interface. Relational ontology, complete process composition, internal time,
and gravity remain new questions.

### F9 — unistochasticity is not treated as a selector

The recent unistochastic dilation theorem is used only as an external premise
to audit. If correct, it strengthens the point that endpoint unistochastic
representability is broad. It cannot by itself identify quantum laws.

### F10 — the Tsirelson route is framed correctly

The blueprint does not award the recent causal-locality proposal. It asks
whether local tensor factorization follows from stochastic physical premises or
is imported through the Hilbert representation. A Hilbert-assisted proof can
earn equivalence or premise identification, but not positivity-only selection.

### F11 — uniformity has a literal witness

The added G7 requirement prevents an indexed family of answer arrays from being
called one law merely because it is written as \(F_n\). Noncomputable or oracle
inputs remain allowed only as charged physical data and cannot support a
compression claim.

### F12 — no ontology truth is awarded by economy

The gate distinguishes derived-input/scaling gain from empirical ontology
selection. Even a successful compact generator could remain empirically
equivalent to standard quantum theory.

### R1 — the law class is not yet a set-sized formal category

The packet is typed schematically. A theorem pin must choose exact measurable
spaces, morphisms, naturality data, and admissible equivalences. Until then,
"every candidate" is not a valid theorem quantifier.

**Disposition:** expected at blueprint stage; mandatory before construction.

### R2 — uniformity cannot use an unfrozen encoding

Description size and advice growth depend on the selected physical signature
and encoding. A future pin must freeze the primitive signature and free

**Disposition:** G7 supplies the form; exact encoding remains pin work.

### R3 — causal-port locality uses supplied wiring

This is appropriate for the current quantum-foundation target, but it does not
derive causal order or spacetime. A later paper may not promote the supplied
experiment DAG into ontology.

**Disposition:** explicit scope, not a present defect.

### R4 — external stochastic theorems need reconstruction

The August 2026 Unistochastic Theorem and the August 2026 published
CHSH/Tsirelson result are recent. Their exact quantifiers and proofs must be
reconstructed independently before serving as load-bearing premises.

**Disposition:** mandatory literature/mathematics audit in the corresponding
official unit.

### R5 — the gate cannot be one construction

Multi-time completeness, uniform-law classification, BHM information location,
stochastic-native causal locality, Tsirelson selection, QFT scaling, and reality
identification would make one pin too broad to adjudicate honestly.

**Disposition:** split as described in Section 6.

### R6 — no physical memory or energy theorem yet

Mutual information is not energy, volume, duration, stability, or apparatus
complexity. A candidate could accept the information burden while remaining
physically economical.

**Disposition:** permanent scope wall until an independently motivated bridge
law is supplied.

### R7 — no empirical wedge yet

The cut theorem uses established quantum behavior and the uniformity gate may
remain empirically equivalent. The work can earn a no-go or explanatory-input
classification, not a new prediction by itself.

**Disposition:** correctly scoped; later empirical promotion requires a forced
deviation or independently selected principle.

## 5. Attempted countermodels

### C1 — one continuous hidden variable

Encode the complete preparation in one real number and let every future reader
decode it.

**Outcome:** does not defeat the continuous theorem. The mutual information and
precision are charged. It remains a positive cut model with a large burden.

### C2 — whole response table as law

For each \((x,y)\), store the correct output probability in a global table and
declare no cut state.

**Outcome:** violates G1--G5 and causal-port locality unless a global-constraint
branch with explicit physical status is declared. It is a representation/table,
not a generative law.

### C3 — standard unitary theory with actual basis configurations

Use a fixed Hilbert law to evolve a composite apparatus and read one actual
configuration outcome.

**Outcome:** likely passes operational adequacy and may pass uniformity, but the
unitary/Hamiltonian structure remains nomological input. This is an important
control: the gate must classify it coherently rather than reject it by fiat.

### C4 — endpoint-only Barandes process

Supply \(\Gamma(t\leftarrow t_0)\) for each target time and claim complete
quantum dynamics.

**Outcome:** fails Gate A unless its response to all middle interventions is
derived. Equal endpoints can conceal different process tensors.

### C5 — compact all-at-once constraint

Use one simple variational or boundary law over complete histories that depends
on both earlier and later settings.

**Outcome:** potentially serious, not automatically rejected. It enters I5 and
must prove intervention consistency, causal-port behavior or its replacement,
fine-tuned no-signalling where claimed, scaling, and empirical/nonselection
status.

### C6 — native stochastic Tsirelson principle

Postulate causal locality for configuration transitions, then translate to
unitaries and apply the standard operator bound.

**Outcome:** valid only at representation/equivalence scope unless the tensor
factorization and bound are independently reconstructed from the stochastic
premises. This is exactly the purpose of separate Gate D.

No countermodel defeats the blueprint's central classification question.
