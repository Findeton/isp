# ISP v17 — feasibility study for an invariant positive-history cost theorem

**Status:** PRIVATE / NONBINDING / NOT A PIN / NOT AN AUTHORIZED PAPER  
**Purpose:** determine whether the next high-leverage theorem can test the
physical explanatory content of ordinary positive histories without assuming
that a stochastic, classical, local, Markovian, or discrete ontology is true.

This document makes no change to the terminal Paper 04B adjudication. It does
not authorize a Paper 04B successor, Paper 05, chronology, geometry, gravity,
or a new ontology.

---

## 1. Executive verdict

The proposed theorem programme is scientifically worthwhile, but the slogan

> “positive histories must be exponentially expensive”

is not presently a theorem and is false or meaningless without a carefully
restricted comparison class.

For any fixed finite experiment, an ordinary probability distribution over
complete recorded transcripts is easy to write. One can also hide an entire
quantum state, process tensor, experiment description, or infinite lookup
table in an ontic label. Positivity alone therefore implies no invariant cost.

The honest target is a **cost displacement theorem** for a scalable family of
complete quantum processes. It should ask whether an ordinary-positive
realizer can simultaneously have:

1. bounded or controlled predictive state across cuts;
2. bounded context, coordination, and communication requirements;
3. a uniform, compositional, intervention-compatible update law;
4. an explicitly registered causal/locality convention;
5. complete operational accuracy, including adaptive interventions and
   retained records.

The plausible result is a trilemma, not a blanket impossibility:

$$
\text{phase-complete predictive state}
\quad\lor\quad
\text{growing contextual/global coordination}
\quad\lor\quad
\text{loss of uniform compositional realization}.
$$

A model may escape by rejecting a premise—through preferred structure,
measurement dependence, retrocausality, indefinite causal order, or a genuine
empirical deviation—but that escape must be printed as physics, not concealed
as a representation trick.

This is more physically informative than another finite fixture. It can
either identify a genuine compression supplied by the ISP/Barandes ontology,
or show that the positive language relocates the quantum predictive structure
rather than explaining it.

## 2. Starting point inherited from v17 Paper 01

Paper 01 earned a finite-domain representation theorem. For each registered,
causally ordered finite-dimensional finite-slot quantum process and complete
experiment program, it constructs an ordinary normalized probability measure
over typed physical record histories reproducing the operational statistics.

That theorem is compatible with one actual recorded history per run. It does
not select:

- a unique microscopic configuration space;
- a unique trajectory realizer;
- a fundamental Hamiltonian or process state;
- a law choosing which process occurs;
- a relativistic locality structure;
- a scalable ontology cheaper than the quantum process it represents.

Most importantly, the construction retains phase-complete quantum process data
as contingent input. It therefore establishes compatibility of ordinary
probability with quantum records, not an independent derivation of quantum
physics from a smaller positive law.

The cost question is exactly the unclosed gap:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Does the positive-history realization compress, explain, or physically
reorganize the quantum process—or only recode it?}
}
$$

## 3. Why raw ontology size is not an invariant

The following proposed costs are invalid unless further structure is frozen.

### 3.1 Cardinality

One real number can encode an arbitrarily long finite table. Conversely, an
ontic space can be inflated with idle labels without altering any experiment.
Cardinality alone is neither an operational resource nor an invariant of the
empirical equivalence class.

### 3.2 Coordinate dimension

A discontinuous or noncomputable injection can compress many coordinates into
one. Dimension becomes meaningful only after regularity, dynamics, precision,
and admissible maps are specified. Markovian manifold-dimension theorems must
not be generalized to unrestricted indivisible histories.

### 3.3 Description length

Description length depends on a language, universal machine, precision, and
whether a nonuniform table is free. It can be a useful secondary diagnostic,
but not a primitive physical selector.

### 3.4 Classical simulation time

Computational cost of sampling or calculating a distribution is not
automatically a property of nature's ontology. It becomes relevant only if the
candidate claims a physically local, uniform mechanism whose operations are
identified with the simulator's resources.

### 3.5 Number of histories

A history ontology may be uncountable while its predictive sufficient state is
small, or have few named histories while each label hides a complete quantum
object. The count must be replaced by a cut-relative sufficient-state or
communication quantity.

### 3.6 “Classicality”

Ordinary positivity does not entail Bell locality, noncontextuality, Markov
division, forward-only causation, or finite memory. Calling a positive
whole-program law “classical” would silently add the very premises needed to
obtain a no-go theorem.

## 4. The scalable target family

Freeze a family

$$
\mathfrak Q
=
\{Q_{n,d,L,\varepsilon}\}
$$

of causally registered process experiments, where:

- \(n\) is system or subsystem number;
- \(d\) is local operational dimension;
- \(L\) is intervention depth or slot number;
- \(\varepsilon\) is the complete-profile approximation tolerance;
- the admitted instruments include ancillas, adaptive choices, erasure,
  coherent memory, and complete retained readouts;
- every target has an explicit preparation and resource convention;
- the comparison is uniform across the family, not separately compiled for
  each experiment after its identity is known.

The first theorem should not start with all quantum field theory. A staged
family is safer:

1. finite stabilizer processes as an efficient positive control;
2. stabilizer plus a controlled non-Clifford resource;
3. universal finite-depth circuit/process families;
4. non-Markovian process-tensor families with adaptive causal breaks;
5. only later, local-net or continuum families.

Each enlargement must state which old theorem premises survive.

## 5. Candidate realizer classes

The theorem must classify, rather than conflate, at least the following.

### C0 — whole-program predictor

For every complete program \(e\), directly assign the Born transcript law
\(P_Q(\cdot\mid e)\). This is always positive and operationally exact. It is a
baseline representation, not a microscopic explanatory mechanism.

### C1 — Markov ontological realizer

A cut state \(\lambda_k\) is updated by a positive kernel after each admitted
intervention, and screens the future from the past relative to the registered
interface. Montina-type dimension bounds apply only to explicitly matched
versions of this class.

### C2 — finite-state sequential contextual realizer

The next update may depend on the current measurement/instrument context, but
the accessible memory state belongs to a finite or precision-bounded set.
Automata and contextual-memory bounds naturally live here.

### C3 — uniform compositional indivisible-history realizer

The law may be non-Markovian and need not factor across intermediate
happenings. Nevertheless, one fixed typed rule must generate every admitted
complete program, respect extension and coarse-graining, and expose the
resources retained across a cut. This is the most relevant class for a
Barandes-style or ISP-style proposal.

### C4 — relativistically local realizer

In addition to C3, spacelike operations must obey a frozen locality and
measurement-independence contract, with communication and common-cause
resources accounted for. This class is not available until the background or
endogenous causal structure used in the contract is explicit.

### C5 — premise-escaping realizer

Models using preferred foliation, measurement dependence, retrocausal
boundary conditions, indefinite causal order, nonuniform cosmological data, or
an empirical deviation belong here. They are not automatically rejected, but
their extra physical input is a charged resource and cannot be called a free
positive-history explanation.

## 6. Complete operational equivalence

Let \(\mathcal E_{n,d,L}\) be the registered complete experiment category and
let

$$
\Phi_{\mathcal E}(M):
\mathcal E_{n,d,L}
\longrightarrow
\mathsf{Instr}_{\rm op}
$$

assign the complete instrument law, including outcome probabilities,
post-intervention states available to later tests, retained records, failure
flags, and resource readouts.

Exact adequacy is natural equality of complete profiles. Approximate adequacy
uses a stable process or strategy norm:

$$
d_{\mathcal E}(M,Q)
=
\sup_{e\in\mathcal E}
d_{\rm op}\!\left(\Phi_{\mathcal E}(M)(e),
\Phi_{\mathcal E}(Q)(e)\right)
\le \varepsilon.
$$

Final-outcome agreement alone is insufficient. It can hide wrong conditional
states, wrong adaptive behavior, erased records, or context-dependent
recompilation.

This definition also prevents a category mistake: two models with equal
complete profiles remain empirically equivalent at this domain even if one is
described in amplitudes and the other in positive histories. Resource claims
can discriminate them only when the resources are operationally or physically
registered, not merely counted in the theorist's notation.

## 7. Resource vector

Use a vector, not a single aesthetic score:

$$
\mathbf R(M;\mathfrak Q)
=
(R_{\rm pred},R_{\rm mem},R_{\rm ctx},R_{\rm comm},
R_{\rm comp},R_{\rm unif},R_{\rm phys}).
$$

### 7.1 Predictive-state capacity \(R_{\rm pred}\)

Minimal operationally sufficient information retained across a cut to predict
all admitted future instruments to tolerance \(\varepsilon\). Candidate
measures include a minimal sufficient-state dimension, covering number,
entropy under a registered ensemble, or distinguishable-state packing number.

### 7.2 Sequential memory \(R_{\rm mem}\)

Minimal persistent carrier required by an online or compositional realization.
For finite-state models use bits or state count; for regular continuous models
use precision-aware covering dimension; for process tensors use a matched
memory/bond parameter.

### 7.3 Context bandwidth \(R_{\rm ctx}\)

Information about the preparation, future measurement choice, complete program,
or global boundary condition that must be supplied outside the ordinary local
state. This distinguishes honest contextuality from a hidden program oracle.

### 7.4 Coordination/communication \(R_{\rm comm}\)

Minimal information exchanged or pre-coordinated between registered regions
to reproduce the complete correlations, with shared randomness and causal
direction explicitly specified.

### 7.5 Composition debt \(R_{\rm comp}\)

Failure of one fixed local/typed realization to compose under extension,
parallel product, adaptive insertion, and coarse-graining. A whole-program
recompilation cost belongs here.

### 7.6 Uniformity debt \(R_{\rm unif}\)

Size or complexity of the family-generating rule once nonuniform instance
tables are charged. This is not raw Kolmogorov complexity; the representation
language and admissible compiler must be frozen.

### 7.7 Physical resource \(R_{\rm phys}\)

Energy, time, spatial support, clock quality, control precision, entropy dump,
or other resources that are themselves experimentally registered. This is the
strongest kind of cost because it can create an empirical wedge.

No scalar total should be introduced until a physical exchange relation among
coordinates is independently justified.

## 8. Free simulations and the anti-laundering rule

A comparison requires a preorder \(M\succeq M'\): model \(M\) can simulate
\(M'\) by admitted free transformations while preserving the complete profile
to tolerance \(\varepsilon\).

Free transformations may include:

- relabeling and measurable isomorphism;
- removal of empirically idle fibers;
- registered coarse-graining;
- bounded local ancillary randomness returned to a standard state;
- uniform compilation whose resource is explicitly charged.

They must not include:

- an oracle for the target process or future setting;
- a separate table for every experiment instance;
- infinite precision at zero cost;
- hidden communication across a locality cut;
- changing the ontology after holdout data are read;
- moving the full quantum process into an uncounted “law parameter.”

The anti-laundering requirement is:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Any information removed from one coordinate must reappear in another
counted coordinate or be proved operationally idle.}
}
$$

## 9. Candidate theorem

For a registered scalable family \(\mathfrak Q\), candidate class C3, accuracy
sequence \(\varepsilon_{n,L}\), and frozen free-simulation preorder, prove one
of the following.

### 9.1 Cost-displacement lower bound

There exists a monotone \(F(n,d,L,\varepsilon)\) such that every adequate
ordinary-positive realizer satisfies

$$
\max\{
R_{\rm pred},
R_{\rm ctx}+R_{\rm comm},
R_{\rm comp}+R_{\rm unif}
\}
\ge
F(n,d,L,\varepsilon).
$$

The rate might be linear, polynomial, exponential, or merely unbounded. It
must be derived rather than desired.

### 9.2 Structural trilemma

Even without a sharp numeric rate, show that no family can keep all three
coordinates uniformly bounded while reproducing the complete target family.
This would already establish that positivity does not erase the phase-complete
predictive burden; it relocates it.

### 9.3 Constructive compression

Exhibit one uniform C3 realizer with strictly lower invariant costs than the
best phase-complete quantum comparator, without an uncounted context oracle or
composition failure. This would be a genuine positive result for ISP-style
ontology.

### 9.4 Premise-sensitive classification

Classify exactly which premise must be relaxed to achieve a given cost. This
may be the most realistic first result and is scientifically better than a
false universal no-go.

## 10. Available proof routes and their honest reach

### 10.1 Markov ontological dimension

Montina proves that, for a matched class of ontological theories with Markovian
deterministic or stochastic evolution, an \(N\)-dimensional quantum system
requires at least \(2N-2\) continuous variables. For qubit composites this is
exponential in qubit number.

Use: a strong theorem for C1 and a regression control for any model claiming a
small Markov sufficient state.

Non-use: it does not settle C3. An indivisible whole-history law may reject the
Markov cut assumed by the theorem.

### 10.2 Sequential contextual memory

Kleinmann et al. show that exact classical simulation of sequential
state-independent contextuality has a nontrivial memory cost, including cases
where a two-qubit sequence requires more classical memory than the system's
own information capacity. Karanjai, Wallman, and Bartlett connect contextuality
to lower bounds on classical simulation memory; for the qubit stabilizer
subtheory their bound scales quadratically.

Use: finite-state C2 lower bounds and a bridge from operational contextuality
to memory.

Non-use: contextuality by itself does not imply exponential cost.

### 10.3 Efficient contextual positive control

Hindlycke and Larsson construct an outcome-deterministic contextual ontological
model for \(n\)-qubit stabilizer quantum mechanics with quadratic memory and
computational complexity despite assigning values to exponentially many Pauli
observables.

Use: mandatory hostile control. Any theorem claiming “contextuality forces
exponential positive-history cost” must fail this case or state premises that
exclude it for a physical reason.

### 10.4 Signaling dimension and classical communication

Doolittle and Chitambar define the signaling dimension of a channel as the
minimum classical communication needed to reproduce its input-output
correlations with unlimited shared randomness, and give device-independent
bounds.

Use: operationally anchored \(R_{\rm comm}\) lower bounds for matched
prepare-transform-measure or regional cuts.

Non-use: channel communication cost is not automatically ontological memory or
a gravity/locality theorem.

### 10.5 Randomization and complete-profile comparison

Quantum randomization theorems compare channels through performance in all
decision problems and characterize approximate post-processing using norms
including the diamond norm.

Use: define empirical deficiency and free simulation without relying on one
chosen observable.

Non-use: the theorem compares operational experiments; it does not select an
ontology inside an exact empirical-equivalence fiber.

### 10.6 Process-tensor memory

Purified process tensors and related matrix-product representations provide
cut-sensitive memory-complexity measures for non-Markovian quantum processes.

Use: a quantum comparator and a possible bridge to minimal sufficient memory
across sequential cuts.

Non-use: a tensor-network bond dimension must not simply be declared equal to
physical ontic memory. A two-way realization theorem is needed.

### 10.7 Contextual causal models

Montina and Wolf show, under their causal and unitary-compatibility premises,
that nontrivial classical models require measurement context; related work
connects faithful causal simulation to information erasure.

Use: evidence that context and erasure are promising charged coordinates.

Non-use: the premises must be matched exactly, especially for a theory that may
be indivisible or deny ordinary intermediate causal states.

### 10.8 Operational cut-capacity anchor

The \(\alpha\)-Partial Matching family has bounded-error one-way complexity

$$
Q^1=O(\log n)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
R^1=\Omega(\sqrt n)
$$

for fixed \(\alpha\). Any finite ordinary-positive boundary satisfying a
preparation/future screening factorization would induce the classical one-way
protocol and therefore inherit the lower bound.

Use: this supplies the first theorem-shaped branch of the v17 trilemma. A
compact positive ontology must use scalable nondivision at the cut, an
unbounded/continuous resource, intervention dependence, or another charged
premise.

Non-use: the result is finite-alphabet, one-way, bounded-error, and based on a
particular promise-task family. It does not rule out continuous or indivisible
histories and does not select an ontology.

The complete private reduction and escape ledger are recorded at
`/private/tmp/v17_operational_cut_nondivision_theorem_candidate.md`.

### 10.9 Continuous cut-information extension

The hard-distribution argument used in the classical-shadows literature turns
the Boolean Hidden Matching communication lower bound into

$$
I(X:M)=\Omega(\sqrt{n/\alpha})
$$

for every successful classical one-way message under the registered hard
ensemble. For an arbitrary standard-Borel positive cut carrier, quantizing the
finite vector of all registered response probabilities produces such a finite
message without increasing its mutual information with \(X\). Consequently,
approximate positive cut sufficiency implies

$$
I(X:\Lambda\mid S)=\Omega(\sqrt{n/\alpha})
$$

whenever prediction, factorization, and quantization errors retain a constant
success margin.

Use: this defeats coordinate-count laundering and the “one exact real” escape
without assuming a discrete ontology.

Non-use: mutual information is hard-ensemble and cut-relative; it is not yet a
physical energy, volume, precision, or total explanatory-cost theorem. Genuine
whole-history nondivision remains outside the screening premise.

The proof and source/quantifier audit are recorded at
`/private/tmp/v17_continuous_cut_information_theorem_candidate.md` and
`/private/tmp/v17_continuous_cut_source_quantifier_audit.md`.

## 11. Hostile controls

Any official pin should preregister at least these attacks.

1. Encode the full quantum state in one exact real number.
2. Supply the full future experiment as an uncounted context label.
3. Use one nonuniform lookup table per target process.
4. Recompile the history law after every intervention.
5. Inflate the ontology with idle labels and claim a larger cost.
6. Quotient active labels away and accidentally change conditional futures.
7. Measure final probabilities only and hide wrong post-states.
8. Allow infinite precision at zero physical cost.
9. Count simulator runtime as ontic resource without a physical bridge.
10. Count Hilbert-space coordinates but not stochastic-law parameters.
11. Count contextual settings on one side but treat quantum controls as free on
    the other.
12. Assume Markov division and advertise the result against indivisible laws.
13. Assume Bell locality and advertise the result against all positive laws.
14. Mistake a tensor-network gauge choice for physical memory.
15. Use average-case compression while claiming worst-case adequacy.
16. Train and test on the same process family.
17. Let approximation error grow with system size without printing it.
18. Ignore composition, parallel use, or adaptive insertion.
19. Mistake a stabilizer-subtheory bound for universal quantum theory.
20. Claim contextuality alone forces exponential cost despite the efficient
    stabilizer control.
21. Hide retrocausality, preferred order, or measurement dependence in a
    boundary condition.
22. Count a premise escape as a derivation rather than a new physical
    postulate.
23. Infer ontological truth from lower resource cost without an independently
    physical resource interpretation.
24. Infer falsity from equal cost when the models are empirically equivalent.

## 12. Outcome ladder

### L0 — representation restatement

The positive history exists for each finite target, with no scalable or
resource statement.

### L1 — invariant definitions

Complete operational profile, free simulations, approximation convention, and
resource vector are mathematically well typed.

### L2 — restricted-family bounds

One or more bounds are proved for Markov, finite-state, stabilizer, or channel
subfamilies with exact premise ledgers.

### L3 — premise-sensitive trilemma

A broad C3 theorem shows that cost must appear in predictive state, context or
coordination, or compositional uniformity.

The private continuous cut candidate now reaches a restricted L3 branch for
positive screening boundaries. It does not yet cover all C3 indivisible laws.

### L4 — quantitative scalable separation

A nontrivial asymptotic lower or upper bound is proved for a universal process
family and an explicitly matched comparator.

### L5 — physical resource separation

The cost is tied to measurable memory, energy, communication, precision, or
clock resources and yields a held-out empirical wedge.

### L6 — ontology selection

Only if the result, together with independent physical principles or data,
separates empirical equivalence classes may it contribute to selecting an
ontology. Mathematical economy alone cannot reach L6.

## 13. What this could say about Barandes

A Barandes-style indivisible stochastic process should not be tested by
silently forcing it into a Markov hidden-variable template. Its strongest
possible advantage is precisely that complete transition data need not admit
an intermediate stochastic division.

The fair question is therefore:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Can one uniform indivisible law reproduce a scalable complete quantum
process family with less predictive-state, context, and composition debt?}
}
$$

Three outcomes are clean:

- **yes:** indivisibility supplies an invariant compression or explanatory
  organization;
- **no:** the phase-complete quantum process reappears somewhere in the
  positive law's state, context, or instance data;
- **underdetermined:** the two descriptions remain equally adequate and no
  registered physical resource separates them.

The third outcome is not a failure. It is an ontology-nonselection result.

## 14. Relation to clocks, chronology, and gravity

The cost theorem should run independently of the closed Paper 04B cycle.

For clocks, it can ask whether replacing an external parameter by a physical
record merely transfers timing information into an uncounted controller,
boundary condition, or retained memory.

For chronology, it can ask whether an allegedly endogenous influence order
requires the entire laboratory schedule as hidden context.

For geometry, it can ask whether a decoder compresses stable operational
invariants or simply inserts a supplied manifold.

For gravity, it can ask whether a common bidirectional matter-geometry law
reduces independent input or merely fits two feed-forward response tables.

None of those later units is opened by this feasibility study.

## 15. Recommended first official theorem, if separately authorized

Do not begin with “arbitrary quantum physics” or a universal exponential
claim. The best first unit is now the operational cut-capacity classification:

1. freeze the \(\alpha\)-Partial Matching preparation--measurement family;
2. authenticate the public-randomness and worst-case-error communication
   theorem;
3. prove the positive cut-sufficiency reduction independently;
4. separate finite carrier, continuous precision, lawful nondivision,
   intervention dependence, nonuniform compilation, and empirical-deviation
   branches; and
5. prohibit promotion beyond the exact one-way family.

A later widening unit can then use:

1. **Stabilizer control:** interface the known efficient contextual model and
   verify that the cost coordinates do not invent a false exponential burden.
2. **Magic-extension gate:** add a registered family of non-Clifford resources
   and derive either a quantitative growth bound or an explicit uniform
   positive construction.

The pin must freeze:

- experiment and process families;
- exact versus approximate accuracy;
- online/compositional interface;
- permitted context access;
- locality and causal assumptions, if any;
- free transformations;
- resource vector and comparison baseline;
- positive and negative outcome ladders;
- all premise escapes.

This unit would be a classification/cost theorem. It would not be a proposed
new physical law, a clock paper, or an ontology-selection result.

## 16. Present decision

The external feedback correctly identifies the explanatory-cost question as a
high-value research front. Existing literature now supplies both strong scoped
lemmas and two nested cut theorems by direct reduction: finite-alphabet capacity
and continuous hard-ensemble mutual information. It still does not supply the
universal result the programme needs because genuinely indivisible histories
deny the screening premise.

The next intellectually honest move is to develop the theorem class privately
until all costs are invariant under relabeling, idle-fiber inflation, and
information laundering. An official unit would require fresh authorization.

The surviving indivisibility branch now has a separate private gate blueprint
and hostile root audit. They require complete multi-time interventions,
uniform generation, causal-port locality, explicit temporal premises, and an
information-displacement ledger before an indivisible law can count as more
than a whole-program table. The audit recommends that this broader gate remain
split from the narrow continuous cut theorem and that causal-locality/Tsirelson
selection be tested only after the complete interface exists.

The current physical conclusion is therefore:

> Ordinary positive histories are compatible with finite quantum records, but
> positivity alone has not explained quantum structure. The live question is
> whether a uniform indivisible history law can reduce an operationally or
> physically meaningful resource without hiding the phase-complete process in
> state, context, coordination, or nonuniform composition.
