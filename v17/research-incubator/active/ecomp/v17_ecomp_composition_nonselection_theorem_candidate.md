# ISP v17 — positive endpoint quotient is not compositional

**Status:** ACTIVE AUTHOR-SIDE THEOREM CANDIDATE / NOT REVIEWED
**Date:** 2026-08-23
**Scientific result awarded:** none
**Authority created:** none

This note isolates the smallest exact reason that positive primitive endpoint
transition matrices do not determine phase-sensitive multi-operation
behavior. Its central statement is a quotient/no-congruence theorem, not a
finite fitted fixture and not a refutation of the fixed-system
stochastic--quantum correspondence.

---

## 1. Statement

Let the configuration set be

$$
\mathcal C=\{0,1\}.
$$

Let the physical primitive $b$ have isolated endpoint transition matrix

$$
G_b
=
\begin{pmatrix}
1/2&1/2\\
1/2&1/2
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

There exist at least three finite, uniform, typed, positive completed-record
laws that:

1. use the same configuration set;
2. agree on $G_b$ for every basis-configuration input;
3. use the same physical program syntax and basis reader;
4. contain no per-word answer table; and
5. disagree on the two-operation program $bb$.

The standard-H, Markov, and alternative-V completions satisfy

$$
\Gamma^{Q}_{bb}=I_2,
\qquad
\Gamma^{M}_{bb}=G_b,
\qquad
\Gamma^{V}_{bb}=X.
$$

Therefore the isolated positive primitive transition law $G_b$ does not
determine the complete sequential composition law.

More sharply, let

$$
q:U(2)\longrightarrow\mathsf{Stoch}_2,
\qquad
q(U)=|U|^{\odot2},
$$

and define $U\sim V$ exactly when $q(U)=q(V)$. Then $\sim$ is not a
congruence for multiplication. There cannot exist any binary operation
$\star$ on $q(U(2))$ for which

$$
q(U_2U_1)=q(U_2)\star q(U_1)
$$

holds for all $U_1,U_2\in U(2)$. The proof uses the same registered witnesses:
the input pair is $(G_b,G_b)$ for both $(H,H)$ and $(V,V)$, while the outputs
are $I_2$ and $X$. Thus the positive endpoint quotient has forgotten data
required for sequential composition.

## 2. Quantum-coherent completion

Let

$$
H
=
\frac1{\sqrt2}
\begin{pmatrix}
1&1\\
1&-1
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

For a word $w=b^m$, define

$$
U_w=H^m,
\qquad
\Gamma^Q_w(i\mid j)=|(U_w)_{ij}|^2.
$$

For $m=1$,

$$
\Gamma^Q_b=|H|^{\odot2}=G_b.
$$

For $m=2$, $H^2=I_2$, hence

$$
\Gamma^Q_{bb}=I_2.
$$

The law is positive and normalized on completed configuration-basis records.
It is specified by one finite evaluator: multiply the fixed algebraic matrix
$H$ according to the typed word and square the final matrix entries. There is
no word-indexed advice.

At the intermediate $b$ boundary,

$$
\Gamma^Q_{bb}
\ne
G_b^2,
$$

because

$$
G_b^2=G_b\ne I_2.
$$

Thus that boundary is not a stochastic division for this carrier.

The conclusion does not depend on assuming the later factor is another copy of
$G_b$. For every column-stochastic $K$ on the two-state carrier,

$$
\operatorname{rank}(KG_b)\le\operatorname{rank}(G_b)=1,
$$

whereas $\operatorname{rank}(I_2)=2$. Hence no stochastic continuation through
the printed intermediate configuration can factor the $Q$ law.

The statement is relative to the printed two-state carrier. A larger hidden
intermediate carrier is a different ontological extension and is not ruled out.

## 3. Markov completion

Using the same isolated primitive matrix, define

$$
\Gamma^M_{b^m}=G_b^m.
$$

Since $G_b$ is idempotent,

$$
G_b^m=G_b
\qquad(m\ge1).
$$

Therefore

$$
\Gamma^M_b=G_b=\Gamma^Q_b,
\qquad
\Gamma^M_{bb}=G_b\ne I_2.
$$

This is also one finite uniform positive law. It composes the fixed stochastic
kernel at every primitive boundary and is Markov/divisible there.

### 3.1 A second fixed-unitary nondivisible completion

The nonuniqueness is not caused merely by allowing a Markov control. Let

$$
X=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&1\\
1&0
\end{pmatrix}
$$

and define

$$
V
=
e^{-i\pi X/4}
=
\frac1{\sqrt2}
\begin{pmatrix}
1&-i\\
-i&1
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\Gamma^V_{b^m}=|V^m|^{\odot2}.
$$

$V$ is unitary and is used unchanged at every depth. Because every entry of
$V$ has modulus $1/\sqrt2$, this completion agrees with both earlier
completions on the isolated primitive,

$$
\Gamma^V_b=G_b,
$$

whereas

$$
V^2=-iX,
\qquad
\Gamma^V_{bb}=X.
$$

Because $\operatorname{rank}(KG_b)\le1$ for every stochastic $K$, while
$\operatorname{rank}(X)=2$, the intermediate $b$ boundary is not a stochastic
division for this completion either. Thus even restricting attention to laws
that are nondivisible at $bb$ and generated by powers of one fixed unitary does not
select the standard-Hadamard answer: the two coherent completions return $I_2$
and $X$, respectively.

For programs containing an actual configuration measurement, apply the
applicable unitary-power rule separately to each unmeasured block between
division records. The measurement copies the current configuration into a
retained record and starts the next conditioned block. No trajectory law is
assigned inside an unmeasured block.

### 3.2 Exact coherent-fiber classification

The two coherent witnesses lie in the one-parameter family

$$
U_\theta
=
\frac1{\sqrt2}
\begin{pmatrix}
1&e^{i\theta}\\
e^{i\theta}&-e^{2i\theta}
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Its columns are orthonormal and every entry has modulus $1/\sqrt2$, so

$$
|U_\theta|^{\odot2}=G_b
$$

for every real $\theta$. Direct multiplication gives

$$
|U_\theta^2|^{\odot2}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
\cos^2\theta&\sin^2\theta\\
\sin^2\theta&\cos^2\theta
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

At $\theta=0$ this is the $H$ completion; at $\theta=-\pi/2$ it is the $V$
completion. The two-step matrix has rank two whenever
$\cos^2\theta\ne1/2$, and is then nondivisible through the rank-one positive
carrier $G_b$.

This continuum is an algebraic classification of complete laws compatible with
the isolated endpoint. It is not a claim that every real $\theta$ has a finite
effective physical specification. The two registered witnesses use fixed
algebraic values.

### 3.3 No-congruence proof

Assume for contradiction that $\star$ is a well-defined binary operation on
positive endpoint images and that it reproduces unitary sequential
composition. Since

$$
q(H)=q(V)=G_b,
$$

well-definedness forces

$$
q(H)\star q(H)=G_b\star G_b=q(V)\star q(V).
$$

Composition preservation would then give

$$
q(H^2)=q(V^2),
$$

contrary to $q(H^2)=I_2$ and $q(V^2)=X$. Therefore the endpoint equivalence
relation is not multiplicative and no such $\star$ exists.

This result rules out only composition from the endpoint quotient alone. A
lift, a larger carrier, a direct whole-program law, or another registered
piece of nomological data can restore composition, but each adds information
not present in $G_b$.

## 4. Operational separator

Prepare configuration $0$, run $bb$, and read the final configuration.

The quantum-coherent law gives

$$
P_Q(0\mid0,bb)=1,
\qquad
P_Q(1\mid0,bb)=0.
$$

The Markov law gives

$$
P_M(0\mid0,bb)=P_M(1\mid0,bb)=\frac12.
$$

The total-variation separation is

$$
\frac12
\sum_{i=0}^{1}
|P_Q(i\mid0,bb)-P_M(i\mid0,bb)|
=
\frac12.
$$

The alternative coherent completion gives

$$
P_V(0\mid0,bb)=0,
\qquad
P_V(1\mid0,bb)=1,
$$

and is separated from the quantum-coherent law by total variation $1$.

No intermediate measurement is required. Inserting a registered
configuration measurement after the first $b$ makes all three laws return the
Markov result, providing the division/decoherence control.

The fixed rules also give the held-out table

| $m$ | $\Gamma^Q_{b^m}$ | $\Gamma^M_{b^m}$ | $\Gamma^V_{b^m}$ |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 3 | $G_b$ | $G_b$ | $G_b$ |
| 4 | $I_2$ | $G_b$ | $I_2$ |
| 6 | $I_2$ | $G_b$ | $X$ |
| 7 | $G_b$ | $G_b$ | $G_b$ |
| 8 | $I_2$ | $G_b$ | $I_2$ |
| 10 | $I_2$ | $G_b$ | $X$ |

because $H^{2k}=I_2$, $G_b^m=G_b$ for $m\ge1$, and
$V^4=-I_2$. These are consequences of the fixed rules, not additional fitted
data.

## 5. Extension to the fixed scalable grammar

The separation remains a two-state lemma, but it is not tied to a depth-two
lookup or one-off fitted parameter.

### 5.1 Quantum rule

For every gate-language program, use the fixed standard instrument semantics,
compose amplitudes/channels according to the physical wiring, and take the Born
law for completed records. This is the `UNIFORM-QUANTUM-NOMOLOGY` control in
the exact U-Gen class.

### 5.2 Markov rule

For every unitary primitive $g$, use the configuration-basis stochastic matrix

$$
G_g(i\mid j)=|(U_g)_{ij}|^2
$$

and compose these matrices sequentially. Use Cartesian/Kronecker composition
for independently wired systems, the deterministic classical map for
$\mathtt{CNOT}$, a nondestructive configuration reader for $M_Z$, and ordinary
recorded stochastic branching for adaptive control.

This is one fixed positive classical circuit semantics. It agrees with the
quantum rule on every isolated primitive configuration transition by
construction, but fails interference, beginning with $bb$.

Both rules are syntactically uniform and locally substitutive. The difference
is not a new number fitted at depth two; it is the fixed composition law used
at every depth.

The $V$ completion extends in the same finite repeated-$b$/measurement
subgrammar by one fixed unitary-power rule. It is a countermodel to what the
positive endpoint packet selects, not a claim that a calibrated Hadamard is
physically $V$.

## 6. Gauge audit

This theorem does not compare two arbitrary Schur--Hadamard phase choices for
one fixed complete stochastic law. Barandes treats those lift phases as gauge
when the entire Hilbert representation transforms consistently.

Instead, it compares two different native rules for assigning the **complete
stochastic law of a composite physical program** from the same primitive
endpoint data:

1. compose the fixed lift $H$ and then take modulus squares;
2. compose the fixed lift $V$ and then take modulus squares; or
3. compose the positive endpoint matrices themselves.

The $V$ completion uses a different coherent lift of the same isolated positive
endpoint matrix. The lift choice is gauge at the isolated endpoint. Powering
the two lifts and then taking completed-record probabilities produces two
different native whole-word laws, however, so the completed laws are not gauge
copies of one experiment.

Their final configuration probabilities differ, so they are not gauge-related
presentations of one complete experiment law.

The standard-H completion may still be expressed entirely as an indivisible
stochastic law by taking $\Gamma^Q_w$ as the native whole-word transition
matrix. The theorem says only that $\Gamma^Q_w$ is not determined by $G_b$
through ordinary positive kernel composition.

## 7. Exact implication for Barandes

Barandes's fixed-system dynamical law may supply
$\Gamma(t\leftarrow t_0)$ for arbitrary target times. If the physical interval
contains both $b$ operations, then the complete value
$\Gamma^Q_{bb}=I_2$ can be part of that fixed law. Nothing here contradicts
that possibility.

The implication is narrower and more important for a universal theory:

> A scalable family of isolated primitive endpoint matrices does not select
> the empirically calibrated phase-sensitive whole-program family. A uniform
> indivisible theory must
> provide an additional rule that generates the whole-program transition laws.

That rule is where phase-sensitive predictive capacity lives when it is absent
from every positive primitive endpoint matrix.

Calling the rule “indivisibility” describes its refusal to factor through
$G_b$; it does not yet derive why the rule is the empirically calibrated
coherent one rather than another nondivisible assignment. If the fixed model
already supplies every target transition, E-Comp classifies that supply as
nomological input rather than a consequence of the isolated primitive.

## 8. What the theorem does and does not prove

### It would prove, after review

1. primitive positive endpoint transition matrices are compositionally
   incomplete;
2. the two coherent completions and the Markov completion can all be finite
   and uniform;
3. two distinct fixed-unitary nondivisible completions can share the same
   primitive positive endpoint;
4. nondivision is a property of the complete composition law, not of the
   isolated one-step matrix alone; and
5. the missing phase capacity can reside nomologically in the whole-program
   assignment rule.
6. the modulus-square endpoint equivalence is not a congruence for sequential
   composition, so no endpoint-only binary product can recover all coherent
   sequences.

### It would not prove

1. that every published Barandes model is incomplete;
2. that complex amplitudes are ontic;
3. that no stochastic-native principle can select $\Gamma^Q$;
4. that the standard-Hadamard completion is unique among nondivisible
   completions;
5. that the Markov completion is physically adequate;
6. a lower bound on physical memory, energy, or description length;
7. nondivision through every enlarged or dilated carrier; or
8. internal time, locality, QFT, spacetime, or gravity.

## 9. Hostile variants

1. **Measured middle:** insert $M_Z$ after the first $b$; all three laws must
   return the mixture because each post-division one-step endpoint is $G_b$.
2. **Relabeled configurations:** conjugate all laws and readers by the same
   permutation;
   the separation must survive.
3. **Idle labels:** tensor every law with an unread input-independent label;
   both separator magnitudes must remain unchanged.
4. **Fresh depth-two table:** replace the quantum rule by a separately supplied
   $bb$ answer; this fails uniformity.
5. **Whole quantum process input:** supply $U_w$ independently for each word;
   this is target-process injection.
6. **Gauge-only mutant:** change phases in $H$ while transforming the complete
   experiment consistently; no physical separation may be claimed.
7. **Vanishing separator:** read only a constant output; incomplete readers may
   not erase the basis-record separator.
8. **Stabilizer dismissal:** the example is Clifford/stabilizer; it establishes
   compositional underdetermination, not universal quantum resource hardness.
9. **False quotient product:** define $G_b\star G_b$ to be $I_2$ or $X$ after
   inspecting the desired completion; the other registered completion must
   refute well-definedness.
