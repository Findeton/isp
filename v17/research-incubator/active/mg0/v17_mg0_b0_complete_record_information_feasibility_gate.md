## 0. Executive verdict

A raw gravitational phase, nominal force, coherence time, or total mass does
not establish that an apparatus can distinguish two physical laws. The
relevant object is the separation of their **complete attempted-run record
laws after every calibrated nuisance direction is allowed to move**.

For finite registered records, that statement has an exact form. If a test
must distinguish complete joint `N`-attempt laws `P_1^(N)` and `P_0^(N)` with
type-I error at most `alpha` and type-II error at most `beta`, then

$$
D(P_1^{(N)}\Vert P_0^{(N)})
\ge
d(1-\beta\Vert\alpha),
\tag{1}
$$

where `d` is binary relative entropy. Only for independent identical attempts
does the left side reduce to `N D(P_1||P_0)`. With nuisance families, the
divergence is replaced by the closest admitted pair. Locally, the surviving
sensitivity is the component of the signal score orthogonal to the
nuisance-score span. If that component vanishes, first-order sensitivity is
absent.

The author-side disposition is

$$
\boxed{
\text{RAW PHASE IS NOT READINESS / COMPLETE-RECORD INFORMATION GATE REQUIRED}.}
\tag{2}
$$

This is a necessary feasibility gate, not a gravity candidate, apparatus
selection, discovery threshold, or sufficient experimental design.

## 2. Proposition IF-1 — exact complete-record discrimination bound

Let `T` be any decision rule applied to the complete joint law of `N`
attempted-run records.
Suppose `0<alpha<1-beta<1` and

$$
P_0^{(N)}(T=1)\le\alpha,
\qquad
P_1^{(N)}(T=1)\ge 1-\beta.
\tag{4}
$$

Then

$$
D(P_1^{(N)}\Vert P_0^{(N)})
\ge
d(1-\beta\Vert\alpha),
\tag{5}
$$

with

$$
d(x\Vert y)
=
x\log\frac{x}{y}
+(1-x)\log\frac{1-x}{1-y}.
\tag{6}
$$

All logarithms are natural.

### Proof

Push the two joint measures through the binary decision map `T`. Relative
entropy cannot increase under a measurable map, so

$$
D(P_1^{(N)}\Vert P_0^{(N)})
\ge
d(P_1^{(N)}(T=1)\Vert P_0^{(N)}(T=1)).
$$

Monotonicity of binary relative entropy in the stated error region gives
Equation (5). This proves a necessary bound for every decision rule; it does
not assert that the bound is achievable. For independent identical attempts,
product additivity gives

$$
D(P_1^{(N)}\Vert P_0^{(N)})=N D(P_1\Vert P_0).
$$

For a fixed nonidentical context schedule with `N_c` uses of context `c`, the
same proof gives

$$
\sum_c N_c
D\!\left(P_1(\cdot\mid c)\Vert P_0(\cdot\mid c)\right)
\ge d(1-\beta\Vert\alpha).
\tag{7}
$$

If attempts share drift, memory, adaptive feedback, or correlated failures,
neither simplification is licensed. The full joint divergence in Equation (5)
must be generated by the retained apparatus model.

## 3. Proposition IF-2 — null and failure records cannot be discarded

Let `S` mark successful registration and let `R` contain the subsequent
record. Write `q_j=P_j(S=1)`. The chain rule gives the exact identity

$$
\begin{aligned}
D(P_1(S,R)\Vert P_0(S,R))
={}&d(q_1\Vert q_0)\\
&+q_1D(P_1(R\mid S=1)\Vert P_0(R\mid S=1))\\
&+(1-q_1)D(P_1(R\mid S=0)\Vert P_0(R\mid S=0)).
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
$$

Consequences:

1. if the laws predict different success rates, that difference is part of
   the complete physical prediction;
2. if the success rates and failure laws agree, the attempted-run information
   is only `q` times the postselected information; and
3. quoting detected events rather than attempted runs can overstate reach by
   `1/q` even before coherence and nuisance costs.

Deleting failures is therefore not an innocent normalization. It changes the
experiment being compared.

## 4. Proposition IF-3 — nuisance-efficient information

For one smooth complete record family `p(r|c,lambda,eta)`, let

$$
s_\lambda=\partial_\lambda\log p,
\qquad
s_\eta=\nabla_\eta\log p.
\tag{9}
$$

Average over the frozen context schedule and complete record law. The Fisher
matrix is the Gram matrix of these scores. The information about `lambda`
remaining after nuisance profiling is

$$
I_{\lambda\mid\eta}
=
I_{\lambda\lambda}
-I_{\lambda\eta}I_{\eta\eta}^{+}I_{\eta\lambda}
=
\left\|s_\lambda-\Pi_\eta s_\lambda\right\|_p^2,
\tag{10}
$$

where `+` is the Moore--Penrose inverse and `Pi_eta` projects onto the span of
the nuisance scores.

Therefore

$$
\boxed{
I_{\lambda\mid\eta}=0
\iff
s_\lambda\ \text{lies in the nuisance tangent span}.}
\tag{11}
$$

### Proof

Minimize

$$
\mathbb E_p[(s_\lambda-a^\mathsf T s_\eta)^2]
$$

over `a`. The normal equations give the orthogonal projection and Equation
(10). The minimum is zero exactly when the signal score is a nuisance-score
linear combination on the support of the complete law.

This is a local, first-order theorem; a zero value does not by itself exclude
higher-order separation. For the full joint `N`-attempt nuisance families, a
robust finite-separation gate uses

$$
\mathcal D_{\rm sep}^{(N)}
=
\inf_{\eta_1\in H_1,\eta_0\in H_0}
D\!\left(P_1^{(N)}(\cdot;\eta_1)\Vert
P_0^{(N)}(\cdot;\eta_0)\right).
\tag{12}
$$

Any test satisfying uniform error bounds over both nuisance families must
obey `D_sep^(N) >= d(1-beta||alpha)`. Under independent identical attempts
with fixed nuisance coordinates, product additivity recovers `N` times the
closest one-run divergence. If `D_sep^(N)=0`, no uniform test at that resource
count can meet the registered errors.

## 5. Ideal binary-fringe specialization

At a calibrated quadrature, an ideal successful fringe read may have

$$
p_\Phi(+\mid S=1)
=\frac{1+V\sin\Phi}{2},
\qquad
p_\Phi(-\mid S=1)
=\frac{1-V\sin\Phi}{2},
\tag{13}
$$

where `V` is the independently calibrated retained visibility. Relative to
`Phi=0`, the exact successful-run divergence is

$$
D_{\rm fringe}(\Phi,V)
=
\frac{1+x}{2}\log(1+x)
+\frac{1-x}{2}\log(1-x),
\qquad
x=V\sin\Phi.
\tag{14}
$$

For small phase,

$$
D_{\rm fringe}
=\frac{V^2\Phi^2}{2}+O(\Phi^4),
\qquad
I_\Phi(0)=V^2.
\tag{15}
$$

If the null/failure law is common and the success probability is `q`, then

$$
D_{\rm attempt}=qD_{\rm fringe},
\tag{16}
$$

so the ideal local information per attempted run is `q V^2`. This is already
an upper ceiling: readout noise, phase drift, nuisance projection, correlated
failures, and incomplete coherence lower it.

## 7. Proposition IF-4 — uncontrolled phase offset is exactly degenerate

Suppose the registered fringe depends only on `Phi+delta`, where `delta` is an
unconstrained ordinary phase offset. Then

$$
s_\Phi=s_\delta,
$$

so Equation (11) gives

$$
\boxed{I_{\Phi\mid\delta}=0.}
\tag{19}
$$

The obstruction is also global, not merely local:

$$
p(r\mid\Phi,\delta)
=p(r\mid0,\delta+\Phi).
$$

The two composite families are identical, so `D_sep=0`. No number of
repetitions uniformly separates a gravitational phase from a freely moving
phase offset in that context. A platform must instead supply an
independent phase reference or a physical reversal whose gravitational
response template is not in the calibrated nuisance span.

For example, a balanced two-context response proportional to `(1,-1)` is
locally distinguishable from a common drift proportional to `(1,1)`. This
statement is conditional on the physical reversal genuinely changing the
registered response while leaving the common-drift model valid. Writing two
signs in a table is not an intervention.

This is the root reason the B0 packet needs physical source reversals,
independent coherence witnesses, shared-reference records, and covariance—not
only a large nominal phase.
