## 0. Purpose

This document turns “gravity may select the quantum ontology” into a bounded
experimental and mathematical question. It does not select a model, calculate a
new gravitational prediction, or claim that the proposed laboratory controls
are presently feasible.

The first benchmark must determine where a candidate places the information
needed for all three links

$$
\text{matter alternatives}
\longrightarrow
\text{gravity-sensitive response}
\longrightarrow
\text{matter records},
$$

and whether those links belong to one immutable parent law.

## 1. The reciprocal source–gravity–probe diamond

The benchmark uses a declared low-energy laboratory scaffold. That scaffold is
a control, not a derivation of fundamental time or spacetime.

### 1.1 Registered subsystems

- $S$: a source mass with two resolved configurations $L,R$;
- $Q_S$: a controllable coherence/which-configuration degree of freedom for
  $S$;
- $G$: whatever candidate variable carries gravity-sensitive response;
- $P$: an independent matter probe or probe interferometer;
- $C$: complete apparatus, traps, supports, clocks, shields, lasers, and
  environmental controls;
- $R_S,R_G,R_P,R_C$: retained physical records.

The notation $G$ does not assert a quantum field, classical metric, Newtonian
potential, or fundamental subsystem factorization.

### 1.2 Preparation family

Freeze at least these source preparations before evaluating a candidate:

1. $E_L$: source localized near $L$;
2. $E_R$: source localized near $R$;
3. $E_{\rm mix}$: registered incoherent equal mixture of $L,R$;
4. $E_{+}$: coherent equal superposition with relative phase $0$;
5. $E_{-}$: coherent equal superposition with relative phase $\pi$;
6. $E_{\varphi}$: held-out relative phase;
7. $E_{\rm erase}$: temporary source-path mark followed by a coherent eraser;
8. $E_{\rm retain}$: the same mark retained as a complete record.

For well-separated equal packets, $E_{\rm mix}$ and $E_\pm$ may share the same
one-body diagonal mass density while differing in coherence. That makes them a
source-functional discriminator; it does not guarantee that gravity can
distinguish them.

### 1.3 Probe family

Freeze:

1. a gravity-insensitive/null probe setting;
2. two matter paths with a calibrated fixed-background gravitational phase;
3. a direct gravity-sensitive readout when the candidate admits one;
4. two source masses in a mediator-entanglement geometry;
5. retained versus erased path information; and
6. at least one held-out mass, separation, duration, or phase setting.

### 1.4 Complete registered output

The target is not one fringe number. It is the complete joint operational
profile

$$
\mathcal O_e=
\left{
p(r_S,r_G,r_P,r_C\mid b,c)
\right}_{b,c\in\mathcal C_e},
$$

or the rival's exactly typed non-Kolmogorov precursor plus every licensed
record probability.

## 2. Four nested calibration stages

### Stage A — nongravitational closure

Calibrate traps, electromagnetic forces, Casimir effects, vibrations, thermal
noise, measurement backaction, and cross-talk. A candidate does not earn a
gravitational response from an unexplained residual.

### Stage B — fixed-background quantum control

With $S$ effectively classical, reproduce the independently calibrated
gravitational phase or matter response of $P$. This tests the

$$
G\to M
$$

interface while allowing one-way test-field scope.

### Stage C — matter-to-gravity control

With $P$ removed or made insensitive, vary $E_L,E_R,E_{\rm mix},E_\pm$ and
predict the gravity-sensitive record. This locates the candidate source
functional.

### Stage D — reciprocal joint parent

Run the source and probe together. The joint prediction must follow from the
same law and parameters used in B and C. Multiplying independently fitted
conditional channels after the fact fails this stage.

## 4. Four discriminator subexperiments

### 4.1 PG — expectation-source/actual-record control

Use macroscopically or operationally distinct source alternatives whose
recorded realization is known. Test whether the predicted gravity-sensitive
record follows an ensemble average, the actual record, a stochastic signal, or
another object.

This is a Page–Geilker-type control, not a repetition claim. Its purpose is to
reject the simplistic inference

$$
\text{quantum state}
\longmapsto
\langle T_{\mu\nu}\rangle
\longmapsto
\text{one averaged classical field}
$$

when the registered source and field do not behave that way. It does not rule
out every semiclassical or classical–quantum theory.

### 4.2 CP — coherent-source phase control

Compare $E_{\rm mix}$, $E_+$, $E_-$, and $E_\varphi$ while matching all
declared diagonal source data. Ask whether any gravity-sensitive or probe
record depends on relational coherence.

A null result can mean:

1. gravity is insensitive to that coherence at the tested scale;
2. decoherence destroyed it;
3. the observable was insufficient;
4. the candidate deliberately uses only diagonal/actual source data; or
5. the effect is below resolution.

It does not by itself prove classical gravity.

### 4.3 ME — mediator-entanglement control

Prepare two initially separable matter systems coupled only through the
admitted gravitational channel. Test whether the complete joint matter record
becomes entangled under the frozen assumptions.

Positive entanglement excludes a mediator restricted to local classical
communication in the frozen class. It does not, without its independence and
locality assumptions, establish a unique microscopic gravitational ontology.

### 4.4 DD — decoherence–diffusion control

For any classical–quantum hybrid, jointly predict:

1. loss of matter coherence;
2. diffusion/noise in the classical gravitational variables;
3. heating or momentum diffusion where applicable;
4. their common dependence on the coupling kernel; and
5. existing experimental bounds.

The candidate may not tune decoherence and gravitational diffusion
independently when its own consistency conditions relate them.

## 5. Exact reciprocity checks

For fixed complete controls, construct the response matrices

$$
R^{M\to G}_{y,x}
=p(Y_G=y\mid\operatorname{do}X_M=x,b,c),
$$

and

$$
R^{G\to M}_{r,z}
=p(Y_M=r\mid\operatorname{do}X_G=z,b,c).
$$

The benchmark asks:

1. Are two columns of each matrix operationally distinct?
2. Are both matrices derived from one normalized joint parent?
3. Do their parameters match the separate calibration stages?
4. Does the joint parent remain valid for $E_{\rm erase}$ and
   $E_{\rm retain}$?
5. Does it handle an entangled source–probe input?
6. Does it preserve the candidate's causal/no-signalling conditions?
7. Does conditioning on complete records agree with the declared actuality
   semantics?

Nonzero response alone is not enough. A hard-coded lookup table can satisfy
both matrices.

## 6. Common-parent and intervention firewall

### 6.1 Full apparatus inclusion

Changing a source configuration requires supports, work, momentum exchange,
and records. A gravitational candidate must include or control the apparatus
well enough that conservation cannot be “repaired” by an invisible external
agent.

### 6.2 No conditional-channel splicing

The following is forbidden:

1. fit $p(G\mid M)$ on Stage C;
2. fit $p(M\mid G)$ on Stage B;
3. multiply them with an arbitrary normalization in Stage D.

The Stage-D law must already exist and reduce to the earlier stages.

### 6.3 No postselected retrofitting

A branch-conditioned source must specify its conditioning record and physical
formation. It may not use the final probe result to decide which earlier field
was present unless the complete joint law makes that correlation causal or
time-symmetric in a typed way.

### 6.4 No external metric solution

Supplying the solved potential or metric for every source configuration counts
as the gravitational answer table. A candidate may take independently
calibrated constants and boundary conditions, but must generate its response
using one fixed rule.

## 7. Hostile mutant battery

Every future candidate must face at least the following mutants.

### Source and actuality mutants

1. Replace an actual source by its ensemble expectation.
2. Replace a coherent superposition by the corresponding mixture.
3. Replace a retained source record by a temporary erasable mark.
4. Source geometry from an unregistered branch selected after readout.
5. Treat record probabilities as a law for unrecorded fine paths.
6. Average stochastic geometry before coupling it back to matter.
7. Replace a pair-history law by its diagonal.
8. Use two ensemble decompositions of the same density operator and predict
   different remote records without a physical decomposition label.

### Dynamics and reciprocity mutants

9. Supply only matter on a fixed background.
10. Supply only a gravity field sourced by matter with no feedback.
11. Fit the two response directions independently.
12. Change the coupling after the held-out source phase is revealed.
13. Put a future setting in the initial joint state.
14. Use a deterministic nonlinear matter equation on one half of an entangled
    state without the complete signalling check.
15. Add collapse or noise but omit its physical record and energy accounting.
16. Add exactly enough noise to pass one experiment after seeing its bound.

### Gauge, locality, and composition mutants

17. Count coordinate-related metric representatives as distinct outcomes.
18. Gauge away a relational boundary displacement or holonomy that changes a
    record.
19. Define microscopic locality using the target spacetime light cone.
20. Glue two regions while discarding boundary/edge data.
21. Let triple gluing depend on bracket order without declaring the coherence.
22. Treat an entangled boundary as a product because the regions are drawn
    separately.
23. Infer chronology from code order or an index counter.
24. Hide a preferred foliation inside a stochastic update.

### Conservation and limit mutants

25. Use a source that violates the candidate's gravitational constraint with
    no compensating degree of freedom.
26. Recover a Newtonian force while failing universal free fall for a held-out
    probe species.
27. Import Einstein's equation and count the GR limit as derived.
28. Import the Schrödinger/QFT process and count the quantum limit as derived.
29. Calibrate separate fundamental laws in the quantum and gravity regimes.
30. Call agreement in a finite toy model a continuum or relativistic result.

### Empirical mutants

31. Choose the benchmark after finding where a candidate differs.
32. Treat a tunable difference as a prediction without independent calibration.
33. Call gravitational entanglement a proof of every form of quantized
    spacetime.
34. Call a null entanglement witness proof of classical gravity.
35. Ignore electromagnetic, Casimir, seismic, thermal, or apparatus-mediated
    channels.
36. Promote an unmeasurably small formal difference without an uncertainty or
    feasibility ledger.

## 12. Stop rule

MG0 must stop rather than generate a repair chain when:

1. the source functional is changed to evade a hostile control;
2. noise or collapse is retuned after evaluation;
3. the gravity carrier changes type;
4. an external time becomes a relational clock or vice versa;
5. the actual referent changes;
6. the joint parent is replaced by a different coupling architecture; or
7. the claimed quantum or GR limit changes.

Each is a new semantic candidate requiring a new authorization. Code-only
defects may be repaired after mathematical acceptance without retuning these
choices.
