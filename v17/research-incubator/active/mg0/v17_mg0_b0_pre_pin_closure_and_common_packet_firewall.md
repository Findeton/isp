A common B0 model is useful only if it does not strip out the physics by which
gravity candidates genuinely differ.

One may reuse a conventional apparatus kernel

$$
K_0(r_C\mid x,c,\nu)
\tag{7}
$$

established at the declared uncertainty. If a support, shield, clock,
controller, bath, or reader couples to the candidate's gravity-sensitive
object, that coordinate belongs inside the candidate's reciprocal parent and
cannot be subtracted as a fixed nuisance.

The required decomposition is therefore typed, not assumed:

$$
\mathfrak N
=
\mathfrak N_{\rm certified\ spectator}
\sqcup
\mathfrak N_{\rm gravity\ coupled}
\sqcup
\mathfrak N_{\rm unresolved}.
\tag{8}
$$

- The first class may use the common calibrated kernel.
- The second must be generated by every entrant from its same parent law.
- The third is propagated as uncertainty and may erase a claimed
  discriminator; it cannot be assigned to the preferred candidate.

This blocks both double counting and over-subtraction.

## 9. Alternative-versus-consequence typing

The B0 and MG0 probabilities normalize over complete mutually exclusive run
records for one licensed context. Preparation contexts such as `E_L`, `E_R`,
`E_mix`, and `E_phi` are alternative controls. Record coordinates
`R_S,R_G,R_P,R_C` are simultaneous components of one complete consequence.

Thus

$$
\sum_{r\in\mathcal R_c}p(r\mid c)=1
\tag{9}
$$

for each context `c`. One must not normalize separately over the simultaneous
fields and then interpret their sum as a probability over events. Temporary
marker states are physical intermediate memory; stable retained records are
complete-run consequences. Erased markers are neither outcomes nor missing
records unless a licensed reader records their erasure.
