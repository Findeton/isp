## 0. Purpose and source-level verdict

This audit asks whether the MG0 contract follows real constraints in quantum
theory and gravity or merely manufactures an attractive unification story.

The source-level answer is:

1. the simplest expectation-value semiclassical gravity is not an adequate
   universal ontology;
2. this does not prove that every consistent matter–gravity theory must use a
   quantized metric;
3. consistent Markovian classical–quantum dynamics exist under strong
   positivity, noise, decoherence, and diffusion constraints;
4. actual-configuration and collapse-signal sourcing are genuine alternative
   research programmes, not consequences of Barandes;
5. covariant histories and finite-region gluing are established structural
   languages, but neither selects nature's joint law;
6. relational time and boundary gauge data are real load-bearing problems;
7. low-energy quantum gravity has a standard effective-field-theory control;
   and
8. gravity-sensitive entanglement and diffusion experiments can discriminate
   frozen classes, but do not by themselves identify a unique ontology.

MG0 therefore has a legitimate scientific target. It has not solved it.

## 2. Barandes: stochastic–quantum correspondence, not gravity law

Jacob A. Barandes,
[*Quantum Systems as Indivisible Stochastic
Processes*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.21192), arXiv:2507.21192v1.

The source argues that quantum systems can be represented as indivisible
stochastic processes on ordinary configuration spaces, with Hilbert structures
playing secondary representational roles. It develops stochastic laws, gauge
freedom, dynamical symmetries, and dilations.

For MG0, the source supports keeping an ordinary-positive indivisible
nomological rival open. It does **not** supply:

1. gravitational variables;
2. a matter source functional;
3. reciprocal backreaction;
4. diffeomorphism or gravitational constraint closure;
5. an internal clock;
6. a relativistic QFT compiler; or
7. a gravity-sensitive empirical discriminator.

MG0 is therefore not “Barandes gravity.” It is a neutral test of whether a
Barandes-inspired rival, a decoherence-functional rival, or an actualization
rival can meet the same matter–geometry duties.

## 3. Page–Geilker: a precise exclusion, not a universal theorem

Don N. Page and C. D. Geilker,
[*Indirect Evidence for Quantum
Gravity*](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.47.979),
*Physical Review Letters* **47**, 979 (1981).

The experiment was inconsistent with the simplest use of the semiclassical
Einstein equation in which one classical field follows the expectation value
of a quantum source across macroscopically distinct recorded outcomes. The
authors explicitly described the result as evidence supporting, not proving,
quantized gravity.

MG0 may therefore use a Page–Geilker-type source control to reject an averaged
field in the relevant class. It may not infer:

$$
\text{Page--Geilker failure}
\Longrightarrow
\text{all classical gravity theories impossible}.
$$

Stochastic, branch-conditioned, actual-configuration, nonlinear, or other
hybrid theories require separate consistency and empirical tests.

## 4. Stochastic semiclassical sourcing is possible but charged

Antoine Tilloy and Lajos Diósi,
[*Sourcing semiclassical gravity from spontaneously localized quantum
matter*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.08705), arXiv:1509.08705.

This work constructs a Newtonian semiclassical model in which a continuous
measurement/collapse signal sources a classical gravitational field and feeds
back on quantum matter. The model avoids simply sourcing from an unconditioned
Schrödinger-cat expectation, but pays through a collapse/noise structure,
short-distance regularization, and additional gravitational decoherence.

It supports three MG0 requirements:

1. “classical gravity plus quantum matter” is not refuted by slogan;
2. the exact stochastic signal must be distinguished from its ensemble mean;
3. noise, regularization, and decoherence are physical inputs and predictions,
   not disposable implementation details.

The construction is a prior-art control. MG0 claims no derivation or novelty
for measurement-feedback gravity.

## 5. General completely positive classical–quantum dynamics

Jonathan Oppenheim, Carlo Sparaciari, Barbara Šoda, and Zachary
Weller-Davies,
[*The two classes of hybrid classical–quantum
dynamics*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.01332), arXiv:2203.01332.

Within the paper's Markovian, linear, probability-preserving classical–quantum
framework, complete positivity strongly constrains the generator and yields
continuous and jump classes. This is direct evidence that a consistent hybrid
interface can exist under a precisely declared scope.

Jonathan Oppenheim et al.,
[*Gravitationally induced decoherence vs space-time
diffusion*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.01982), arXiv:2203.01982;
and Isaac Layton et al.,
[*The weak field limit of quantum matter back-reacting on classical
spacetime*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.02557), arXiv:2307.02557.

These works derive a decoherence–diffusion tradeoff for admitted hybrid
dynamics and construct a weak-field gravitational realization. They justify
MG0's demand that a classical–quantum candidate jointly expose matter
decoherence and diffusion/noise of the classical gravitational variables.

The scope firewall is essential:

1. the results do not classify every non-Markovian, retrocausal, global, or
   nonclassical theory;
2. they do not prove the Oppenheim model is nature's law;
3. they do show that “keep gravity classical” is not cost-free; and
4. their experimental figures of merit are class-relative constraints.

## 6. Classical-channel gravity as a hostile control

D. Kafri, J. M. Taylor, and G. J. Milburn,
[*A classical channel model for gravitational
decoherence*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.0946), arXiv:1401.0946.

The model treats a Newtonian interaction as measurement and feedback through a
classical channel. The same mechanism that prevents matter–matter entanglement
introduces decoherence/heating at a scale tied to the coupling.

This supports the MG0 mediator-entanglement and decoherence controls. It does
not establish that every theory called “classical gravity” is a local classical
channel.

## 7. Actual-configuration sourcing is a distinct ontology

Ward Struyve,
[*Towards a novel approach to semi-classical
gravity*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.02188), arXiv:1902.02188.

The proposal explores sourcing a classical gravitational field from the actual
Bohmian matter configuration rather than an expectation value. This is exactly
the distinction MG0 labels ensemble versus actual-source data.

It supports inclusion of an actual-history control. It does not show that such
sourcing is covariantly complete, empirically correct, or derivable from ISP.
Conservation, gravitational constraints, empty-wave feedback, QFT, and the
relativistic limit remain candidate-specific duties.

## 8. Deterministic nonlinearity requires the full signalling audit

Nicolas Gisin,
[*Weinberg's non-linear quantum mechanics and supraluminal
communications*](https://doi.org/10.1016/0375-9601(90)90786-N),
*Physics Letters A* **143**, 1 (1990);
and Joseph Polchinski,
[*Weinberg's nonlinear quantum mechanics and the Einstein–Podolsky–Rosen
paradox*](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.66.397),
*Physical Review Letters* **66**, 397 (1991).

These sources exhibit signalling or branch-communication problems for the
specified nonlinear quantum frameworks. They justify MG0's complete entangled
ensemble control against naive deterministic Schrödinger–Newton-like feedback.

They do not license the overstatement that every mathematically possible
nonlinear or stochastic theory signals. The operational state space,
preparation equivalences, stochastic records, and complete dynamics must be
specified before the conclusion is drawn.

## 9. Gravity-mediated entanglement is discriminating but assumption-bound

Chiara Marletto and Vlatko Vedral,
[*Gravitationally-induced entanglement between two massive particles is
sufficient evidence of quantum effects in
gravity*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.06036), arXiv:1707.06036;
and Sougato Bose et al.,
[*A Spin Entanglement Witness for Quantum
Gravity*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.06050), arXiv:1707.06050.

These proposals use gravitationally mediated entanglement to witness
nonclassical mediator capacity. Within a local-mediator account, a purely
classical channel cannot generate the target entanglement.

MG0 therefore includes a mediator-entanglement control. Its correct maximum
claim is class-relative:

> observed entanglement excludes the frozen local-classical-mediator class
> after nongravitational channels and independence assumptions are controlled.

It does not uniquely choose a metric quantization, graviton ontology,
indivisible process, pair-history law, collapse theory, or fundamental
spacetime structure.

## 10. Covariant histories supply a serious Rival-B language

James B. Hartle,
[*Spacetime Quantum Mechanics and the Quantum Mechanics of
Spacetime*](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9304006), arXiv:gr-qc/9304006.

Hartle constructs generalized sum-over-histories quantum theories for closed
systems and general relativity using fine-grained histories, coarse grainings,
and a decoherence functional. In the GR case, diffeomorphism-invariant classes
of four-metrics and matter fields provide the spacetime formulation.

This is direct prior art for a joint matter–geometry decoherence functional and
for avoiding a preferred external measurement time. It also demonstrates why
Rival B is structurally mature.

MG0 does not count that maturity as discovery of nature's law. The measure,
action, boundary condition, coarse graining, actuality account, and classical
limit remain inputs or derivation duties.

## 11. Finite-region gluing is established structure, not a result of ISP

Robert Oeckl,
[*A “general boundary” formulation for quantum mechanics and quantum
gravity*](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0306025), arXiv:hep-th/0306025;
[*General boundary quantum field theory: Foundations and probability
interpretation*](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0509122),
arXiv:hep-th/0509122; and
[*Schrödinger–Feynman quantization and composition of observables in general
boundary quantum field theory*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1201.1877),
arXiv:1201.1877.

These sources associate quantum data with general boundaries and amplitudes or
observables with regions, with gluing replacing a privileged global temporal
evolution. They justify finite-region composition as a serious candidate
interface for MG0.

They do not imply:

1. that fundamental reality is divided into regions;
2. that ISP's positive laws generate the needed boundary data;
3. that quantum and gravitational gluing share one microscopic origin; or
4. that the gluing law selects a metric, state, or actual history.

MG0 therefore treats regional composition as a gate, not an axiom imposed on
every ontology.

## 12. Gauge boundaries can carry physical composition data

William Donnelly and Laurent Freidel,
[*Local subsystems in gauge theory and
gravity*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1601.04744), arXiv:1601.04744.

Their analysis shows that constructing gauge-invariant localized subsystems in
gauge theory and gravity can require additional boundary degrees of freedom
and surface-symmetry data. This supports MG0's prohibition against gluing
regions while silently discarding boundary/edge information.

It also supports a distinction already central to G1: a presentation change
may be gauge, while relative boundary orientation, connection, or holonomy can
remain operationally load-bearing.

## 13. Time and relational observables are not optional bookkeeping

Chris J. Isham,
[*Canonical Quantum Gravity and the Problem of
Time*](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9210011), arXiv:gr-qc/9210011;
and Carlo Rovelli,
[*Partial observables*](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0110035),
arXiv:gr-qc/0110035.

These works document the structural conflict between external quantum time and
generally covariant gravitational dynamics, and develop relational/partial
observable language.

MG0 draws only a conservative lesson: a fundamental gravity candidate must
state what time is and how registered correlations are gauge-invariant. It does
not assume a particular solution of the problem of time or revive the rejected
Paper 04B clock parent.

## 14. Low-energy quantum gravity is a mandatory comparator

John F. Donoghue,
[*General relativity as an effective field theory: The leading quantum
corrections*](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9405057), arXiv:gr-qc/9405057.

The effective-field-theory treatment separates robust low-energy quantum
effects of the known gravitational degrees of freedom from unknown ultraviolet
completion. MG0 must compare any proposed fixed-background quantum/gravity
limit with this standard low-energy control where their domains overlap.

S. Deser,
[*Self-Interaction and Gauge
Invariance*](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0411023), arXiv:gr-qc/0411023.

Deser's construction illustrates how locality, gauge consistency, conserved
currents, and spin-two self-coupling severely constrain the route from a linear
gravitational field to nonlinear GR. It supports MG0's universal-coupling,
conservation, and self-response gates.

Neither source proves that GR is the exact microscopic theory or that the
metric must be fundamental. They make the low-energy target harder to evade.

## 15. Scope synthesis: what gravity can and cannot presently select

The primary sources support this inference:

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\text{source choice}
+\text{hybrid consistency}
+\text{reciprocity}
+\text{conservation}
+\text{composition}
+\text{experiment}\\
&\hspace{3em}\text{can shrink a frozen ontology class.}
\end{aligned}
$$

They do **not** support:

$$
\text{known physics already uniquely selects one fundamental ontology.}
$$

The strongest near-term use of gravity is therefore discriminatory rather than
foundationally declarative. It can expose which hidden quantum object a theory
makes gravitationally active and what consistency cost follows.
