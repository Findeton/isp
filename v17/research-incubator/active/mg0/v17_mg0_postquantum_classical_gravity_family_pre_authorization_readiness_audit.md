## 5. The common CP-CQ predictive state

Let $M$ be a measurable classical configuration or phase space and
$\mathcal H$ a quantum Hilbert space. A hybrid state is a positive
operator-valued density

$$
z\longmapsto \rho(z)\in\mathcal T_+(\mathcal H),
\qquad
\int_M dz\,\operatorname{Tr}\rho(z)=1.
\tag{4}
$$

Its marginals are

$$
p_C(z)=\operatorname{Tr}\rho(z),
\qquad
\rho_Q=\int_M dz\,\rho(z).
\tag{5}
$$

This is stronger than replacing a quantum stress tensor by its expectation
value. It retains classical–quantum correlations and permits the quantum state
conditioned on a classical configuration.

A general linear CQ channel can be represented schematically by a CP kernel

$$
\rho'(z)
=
\int_M dz'\sum_{\mu,\nu}
\Lambda^{\mu\nu}(z\mid z')
L_\mu\rho(z')L_\nu^\dagger,
\tag{6}
$$

with kernel positivity and a normalization condition. Equation (6) is a
predictive law on an already supplied split

$$
\text{classical carrier }M
\quad+\quad
\text{quantum carrier }\mathcal H.
\tag{7}
$$

It does not derive that split, select the gravity variables, choose the
Hilbert space, choose a state, or identify a laboratory reader.

### 5.1 What complete positivity earns

At the printed scope, complete positivity earns:

1. nonnegative probabilities for all admitted quantum measurements;
2. consistency when an untouched quantum ancilla is appended;
3. linear statistical evolution;
4. preservation of the classical/quantum split; and
5. a controlled stochastic alternative to nonlinear expectation-source
   semiclassical equations.

It does not earn:

1. unitary closure;
2. energy–momentum conservation;
3. a stable vacuum;
4. relativistic locality;
5. gravitational constraint closure;
6. ultraviolet completeness;
7. one-run actuality for the quantum sector; or
8. a fundamental rather than effective interpretation.

## 6. The two-class theorem and the reciprocity cost

For time-local Markovian CQ dynamics with a suitable short-time moment
expansion, the generalized Pawula theorem gives two possibilities:

1. finite classical jumps, represented by an infinite moment hierarchy; or
2. an almost-surely continuous classical process governed by a second-order
   CQ master equation.

For the continuous class, the coefficients obey the block positivity
condition

$$
\begin{pmatrix}
2D_2 & D_1\\
D_1^\dagger & D_0
\end{pmatrix}
\succeq0,
\tag{8}
$$

equivalently

$$
2D_2\succeq D_1D_0^{-1}D_1^\dagger,
\qquad
(I-D_0D_0^{-1})D_1=0,
\tag{9}
$$

where $D_0^{-1}$ is the generalized inverse. In this notation:

- $D_0$ controls purely quantum Lindblad/decoherence terms;
- $D_1$ carries classical–quantum drift/backreaction; and
- $D_2$ controls classical diffusion.

Thus

$$
D_1\ne0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
D_0\ne0
\quad\text{and}\quad
D_2\ne0
\tag{10}
$$

at the theorem's scope. Nontrivial backreaction costs both quantum decoherence
and classical noise.

In gravity-specialized normalizations the sources often write

$$
4D_2\succeq D_0^{-1}.
\tag{11}
$$

Equations (9) and (11) are not two independent universal constants. Equation
(11) follows after a special normalization or saturation of the backreaction
coefficient. The audit must retain the convention used by each member.

### 6.1 Exact domain of the theorem

The result is load-bearing but conditional on:

1. linear CQ dynamics;
2. complete positivity and normalization;
3. a supplied classical/quantum split;
4. Markovian or time-local evolution;
5. the regularity needed for the moment expansion; and
6. the chosen classical trajectory type.

The source explicitly does not classify general non-Markovian laws. An
effective theory can leave the Markovian comparison class. A fundamental
candidate that does so must nevertheless type its memory carrier, initial
correlations, composition rule, and complete records.

### 6.2 What the theorem does not select

The inequality does not select:

- the classical phase space $M$;
- the quantum Hilbert space $\mathcal H$;
- Lindblad operators;
- $D_0,D_1,D_2$;
- a regulator or smearing scale;
- a cosmological state;
- the classicality of gravity;
- a metric ontology; or
- an empirical member.

It constrains a family; it is not the occurrence law of our universe.

## 7. Objective trajectories and the actuality boundary

`CQ-OBJ` supplies more than an ensemble density in special realizations. If
each distinct classical jump uniquely identifies the Lindblad operator that
acted, continuous monitoring of the classical trajectory determines the
conditioned pure quantum trajectory.

Schematically, if

$$
\Delta z_\alpha=\Delta z_\beta
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\alpha=\beta,
\tag{12}
$$

then the observed classical jump record can determine the quantum update.
This removes the usual unravelling ambiguity for that realization.

Three qualifications are binding.

1. The source explicitly does not decide which degrees of freedom are
   fundamentally classical and does not analyze gravity in that paper.
2. The uniqueness statement depends on the association between classical
   shifts and quantum operators; it is not automatic for an arbitrary CQ
   generator.
3. Calling the trajectory actual additionally assumes that the classical
   system has a definite, monitorable trajectory.

Therefore the hybrid density alone remains a predictive object. One-run
actuality is earned only for a member that specifies:

$$
(M,\mathcal H,\mathcal L,\text{unravelling},\text{uniqueness},

The existing sources do not yet provide a complete material reader, eraser,

## 9. The covariant path-integral family

The general CQ path-integral construction derives a doubled quantum-field
path integral coupled to a classical stochastic path from CP-CQ master
equations, together with positivity and normalization conditions. It provides
a natural arena for Lorentz and diffeomorphism covariance.

For gravity, the family has at least two distinct levels.

### 9.1 Trace-Einstein member

A positive-semidefinite tensor proportional to

$$
D_{0,\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
\propto
(-g)^{-1/2}g_{\mu\nu}g_{\rho\sigma}
\tag{15}
$$

suppresses paths away from the trace of Einstein's equation and decoheres the
quantum state in the trace of the stress tensor. This supplies an explicit
diffeomorphism-invariant CQ example.

It does not reproduce the transverse Einstein equations and constraints. It
is an existence control for covariant CQ dynamics, not a complete theory of
GR coupled to quantum matter.

### 9.2 Full-Einstein proposal

The more ambitious family uses a generalized Wheeler–DeWitt-type tensor and
weights deviations from

$$
G_{\mu\nu}+\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}
-8\pi G_N\overline T_{\mu\nu}=0.
\tag{16}
$$

The associated tensor is indefinite on unreduced components. Later work
argues that the scalar and tensor dynamical modes have positive-semidefinite
two-point functions while the indefinite vector sector represents a
constraint rather than a propagating mode.

This is real progress, but its exact scope is:

1. pure or specified matter actions rather than the complete Standard Model;
2. linearized mode analysis around Minkowski space for the strongest PSD
   statement;
3. a particular pole prescription and coupling sector;
4. unresolved nonlinear constraint implementation; and
5. unresolved full matter-sector renormalization.

### 9.3 Canonical/path-integral nonidentity

The sources explicitly do not establish that the canonical master-equation
and covariant path-integral gravity families are equivalent. The standard
master-equation-to-path-integral derivation has momentum-order restrictions
not automatically met by gravity.

Therefore

$$
\text{CQ-CAN pass}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{CQ-COV pass},
\qquad
\text{CQ-COV pass}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{CQ-CAN pass}.

## 15. Dilation is representation, not ontology

For a discrete CQ channel, the classical label can be represented by a
commutative pointer algebra on an auxiliary Hilbert space. The CQ dynamics
then appears as the diagonal sector of a CPTP map and admits a Stinespring
unitary dilation after adding an environment.

This establishes:

$$
\text{CP-CQ channel}
\hookrightarrow
\text{enlarged quantum CPTP representation}.
\tag{26}
$$

It does not establish:

$$
\text{fundamental classical metric}
=
\text{canonically quantized gravitational field}.
\tag{27}
$$

The embedding is explicitly noncanonical: classical position and momentum,
or metric and conjugate momentum, are encoded as mutually commuting pointer
observables rather than canonical conjugates. Every CPTP map has a dilation;
the existence of one does not select its dilation as physical reality.

The same source gives a fully closed rotationally invariant toy CQ model in
which total angular momentum decays. It therefore proves that symmetry of the
hybrid equations plus complete positivity does not automatically yield the
corresponding Noether charge conservation.

The toy model is a counterexample to an implication, not a universal theorem
