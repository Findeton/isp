## 1. Three different notions of source identity

The following types must not be conflated.

### 1.1 Scholarly-object identity

A publisher DOI identifies a published scholarly object and, where stated
below, its version of record. It does not by itself authenticate the bytes a
reviewer opened. Publisher PDFs can be regenerated, watermarked, or served
through different wrappers.

### 1.2 Version identity

An arXiv identifier without a suffix resolves to the latest version and is
therefore mutable as review evidence. Every arXiv source consumed by B0 is
bound here to an explicit `vN` endpoint.

### 1.3 Byte identity

Only a receipt containing the retrieval endpoint, retrieval date, byte count,
and SHA-256 authenticates the exact reviewed file. If a load-bearing numerical
claim comes from supplementary information, the supplement requires its own
receipt. No such receipt is conferred by this author-side audit.

The future pin rule is therefore

$$
\boxed{
\text{object identity}
\ne
\text{version identity}
\ne
\text{byte identity}.}
\tag{2}
$$

## 2. Evidence classes

Each consumed claim is assigned one of four roles:

| class | meaning | permitted B0 use |
|---|---|---|
| `D` | demonstrated experiment or measured component | establishes that one named operation, scale, or nuisance is real at the reported scope |
| `P` | prospective apparatus or parameter study | defines constraints and possible reach; never closes a preparation or reader arrow |
| `T` | theoretical counterexample or ceiling | limits an inference within its stated model class; never selects ontology |
| `R` | representation or accounting framework | supplies a language or ledger conditional on its inputs; never sources the physical law |

No source may donate a coordinate it did not establish. In particular,
components demonstrated on different apparatuses do not compose into one B0-E
platform.
