## 1. Mathematical audit

### 1.1 Zero-support rows

For every positive-semidefinite $D$, each $2\times2$ principal minor gives

$$
|D_{jk}|^2\le D_{jj}D_{kk}.
$$

Therefore $D_{jj}=0$ forces the full row and column to vanish. The support
restriction used in the normalized correlation matrix is well typed.

### 1.2 Correlation decomposition

On positive support,

$$
C=W^{-1}DW^{-1},
\qquad
W=\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt{D_{jj}}),
$$

is Hermitian, positive semidefinite, and unit diagonal. Congruence proves both
directions, uniqueness, and rank preservation.

### 1.3 Rank-one characterization

A rank-one positive-semidefinite unit-diagonal matrix is $zz^\dagger$ with
$|z_j|=1$. General rank requires a Gram family of unit vectors. The candidate
does not overgeneralize scalar phase to mixed or environmentally correlated
kernels.

### 1.4 Tensor rule

The identity

$$
(WCW)\otimes(VKV)
=(W\otimes V)(C\otimes K)(W\otimes V)
$$

proves the composite weight and correlation formulas. The theorem remains
conditional on the standard independent product rule.

### 1.5 Phase-member family

For

$$
T_\theta=\operatorname{diag}(1,e^{i\theta}),
$$

all members have endpoint kernel $I_2$. A fixed relative phase reference
produces

$$
p_0=\frac{1+\cos(\theta-\phi)}2.
$$

Hence properties shared by the full $\theta$ family cannot select the
physical member. Periodicity restricts but need not uniquely select the
member.

The note distinguishes

$$
\operatorname{tr}D=\sum_jD_{jj}
$$

from

$$
D(\Omega,\Omega)=\sum_{j,k}D_{jk}=1.
$$

Only a decoherent event partition receives an ordinary probability law.

### 2.2 Correlation is not ontology

The Gram vectors are a representation of $C$. Their existence does not make
Hilbert vectors, complex scalars, or two histories material objects.

The candidate restricts the automatic gauge claim to typed boundary-object
rephasings. It does not call every independent history rephasing gauge. Loop
holonomy and same-boundary relative phase remain invariant data.

$$

$$

does not derive $S$ or $\hbar$. A real additive lift may not be globally
available from a supplied $U(1)$ functor, and an endpoint potential need not
exist even when a path action does.
