## 2. Primary-source route map

### 2.1 Feynman action phase

Feynman's 1948 formulation assigns alternatives complex contributions, sums
them, and postulates the contribution of a path to have phase equal to the
classical action in units of $\hbar$:

- R. P. Feynman, “Space-Time Approach to Non-Relativistic Quantum
  Mechanics,” *Rev. Mod. Phys.* **20**, 367 (1948),
  <https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.20.367>.

This is the strongest known prototype of a compact native generator:

$$
a[h]\propto e^{iS[h]/\hbar}.
$$

It earns exact sequential gluing, interference, and the classical
stationary-phase limit once the action and measure are supplied. It does not
derive the action, $\hbar$, boundary state, path-integral measure, field
content, or actualization rule. Feynman's source explicitly presents the
single-path contribution as a postulate and the formulation as equivalent to
ordinary quantum mechanics.

### 2.2 Operational reconstructions

They offer serious candidates for selecting the quantum **framework** from

Their premises do not select the Standard Model action, a Hamiltonian member,

### 2.3 Barandes's stochastic potential

Barandes starts with ordinary-positive first-order laws $\Gamma$ and defines
a nonunique potential $\Theta$ satisfying

$$
\Gamma=\overline\Theta\odot\Theta.
$$

Arbitrary entrywise phases are Schur--Hadamard gauge when the downstream
Hilbert ingredients are transformed consistently:

- J. A. Barandes, “Quantum Systems as Indivisible Stochastic Processes,”
  arXiv:2507.21192v1,
  <https://arxiv.org/html/2507.21192v1>;
- J. A. Barandes, “The Stochastic-Quantum Theorem,”
  arXiv:2309.03085v2,
  <https://arxiv.org/html/2309.03085v2>.

This makes one point decisive: an arbitrary phase lift of one fixed isolated
$\Gamma$ is not new physics. Relative phase becomes empirically meaningful
only through a complete continuation or parent experiment, whose positive
law changes if the relative composition changes.

The audited sources do not provide one general rule that derives every
interacting parent $\Gamma$ from subsystem laws plus typed physical
interactions. The candidate hierarchy

$$
D\longrightarrow\Gamma_{\rm record}
$$

is therefore a v17 proposal, not Barandes's published ontology.

Hartle's framework is especially relevant to gravity because fine histories

These sources supply architecture, not a completed theory of quantum gravity.

### 2.5 Forward/backward influence structure

The Feynman--Vernon influence functional shows that open-system dynamics
naturally depends on pairs of forward and backward histories:

- R. P. Feynman and F. L. Vernon, “The theory of a general quantum system
  interacting with a linear dissipative system,” *Annals of Physics* **24**,
  118 (1963), <https://doi.org/10.1016/0003-4916(63)90068-X>.

This supports higher-rank relational kernels as genuine composition data for
unresolved environments. It does not make the forward/backward paths two
actual worlds, nor does it derive the environment or its state.

### 2.6 Holonomy and topology

The Aharonov--Bohm effect and multiply connected path integrals show why
relative phase cannot always be compressed into a globally trivial endpoint
label:

- Y. Aharonov and D. Bohm, “Significance of Electromagnetic Potentials in
  the Quantum Theory,” *Phys. Rev.* **115**, 485 (1959),
  <https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.115.485>;
- M. G. G. Laidlaw and C. Morette DeWitt, “Feynman Functional Integrals for
  Systems of Indistinguishable Particles,” *Phys. Rev. D* **3**, 1375
  (1971), <https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.3.1375>.

Laidlaw--DeWitt find distinct propagators associated with scalar
representations of the fundamental group. This proves that topology can
classify allowed phase sectors. It does not select which topology or sector
the universe realizes.

The physical lesson is relational: boundary rephasing can be gauge while a
closed-loop phase or comparison of two paths with the same endpoints remains
observable.

### 2.7 Positive stochastic reconstructions and the topology debt

Nelsonian and Madelung-style approaches demonstrate that ordinary stochastic

- E. Nelson, “Derivation of the Schrödinger Equation from Newtonian
  Mechanics,” *Phys. Rev.* **150**, 1079 (1966),
  <https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.150.1079>;
- T. C. Wallstrom, “Inequivalence between the Schrödinger equation and the
  Madelung hydrodynamic equations,” *Phys. Rev. A* **49**, 1613 (1994),

This is a mandatory hostile control. A positive trajectory theory that
recovers only local phase gradients can still miss global circulation and

the phase debt rather than eliminating it.

### 2.8 Euclidean positive measures

Osterwalder--Schrader reconstruction is the strongest established counterpoint
to the claim that the fundamental mathematical input must look Lorentzian and
complex. Suitable Euclidean Green functions, including reflection positivity,
reconstruct a Wightman QFT:

- K. Osterwalder and R. Schrader, “Axioms for Euclidean Green's functions,”
  *Commun. Math. Phys.* **31**, 83 (1973),
  <https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01645738>;
- K. Osterwalder and R. Schrader, “Axioms for Euclidean Green's functions
  II,” *Commun. Math. Phys.* **42**, 281 (1975),
  <https://projecteuclid.org/journals/communications-in-mathematical-physics/volume-42/issue-3/Axioms-for-Euclidean-Greens-functions-II-with-an-Appendix-by/cmp/1103899050.pdf>.

A positive Euclidean random field can therefore encode a quantum theory when
the full covariance, symmetry, regularity, clustering, and reflection-
positivity structure is present.

This is a live U-Gen branch, not a victory for positivity alone. Reflection
positivity and analytic continuation carry highly nontrivial quantum and time
structure. The Euclidean field is not automatically an actual Lorentzian
history. General interactions, gauge theories, fermions, and gravity add
further measure and sign difficulties.

### 2.9 Gravity as a conditional action selector

Known gravity results strongly constrain a low-energy action after spacetime,
dimension, signature, locality, and field content are supplied:

- S. Deser, “Self-Interaction and Gauge Invariance,” *Gen. Rel. Grav.*
  **1**, 9 (1970), arXiv:gr-qc/0411023,
  <https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0411023>;
- D. Lovelock, “The uniqueness of the Einstein field equations in a
  four-dimensional space,” *Arch. Rational Mech. Anal.* **33**, 54 (1969),
  <https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00248156>;
- J. F. Donoghue, “General relativity as an effective field theory: The
  leading quantum corrections,” arXiv:gr-qc/9405057,
  <https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9405057>.

These routes show, under different printed assumptions, why consistent local
massless spin-two self-coupling, four-dimensional second-order metric
dynamics, or low-energy effective-field-theory reasoning lead toward general
relativity and controlled corrections.

They cannot be used to claim that v17 derived spacetime, four dimensions,
Lorentzian signature, a massless spin-two field, or the metric ontology. Those
are antecedents of the gravity restrictions.

Euclidean gravity adds a specific warning. The gravitational Euclidean action
is not positive definite because its conformal mode is unbounded below:

- G. W. Gibbons, S. W. Hawking, and M. J. Perry, “Path Integrals and the
  Indefiniteness of the Gravitational Action,” *Nucl. Phys. B* **138**, 141
  (1978), <https://doi.org/10.1016/0550-3213(78)90161-X>.

Thus ordinary Euclidean positivity cannot simply be extrapolated from scalar
field theory to quantum gravity.

### 2.10 Empirical gravity--phase bridge

Gravity already changes measured quantum relative phases:

- R. Colella, A. W. Overhauser, and S. A. Werner, “Observation of
  Gravitationally Induced Quantum Interference,” *Phys. Rev. Lett.* **34**,
  1472 (1975), <https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.34.1472>;
- C. Overstreet, P. Asenbaum, J. Curti, M. Kim, and M. A. Kasevich,
  “Observation of a gravitational Aharonov--Bohm effect,” *Science* **375**,
  226 (2022), <https://doi.org/10.1126/science.abl7152>.

These experiments do not quantize the gravitational field. They do establish
that any eventual ontology must reproduce gravitationally induced action
differences and their interference, not merely classical trajectories in a
potential.
