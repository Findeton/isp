## 2. Three distinct selection steps

The comparator separates three statements that are often conflated.

### 2.1 Born equivariance

The process preserves the quantum one-time distribution:

$$
\mathcal L_t p_t=\dot p_t,
\qquad
p_i(t)=|\psi_i(t)|^2.
$$

This fixes only the divergence of the stochastic current.

### 2.2 Standard-current matching

The net stochastic current on every edge is required to equal the quantum
current:

$$
K_{ij}=J_{ij}.
$$

This is stronger than equivariance. On three or more configurations,
equivariance permits divergence-free circulation that standard-current
matching excludes.

### 2.3 Minimal no-counterflow

Even standard-current matching leaves

$$
C_{ij}=J_{ij}^{+}+S_{ij},
\qquad
S_{ij}=S_{ji}\ge0.
$$

The familiar minimal rates impose

$$
S=0.
$$

This uniquely eliminates simultaneous flow in both directions on one
unordered edge and minimizes instantaneous jump traffic after $J$ has already
been fixed.

Therefore the full logical chain is

$$
\boxed{
\text{Born law}
+\text{equivariance}
+\text{edgewise quantum current}
+\text{no counterflow}
\Longrightarrow
\text{minimal Bell jump law}.}
$$

Deleting either of the last two premises is a physical change, not an
algebraic simplification.

## 3. What is physically actual in this comparator

The primitive ontology is configuration-valued. Depending on the model, that
may mean particle positions, occupation configurations, or another carrier
selected by a PVM/POVM. The wavefunction and Hamiltonian enter the nomology
that drives the configuration law.

This gives a clear ontology, but not an ontology-free derivation. The same
abstract quantum system can admit different candidate configuration
observables. The rate formula is covariant under a simultaneous re-description
of $P$, $H$, and $\psi$; it does not choose $P$ from operational data.

The comparator must therefore print two distinct questions:

1. Does a chosen configuration ontology support a complete positive path law?
2. Does nature select that configuration ontology and that member of the
   equivariant-law family?

The construction answers the first. It does not answer the second.

## 4. Selector-principle tournament

### 4.1 Equivariance

Equivariance is empirically important because quantum-equilibrium records have
the Born distribution. It does not fix cycle current or counter-traffic.

**Verdict:** necessary inside this comparator; not selecting.

### 4.2 Full quantum-current matching

Matching $J$ imports more of the Schrödinger dynamics than matching $\dot p$.
It is natural because $J$ is the standard quantum current associated with the
chosen $H$, $\psi$, and configuration observable.

But the fact that quantum theory supplies a current does not prove that every
ordinary-positive ontology must realize that current edge by edge.

**Verdict:** canonical quantum lift; additional physical premise.

### 4.3 Least jump traffic

For fixed $J$, $S=0$ uniquely minimizes every edge traffic and total expected
jump rate. This is an exact variational property.

It is not automatically a law of nature. “Fewer jumps” depends on the chosen
configuration ontology and time parameter. It is also not inferred from Born
marginals, which are identical for all admitted $S$.

**Verdict:** mathematically sharp parsimony; empirically unselected here.

### 4.4 Hamiltonian graph locality

Forbidding jumps where the Hamiltonian has no matrix element removes traffic
on absent edges. It leaves arbitrary symmetric traffic on present edges.

**Verdict:** constraining; insufficient.

### 4.5 Symmetry covariance

A symmetric $S$ can itself respect all edge permutations or other admitted
symmetries. Covariance therefore need not eliminate counter-traffic.

**Verdict:** insufficient without a stronger uniqueness theorem.

### 4.6 Time reversal

Minimal rates have a controlled relation to time reversal, but time-reversal
covariance does not derive the choice of configuration observable or the
quantum current from Born marginals. Even the convention for the path value at
a jump time can require an additional left/right-continuity choice.

**Verdict:** important consistency control; not a complete selector.

### 4.7 Product composition

The quantum input packet supplies tensor products, composite Hamiltonians, and
product configuration observables. Minimal rates inherit useful subsystem
properties under appropriate conditions.

This does not show that an ordinary-positive law derived those structures. It
shows that a quantum-driven trajectory law can consume them consistently.

**Verdict:** strong comparator property; zero native explanatory gain.

## 5. Same Born physics, different path physics

A static fair two-state system with zero quantum current admits:

1. the minimal member, in which the configuration never jumps; and
2. symmetric counter-traffic, in which it jumps in both directions while the
   one-time distribution remains fair.

The laws agree on every single-time Born marginal. They disagree on

$$
P(Q_{t+\tau}=Q_t).
$$

This does not immediately produce a laboratory discriminator. A detector that
continuously records $Q_t$ is a new physical coupling. The detector may alter
the Hamiltonian, decohere alternatives, or change the rate law. Calling the
detector “passive” does not make the unmonitored path observable.

A legitimate empirical test must therefore freeze:

1. the apparatus Hamiltonian and configuration observable;
2. the unmonitored and monitored experiments as distinct typed procedures;
3. which record statistic depends on $S$;
4. whether standard quantum theory already fixes that statistic independently
   of the alleged hidden path; and
5. whether any new matter, gravity, or noise coupling has been introduced.

No such passive path-record discriminator is constructed here.

## 6. The explicit nomology fork

The current v17 programme keeps four rivals live.

### Rival A — ordinary-positive indivisible whole-history law

The fundamental law is ordinary positive on its asserted actual referent. It
must generate complete quantum operational predictions without receiving the
target quantum process as an input.

The Bell comparator is ordinary positive, but Markovian and quantum-driven. It
therefore proves logical feasibility, not Rival A's explanatory target.

### Rival B — fundamental strongly positive pair-history law

A complex decoherence functional $D$ is fundamental. Ordinary probabilities
appear on decoherent record algebras. A separate truth, coevent, record-only,
or other actuality account is required if one asserts more than records.

The Bell comparator can be coupled to standard quantum dynamics, but it does
not derive $D$ or show that $D$ is secondary.

### Rival C — standard quantum process plus modified actualization

Standard quantum predictions are altered by a collapse or other actuality
law. This branch can be empirically distinct, but must print its new constants,
noise, covariance, and conservation costs.

The Bell comparator normally preserves standard quantum predictions at
quantum equilibrium; it is not a collapse model.

### Rival D — plural contest

No rival receives narrative priority. Each is scored under the same complete
operational profile and the same ledger for predictive inputs, composition,
context, actuality, locality, QFT, and gravity.

**Current v17 choice: Rival D.** The original ISP/Barandes aspiration keeps
Rival A especially important as a constructive challenge, but not as an
accepted ontology.

## 7. Relation to Barandes

Barandes' programme gives ordinary probability and configuration trajectories
fundamental status while treating Hilbert-space ingredients as secondary
representational machinery. Its central dynamical objects are first-order
transition laws that need not divide through arbitrary intermediate times.

The Bell comparator differs in three load-bearing ways.

1. It starts from $H$, $\psi_t$, a configuration observable, and external
   time.
2. It is a Markov path law, so its transition kernels divide through admitted
   times under ordinary existence conditions.
3. Its member selection uses the quantum current plus a least-traffic rule.

Integrating its generator does produce a family

$$
\Gamma(t\leftarrow t_0),
$$

but this does not derive a generic indivisible $\Gamma$ architecture. It gives
one divisible quantum-driven realizer. Conversely, the existence of a
Barandes first-order $\Gamma$ does not by itself determine the Bell path law,
because many fine path laws can share lower-order transition data.

The proper comparison is therefore neither identification nor refutation.

## 8. QFT and relativistic ceiling

Bell-type quantum field constructions provide an important proof of scope:
configuration trajectories with stochastic creation and annihilation can be
defined for broad regularized QFTs.

The primary source also prints the restrictions:

1. the Hamiltonian is assumed well defined, often after cutoffs;
2. a position/configuration observable is assumed chosen;
3. finiteness and global existence require additional hypotheses;
4. Lorentz invariance is difficult because equal-time configurations are not
   mapped to equal-time configurations by boosts; and
5. interacting composition remains quantum-nomological input.

Therefore “Bell-type QFT exists” does not mean that v17 has derived a
relativistic native generator.

## 9. Gravity ceiling

An actual particle or occupation path does not by itself determine geometry.
Coupling that path to a classical gravitational field would add a new source
law. Coupling the wavefunction expectation value to gravity would be a
different hybrid theory. Quantizing geometry would require a configuration
observable and constraint treatment for geometry itself.

Any gravity extension must separately solve:

1. diffeomorphism/gauge-invariant configuration typing;
2. the Hamiltonian-constraint and external-time problem;
3. matter-to-geometry backreaction;
4. geometry-to-matter response;
5. conservation and constraint propagation;
6. a measure/path law on the physical state space; and
7. weak-field, post-Newtonian, radiation, and quantum-matter held-outs.

The Bell comparator supplies none of these. Adding a stochastic path to
ordinary quantum mechanics does not move v17 closer to GR unless a common
matter--geometry law is actually constructed.
