## 5. Theorem NRL-A — arbitrary law families can be universalized by a static sector

### Statement

Every family (2) admits the single ordinary-positive enlarged rule (4).
Marginalizing the conserved sector reproduces the original member:

$$
(\operatorname{pr}_{\mathcal R_e})_*
\widetilde Q_e(\,\cdot\mid\theta)
=Q_{\theta,e}.
\tag{5}
$$

If the family is covariant under verified presentation changes and the sector
transforms covariantly, the enlarged rule can be made covariant as well.

### Proof

For every measurable $A\subseteq\Theta$ and
$B\in\Sigma_{\mathcal R_e}$,

$$
\widetilde Q_e(A\times B\mid\theta)
=\mathbf 1_A(\theta)Q_{\theta,e}(B)\ge0.
\tag{6}
$$

Normalization follows from

$$
\widetilde Q_e(\Theta\times\mathcal R_e\mid\theta)
=Q_{\theta,e}(\mathcal R_e)=1.
\tag{7}
$$

Marginalization gives (5). For a verified transformation
$g:(e,\theta)\mapsto(e',g\theta)$ with record transport $\rho_g$, family
covariance

$$
Q_{g\theta,e'}=(\rho_g)_*Q_{\theta,e}
\tag{8}
$$

implies covariance of (4) under $(\theta,r)\mapsto(g\theta,\rho_g r)$.
$\square$

### Exact meaning

The theorem has no quantum premise. It applies equally to:

1. a family of Hamiltonians;
2. arbitrary response tables;
3. different stochastic transition laws;
4. different whole-history measures;
5. different collapse parameters;
6. different readers; or
7. different empirical theories.

Consequently, the sentence “one enlarged stochastic law covers every member”
has no explanatory force until the sector coordinate is physically sourced.

## 10. Theorem NRL-C — strict monoidal anonymity leaves only identity or uniform reset

### Premises

For every integer $n\ge2$, let $X_n$ be an anonymous $n$-element carrier and
let $K_n$ be a column-stochastic kernel satisfying full $S_n$ relabeling
covariance. Assume that for every $m,n\ge2$, after an arbitrary fixed
bijection $X_m\times X_n\cong X_{mn}$,

$$
K_{mn}=K_m\otimes K_n.
\tag{13}
$$

Crucially, the composite carrier is still declared fully anonymous: every
element of $S_{mn}$ is neutral, including permutations that do not preserve
the displayed factorization.

### Statement

Exactly two families satisfy these premises:

$$
K_n=I_n\quad\forall n\ge2,
\tag{14}
$$

or

$$
K_n=U_n\quad\forall n\ge2,
\qquad
(U_n)_{ij}=\frac1n.
\tag{15}
$$

### Proof

By (12), write the diagonal and off-diagonal values as $a_n,b_n$. In the
product kernel $K_m\otimes K_n$, consider three distinct off-diagonal types:

$$
\begin{array}{ccl}
\text{first same, second different} &:& a_m b_n,\\
\text{first different, second same} &:& b_m a_n,\\
\text{both different} &:& b_m b_n.
\end{array}
\tag{16}
$$

Full $S_{mn}$ covariance makes all three values equal. If $b_n>0$ for some
$n$, then for every $m$,

$$
a_m b_n=b_m b_n
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
a_m=b_m.
\tag{17}
$$

Normalization in (12) gives $a_m=b_m=1/m$ for every $m$, yielding (15).
If no such $n$ exists, then $b_n=0$ for every $n$, normalization gives
$a_n=1$, and (14) follows. Both families plainly satisfy the premises.
$\square$

### Physical interpretation

This is not a theorem that nature must be identity or uniform noise. It proves
that the following package is too poor for nontrivial physics:

```text
ANONYMOUS CONFIGURATIONS
+ EVERY BIJECTION IS PHYSICALLY NEUTRAL
+ THE COMPOSITE RETAINS NO FACTOR RELATION
+ STRICT INDEPENDENT PRODUCT
```

A physical tensor factor, common source, boundary relation, interaction port,
or composition history must therefore survive as physical structure whenever
it affects predictions. That structure may be discrete, continuous, or
neither. The finite theorem does not select its form.

## 11. Theorem NRL-D — a typed factor relation creates response channels but not weights

Let

$$
X=\{1,\ldots,m\}\times\{1,\ldots,n\},
\qquad m,n\ge2,
\tag{18}
$$

and now declare the factor relation physical. Neutral relabelings form only
$S_m\times S_n$, acting separately on each coordinate.

### Statement

Every $(S_m\times S_n)$-covariant stochastic kernel is constant on four
ordered-pair types:

$$
K((i',j')\mid(i,j))=
\begin{cases}
c_{00},&i'=i, j'=j,\\
c_{10},&i'\ne i, j'=j,\\
c_{01},&i'=i, j'\ne j,\\
c_{11},&i'\ne i, j'\ne j,
\end{cases}
\tag{19}
$$

where

$$
c_{uv}\ge0,
\qquad
c_{00}+(m-1)c_{10}+(n-1)c_{01}
+(m-1)(n-1)c_{11}=1.
\tag{20}
$$

Conversely, every tuple satisfying (20) defines such a kernel.

### Proof

The diagonal action of $S_m\times S_n$ on source--target pairs has exactly
the four equality-pattern orbits in (19). Covariance makes the kernel
constant on each orbit. A source column contains one $00$ target, $m-1$
$10$ targets, $n-1$ $01$ targets, and $(m-1)(n-1)$ $11$ targets, yielding
(20). Nonnegativity and the converse are immediate. $\square$

### Independent-product locus

If the two factors have bare kernels with diagonal/off-diagonal pairs
$(a_m,b_m)$ and $(a_n,b_n)$, their product occupies

$$
(c_{00},c_{10},c_{01},c_{11})
=
(a_ma_n,b_ma_n,a_mb_n,b_mb_n),
\tag{21}
$$

and hence obeys

$$
c_{00}c_{11}=c_{10}c_{01}.
\tag{22}
$$

Violation of (22) can witness interaction or a common source at this bounded
scope. It does not select the interacting values in (19).

### Physical interpretation

The factor relation increases the admissible normalized family from one free
parameter to a three-dimensional polytope. Physical structure has made
additional responses expressible; it has not chosen them. This is the exact
finite analogue of the source/reference conclusion in ORS-C and ORS-D.

## 12. Theorem NRL-E — general finite symmetry yields orbital, not coefficient, selection

Let a finite physical automorphism group $G$ act on a finite configuration
carrier $X$. A stochastic kernel is equivariant when

$$
K(gx'\mid gx)=K(x'\mid x)
\quad\forall g\in G.
\tag{23}
$$

### Statement

Every equivariant kernel is constant on the orbits of the diagonal $G$ action
on $X\times X$. Conversely, any nonnegative assignment to those orbitals
that satisfies the source-column normalization conditions defines an
equivariant kernel.

### Proof

Equation (23) identifies every pair in a common diagonal orbit, proving
constancy. An orbital assignment is invariant by construction; imposing the
linear normalization equations gives a stochastic kernel. $\square$

### Consequence

Symmetry can:

1. forbid response channels;
2. identify coefficients;
3. create selection rules;
4. reduce the admissible polytope; and
5. in special cases, leave a singleton.

But in general it provides an orbital basis and a feasible region, not the
coefficient values. A uniqueness claim must print every positivity,
composition, regularity, boundary, and empirical premise responsible for
the singleton.

## 13. Framework reconstruction versus dynamical selection

The primary-source routes now separate cleanly.

### 13.1 Barandes

At the audited scope, a model chooses a configuration space and its fixed
transition probabilities. The contingent standalone probability distribution
then propagates by those laws. The stochastic--quantum dictionary and its
dilations show how Hilbert structure can represent the supplied positive
law.

This supports Hilbert-secondary ontology. It does not provide a universal map
from raw matter, source, intervention, and reader descriptors to the correct
transition law for an unseen experiment.

### 13.2 Operational reconstructions

Hardy, Chiribella--D'Ariano--Perinotti, and Masanes--Mueller show that strong
physical/informational postulates can select quantum operational structure
within declared comparison classes. Their postulates concern capacities,
distinguishability, purification, composition, reversible transformations,
and related framework structure.

Even a successful framework reconstruction admits many physical
transformations, preparations, and interactions. It does not say which member
is implemented by a particular target-blind source packet. Feeding the chosen
quantum transformation back into a positive representation would be a
compiler.

### 13.3 Effective action

Symmetry and locality organize the most general allowed effective action.
They can forbid terms and relate coefficients. The field content, symmetry
breaking pattern, scale regime, and surviving coefficients still carry
physical input. For U0, an effective action is an excellent late comparator
but remains an answer-bearing source if handed to the positive law.

### 13.4 S-matrix bootstrap

Unitarity, analyticity, crossing, symmetry, and spectral assumptions can
bound an allowed S-matrix space and sometimes identify distinguished or
extremal members. This is a serious model for principle-driven dynamical
constraint.

It is not presently a U0 source law because it begins with Lorentzian
dimension, particle/spectral data, complex scattering structure, and
asymptotic notions that v17 has not derived. Moreover, a bounded allowed
region is not automatically a unique member, and an extremality criterion
needs its own physical justification.

### 13.5 Locally covariant QFT

Functorial covariance can express the same physics across backgrounds without
supplying a preferred state for every background. This is a useful warning:
natural transformation structure and state/member selection are different
problems. U0 imports neither the spacetime category nor the QFT algebra.
