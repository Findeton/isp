$$
Q=C-M=C-BA.
\tag{5}

1. an unresolved program with complete law $C$; and

The operational interpretation is important. It makes interference a

## 4. Theorem QC-A — exact affine classification of `qCor`

### Statement

For any finite column-stochastic $A,B,C$, let $M=BA$ and $Q=C-M$. Then:

1. every column of $Q$ sums to zero,

   $$
   \mathbf 1_Z^{T}Q=0;
   \tag{6}
   $$

2. entrywise,

   $$
   -M_{zx}\le Q_{zx}\le 1-M_{zx};
   \tag{7}
   $$

3. each column obeys

   $$
   \lVert Q_{\bullet x}\rVert_1\le2,
   \tag{8}
   $$

   and $\tfrac12\lVert Q_{\bullet x}\rVert_1$ is exactly the total-variation
   distance between the direct and divided endpoint distributions for input
   $x$;
4. if $Q$ is entrywise nonnegative, then $Q=0$;
5. conversely, for fixed $A,B$, every real matrix $Q$ satisfying

   $$
   \mathbf 1_Z^{T}Q=0,
   \qquad
   BA+Q\ge0
   \tag{9}
   $$

   defines a column-stochastic direct endpoint $C=BA+Q$; and
6. in the square bistochastic case, $Q\mathbf 1=0$ as well.

Thus the set of admissible corrections for a fixed divided endpoint is the
translated stochastic polytope

$$
\mathcal Q_M
=
\{Q:\mathbf 1^TQ=0,\ M+Q\ge0\}.
\tag{10}
$$

### Proof

Column stochasticity gives

$$
\mathbf 1^TC=\mathbf 1^T,
\qquad
\mathbf 1^TBA=(\mathbf 1^TB)A=\mathbf 1^TA=\mathbf 1^T,
$$

so (6) follows. Since every $C_{zx}$ lies in $[0,1]$, equation (7) follows
from $Q_{zx}=C_{zx}-M_{zx}$. Equation (8) is the standard $\ell^1$ bound for
the difference of two probability vectors. A nonnegative vector with zero
sum vanishes, proving part 4.

Conversely, (9) makes $C=M+Q$ nonnegative and gives
$\mathbf 1^TC=\mathbf 1^TM=\mathbf 1^T$. Hence $C$ is column stochastic.
For square bistochastic $A,B,C$, both $C$ and $BA$ have unit row sums, so
$Q\mathbf 1=0$. $\square$

### Meaning

Nonzero `qCor` cannot itself be a transition-probability matrix. Its negative
entries are bookkeeping differences between two positive experiments, not
negative actual chances. More importantly, every stochastic direct endpoint
has one such residual. The affine constraints identify the allowed container;
they do not identify the quantum subset.

## 6. Theorem QC-C — four-boundary residual cocycle

For every typed endpoint family $\Gamma_{v:u}$ define

$$
Q_{c:b:a}
=
\Gamma_{c:a}-\Gamma_{c:b}\Gamma_{b:a}.
\tag{17}
$$

For $a<b<c<d$ in the supplied boundary grammar,

$$
\boxed{
\Gamma_{d:c}Q_{c:b:a}+Q_{d:c:a}
=
Q_{d:c:b}\Gamma_{b:a}+Q_{d:b:a}.}
\tag{18}
$$

### Proof

Expand $\Gamma_{d:a}$ first through $c$ and then through $b$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Gamma_{d:a}
&=\Gamma_{d:c}\Gamma_{c:a}+Q_{d:c:a}\\
&=\Gamma_{d:c}\Gamma_{c:b}\Gamma_{b:a}
  +\Gamma_{d:c}Q_{c:b:a}+Q_{d:c:a},
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
$$

while

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Gamma_{d:a}
&=\Gamma_{d:b}\Gamma_{b:a}+Q_{d:b:a}\\
&=\Gamma_{d:c}\Gamma_{c:b}\Gamma_{b:a}
  +Q_{d:c:b}\Gamma_{b:a}+Q_{d:b:a}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{20}
$$

Cancel the common term. $\square$

Equation (18) resembles a cocycle or associator law, but it is an identity for
every endpoint family once the residual is defined by (17). Satisfying it is
necessary bookkeeping consistency, not evidence of quantum composition.
