## 0. Reality-first question

C4 does not ask which interpretation sounds most intuitive. It asks what each
candidate says exists, which law governs it, and what observation could make
that claim more than a choice of representative inside an empirical
equivalence class.

The governing question is:

$$
\boxed{
\text{What physical object is actual, what law gives its chances, and how can
nature distinguish that account?}}
$$

The investigation preserves four separations.

1. Decoherence is not automatically outcome selection.
2. A probability law is not a deterministic selector for its random sample.
3. A complete record law is not automatically a fine-trajectory law.
4. A different mathematical representation is not automatically different
   physics.

---

## 1. Common actuality contract

Every candidate account should be expressible, at least schematically, as

$$
\mathfrak M=
(\mathcal E,\Omega,\mathcal A,\mathcal L,\pi,\mathcal R,
\mathcal X_{\rm act},\mathsf P_{\rm act},\mathsf{Exec},\mathsf{Comp}).
$$

The coordinates are:

| Coordinate | Required meaning |
|---|---|
| $\mathcal E$ | registered physical experiments and interventions |
| $\Omega,\mathcal A$ | candidate history/event carrier and event algebra |
| $\mathcal L$ | predictive law: $D$, $\Gamma$, path measure, wave law, or modified dynamics |
| $\pi$ | physical quotient from microscopic histories to stable records |
| $\mathcal R$ | record algebra on which actual frequencies are compared |
| $\mathcal X_{\rm act}$ | asserted actual referent |
| $\mathsf P_{\rm act}$ | chance law for that referent, if one is asserted |
| $\mathsf{Exec}$ | typed interventions and readers |
| $\mathsf{Comp}$ | rule for composing systems and experiments |

No separate $\mathsf P_{\rm act}$ is needed when $\mathcal L$ is already an
ordinary probability measure on exactly $\mathcal X_{\rm act}$. It is needed,
or the claim must be weakened, when the predictive law is only a quantum
measure, a first-order transition family, a law on records rather than fine
paths, or a universal state with no single-history chance interpretation.

The complete operational profile is

$$
\Phi_{\mathcal E}(\mathfrak M),
$$

including probabilities, instruments, adaptive conditionals, post-
intervention states, failures, composition behavior, records, and resource
outputs. If

$$
\Phi_{\mathcal E}(\mathfrak M)
=\Phi_{\mathcal E}(\mathfrak M'),
$$

then no experiment already in $\mathcal E$ selects between the two accounts.
The difference may still be an independently postulated ontology, but it has
not been empirically derived.

## 2. What C4 has already fixed mathematically

The companion theorem candidate proves:

1. $\mu(A)=D(A,A)$ is an ordinary probability on the full finite event
   algebra exactly when all real interference terms between disjoint events
   vanish;
2. a declared record partition supports ordinary probabilities exactly under
   weak decoherence/consistency;
3. those record probabilities admit many hidden refinements whenever a
   positive record fiber contains several fine histories; and
4. even all pairwise first-order transition laws can leave higher-order path
   probabilities undetermined.

Thus the common input ledger must not jump directly from

$$
D
\quad\text{or}\quad
\Gamma
$$

to “one fine trajectory is sampled” without typing the missing map.

## 3. Branch A — operational record actuality

### Referent

Completed stable laboratory records are actual. No unobserved microscopic
trajectory is asserted.

### Law

Standard quantum instruments or Paper 01's positive pushforward supply an
ordinary law on records. At a decoherent record partition,

$$
p_i=D(R_i,R_i).
$$

### Merit

This is empirically sufficient for laboratory prediction and avoids inventing
unmeasurable microscopic variables.

### Debt

It is deliberately not a deep ontology of closed systems, cosmology, or
unrecorded processes. It does not explain why the complete quantum process
law has its form.

### Empirical status

It is an agnostic stance rather than an empirically distinct dynamics.

## 4. Branch B — Barandes-style indivisible stochastic actuality

### Source-faithful core

Barandes describes quantum systems as stochastic processes in configuration
spaces governed by ordinary probability and indivisible, non-Markovian laws.
The native minimalist dynamics use first-order transition probabilities

$$
\Gamma_{ij}(t\leftarrow t_0)
=p(i,t\mid j,t_0).
$$

The 2025/2026 source explicitly states that there are generically many or
infinitely many compatible complete Kolmogorov towers, called non-Markovian
realizers, and that a process therefore encompasses an equivalence class of
realizers. The Hilbert-space potential is nonunique and gauge-dependent.

Primary source:

- Jacob Barandes, [*Quantum Systems as Indivisible Stochastic Processes*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.21192), especially the first-order law and realizer discussion;
- Jacob Barandes, [*The Stochastic-Quantum Theorem*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.03085).

### Fair reading

This nonuniqueness is not automatically a defect. Barandes can consistently
take the minimalist transition structure, rather than one complete hidden
Kolmogorov tower, as the empirically meaningful law. A particular realization
may be unknowable or physically meaningless beyond the licensed configuration
facts.

### Exact question exposed by C4

The word “trajectory” then admits two readings:

1. configurations are actual at licensed targets, while unlicensed multi-time
   joint facts are not part of the physical claim; or
2. one complete fine path is actual, although its joint law is not fixed by
   the minimalist $\Gamma$.

The first is C4's thin event actuality. The second is thick trajectory
actuality and requires a realizer or a non-Kolmogorov actuality rule.

### What would advance the branch

1. One uniform rule generating the complete process/interface $\Gamma$ from
   physical action, fields, interactions, and boundary data.
2. A clear statement of which multi-time configuration propositions possess
   truth values.
3. A derived or independently motivated law for any claimed complete path.
4. A held-out prediction, resource advantage, or invariant structural result
   not already imported through the quantum target law.

### What would not advance it

- choosing a convenient Markov realizer;
- treating a random seed as the physical law;
- declaring all potential phases gauge while using phase-complete target
  laws as contingent inputs;
- calling empirical underdetermination proof that all realizers are the same
  ontology.

## 5. Branch C — consistent/decoherent histories

### Referent and law

Consistent-histories approaches assign ordinary probabilities to histories in
a family satisfying a consistency/decoherence condition. Griffiths introduced
a criterion under which classical probability rules apply to selected
multi-time event sequences. Gell-Mann and Hartle developed decoherent histories
and quasiclassical realms for closed systems and cosmology.

Primary sources:

- Robert Griffiths, [*Consistent histories and the interpretation of quantum mechanics*](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01015734), *J. Stat. Phys.* **36**, 219 (1984);
- Murray Gell-Mann and James Hartle, [*Classical Equations for Quantum Systems*](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9210010);
- Murray Gell-Mann and James Hartle, [*Quantum Mechanics in the Light of Quantum Cosmology*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.04605).

### Exact contribution

The approach makes probability claims only where interference is sufficiently
controlled. This aligns with Proposition C4-B.

### Remaining selection coordinate

Different consistent families can answer different questions. A claim about
one universal fine history requires a rule for relating, choosing, or limiting
those families. C4 does not presume that every version of consistent histories
needs one preferred realm; it records that probabilities inside a declared
realm do not by themselves select a unique global fine-history algebra.

### Empirical status

At standard scope this is generally an interpretation/formulation of ordinary
quantum predictions, not a modified dynamics. A realm-selection principle
would become new physics only if it changes or further constrains complete
operational predictions.

## 6. Branch D — quantum measure, preclusion, and coevents

### Referent and law

Sorkin's quantum-measure programme weakens Kolmogorov additivity and treats

$$
\mu(A)=D(A,A)
$$

as a generalized measure or propensity. The early realist proposal speaks of
one history and uses zero-measure preclusion. Later coevent programmes replace
naive single fine histories with truth-valued maps on events when quantum
preclusion makes the classical reading fail.

Primary sources:

- Rafael Sorkin, [*Quantum Mechanics as Quantum Measure Theory*](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9401003);
- Rafael Sorkin, [*Quantum Measure Theory and its Interpretation*](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9507057);
- Yousef Ghazi-Tabatabai, [*Quantum Measure Theory: A New Interpretation*](https://arxiv.org/abs/0906.0294).

### Merit

This branch takes the history event algebra seriously without pretending that
$\mu$ is a classical probability. It is naturally suited to closed-system and
spacetime-history questions.

### Debt

Preclusion alone need not select a unique reality, and naive single-history
preclusion can fail in contextual examples. A coevent scheme changes the
actual referent and adds a reality filter. Conditional preclusion, approximate
prediction, continuum/QFT control, and physical composition require explicit
laws.

### C4 classification

This is not a hidden Kolmogorov refinement of record probabilities. It is a
different actuality architecture and must be compared through its complete
operational profile.

## 7. Branch E — Bohm-type configuration actuality

### Referent and law

Bohm's 1952 construction supplements the wavefunction with an actual
configuration and a guiding dynamics. In quantum equilibrium it reproduces
the standard nonrelativistic quantum predictions while providing continuous
individual trajectories.

Primary source:

- David Bohm, [*A Suggested Interpretation of the Quantum Theory in Terms of “Hidden” Variables. I*](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.85.166), *Phys. Rev.* **85**, 166 (1952), together with Part II.

### What it pays

The configuration referent and trajectory law are explicit. The wavefunction
or equivalent phase-complete guiding object remains nomologically active; it
has not been eliminated by asserting particle positions.

### Empirical status

At equilibrium and ordinary nonrelativistic scope the interpretation is
constructed to agree with standard quantum predictions. The ontology is not
selected merely by that agreement. Relativistic QFT, particle creation,
foliation, and gravity require additional model choices; C4 makes no universal
no-go claim about them.

### Discriminator requirement

A Bohm-type successor earns physical selection only through an invariant
structural advantage, a derived input, or a controlled deviation—not merely
through visualizable paths.

## 8. Branch F — objective stochastic collapse

### Referent and law

GRW-type theories modify the ordinary Hamiltonian dynamics with spontaneous
stochastic localization. Depending on the formulation, the primitive
ontology may be collapse events, localized matter density, or another
explicit macroscopic referent.

Primary source:

- G. C. Ghirardi, A. Rimini, and T. Weber, [*Unified dynamics for microscopic and macroscopic systems*](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.34.470), *Phys. Rev. D* **34**, 470 (1986).

### Scientific distinction

Unlike empirically equivalent interpretations, an objective-collapse law
changes the dynamics. Its rates, localization scales, energy/noise effects,
and amplification behavior are physical parameters and can in principle be
constrained by experiment.

### Merit

This is the cleanest branch in which actuality and the quantum-to-classical
transition become new testable physics rather than a representational choice.

### Debt

The modification and its constants are postulates unless independently
derived. Relativistic, field-theoretic, conservation-law, and gravitational
completion remain nontrivial. C4 does not derive a collapse law from
decoherence, $D$, or $\Gamma$.

## 9. Branch G — Everett/relative-state actuality

### Referent and law

Everett retains universal unitary dynamics and describes subsystem states
relative to correlated records. There is no fundamental stochastic collapse
selecting one global branch in the original architecture.

Primary source:

- Hugh Everett III, [*“Relative State” Formulation of Quantum Mechanics*](https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.29.454), *Rev. Mod. Phys.* **29**, 454 (1957).

### C4 classification

This branch rejects the single-global-history actuality premise rather than
failing to provide its selector. Decoherence can identify stable branch-like
record structure, but a single realized branch is not part of the basic law.

### Open coordinates

The ontology of branches, quantitative probability/typicality, and exact
branch identity must be stated by a particular Everettian account. C4 does not
adjudicate those debates by counting branches or by assuming a collapse
question the branch explicitly rejects.

## 10. Decoherence: what it earns and what it does not

Environmental decoherence suppresses interference between stable pointer
records and helps explain why a classical record algebra is robust. Zurek's
review describes environment-induced superselection and stable pointer
states.

Primary source:

- Wojciech Zurek, [*Decoherence, einselection, and the quantum origins of the classical*](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0105127), *Rev. Mod. Phys.* **75**, 715 (2003).

Within C4, decoherence can earn:

1. approximate weak or medium decoherence of a record partition;
2. stability and redundancy of particular record observables;
3. suppression bounds for interference terms; and
4. a controlled classical-probability approximation on that record algebra.

It does not by itself earn:

1. one selected fine history;
2. a collapse event;
3. a unique hidden refinement of each record;
4. a unique consistent family;
5. the Born weights without the surrounding quantum law; or
6. a native gravitational ontology.

This is a typing statement, not a criticism of decoherence. Decoherence solves
the dynamical stability/interference part of the classical-record problem.
Different actuality branches deliberately add or reject different remaining
questions.

## 11. Empirical discriminator map

The following tests are logically distinct.

### 11.1 Complete operational equivalence

If two candidates agree on every registered intervention, adaptive
continuation, record, and composition, no experiment in the registered class
selects between them. The correct result is an empirical equivalence class,
not a winner chosen by taste.

### 11.2 Newly physical cut

Turning an unrecorded intermediate cut into a controlled interaction can test
the candidate's complete process law. It does not reveal what would have
happened in the original unmeasured experiment unless the theory supplies a
cross-experiment identity theorem.

### 11.3 Collapse deviations

Mass, coherence time, spatial separation, spontaneous noise, heating, and
radiation can constrain objective-collapse parameters. The protocol must
freeze the model and parameters before inspecting the held-out data.

### 11.4 Locality and causal structure

Bell and sequential-contextuality experiments constrain combinations of
locality, measurement independence, noncontextuality, and definite values.
They do not refute every one-history ontology. Each candidate must print which
premise it rejects and where the required nonlocal, contextual, retrocausal,
or global information lives.

### 11.5 Gravity coupling

Different primitive ontologies could source gravity differently: expectation
values, branch-relative matter, stochastic collapse density, particles,
events, or a quantum gravitational history. But a proposed sourcing law is new
physics. Existing agreement with ordinary quantum experiments does not select
one, and a gravity-mediated entanglement observation alone does not uniquely
adjudicate every ontology.

### 11.6 Uniform-law advantage

A candidate can advance without an immediate numerical deviation if it derives
a previously independent action, composition rule, field content, coupling,
or limit while competitors must declare it. The dependency reduction must be
invariant and scalable, not a relabeling of the quantum target law.

## 12. Gravity relevance without a gravity claim

A closed-system quantum-gravity history law might take the schematic form

$$
D(A,B)
\sim
\int_A\mathcal Dg\,\mathcal D\phi
\int_B\mathcal Dg'\,\mathcal D\phi'
\exp\left(
\frac{i}{\hbar}
\bigl[S[g,\phi]-S[g',\phi']\bigr]
\right)
\rho_{\rm bdry}.
$$

This form is motivation, not a v17 construction. It presupposes or owes:

1. the history carrier and event algebra;
2. gauge quotient and measure;
3. gravitational and matter action;
4. boundary/cosmological state;
5. record/decoherence mechanism;
6. composition and locality;
7. an actuality stance for geometry; and
8. recovery of common operational geometry for matter and clocks.

Decoherence between semiclassical geometries would license approximate
probabilities for a geometry-record partition. It would not alone select one
fine metric history or derive Einstein dynamics.

## 13. Selector-principle tournament

C4 permits a principle to enter only through one of five doors.

| Door | What must be shown |
|---|---|
| empirical | a frozen deviation or new prediction |
| derivational | a previously declared input follows from narrower physics |
| invariant | the claimed actuality survives gauge/representation quotient |
| compositional | one uniform law governs subsystems, interactions, and held-outs |
| limiting | a controlled quantum, classical, relativistic, or gravitational limit |

The following do not select a physical actuality law by themselves:

- simplicity without a fixed coding language;
- maximum entropy without a physical constraint theorem;
- “one outcome is observed”;
- classical visualizability;
- a preferred basis named after the apparatus;
- environmental decoherence alone;
- stochastic simulation code;
- adding a random seed;
- philosophical preference for determinism or indeterminism; or
- exact reproduction of a target law that was supplied as input.

## 15. Best current inference

The strongest reality-first conclusion is:

$$
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c}
\text{interference law }D\\
\Downarrow\ \text{decoherent physical records}\\
\text{ordinary record probabilities}\\
\not\Downarrow\\
\text{unique fine-history law or ontology}.
\end{array}}
$$

Barandes supplies an important candidate answer: make ordinary stochastic
configuration facts primary and treat Hilbert ingredients as secondary. C4's
remaining question is not whether one random outcome requires a hidden cause.
It is whether the native $\Gamma$ architecture supplies a uniform physical law
and a sufficiently precise actuality referent without importing the complete
quantum process it is meant to explain.

Objective collapse supplies the clearest empirical route but changes the
physics. Bohm and Everett supply clear but very different ontologies while
retaining phase-complete quantum nomology. Consistent histories and quantum
measure theory take the history algebra seriously but need explicit rules for
which propositions receive ordinary probability or truth. Operational
record-only quantum theory remains empirically adequate but ontologically
modest.

The current data select none of them.
