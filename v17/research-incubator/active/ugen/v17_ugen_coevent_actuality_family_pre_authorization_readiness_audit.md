## 4. Static multiplicative scheme: exact finite object

Let $\Omega$ be a finite nonempty history space and
$\mathcal A=2^\Omega$ its event algebra. Let

$$
\mu:\mathcal A\longrightarrow[0,\infty)
$$

be a normalized level-2 quantum measure, ordinarily obtained as

$$
\mu(A)=D(A,A)
$$

from a supplied decoherence functional $D$.

A coevent is a map

$$
\phi:\mathcal A\longrightarrow\mathbb Z_2.
$$

MPS requires a nonzero multiplicative coevent:

$$
\phi(A\cap B)=\phi(A)\phi(B).
$$

For finite $\Omega$, every such coevent has a unique nonempty support
$F\subseteq\Omega$ and is of the form

$$
F^*(A)=
\begin{cases}
1,&F\subseteq A,\\
0,&F\nsubseteq A.
\end{cases}
$$

Define the exact null-event family

$$
\mathcal Z_\mu
=
\{Z\in\mathcal A:\mu(Z)=0\}.
$$

The phrase "null family" is binding. For a quantum measure it need not be an
ideal under subsets or unions.

### 4.1 Preclusion

$F^*$ is preclusive exactly when

$$
F\nsubseteq Z
\qquad
\text{for every }Z\in\mathcal Z_\mu.
$$

This follows immediately because $F^*(Z)=1$ precisely when $F\subseteq Z$.

### 4.2 Primitivity

For multiplicative coevents, $G^*$ dominates $F^*$ exactly when
$G\subseteq F$. Therefore $F^*$ is primitive preclusive precisely when $F$
is inclusion-minimal among the nonempty subsets satisfying preclusion.

The MPS possibility set is consequently

$$
\mathfrak M(\mu)
=
\left\{
F^*:
\begin{array}{l}
F\ne\varnothing,\\
F\nsubseteq Z\text{ for every }Z\in\mathcal Z_\mu,\\
\text{no proper nonempty }G\subset F\text{ has that property}
\end{array}
\right\}.
$$

The one-world claim is:

$$
\text{one }\phi_{\rm actual}\in\mathfrak M(\mu)\text{ is realized}.
$$

MPS itself gives the admissible set. It does not attach an ordinary
probability to its elements.

## 5. Theorem COE-A — exact null-family invariance

### Statement

Let $\mu$ and $\nu$ be normalized quantum measures on the same finite event
algebra. If

$$
\mathcal Z_\mu=\mathcal Z_\nu,
$$

then

$$
\boxed{\mathfrak M(\mu)=\mathfrak M(\nu).}
$$

### Proof

Preclusivity of $F^*$ is the condition that $F$ is not contained in any member
of the exact null family. Equal null families therefore give exactly the same
preclusive supports. Primitivity is inclusion-minimality within that same set
of supports. The primitive sets are consequently equal. $\square$

### Exact meaning

MPS factors through

$$
\mu
\longmapsto
\mathcal Z_\mu
\longmapsto
\mathfrak M(\mu).
$$

All nonzero quantitative values of $\mu$ are discarded by this eligibility
map. This is not a criticism of preclusion as ontology; it is a precise account
of which part of the predictive law MPS reads.

The primary literature already states that the measure cannot in general be
reconstructed from the potentially real coevents. COE-A is the explicit
finite factorization behind that statement, not a priority claim.

## 6. Corollary COE-B — binary quantitative blindness

Let

$$
\Omega=\{h,t\}
$$

and for any $p\in(0,1)$ define the ordinary classical measure

$$
\mu_p(\{h\})=p,
\qquad
\mu_p(\{t\})=1-p.
$$

For every such $p$,

$$
\mathcal Z_{\mu_p}=\{\varnothing\}.
$$

Every nonempty support is preclusive, but the primitive supports are the two
singletons. Hence

$$
\boxed{
\mathfrak M(\mu_p)=\{h^*,t^*\}
\quad\text{for all }p\in(0,1).
}
$$

Yet the observable record law is

$$
P(h)=p,
\qquad
P(t)=1-p.
$$

Thus the MPS admissibility set does not contain the bias.

This does not show that classical probability is incomplete. The supplied
measure $\mu_p$ is already its chance law. It shows only that the MPS
possibility-set map is not itself that chance law.

## 7. Theorem COE-C — no null-family-only quantitative selector

Suppose a proposed rule assigns a probability distribution
$\rho_\mu$ on $\mathfrak M(\mu)$ using only either $\mathcal Z_\mu$ or
$\mathfrak M(\mu)$. Suppose further that for every binary classical
$\mu_p$ it is required to reproduce

$$
\rho_{\mu_p}(h^*)=p.
$$

Then no such rule exists for all $p\in(0,1)$.

### Proof

COE-B gives the same input null family and the same MPS possibility set for
every $p$. Any function of that input alone must return the same distribution.
The required value $p$ varies. $\square$

### Scope

COE-C excludes only selectors whose input is the MPS eligibility data. It does
not exclude:

1. using the full quantum measure directly;
2. weak Cournot prediction;
3. a separately supplied probability law on coevents;
4. Gudder generation filters;
5. a future physical law with additional structure; or
6. irreducible possibilistic actuality with no coevent chance distribution.

Any full-measure selector must be printed as an additional law, not attributed
to preclusion and primitivity alone.

## 8. Prediction is retained quantum measure plus weak Cournot

The coevent literature explicitly distinguishes potential reality from
quantitative prediction.

### 8.1 Why a direct probability on MPS coevents is not automatic

One might seek an ordinary probability $P_{\mathfrak M}$ on coevents satisfying

$$
P_{\mathfrak M}\{\phi:\phi(A)=1\}=\mu(A)
$$

for every event $A$. Ghazi-Tabatabai and Wallden prove that, if the support is
multiplicative and the equality is required for all events, positive-mass
coevents must also satisfy the grade-2/quadratic coevent condition. Since the
quadratic scheme had independent counterexamples, this blocks the naive
all-event transfer onto MPS.

The quantifier "all events" is essential. The theorem does not exclude an
ordinary distribution reproducing probabilities only on one qualified record
partition.

### 8.2 Weak Cournot

The adopted published route instead keeps exact PPC coevents as the possible
realities and uses the quantum measure on repeated trials. For a question
$A$ selected in advance and a declared tolerance $\epsilon$,

$$
\mu(A)\le\epsilon
$$

is treated as a prediction that $A$ will not occur in the repeated trial; if it
does occur, the assumed quantum measure is regarded as falsified.

This earns an empirically meaningful frequentist bridge. It also leaves three
separate objects:

$$
\boxed{
\text{quantum measure}
\quad+
\text{coevent possibility scheme}
\quad+
\text{weak-Cournot prediction rule}.
}
$$

The tolerance and preselected question are part of the prediction contract.
They are not outputs of MPS.

### 8.3 Approximate preclusion is not a free repair

Replacing exact preclusion by

$$
\mu(A)<\epsilon\Longrightarrow\phi(A)=0
$$

would make the ontology sensitive to nonzero magnitudes. The source analysis
shows that this strong/approximate route can exclude every fine outcome in a
repeated classical trial or admit coevents that answer ordinary recorded
alternatives nonclassically. The published treatment therefore returns to
exact PPC ontology plus weak Cournot.

This is a semantic choice, not a numerical implementation issue.

## 9. Classical recovery and the record gate

### 9.1 Exact finite classical recovery

For a finite Kolmogorov measure, the union of null events is null. Every
primitive preclusive multiplicative coevent is then a singleton classical
coevent supported on a nonnull history:

$$
\mathfrak M(\mu)=\{\gamma^*: \gamma\text{ lies in no null event}\}.
$$

This is a genuine classical-limit pass.

### 9.2 Conditional record classicality

For quantum measures, the literature proves classical action of MPS on
qualified partitions, including preclusively separable partitions and, under
the stated conditions, algebras associated with sufficiently permanent
macroscopic records or final orthogonal measurement outcomes.

This supports a possible measurement-record story:

$$
\text{anhomomorphic microscopic truth}
\longrightarrow
\text{Boolean truth on a licensed record algebra}.

It does not itself construct:

1. the material detector;

## 10. Static MPS and the indefinite-time obstruction

The $n$-site hopper supplies an exact semantic stress test. It is a discrete
unitary particle model with arbitrarily extended time and a standard quantum
measure on time-finite cylinder events.

For any nontrivial time-finite event $E$ in the stated class, the source proves
the existence of a later time-finite null event $F$ with

$$
E\subseteq F,
\qquad
\mu(F)=0.
$$

Multiplicativity implies downward denial:

$$
\phi(F)=0, E\subseteq F
\Longrightarrow
\phi(E)=0.
$$

Consequently every nontrivial time-finite event is denied by every allowed
static multiplicative coevent in that model.

### 10.1 What fails

1. Ordinary finite-time happenings cannot be affirmed.
2. Later null events can erase the possibility of an earlier event.
3. Determining present possibility can require surveying the indefinite
   future.
4. Operational prediction becomes impractical even before QFT or gravity.

### 10.2 What does not follow

The theorem does not show that:

1. every quantum measure has this property;
2. every all-at-once block ontology is inconsistent;
3. every coevent scheme fails;
4. no positive indivisible law exists; or
5. the universe must have a becoming ontology.

It does show that global static MPS is not an acceptable generic indefinite
process law without an independently justified restriction or changed
scheme.

## 11. Evolving coevents are a different law

ECS begins with an explicitly staged family

$$
(\Omega_t,\mathcal A_t,\mu_t)_{t\ge0}
$$

and restriction maps from stage $t$ to $t-1$. A coevent $\phi_t$ is a
prolongation of $\phi_{t-1}$ when it agrees with the previous truth valuation
on every event carried from the earlier algebra.

The basic evolving rule chooses

$$
\phi_t
\in
\operatorname{MinSupp}
\left(
\operatorname{PreclusiveProlongations}(\phi_{t-1})
\right).
$$

### 11.1 Earned passes

At the source's finite discrete scope, ECS:

1. never consults stages later than $t$ when selecting stage-$t$ possibilities;
2. preserves the truth values of the strict past by exact prolongation;
3. excludes null histories from the support of allowed coevents;
4. is invariant under adding or removing null histories in the stated sense;
5. reduces to the classical evolving scheme when the supplied measure is
   classical; and
6. always has a next classical prolongation in the classical case.

### 11.2 Binding debts printed by the source

ECS also states that it assigns no probability to the allowed coevents. More
importantly, its own discussion leaves open:

1. whether all ordinary measurement alternatives are treated classically;
2. whether changing the stage at which the scheme begins changes late reality;
3. whether evolving isolated laboratories sequentially or simultaneously
   gives the same result;
4. whether adding a disjoint unentangled system changes the local reality;
5. whether every nonzero-probability measurable event is affirmed by some
   allowed coevent; and
6. which of the basic, maximally affirmative, or globally minimal variants is
   physically correct.

The source reports explicit ordering differences in GHZ controls for some
variants and nonclassical single-time measurement outcomes for the basic and
maximally affirmative schemes.

### 11.3 v17 disposition

ECS solves the specific future-dependence defect by adding a staged actuality
grammar. It thereby consumes exactly the order structure that Paper 04/04B is
trying to earn physically. It is a valuable hostile control, but not a
dimension-neutral, clock-free parent and not a semantic repair of MPS.

## 12. Gudder generation filters: the genuine selector opening

GEN uses the full quantitative quantum measure rather than only its exact null
events. In finite scope, a quantum measure $\mu$ 1-generates a coevent $\phi$
when there exists a strictly positive density $f$ such that, for every event
$A$,

$$
\mu(A)=\int_A f\,d\phi.
$$

It 2-generates $\phi$ when a corresponding positive symmetric two-variable
density gives a double quantum integral representation. The later
actualization filter further broadens the double-integral condition.

### 12.1 What this route earns

1. It is sensitive to the nonzero quantitative content of $\mu$.
2. A generated coevent is preclusive.
3. At the source's finite scope, a quantum measure cannot 1-generate two
   distinct coevents.
4. If two 2-generated coevents are quadratic, they are equal.
5. The scheme therefore contains a real mathematical route from a supplied
   quantum measure to a unique actual coevent when existence and the required
   class conditions hold.

This directly blocks any claim that all coevent approaches are merely
null-family possibility filters.

### 12.2 What remains open or fails

1. Many quantum measures do not 1-generate any coevent.
2. The sources do not prove universal 2-generation.
3. Ordinary measures generally do not 1- or 2-generate coevents except in
   special simple cases, so classical recovery is not generic for this filter.
4. Actualization is broader but can select more than one coevent.
5. An actualized coevent need not be preclusive.
6. The density functions need not be physically selected or unique.
7. All proved constructions are finite event-space mathematics.
8. No general intervention, reader, subsystem, regional-gluing, QFT, or
   gravity theorem is supplied.

### 12.3 Scientific interpretation

GEN is more than a relabeling of MPS, but it still starts from

$$
\boxed{\text{the complete quantum measure }\mu.}
$$

It may solve actuality selection for special supplied quantum processes. It
does not generate the quantum predictive nomology from a narrower physical
law. It therefore belongs to Rival B's actuality column, not to the native U0
source-completion column.

## 13. Positive transfer is representation, not physical descent

Gudder's finite transfer theorem shows that a quantum measure on an event
algebra can be represented by an ordinary measure on a sufficiently large
anhomomorphic-logic space.

This establishes:

$$
\boxed{
\text{finite quantum-measure data admit an ordinary-positive enlarged-space lift}.
}
$$

The same source also establishes the walls:

1. the transferred ordinary measure is not unique in general;
2. the full anhomomorphic space can be much larger than the original event
   space;
3. the multiplicative coevent subspace is not adequate for transferring all
   quantum measures; and
4. the transfer receives the target quantum measure as input.

Therefore XFER is a positive representation control analogous to the Gaussian
positive lift in the R1/native-gap synthesis. It does not by itself select:

1. the physical latent variables;
2. the actual coevent;
3. the source law;
4. the intervention grammar;
5. the composition law; or
6. the gravity-coupled referent.
