**Theorem EDR-A.** The exchange packet $\mathsf X(A)$ does not identify a

### Proof

$$
\operatorname{Obs}_{\mathsf X}(R)
=

\text{for all }R,R'\in\mathcal R.

Because $\mathcal R$ contains at least two elements, the fiber of
$\operatorname{Obs}_{\mathsf X}$ is nontrivial. No function of the exchange

## 6. Theorem EDR-B — continuous real exchange/deformation classification

Take

$$
A=(\mathbb R,+),
\qquad
G=(\mathbb R,+).
\tag{12}
$$

**Theorem EDR-B.** Every jointly continuous bicharacter

$$
\beta:\mathbb R\times\mathbb R\longrightarrow U(1)
\tag{13}
$$

has a unique $\kappa\in\mathbb R$ such that

$$
\boxed{
\beta_\kappa(a,s)=e^{i\kappa as}.
}
\tag{14}
$$

### Proof

For fixed $a$, the map $s\mapsto\beta(a,s)$ is a continuous character of
$(\mathbb R,+)$. Hence there is a unique real number $k(a)$ such that

$$
\beta(a,s)=e^{ik(a)s}.
\tag{15}
$$

The first identity in (11) gives

$$
e^{ik(a_1+a_2)s}
=
e^{i[k(a_1)+k(a_2)]s}
\quad\text{for every }s.
\tag{16}
$$

Uniqueness of the real character coefficient gives

$$
k(a_1+a_2)=k(a_1)+k(a_2).
\tag{17}
$$

Joint continuity of $\beta$ implies continuity of $k$ at zero. To see this,
take any sequence $a_n\to0$. If a bounded subsequence of $k(a_n)$ converged
to a nonzero value, (15) would contradict
$\beta(a_n,s)\to\beta(0,s)=1$ for a suitable fixed $s$. If an unbounded
subsequence existed, choose a further subsequence with
$|k(a_n)|\to\infty$ and set $s_n=\pi/|k(a_n)|$ with the sign chosen so that
$k(a_n)s_n=\pi$. Then $(a_n,s_n)\to(0,0)$ while
$\beta(a_n,s_n)=-1$, contradicting joint continuity and
$\beta(0,0)=1$. Hence $k(a_n)\to0$ for every $a_n\to0$. An additive real
function continuous at zero is linear. Therefore $k(a)=\kappa a$ for one
unique $\kappa$, which gives (14). $\square$

## 11. Theorem EDR-F — calibration can test transfer but cannot create a law

$$

**Theorem EDR-F.** Agreement on the finite set $\mathcal K$ does not derive
the global form (31). There exist infinitely many continuous positive record

### Proof

Choose a bounded continuous function $h(a,s)$ that vanishes at every
calibration point and is nonzero at the held-out point. Choose a nonzero

not an integer multiple of $2\pi$ and define

$$
\widetilde f(a,s)
=
\cos\!\left(\kappa as+\epsilon h(a,s)\right).

$$

This automatically lies in $[-1,1]$. If the unperturbed held-out phase is an
integer multiple of $\pi$, choose $\epsilon h(a_\star,s_\star)$ not to be a
multiple of $2\pi$; otherwise choose it outside the discrete set that leaves
the cosine unchanged. Then

$$
\widetilde P(r\mid a,s)
=
\frac12[1+r\widetilde f(a,s)]
\tag{38}
$$

is continuous, normalized, positive, agrees with (31) on $\mathcal K$, and
differs at the held-out point. $\square$

A held-out prediction can therefore support a compact nomological class; it
