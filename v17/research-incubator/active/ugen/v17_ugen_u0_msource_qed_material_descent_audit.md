## 9. A solvable geometry-to-amplitude control

An exact idealized control shows that the source problem is not merely a
change of vocabulary.

Consider a monochromatic laboratory field of angular frequency \(\omega\)
traversing a birefringent plate of measured thickness \(L\), ordinary and
extraordinary refractive indices \(n_o(\omega),n_e(\omega)\), and physical
optic-axis orientation \(\theta\). The retardance is

$$
\delta(\omega)
=
\frac{\omega L}{c}
\bigl[n_e(\omega)-n_o(\omega)\bigr].
\tag{16}
$$

The Maxwell solution induces the secondary Jones/Hilbert representative

$$
J(\theta,\delta)
=
R(-\theta)
\begin{pmatrix}
e^{i\delta/2}&0\\
0&e^{-i\delta/2}
\end{pmatrix}
R(\theta).
\tag{17}
$$

The matrix is not public input. It is an output of measured thickness,
orientation, dispersion, frequency, and the fixed wave law.

For a balanced two-path interferometer with physical optical-length
difference

$$
\Delta\ell_{\rm opt}
=
\int_{\gamma_1}n(\mathbf x,\omega)\,ds
-\int_{\gamma_2}n(\mathbf x,\omega)\,ds,
\tag{18}
$$

the ideal single-event output probabilities are

$$
p_\pm
=
\frac12\left[
1\pm
\cos\!\left(
\frac{\omega\Delta\ell_{\rm opt}}{c}
\phi_{\rm bs}
\right)
\right],
\tag{19}
$$

where \(\phi_{\rm bs}\) is fixed by the physical beam-splitter boundary
solution, not fitted fringe position.

For two generic retarders,

$$
J(\theta_2,\delta_2)J(\theta_1,\delta_1)
\neq
J(\theta_1,\delta_1)J(\theta_2,\delta_2).
\tag{20}
$$

Thus one fixed component law predicts a held-out order effect without a
per-order response table.

An exact-parameter witness uses horizontal input, two quarter-wave plates
with \((\theta_1,\theta_2)=(0,\pi/4)\), and a diagonal output analyzer
\(\lvert D\rangle=(\lvert H\rangle+\lvert V\rangle)/\sqrt2\). Direct
substitution in (17) gives

$$
\Pr(D\mid J_2J_1,H)=\frac12,
\qquad
\Pr(D\mid J_1J_2,H)=0.
$$

No order-dependent coefficient was introduced. The difference comes from
the measured physical axes and the common wave law.

### What this control earns

2. Relative phase can be physical without being a fundamental phase field.
3. A fixed law can predict new depth and order.
4. Presentation changes of axes or time origin act covariantly.

The phrase "input vacuum" hides a physical statement. A complete preparation

1. which material and field degrees are included;
2. the physical cooling, pumping, heralding, and reset operations;
3. the environmental boundary condition;
4. how temperature and residual occupation are measured;
5. whether the prepared state is equilibrium, metastable, driven, or

6. which distinctions are discarded and where they remain; and
7. how the preparation transfers to an independently built source.

### 10.1 Equilibrium control

Given a supplied quantum Hamiltonian \(H_e\), inverse temperature \(\beta\),
and an exact equilibrium postulate, one may define

$$
\rho_{\beta,e}
=
\frac{e^{-\beta H_e}}
{\operatorname{Tr}e^{-\beta H_e}}.
\tag{21}
$$

At optical frequencies and low thermal occupation this can approximate the
field vacuum in relevant modes. But (21):

1. presupposes \(H_e\), quantum statistical mechanics, time translation, and
   equilibration;
2. does not describe a driven pump source by itself;
3. does not certify absence of stray light or nonequilibrium excitations; and
4. does not select the actual microscopic state of the laboratory.

It is a legitimate known-physics preparation control, not a native U0
derivation.

This can improve purity and source certainty, but it uses the herald reader

### 10.3 Physical target for the first bounded preparation map

### 11.1 Linear response

1. atomic species and crystal structure;
2. exchange-correlation approximation and many-body corrections;

4. fabrication-specific strain, domains, interfaces, and disorder; and
5. the background quantum many-body framework.

Agreement for selected materials is evidence for the effective chain, not a
theorem that every fabricated component is predicted exactly.

### 11.3 Classical reverse-process control

Classical sum-frequency generation can constrain the same mode-overlap
integrals that determine low-gain SPDC. This is an excellent pretarget check
if performed on:

1. an independent material coupon;
2. separate component modules;
3. frequencies or port pairs not equal to the held-out target; or
4. a transfer implementation.

It becomes target-equivalent if the full assembled device is probed in every
sealed input/output mode with enough phase-sensitive resolution to reconstruct
\(\Phi^{(e)}\). Calling that "classical" does not restore source credit.
