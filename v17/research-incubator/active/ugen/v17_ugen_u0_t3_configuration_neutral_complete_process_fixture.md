## 2. Configuration-neutral physical procedure type

### Definition T3.1 — physical module manifest

A physical module is

$$
M=
(\mathrm{id},\mathsf{In},\mathsf{Out},U,
 A,\mathfrak T_{\rm cal},R,F,V,K),
\tag{4}
$$

where:

1. $\mathrm{id}$ is a versioned apparatus identity;
2. $\mathsf{In}$ and $\mathsf{Out}$ are empirically connectable port types;
3. $U$ is the measurable setting space;
4. $A$ is the actuator and metrology record at a setting;
5. $\mathfrak T_{\rm cal}$ is the deliberately incomplete tester family;
6. $R$ is the raw reader interface, if any;
7. $F$ is the failure/no-output alphabet;
8. $V$ is the provenance and uncertainty graph; and
9. $K$ is the resource charge.

Equation (4) contains no target-complete transition matrix, wavefunction,
Hamiltonian, action, phase, connection, holonomy, process tensor, or
hidden-state response table. An independently measured engineering model may
enter only as a provenance-typed calibration field after T2 shows, relative
to the frozen decoder closures, that it does not determine the held-out
complete process. Otherwise it belongs in the sealed comparator.

The field $\mathrm{id}$ authenticates which apparatus was used. It may not be
the sole input to a response-table lookup. Any candidate dependence on nominal
identity is exposed in $K$ as advice and tested on a separately built or
independently recalibrated module of the same public calibration type.

### Definition T3.2 — ports

A port type states only:

1. which physical modules can be connected;
2. which macroscopic conditions make the connection executable;
3. which setting or record channels cross the connection;
4. which losses and failures are observable; and
5. the uncertainty and provenance of those facts.

A port is not asserted to carry a particle, qubit, field value, lattice site,
trajectory segment, Hilbert factor, or spacetime event. A candidate may
propose such a referent and must charge it.

### Definition T3.3 — executable program

An executable program $p$ is a finite typed laboratory dependency graph:

$$
p=(V_p,E_p,\prec_p,\rho_p).
\tag{5}
$$

Here $V_p$ contains physical module invocations, $E_p$ contains port
connections, $\prec_p$ contains setting and stable-record dependencies, and
$\rho_p$ is the randomization protocol for freely varied registered settings.

The graph direction is an experimenter-supplied instruction order. It is not
a fundamental global clock, microscopic causal order, or emergent spacetime.

### Definition T3.4 — calibration equivalence

For two module procedures $M,M'$ of the same public type,

$$
M\simeq_{\rm cal}M'
\tag{6}
$$

only when every tester in their frozen $\mathfrak T_{\rm cal}$ agrees within
the registered uncertainty. This statement does not quantify over held-out
continuations and is not complete operational equivalence.

Every equivalence class retains:

1. the tested settings;
2. the tester list;
3. the uncertainty;
4. the untested continuation list; and
5. the implementation identities.

### Definition T3.6 — complete target law

$$

p\longmapsto P_e(\,dr\mid p).
\tag{8}
$$

Endpoint counts or a selected Bell/interference statistic are not the target.
The target is the complete registered transcript law, within the declared

### Definition T3.7 — operational prefix condition

If $q$ is a completed laboratory prefix and $f,f'$ are alternative later

$$
\operatorname{Marg}_{q}
P_e(\,dr\mid q\circ f)
=
P_e(\,dr_q\mid q)
=
\operatorname{Marg}_{q}
P_e(\,dr\mid q\circ f').
\tag{9}
$$

Equation (9) is a no-backwards-control condition for the registered laboratory

## 4. Fixture cell I — interference, markers, and records

Cell I is a physical interferometric parent. It can be implemented with
photonic, neutron, atom, phonon, superconducting, or other apparatus, but each
implementation must fill (4) with real hardware.

### 4.1 Required modules

1. **Source $S_I$.** A versioned preparation procedure with trigger, rate,
   spectrum or other source diagnostics, and failure records.
2. **Alternative-forming module $A_I$.** A physical device with one input and
   at least two operational output connections. No beam-splitter matrix is
   public input.
3. **Continuous actuator $C_I(u)$.** A recorded continuous or fine-grained
   setting such as voltage, displacement, pulse area, delay, field, or
   material orientation. No phase-response table is supplied.
4. **Marker coupling $M_I(m)$.** A physical interaction whose marker output
   has a separately readable diagnostic.
5. **Licensed unmarker $U_I$.** A physical inverse or recombination procedure
   whose actuator construction is public but whose coherent process law is
   held out.
6. **Stable marker reader $R_M$.** A physical amplification/readout path that
   forms a persistent record.
7. **Marker overwrite $E_M$.** A later physical overwrite or erase operation
   on that stable record.
8. **Recombiner $J_I$.** A physical module reconnecting unresolved
   alternatives. No unitary or action is public input.
9. **Output reader $R_I$.** Raw output detectors with all failure records.
10. **Feed-forward controller $F_I$.** A classical apparatus that changes a
    later setting as a registered function of a stable earlier record.

An implementation may realize several roles in one apparatus only if the
joint manifest and crosstalk calibration are printed.

### 4.2 Calibration programs

The public calibration split includes:

1. source-only rate and stability runs;
2. output-reader dark, dead-time, saturation, and independent reference runs;
3. each alternative blocked in turn;
4. actuator metrology across the registered setting range;
5. marker-reader discriminability and failure rate using separately prepared
   reference inputs;
6. the classical truth table and latency of $F_I$;
7. throughput/loss of each module outside the recombined held-out program;
8. null setting, disconnected, and no-source controls; and
9. repeated calibration-equivalence checks interleaved with target runs.

The public split excludes:

1. the recombined continuous fringe;
2. a Jones, unitary, action, phase, or holonomy model;
3. a process or gate tomography of $A_I,C_I,M_I,U_I,J_I$;
4. the retained/unretained marker continuation profile;
5. target-derived visibility, phase offset, or decoherence parameters; and
6. any response table for the held-out compositions.
