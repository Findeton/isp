## 0. Question and answer

> If any finite complete transcript law can be rewritten as a Markov process
> by taking the whole observed past as the current state, is genuine
> indivisibility empty?

No. But indivisibility must be stated at the right level.

1. **Mathematical Markovizability** asks whether some enlarged state can make
   a chosen complete law factor. For a finite-horizon transcript law, the full
   past always does.
2. **Predictive sufficiency** asks for the smallest statistic of that past
   that preserves all licensed future predictions. It is constructed from the
   complete law and can therefore encode the target answer.
3. **Physical closure** asks whether the proposed boundary carrier contains
   every independently evidenced system, reference, memory, record, and
   control channel relevant at that experimental scope.
4. **Native indivisibility** is a claim about failure of positive restart on a
   declared and physically justified configuration/interface. It is not a
   claim that no mathematical dilation could ever be invented.

## 2. Barandes: fixed configurations, minimalist law, and realizers

Barandes's current construction distinguishes a fixed configuration space,
contingent standalone probabilities, and fixed transition laws. His
indivisible laws need not compose through every intermediate label as a
textbook Markov process does. He also distinguishes a minimalist first-order
law from the generally nonunique complete non-Markovian realizers compatible
with it.

Three consequences matter here.

1. Indivisibility is assessed relative to the configuration referent and law
   that the model actually declares.
2. Adding an arbitrary history state to a complete realizer does not show
   that the minimalist first-order law itself selected that state or that
   realizer.
3. The stochastic--quantum representation result begins after a suitable
   positive law is specified. It does not provide the U0 source-completion
   rule that chooses configurations and complete laws for new experiments.

The source does not prove that a declared configuration is ontically complete
against every possible hidden dilation. Nor does it supply the native U0 law.

## 3. Pollock et al.: operational memory requires interventions

Pollock et al. define a causal break that discards the incoming system and
freshly prepares a chosen state. A process is operationally Markov at the cut
when every later state, after every such break, is independent of earlier
controls and outcomes once the fresh preparation is fixed. Dependence that
survives the break witnesses information carried through an external
environment or another omitted channel.

The U0 lesson is precise:

> A passive sequence of marginals cannot establish physical Markovity or
> memory. The carrier and intervention interface must be matched, and a
> physically available reference or memory cannot be silently discarded.

This operational notion is not identical to Barandes stochastic division.
An interference seam may fail a positive restart on a chosen configuration
carrier even when no environment memory crosses an operational causal break.
Conversely, a subsystem can show operational memory while an enlarged
system--memory carrier evolves by ordinary positive steps.

The process-tensor and Hilbert machinery of the source is a comparator. U0
does not inherit Hilbert ontology, an external clock, or a supplied quantum
process.

## 4. Shalizi--Crutchfield: minimal prediction is not ontology

Computational mechanics groups observed pasts into causal states when they
induce the same conditional distribution over futures. Under the source's
stationary-process setting, this representation is minimal among equally
predictive representations.

U0 retains only the information-theoretic lesson:

1. a future-sufficient quotient can remove predictively idle distinctions;
2. any other future-sufficient statistic must refine that quotient; and
3. the quotient is defined using the process's future conditional laws.

The third point is decisive. If the target complete process is used to build
the causal state and that state is then presented as the mechanism generating
the target, the answer has been imported. Minimality does not turn a
target-built statistic into a physical constituent or a nomological
derivation.

A different case must remain open: a fixed learning rule may use only the
licensed calibration packet, freeze a predictive state before target opening,

the declared transfer scope. It does not by itself establish that the learned

## 5. Fritz: kernels, sufficiency, and reversible positivity

Fritz develops Markov categories as a general language for kernels,
conditioning, conditional independence, and sufficient statistics. In a
positive Markov category, isomorphisms are deterministic. For ordinary finite
stochastic matrices this reduces to the elementary statement that a
stochastic map with a stochastic inverse is a permutation.

The legitimate U0 consequence is conditional:

> Exact reversible physics represented functorially by positive kernels on a
> finite closed carrier cannot place its nontrivial reversible capacity in
> stochastic mixing; its invertible maps are deterministic permutations.

This is not a no-go for ordinary-positive ontology. The carrier can be
continuous or otherwise infinite, transformations can be contextual, memory
can be retained, the complete law can be indivisible, or the empirical law
can deviate. Each escape has a different physical and resource burden.

No categorical language is promoted to ontology. The companion exact result
remains finite; the general source is cited only to show that the boundary is
not a peculiarity of one matrix calculation.

## 6. Three closure notions that must not be merged

### 6.1 Source closure

A carrier is **source-closed at declared scope** only when every constituent,
reference, retained record, memory candidate, preparation, intervention,
reader, and communication channel independently evidenced by the physical
source packet is represented or explicitly excluded by a tested isolation.

Source closure is an experimental and metrological claim. It does not mean
that the carrier predicts the future.

### 6.2 Predictive closure

A carrier is **predictively closed at a cut** when its value, together with
the licensed future controls, is sufficient for every registered future
record law. This is a conditional-independence property and can be tested only
relative to a specified experiment family.

A full-history state is predictively closed by construction. That fact says
nothing about whether the state is a present physical configuration.

### 6.3 Ontic completeness

**Ontic completeness** would assert that no additional physically real
variables exist. Finite process data do not establish this unrestricted
claim. U0 may compare explicit rivals and search for omitted-variable
witnesses, but it must not turn failure to find an extension into a theorem
that none exists.

The safe relation is

$$
\text{source closure}
+\text{predictive sufficiency}
\Longrightarrow
\text{a licensed physical division at that scope},
$$

not an assertion of ultimate ontology.

## 7. What counts as evidence for enlargement

An enlarged carrier receives physical credit only if the added variable has
at least one target-independent warrant:

1. it is independently preparable or readable;
2. interventions on it change registered future statistics;
3. isolation or erasure controls remove its influence as predicted;
4. it is required by a common composite-system law fixed before target
   opening;
5. it transfers without refit across implementations; or
6. a new empirical prediction from the enlargement succeeds.

The following receive no such credit by themselves:

1. naming the whole past a present state;
2. fitting a hidden-state model after reading the target;
3. storing a process matrix, wavefunction, action, phase, or holonomy in a
   latent variable;
4. assigning a unique real number whose digits encode future answers; or
5. choosing the smallest predictive state after complete tomography.

Those are representations or compressors of an already supplied law.
