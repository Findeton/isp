## 3. Common complete-record interface

Fix one finite, physically executed parent family. Let

$$
x=(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\in\{0,1\}^n
\tag{4}
$$

denote which of $n$ registered optional alternatives remain available and
unresolved. Let $\mathcal R$ be the complete transcript alphabet, including
source failures, no-clicks, multiple clicks, marker records, and invalid
flags. A formula must output

$$
P_x(r)\geq0,
\qquad
\sum_{r\in\mathcal R}P_x(r)=1.
\tag{5}
$$

The alternatives in (4) are physical control settings. They are not asserted
to be fundamental paths or pieces of an ontic configuration.

For $S\subseteq[n]$, write $P_S=P_{\mathbf1_S}$. Define the Boolean Möbius
coefficient

$$
\widehat P_T(r)
=
\sum_{S\subseteq T}
(-1)^{|T|-|S|}P_S(r).
\tag{6}
$$

Möbius inversion gives

$$
P_S(r)=\sum_{T\subseteq S}\widehat P_T(r).
\tag{7}
$$

A program-response family is degree at most $k$ when
$\widehat P_T(r)=0$ for every record $r$ and every $|T|>k$.

### The all-closed term is mandatory

For real programs $P_\varnothing(r)$ need not vanish: detectors have dark
counts, sources fail, and a fully blocked device has records. Therefore the
experimental third-order response is

$$
\begin{aligned}
I_3^r(i,j,k)
={}&P_{ijk}(r)-P_{ij}(r)-P_{ik}(r)-P_{jk}(r)\\
&+P_i(r)+P_j(r)+P_k(r)-P_\varnothing(r).
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
$$

Omitting $-P_\varnothing$ is legitimate only after the relevant event has
been proved to have zero all-closed measure. Background subtraction is not a
mathematical identity and must remain a frozen record map.

Equation (8) is a relation among different physical programs. It equals the
Sorkin event-algebra expression only when the blockers implement the required
common-parent restrictions to the registered accuracy.

## 4. Entrant K0 — additive ordinary-positive histories

Let $\Omega_p$ be a finite fine-history carrier for program $p$, let
$w_p(h)\geq0$ with $\sum_h w_p(h)=1$, and let
$\rho_p:\Omega_p\to\mathcal R_p$ be the record map. Define

$$
P_{\rm K0}(r\mid p)
=
\sum_{h:\rho_p(h)=r}w_p(h).
\tag{9}
$$

One $h$ may be declared actual. Equation (9) is an ordinary Kolmogorov law.

### Proposition K0-A — an inserted nonselective record has zero intrinsic
division response

Suppose an intermediate record merely partitions one common history set as
$\Omega=\bigsqcup_z\Omega_z$, and the later record event is $A$. Then

$$
P(A)=\sum_zP(A\cap\Omega_z).
\tag{10}
$$

Forgetting the intermediate record reproduces the unresolved endpoint
exactly. Thus the intrinsic `qCor` between unresolved and nonselectively
divided evaluation is zero.

### Proposition K0-B — common-measure alternatives are monotone

If opening alternatives means set inclusion $A\subseteq B$ inside one fixed
positive measure, then

$$
P(A)\leq P(B).
\tag{11}
$$

Destructive interference cannot arise from union or restriction alone.

### The contextual escape and its debt

A blocker, marker, or eraser can physically change the weights, so a family
$p\mapsto w_p$ can reproduce any contextual record table. But then equation
(9) no longer predicts that family. The missing physics has moved into the
program-indexed source map

$$
B_p^{\rm pub}\longmapsto(\Omega_p,w_p,\rho_p).
\tag{12}
$$

Supplying (12) separately for each program is the whole-program table control.

**Tournament classification:** complete ordinary positivity and actuality;
classical composition if the kernels are supplied; no native interference,
Q-layer, or material source law.

## 5. Entrant DR2 — degree-two complete-record response

This is the first direct formula rather than a carrier schema.

For every record $r$, define from the public calibration programs

$$
\begin{aligned}
\alpha_r
&=P_\varnothing(r),\\
\beta_{ri}
&=P_i(r)-P_\varnothing(r),\\
\gamma_{rij}
&=P_{ij}(r)-P_i(r)-P_j(r)+P_\varnothing(r).
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
$$

Freeze these coefficients before any setting with at least three available
alternatives is opened. The prediction is equation (1).

### Proposition DR2-A — normalization is automatic

If every calibration member in (13) is a complete normalized record law, then

$$
\sum_r\alpha_r=1,
\qquad
\sum_r\beta_{ri}=0,
\qquad
\sum_r\gamma_{rij}=0.
\tag{14}
$$

Therefore equation (1) sums to one for every $x$.

#### Proof

The first identity is normalization of $P_\varnothing$. The second is
$\sum_rP_i(r)-\sum_rP_\varnothing(r)=0$. The third is
$1-1-1+1=0$. Substitution in (1) proves the claim. $\square$

Normalization does not guarantee positivity. The candidate is falsified if
any held-out $P_{\rm DR2}(r\mid x)$ lies outside $[0,1]$ beyond registered
uncertainty.

An exact hostile control shows that this is a substantive gate. For one
record, take

$$
P_\varnothing=0,
\qquad
P_i=\frac9{10},
\qquad
P_{ij}=\frac1{10}
\quad(i\ne j).
\tag{15}
$$

Every calibration member is a valid binary-record distribution after adding
its complementary record, but the degree-two prediction is

$$
P_{123}
=3\left(\frac1{10}\right)
-3\left(\frac9{10}\right)
=-\frac{12}{5}.
\tag{16}
$$

Thus low-weight positivity cannot be promoted to a global positive law.

### Proposition DR2-B — unique degree-two completion

Among record laws of Boolean degree at most two, equation (1) is the unique
extension of the empty, singleton, and pair calibration members.

#### Proof

Equations (6)--(7) uniquely identify all coefficients of orders zero, one,
and two. All higher coefficients vanish by hypothesis. $\square$

Consequently, for every distinct $i,j,k$,

$$
I_3^r(i,j,k)=0.
\tag{17}
$$

This is a genuine held-out prediction using only ordinary record
probabilities. It does not require an ontic amplitude.

### Exact interference witness

For an exact comparator only, let

$$
(a_1,a_2,a_3)=\left(\frac14,\frac15,-\frac16\right)
\tag{18}
$$

and define a two-record target

$$
P_1(S)=\left(\sum_{i\in S}a_i\right)^2,
\qquad
P_0(S)=1-P_1(S).
\tag{19}
$$

The amplitude notation in (17) generates a validation target; it is not
available to DR2. Calibration on $|S|\leq2$ reconstructs every value exactly.
All probabilities remain positive, and the third Möbius coefficients of both
records vanish.

For the pair $\{i,j\}$, the difference between unresolved response and the
sum of separately resolved branches is

$$
P_1(ij)-P_1(i)-P_1(j)+P_1(\varnothing)=2a_ia_j.
\tag{20}
$$

It can be positive or negative. Thus an ordinary-positive family of actual
records can exhibit a signed division response without making that residual
an ordinary probability.

### Proposition DR2-C — the PR control passes

For binary Bell settings $x,y\in\{0,1\}$ and any fixed outcomes $a,b$, every
conditional table has the exact expansion

$$
P(a,b\mid x,y)
=c_{ab}+u_{ab}x+v_{ab}y+w_{ab}xy.
\tag{21}
$$

This is simply the complete Boolean basis for two bits. Hence every
two-input/two-output nonsignalling box, including PR, is degree at most two in
the program settings.

Vanishing program-level $I_3$ therefore does not select the quantum
correlation boundary.

### Proposition DR2-D — combined-program degree is not product closed

Let two independent systems have degree-two response components

$$
f(x_1,x_2)=(a_1x_1+a_2x_2)^2,
\qquad
g(y_1)=(b_1y_1)^2.
\tag{22}
$$

Their independent joint record has probability $f g$. The coefficient of
$x_1x_2y_1$ is

$$
2a_1a_2b_1^2,
\tag{23}
$$

which is nonzero in general. With
$a_1=1/4$, $a_2=1/5$, and $b_1=1/3$, it equals $1/90$.

Thus two degree-two program laws need not produce a degree-two law on the
union of their optional controls. A tensor product of pair-history kernels
can remain level two on the *combined history atoms*, but that is a different
grading. Conflating these two statements would be a semantic error.

### Physical scope and resources

DR2 requires

$$
|\mathcal R|\left(1+n+\binom n2\right)
\tag{24}
$$

calibration coefficients per registered alternative family before symmetry
or shared structure is used. It predicts multi-alternative members of that

1. identify which controls share one valid common parent;
2. generate the coefficients from engineering or material descriptors;
3. explain marker formation, stable division, or coherent erasure across new

4. compose arbitrary adaptive programs;
5. close under independent products in the naive program grading;

**Tournament classification:** exact bounded positive prediction and D1

## 6. Entrant SP — strongly positive pair-history law

Let $\Omega_p$ be a finite set of fine program histories and let

$$
D_p:\mathcal A_p\times\mathcal A_p\to\mathbb C
\tag{25}
$$

be Hermitian, biadditive, normalized, and strongly positive. For a stable
record partition $\{A_r\}_{r\in\mathcal R}$ satisfying medium decoherence,

$$
D_p(A_r,A_s)=0\quad(r\ne s),
\tag{26}
$$

define

$$
P_{\rm SP}(r\mid p)=D_p(A_r,A_r).
\tag{27}
$$

Equation (25) is an ordinary normalized probability law on the actual stable
records. The fine-history diagonal $D(A,A)$ is a quantum measure, not an
additive probability law on all fine events. This entrant therefore must say
that the actual chance surface is the stable record partition, unless it adds
a separate actualization rule.

Unresolved alternatives retain the cross terms in

$$
D(A\cup B,A\cup B),
\tag{28}
$$

while a genuine stable record can enforce (24). The distinction naturally
models nondivision versus division when the event and record maps are
physically correct. Independent products may use $D_1\otimes D_2$.

Strong positivity yields a Gram representation

$$
D(A,B)=\langle v_A,v_B\rangle.
\tag{29}
$$

Hilbert space is therefore secondary as a representation, but the full
pair-history predictive information has not disappeared.

### Event positivity is strictly weaker than strong positivity

The real symmetric atomic kernel

$$
G=
\frac16
\begin{pmatrix}
1&2\\
2&1
\end{pmatrix}
\tag{30}
$$

is normalized and has nonnegative values $0,1/6,1/6,1$ on the four subset
indicators. It is even copositive on the nonnegative orthant. But its
eigenvalues are $1/2$ and $-1/6$. Hence nonnegative quantum measures on all
events, including normalization, do not imply a strongly positive
pair-history completion.

### An explicit selector attempt: SP-min

To avoid merely saying “supply $D$,” define the public feasible set

$$
\mathcal F_B
=
\left\{
D\succeq0:
D=D^\dagger,
\mathbf1^\dagger D\mathbf1=1,
L_B(D)=y_B
\right\},
\tag{31}
$$

where $L_B(D)=y_B$ contains only frozen incomplete calibration and registered
record-decoherence constraints. On a declared $N$-atom representation, let

$$
D_B^{\min}
=
\underset{D\in\mathcal F_B}{\operatorname{argmin}}
\left\|D-\frac1N I\right\|_F^2.
\tag{32}
$$

For nonempty closed convex $\mathcal F_B$, the strictly convex projection is
unique. Equations (25) and (30) are an executable target-blind completion
formula at finite scope.

It is also an inference rule rather than earned dynamics.

### Proposition SP-A — conjugation-neutral completion cannot generate
directed imaginary response

If $\mathcal F_B$ is closed under entrywise conjugation, then

$$
D_B^{\min}=\overline{D_B^{\min}}.
\tag{33}
$$

#### Proof

Conjugation preserves feasibility and the real Frobenius objective. Therefore
$D_B^{\min}$ and its conjugate are both minimizers. Uniqueness makes them
equal. $\square$

Thus SP-min cannot generate a directed or chiral imaginary sector from
conjugation-blind calibration. An orientation-bearing physical source could
enter $B$, but the response to that source would still need a law.

### Exact relative-orientation control

The two kernels

$$
D_+=
\begin{pmatrix}
\frac12&\frac{i}{4}\\
-\frac{i}{4}&\frac12
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
D_-=
\begin{pmatrix}
\frac12&-\frac{i}{4}\\
\frac{i}{4}&\frac12
\end{pmatrix}
\tag{34}
$$

have the same diagonal measure on every event. With a fixed second system
$E=D_+$ and composite event $F=\{(0,0),(1,1)\}$,

$$
\mu_{D_+\otimes E}(F)=\frac38,
\qquad
\mu_{D_-\otimes E}(F)=\frac58.
\tag{35}
$$

The neutral midpoint selected from conjugation-symmetric information gives
$1/2$. A minimum-information rule therefore makes a definite prediction, but
the choice of midpoint is not forced by the observed diagonal physics. The
composite experiment can falsify it.

### Composition credit and ceiling

Strong positivity has serious physical motivation:

1. it is stable under the standard independent tensor product;
2. under the joint-history premises of Craig et al. it gives the CHSH
   Tsirelson inequality; and
3. Dowker--Wilkes obtain a maximality/uniqueness result in their declared
   level-two tensor-composition setting.

But it does not finish the Q-layer:

1. an almost-quantum strongly-positive-compatible family is strictly larger
   than the quantum set;
2. an interacting parent is not selected by tensoring independent systems;
3. sequential/adaptive event maps are additional physical structure;
4. equation (30) depends on the chosen atomic representation and Frobenius
   reference, and is not invariant under arbitrary lossless refinements;
5. $N^2$ pair data can grow exponentially with program depth; and
6. no rule maps new material descriptors to the numerical $D_p$.

**Tournament classification:** D2 and part of D3 conditional on a sourced
pair-history law; ordinary positivity only on declared actual record
partitions; no D4, D5, full Q-layer, or M-layer. SP-min is an explicit
falsifiable inference control, not accepted nomology.

## 7. Entrant OPT — ordered operational process law

Let each system have an ordered real vector space $(V,V_+,u)$. A preparation
is $\omega\in V_+$ with $u(\omega)=1$. An instrument at control $c$ is a
family of positive maps $T_{r\mid c}:V\to V$ satisfying

$$
\sum_r u\circ T_{r\mid c}=u.
\tag{36}
$$

For an adaptive program, the complete transcript formula is

$$
P_{\rm OPT}(r_{1:m}\mid c_{1:m})
=
u\,T_{r_m\mid c_m(h_{m-1})}\cdots
T_{r_2\mid c_2(h_1)}T_{r_1\mid c_1}(\omega).
\tag{37}
$$

Equation (35) is an ordinary-positive normalized record law. A monoidal
product and admitted interacting transformations can provide composite
closure.

Operational reconstruction results show that substantive principles such as
causality, purification, purity preservation, sharpness, local tomography,
and suitable composite assumptions can restrict the ordered spaces to
Euclidean Jordan or complex quantum structure. This is a serious route to a
secondary Hilbert framework.

It is not a source-complete material law.

The experiment-specific objects

$$
(V,\omega,\{T_{r\mid c}\},u)
\tag{38}
$$

already determine the predictive process. Supplying (36) in the public packet
is target-equivalent. Without it, the missing map is

$$
B_e^{\rm pub}
\longmapsto
(V_e,\omega_e,\{T^e_{r\mid c}\},u_e).
\tag{39}
$$

Moreover, if the operational state in (36) is declared a complete physical,
future-sufficient, restartable carrier, equation (35) is divisible on that
carrier. If it is only predictive bookkeeping, the actuality ontology remains
the record history and the Barandes-facing nondivision question remains open.

**Tournament classification:** possibly strong Q-framework credit under
declared reconstruction axioms; complete adaptive formula after the
instruments are supplied; no native material member, source completion, or
