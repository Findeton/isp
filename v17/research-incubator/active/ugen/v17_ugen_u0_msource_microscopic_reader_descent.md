## 6. Material cascade and the mean-law boundary

\tag{7}

$$

But equations (7)--(8) are equations for distribution functions or their
moments. They do not by themselves provide a probability measure over

### Theorem MRD-A — drift does not determine click statistics

Knowing a mean cascade variable at every modeled time does not determine the
probability of crossing a nonlinear detector threshold.

**Proof by exact witness.** At one time let two candidate quasiparticle-count
laws be

$$
P_1(N=0)=P_1(N=2)=\frac12,
\qquad
P_2(N=1)=1.
\tag{9}
$$

Both have \(\mathbb E[N]=1\). For a threshold \(N\ge2\), however,

$$
P_1(N\ge2)=\frac12,
\qquad
P_2(N\ge2)=0.
\tag{10}
$$

For a threshold \(N\ge1\), the same laws instead give \(1/2\) and \(1\).
Therefore identical mean response can produce different detector records.
\(\square\)

Consequently a microscopic reader descent requires one of:

1. a full stochastic collision process with sourced rates and correlations;
2. full counting statistics derived from a microscopic open-system law;
3. an independently validated fluctuation functional; or
4. an explicit declaration that only a mean-field control has been earned.

## 13. Reader-factor nonidentifiability

### Theorem MRD-C — absorption and internal response do not descend from one click rate

With zero dark and false-click rates and perfect final electronics,

$$
P(R=1)=a p.
\tag{31}
$$

The two physically distinct readers

$$
(a,p)=\left(\frac12,\frac12\right)
\quad\text{and}\quad
(a,p)=\left(\frac14,1\right)
\tag{32}
$$

both give \(P(R=1)=1/4\). Therefore a target click probability cannot identify
optical absorption and internal detection efficiency separately. \(\square\)

The same ambiguity extends to coupling loss, absorption, cascade survival,
switching, circuit crossing, latch retention, and analysis cuts. Independent
local interventions are required.

### Theorem MRD-D — record laws do not identify a unique latent cascade

Let \(g:\Omega_e\to\mathcal R_e\) be the coarse-graining from complete latent
histories to records. If \(g\) is many-to-one, any redistribution of
probability inside each fiber \(g^{-1}(r)\) that preserves the fiber mass

$$
P'_e(g^{-1}(r))=P_e(g^{-1}(r))
\quad\forall r
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
P'_e(r)=P_e(r).
\tag{33}
$$

Hence even a perfect POVM or complete record table need not determine what
happened microscopically. Additional readers, interventions, and structural
premises are necessary. \(\square\)

This is why detector tomography is an empirical target description rather
than a derivation of detector ontology.
