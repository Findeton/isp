## 0. Executive result

the physical origin of the rule that compares and composes predictions across

The same point appears in several mathematical languages: relative amplitude,
Gram orientation, a pair-history kernel, a process tensor, a boundary

languages is thereby selected as ontology.

This packet locates the missing datum without assuming that the universe is a
graph, bundle, Hilbert space, trajectory space, continuum, lattice, or
spacetime.

Six exact conclusions survive.

1. **Isolated record laws do not identify cross-context transport.** Local
   predictive coordinates admit independent presentation changes. A connector
   between two contexts is additional family-level information unless one
   physical source rule generates it.
2. **Family composition requires compatible transport.** Under a change of predictive
   coordinates at each context, a connector must transform at both ends. A

   presentation independent.

   comparison graph with rank-one complex predictive fibers, every tree-edge

   a theorem about an experimental comparison grammar, not a discrete ontology.
4. **Local route probabilities can miss a complete prediction.** Under a
   licensed balanced quantum recombination, two routes can each have unit

   depending on their relative cycle phase.
5. **A scalar source weight cannot select absent fiber data.** Reweighting
   physical contexts cannot choose a transport realizer that is not measurable

6. **A finite fitted family does not establish a nomology.** Without a printed
   relation tying a held-out physical descriptor to the trained ones, any
   source map has distinct extensions that agree on all fitted contexts and
   disagree on the held-out composition.

## 5. Theorem CCS-A — local prediction does not identify transport

### Statement

The complete collection of isolated probabilities (7) at every $x\in X$ does
not determine a connector (9) between different contexts unless an additional
rule relates their predictive presentations.

For a Hilbert representation, coordinate covariance requires

$$
T_e\mapsto T_e'
=G_yT_eG_x^{-1}.
\tag{10}
$$

The local probabilities determine neither a common choice of the $G_x$ nor a
gauge-equivalence class of the connector family.

### Proof

Equation (8) can be applied independently at every context while preserving
all probabilities in (7). A connector acts on a coordinate at $x$ and returns
a coordinate at $y$. Applying $G_x$ before the connector and expressing the
output in the $G_y$ coordinates gives (10).

The isolated packet contains no equation involving both $x$ and $y$.
Consequently it contains no value of $T_e$ and no constraint that selects one
equivalence class of connector families. Any such constraint must come from a
cross-context experiment or a source law. $\square$

### Exact scope

The theorem does not say that transport is unknowable. It says that it is not
contained in isolated probabilities. A material field equation, action,
source response, symmetry representation, or native stochastic law may
generate it. Supplying one of those is additional physics.

## 6. Theorem CCS-B — composition covariance and loop invariants

Suppose composable connectors obey

$$
T_{f\circ e}=T_fT_e,
\qquad
T_{1_x}=I_x.
\tag{11}
$$

For a path

$$
\gamma=e_n\circ\cdots\circ e_1:x\to y,
$$

define

$$
T_\gamma=T_{e_n}\cdots T_{e_1}.
\tag{12}
$$

Under (10), all internal presentation changes cancel:

$$
T_\gamma'
=G_yT_\gamma G_x^{-1}.
\tag{13}
$$

For a closed path $\ell:x\to x$, its holonomy

$$
H_\ell=T_\ell
\tag{14}
$$

transforms by conjugation,

$$
H_\ell'=G_xH_\ell G_x^{-1}.
\tag{15}
$$

Therefore conjugacy invariants of $H_\ell$, such as its spectrum or traces of
powers when defined, are independent of the predictive presentation.

### Proof

Insert (10) into (12). Each adjacent $G_z^{-1}G_z$ cancels, leaving only the
endpoint factors in (13). Setting $y=x$ gives (15). Conjugacy invariants are
unchanged under (15). $\square$

### Physical meaning

This is a bookkeeping theorem, not a derivation of quantum mechanics. It says
why a loop comparison can retain physical information even when each local
coordinate convention is arbitrary. Berry phase is a known quantum example;
there the Hamiltonian family and its adiabatic path supply the physical law.

## 7. Theorem CCS-C — exact rank-one cycle classification

Let $G=(X,E)$ be a finite connected comparison multigraph. Each unoriented
edge has two formal orientations, and the reversible rank-one predictive
representative assigns

$$
z_e\in U(1),
\qquad
z_{\bar e}=z_e^{-1}.
\tag{16}
$$

An object-gauge choice $\eta_x\in U(1)$ acts as

$$
z_e\mapsto z_e^\eta
=\eta_{t(e)}z_e\eta_{s(e)}^{-1}.
\tag{17}
$$

Choose any spanning tree $\tau$ of $G$.

### Statement

1. Every assignment $z$ is gauge equivalent to one for which
   $z_e=1$ on every tree edge.
2. After this tree gauge is fixed, each non-tree edge carries exactly the
   holonomy of its fundamental cycle.
3. Two assignments are gauge equivalent if and only if all cycle holonomies
   agree.
4. If $m$ is the number of unoriented edges and $n=|X|$, the gauge classes are
   naturally

   $$
   U(1)^{\beta_1},
   \qquad
   \beta_1=m-n+1.
   \tag{18}
   $$

### Proof

Fix a root $x_0$ and set $\eta_{x_0}=1$. Because a tree has a unique path from
$x_0$ to every vertex, define $\eta_y$ recursively so that (17) sends the
tree edge entering $y$ to one. This proves item 1 without inconsistency because
the tree has no cycles.

For a non-tree edge $e:x\to y$, joining $e$ to the unique tree path from $y$
back to $x$ produces one fundamental cycle. All tree labels are one in the
fixed gauge, so the cycle product equals $z_e$. This proves item 2.

Holonomies are gauge invariant in the abelian rank-one case. If two
assignments have the same fundamental-cycle values, tree gauge sends both to
the same normal form. Hence they are gauge equivalent, proving item 3. A
connected graph has $n-1$ tree edges and $m-n+1$ non-tree edges, proving item
4. $\square$

### Consequences

1. If the comparison grammar is a tree, every rank-one edge phase is a local
   presentation convention. There is no invariant phase member to measure.
2. The first invariant appears only when the grammar contains a cycle or two
   alternative routes with common endpoints.
3. A candidate trained only on a spanning tree has not yet predicted the
   cycle data.
4. The theorem does not imply that fundamental reality is graph-like or that
   all quantum processes are rank one. It identifies one exact finite control
   inside the broader problem.

## 8. Theorem CCS-D — exact two-route witness

Take two reversible predictive routes $p,q:x\to y$ with rank-one values

$$
z_q=1,
\qquad
z_p=e^{i\phi}.
\tag{19}
$$

Each route separately has the same endpoint probability,

$$
|z_p|^2=|z_q|^2=1.
\tag{20}
$$

If a licensed balanced coherent recombination prepares the two alternatives
with equal amplitude and reads the symmetric output, quantum composition gives

$$
p_+(\phi)
=\left|\frac{z_p+z_q}{2}\right|^2
=\frac{1+\cos\phi}{2},
\tag{21}
$$

with

$$
p_-(\phi)=1-p_+(\phi).
\tag{22}
$$

The relative value

$$
z_pz_q^{-1}=e^{i\phi}
\tag{23}
$$

is invariant under the common endpoint gauge (17). Thus $\phi=0$ and
$\phi=\pi$ have identical route-by-route positive probabilities while giving

$$
p_+(0)=1,
\qquad
p_+(\pi)=0.
\tag{24}
$$

### Exact meaning

The witness reuses the physical core of the earlier C2 phase-member theorem
and places it in a cross-context source grammar. It proves that isolated
positive route laws do not determine a composed quantum prediction.

Equation (21) is conditional on the quantum coherent-addition rule. It is not
derived from (20), and it does not make $z_p$ an ontic amplitude. A native
positive law earns credit only if it predicts the complete positive records
in (21)--(22) from the physical source and control descriptors, including all
loss, null, failure, and reader branches.

## 9. Theorem CCS-E — scalar base weights are transport-fiber inert

Let $\mathsf X$ be a physical descriptor space and let

$$
\pi:\mathsf R\longrightarrow\mathsf X
\tag{25}
$$

map a family-level predictive realizer to the local descriptor it realizes.
Suppose two realizers $r_1,r_2\in\mathsf R$ satisfy

$$
\pi(r_1)=\pi(r_2)=\chi
\tag{26}
$$

but differ in a held-out composed-context prediction.

Let the proposed joint law be built only from a scalar base measure $\Pi$ and
a local conditional law $P_{\rm loc}$,

$$
P(d\chi,dh)=\Pi(d\chi)P_{\rm loc}(dh\mid\chi),
\tag{27}
$$

where neither factor has $r$ as a measurable argument.

### Statement

Equation (27) cannot determine, select, or make unique $r_1$ versus $r_2$.
Every statistic measurable in $(\chi,h)$ has the same law for both realizers.

### Proof

Both realizers have the same image $\chi$ under (26). By premise,
$P_{\rm loc}(\cdot\mid\chi)$ does not depend on the realizer. Therefore the
measure (27), and hence the expectation of every measurable function of
$(\chi,h)$, is identical under $r_1$ and $r_2$. $\square$

### Escape routes

There are only honest escapes.

1. Enlarge the independently measured descriptor so that physical facts
   distinguishing the transport are present.
2. Supply a fixed conditional source law that generates the transport from
   the existing descriptor.
3. Prove that every licensed composed experiment is constant on the fiber, so
   the distinction is gauge rather than physical.
4. Predict a nonquantum outcome showing that the quantum transport was never
   required.

Calling a phase, action, or kernel a new component of $\chi$ without deriving or
independently measuring it merely moves the answer into the input.

## 10. Theorem CCS-F — unstructured held-out extension is nonidentifiable

Let $D_{\rm train}$ be a proper subset of a descriptor set $D$, and choose
$d_*\in D\setminus D_{\rm train}$. Let the output set $Y$ contain distinct
complete process laws $y_0\ne y_1$.

For every fitted source map

$$
S_{\rm train}:D_{\rm train}\longrightarrow Y,
\tag{28}
$$

there exist two extensions $S_0,S_1:D\to Y$ such that

$$
S_0|_{D_{\rm train}}
=S_1|_{D_{\rm train}}
=S_{\rm train},
\qquad
S_0(d_*)=y_0,
\qquad
S_1(d_*)=y_1.
\tag{29}
$$

### Proof

Assign the same fitted values on $D_{\rm train}$ and choose the two different
values at $d_*$. Assign arbitrary equal values elsewhere. $\square$

### Scope

This elementary result does not say that scientific generalization is
impossible. It says exactly what must make it possible: a physical symmetry,
conservation law, response equation, locality principle, regularity class,
composition rule, or other preregistered relation connecting $d_*$ to the
training family. A per-program table has no such content.

## 13. The spanning-tree / held-out-cycle experiment

The cycle theorem suggests a sharper empirical gate than another isolated
interference fit.

### 13.1 Freeze one common parent family

Choose a bounded apparatus family with:

1. independently measured source and component descriptors;
2. complete click, no-click, loss, dark, null, timeout, controller, and failure

Before opening the target records, the law must predict:

1. the complete recombined output distribution;
2. the change under reversing or replacing the non-tree connector where
   physically defined;
3. the effect of inserting a stable route record;
4. the effect of coherently erasing that record;
5. one product experiment; and
6. one interacting-parent modification.

The R1/QFT comparator predicts these from its supplied quantum and material
nomology. A native law must match or differ prospectively while receiving less

### 13.4 Why this is sharper

Tree-edge fits can absorb local efficiencies and endpoint probabilities.
