## 3. Source S2 — a physical non-gravitational phase control

S. A. Werner, R. Colella, A. W. Overhauser, and C. F. Eagen, “Observation of
the Phase Shift of a Neutron Due to Precession in a Magnetic Field,”
*Phys. Rev. Lett.* **35**, 1053 (1975),
<https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.35.1053>.

The experiment directly observed the sign reversal associated with a
$2\pi$ fermion spin rotation using neutron interferometry. It is a strong
physical phase reference, but it is not a clean scalar spatial-action
calibration: it includes the spin representation, magnetic moment, and
magnetic-field dynamics.

Therefore C3 lists it only as a candidate implementation. A future C0 pin
must decide whether it tests scalar action transport, spinor topology, or both
and must charge the neutron magnetic moment as input.

## 4. Sources S3--S4 — electromagnetic holonomy

Y. Aharonov and D. Bohm, “Significance of Electromagnetic Potentials in the
Quantum Theory,” *Phys. Rev.* **115**, 485 (1959),
<https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.115.485>.

A. Tonomura, N. Osakabe, T. Matsuda, T. Kawasaki, J. Endo, S. Yano, and
H. Yamada, “Evidence for Aharonov--Bohm effect with magnetic field completely
shielded from electron wave,” *Phys. Rev. Lett.* **56**, 792 (1986),
<https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.56.792>.

The theory source identifies the gauge-invariant relative phase associated
with enclosed flux. The shielded toroidal-magnet experiment observed the
expected relative phase while excluding the electron wave from the magnetic
field region.

### Metrology limitation

The superconducting shield quantizes magnetic flux in units involving
$h/(2e)$, and the reported phase sectors were correspondingly discrete. This
is excellent evidence that closed-loop holonomy is physical. It is not by
itself an independent determination of $1/\hbar$ from classically calibrated
flux and charge.

A future H2 packet must choose explicitly between:

1. **holonomy-only scope:** use the shielded quantized-flux experiment to
   reject a local-force-only model; or
2. **scale scope:** supply an independently audited continuous flux
   measurement and quantify leakage/systematic debt.

The two scopes must not be merged in the terminal claim.

## 5. Source S5 — Earth-field neutron gravity phase

R. Colella, A. W. Overhauser, and S. A. Werner, “Observation of
Gravitationally Induced Quantum Interference,” *Phys. Rev. Lett.* **34**, 1472
(1975), <https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.34.1472>.

The experiment observed a neutron-interference phase shift produced by
Earth's gravitational field. It establishes a low-energy matter--gravity
phase constraint.

The source object contains crystal geometry, neutron wavelength, dynamical
diffraction, bending, and systematic corrections. A remembered expression
such as $mgA/(\hbar v)$ is an explanatory sketch, not a sufficient C3 data
contract.

The experiment treats the gravitational field classically. Its success does
not imply gravitons, a superposed source field, or quantum gravity.

## 6. Sources S6--S8 — complete atom-interferometer phase

M. Kasevich and S. Chu, “Atomic interferometry using stimulated Raman
transitions,” *Phys. Rev. Lett.* **67**, 181 (1991),
<https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.67.181>.

P. Storey and C. Cohen-Tannoudji, “The Feynman path integral approach to
atomic interferometry. A tutorial,” *J. Phys. II France* **4**, 1999 (1994),
<https://doi.org/10.1051/jp2:1994103>.

C. Overstreet, P. Asenbaum, and M. A. Kasevich, “Physically significant phase
shifts in matter-wave interferometry,” *Am. J. Phys.* **89**, 324 (2021),
arXiv:2008.05609, <https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.05609>.

These sources establish three obligations for C3:

1. light pulses split, redirect, and recombine the matter waves while also
   imprinting laser phase and momentum;
2. propagation, laser, separation, and boundary terms must be combined
   consistently; and
3. in potentials above quadratic order on the arm-separation scale, the
   complete phase can contain potential information not reducible to midpoint
   deflection data.

Thus an atom-interferometer “action difference” cannot be built from the
gravitational potential integral alone. Omitting the apparatus phases would
manufacture a false anomaly or false success.

## 8. Source S10 — revised SI and the circularity warning

NIST Special Publication 330, *The International System of Units (SI), 2019
Edition*, <https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.SP.330-2019>.

The revised SI fixes exactly:

$$
h=6.62607015\times10^{-34}\ {\rm J\,s},
\qquad
e=1.602176634\times10^{-19}\ {\rm C}.
$$

Their numerical values have no SI uncertainty because they define the units.
This does not make the constants physically conventional; it changes what an
SI-valued experiment can independently determine.

Consequences for C3:

1. M0 may declare $1/\hbar$ without calling it a new calibration;
2. M1 must disclose how mass, electrical, time, and length units are realized;
3. cross-domain dimensionless ratios are preferable where possible;
4. a flux quantum cannot both calibrate and independently test $h/e$; and
5. uncertainty in physical realization remains even when the defining number
   is exact.

potential. The protocol assumes:

1. clocks and rods;
