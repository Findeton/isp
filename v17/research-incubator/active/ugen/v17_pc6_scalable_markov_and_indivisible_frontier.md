## 0. Purpose

PC5 located an exact finite-carrier obstruction for the unbounded calibrated
single-qubit $H,T$ word family, but a continuous positive model survived. PC6
asks the scalable question without turning that finite obstruction into a
claim about all positive histories:

> Can one ordinary-positive account generate complete scalable quantum
> processes from physically stated inputs with less explanatory debt than the
> quantum process law itself, or does the phase-complete capacity reappear in
> a predictive cut, context, memory, composition rule, nonuniform advice, or
> global boundary condition?

This packet has three jobs:

1. reconstruct the strongest relevant short-memory dimension theorem with
   every load-bearing premise exposed;
2. register efficient contextual and non-Markov controls that prevent a false
   positivity or contextuality no-go; and
3. define a fair constructive contest for an indivisible whole-program law.

The packet does not open an official unit. It does not select an ontology,
derive quantum theory, or license QFT, time, spacetime, or gravity.

## 1. Operational object to be explained

For a registered finite laboratory programme $p$, let

$$
\Phi(p)
$$

denote the complete operational profile, not merely an endpoint probability.
It includes:

1. normalized probabilities for every complete retained transcript;
2. every registered instrument outcome and failure branch;
3. conditional output states or future response functionals after each
   intervention;
4. adaptive substitution of later settings as functions of earlier records;
5. ancillary and composite ports;
6. convex mixing, coarse graining, and declared record erasure;
7. identity insertion and operationally equivalent implementations; and
8. containment/no-signalling-to-the-operational-past relations.

The empirical target is the equivalence class of models that produce the same
$\Phi$ on the registered experiment family. A change of notation, carrier, or
representation is not explanatory progress by itself.

For multi-time quantum controls, the process-tensor interface is the current
finite-dimensional adequacy comparator. It maps sequences of completely
positive control operations to output states and is characterized by
linearity, complete positivity under ancillary extension, and containment.
Receiving a target process tensor as input, however, is the zero of
explanatory gain: it supplies the object that the candidate was asked to
generate.

The operational ordering of programme slots is supplied laboratory syntax in
this packet. It is not an endogenous time, causal order, or spacetime result.

## 2. Resource coordinates must remain distinct

Every candidate receives a resource/dependency vector

$$
\mathbf R=
(R_{\rm dim},R_{\rm pred},R_{\rm mem},R_{\rm ctx},R_{\rm comm},
R_{\rm proc},R_{\rm comp},R_{\rm unif},R_{\rm phys}).
$$

| Coordinate | Meaning | What it is not automatically |
|---|---|---|
| $R_{\rm dim}$ | continuous topological dimension of an ontic or predictive carrier | bits, energy, cells, or runtime |
| $R_{\rm pred}$ | information retained by a future-sufficient predictive cut | carrier dimension or hardware memory |
| $R_{\rm mem}$ | cardinality/information of an online internal memory | total law-description size |
| $R_{\rm ctx}$ | dependence on measurement, preparation, implementation, or whole-program context | physical communication unless bridged |
| $R_{\rm comm}$ | information physically or operationally sent between declared ports | ontic dimension |
| $R_{\rm proc}$ | size of a process representation, such as a Choi/MPO bond dimension | an ontic cost without a bridge |
| $R_{\rm comp}$ | extra data or law needed to compose calibrated primitives | endpoint calibration alone |
| $R_{\rm unif}$ | per-size, per-depth, per-program, oracle, or noncomputable advice | an allowed free constant |
| $R_{\rm phys}$ | measured precision, apparatus, energy, volume, preparation time, or other physical cost | any abstract information coordinate |

No conversion between these coordinates is permitted without a printed bridge
theorem. In particular:

$$
R_{\rm dim}\ne R_{\rm pred}\ne R_{\rm mem}\ne R_{\rm phys}.
$$

The anti-laundering rule is:

> Capacity removed from one coordinate must either appear in another charged
> coordinate or be proved operationally idle.

This is an accounting rule, not yet a conservation theorem.

## 3. Source theorem M-SHORT

### 3.1 Exact antecedent class

Fix an $N$-dimensional Hilbert-space target. The source theorem applies only
to an ontological model satisfying all of the following:

1. after separating any discrete index, the continuous ontic space is an
   $M$-dimensional differentiable manifold;
2. pure-state preparations are represented by positive distributions and may
   depend on preparation context;
3. rank-one projective responses reproduce the exact Born probabilities and
   may depend on measurement context;
4. every registered unitary process is represented by a positive Markov
   kernel;
5. sequential kernels obey the Markov composition law;
6. physically attainable building blocks generate every required $SU(N)$
   direction used by the proof;
7. the support of every relevant transition kernel obeys Montina's Property
   1: at least one nonzero-support branch moves locally and smoothly with the
   input ontic state and evolution parameter; and
8. every nonzero process obeys Montina's Property 3: some unitary process can
   return the output ontic state to the input with nonzero probability.

The return unitary in item 8 need not be the inverse unitary. Compactness and
the removal of transient states motivate this property in the source, but do
not replace the printed assumption that all processes used by the theorem are
invertible in this weak sense.

### 3.2 Source conclusion

Under that complete antecedent,

$$
\boxed{M\ge 2N-2.}
$$

For $q$ qubits, $N=2^q$, hence

$$
M\ge 2^{q+1}-2.
$$

This is a continuous-manifold dimension bound. It is not, by itself, an
exponential lower bound on bits, communication, energy, physical memory, law
description, or computation.

### 3.3 Proof-dependency skeleton

Property 1 and kernel concatenation produce a shift matrix for a local
$N^2-1$-parameter unitary packet. If the ontic dimension were $M<2N-2$, a
nonzero ontic transition would be compatible with a unitary manifold of
dimension

$$
D_s\ge N^2-1-M>(N-1)^2.
$$

Weak process invertibility makes the compatible quantum-state support
$S(X)$ invariant rather than merely monotone under this manifold. The Lie
subgroup argument then forces $S(X)$ to be $SU(N)$-invariant, contradicting
the source's Born-support lemma. Therefore $M<2N-2$ is impossible in the
declared class.

At equality, the source further identifies the continuous ontic variables,
up to a possible discrete index, with an axis in the Hilbert space whose
motion obeys the Schrödinger equation. This is rigidity within the M-SHORT
class, not empirical selection of that axis as physical ontology.

### 3.4 Conditional round-off bridge

The source discusses an information cost after additionally assuming:

1. every continuous coordinate lies in a finite interval after rescaling;
2. response functions are sufficiently smooth;
3. a fixed operational round-off error is meaningful;
4. coordinate sensitivities do not collapse exponentially with system size;
   and
5. a chosen discretization cost represents the relevant physical resource.

Only with these extra hypotheses does a bit-like cost scale with $M$. PC6
does not import that bridge as a theorem about laboratory hardware.

### 3.5 Maximum admissible use

M-SHORT may eventually support only:

> Exact regular Markov ontological models with the printed unitary,
> support-regularity, and weak-invertibility interface require at least the
> quantum pure-state manifold dimension in continuous variables.

It cannot support:

1. every positive history is exponentially expensive;
2. every contextual model is expensive;
3. every indivisible or non-Markov law is expensive;
4. Barandes' ontology is refuted;
5. amplitudes are ontic;
6. the cost is physical memory or energy; or
7. a complete adaptive-process generator has been classified.

## 4. Mandatory escape and positive controls

### 4.1 Montina's non-Markov qubit control

The same source constructs an exact qubit projective-measurement model with a
one-dimensional continuous carrier plus a discrete component. Its
distribution cannot obey a positive Markov evolution for general unitaries.
It is a direct witness that the Markov premise is load bearing.

This control is not a scalable, uniform, complete multi-time generator. It
shows only that ontological shrinking can occur after leaving M-SHORT.

### 4.2 Low communication does not imply a small complete ontology

Montina's channel-simulation construction shows that a completely
$\psi$-epistemic preparation/readout model with finite
$I(X:\Psi)$ yields a classical one-way simulation. The amortized communication
cost is the relevant mutual information, with a larger one-shot expected-cost
bound. The single-qubit Kochen--Specker hemisphere model gives a low
amortized communication cost.

Its interface is preparation, identity channel, and later POVM. It is not a
complete adaptive process generator. It demonstrates that $R_{\rm comm}$,
$R_{\rm pred}$, and $R_{\rm dim}$ cannot be silently identified.

### 4.3 Contextual stabilizer memory lower bound

Karanjai--Wallman--Bartlett consider finite classical online simulations with
an internal state, update rule, and exact single-shot distinguishability. The
state representation is not allowed to depend on a future measurement
circuit. Their partitioning argument yields, for the $n$-qubit stabilizer
subtheory, an asymptotic minimum of approximately

$$
\frac12 n(n-1)
$$

classical memory bits.

This is an online finite-state bound. It neither applies to a whole-program
contextual law nor proves an exponential burden.

### 4.4 Efficient contextual stabilizer upper control

Hindlycke--Larsson give an exact contextual, outcome-deterministic stabilizer
model with maximum concurrent memory

$$
4n^2+8n+2n\log_2 n+\log_2 n+4
$$

bits, together with quadratic computational scaling. It is $\psi$-ontic and
its GHZ behavior is nonlocal, but it is a valid efficient positive/contextual
control for the Clifford/stabilizer rung.

Therefore:

$$
\text{contextuality alone}\not\Rightarrow
\text{exponential resource burden}.
$$

Any scalable v17 claim that fails this control is rejected before review.

### 4.5 Quantum generative-memory control

Quantum hidden Markov models show that coherent memory can generate some
ordinary output stochastic processes with fewer memory states than an
incoherent classical HMM. The recent spectral-invariant proposal is promising
but is not imported as a load-bearing theorem here. Its proof assumes
diagonalizable transfer operators and explicitly permits eigenvalues whose
coefficients vanish, so the process-visible spectral set must be repaired or
independently proved before use.

This family concerns stationary repeated-instrument output processes, not the
complete adaptive process interface and not a Barandes whole-law ontology.

### 4.6 Finite-context Gleason control

Montina--Wolf show, within a declared hidden-variable measurement class, that
finite relevant measurement-context information plus their local generalized
Gleason premises constrains probabilities for projective measurements with
three or more outcomes to be linear in the event projector, conditional on
the context data, with the source's additional constant and relaxed density
operator.

The source expressly does not construct the general model whose finite
context information it assumes. It supplies a structural conditional result,
not a quantitative context-cost lower bound and not a complete process
generator theorem.

## 5. Operational Markov cut versus indivisible whole law

For a process with interventions, “Markov” must be tested operationally. The
causal-break condition asks whether, after measuring and freshly preparing at
a cut, every future state is independent of the earlier controls and outcome
history once the fresh preparation is fixed.

The process-tensor result makes this condition necessary and sufficient for
operational quantum Markovity. CP divisibility alone is insufficient.

This yields three physically distinct branches:

1. **positive future-sufficient cut:** Q-Cut or M-SHORT-like resource tests may
   apply after their own premises are matched;
2. **positive online memory with context:** memory/context coordinates are
   charged explicitly;
3. **indivisible whole-program law:** no positive intermediate screening
   state is assumed, so cut theorems do not apply; the law must instead pass
   the uniform generation contest below.

An indivisible law is not a Markov model with an inconvenient state omitted.
Nor does refusing to factorize automatically explain the target process.

## 6. Fair uniform indivisible-generator contest

### 6.1 Candidate type

Let $\mathcal P_{n,K,\epsilon}$ be a typed finite laboratory-program grammar
for system size $n$, maximum depth $K$, and requested accuracy $\epsilon$.
A candidate is one fixed rule

$$
\mathfrak G:
(n,K,\epsilon,p,\mathcal D_{\rm phys})
\longmapsto
\mu_{p}
$$

that returns a normalized ordinary-positive law $\mu_p$ on the complete
retained records and declared failure branches of every
$p\in\mathcal P_{n,K,\epsilon}$.

The same finite/effective law schema must apply across $n$, $K$, context,
adaptivity, and held-out continuation. A global, variational, retrocausal,
measurement-dependent, or all-at-once rule is not excluded by syntax. Its
dependencies are printed and charged.

### 6.2 Permitted physical inputs

The candidate may receive:

1. $n$, $K$, and $\epsilon$;
2. the finite laboratory-program syntax;
3. independently calibrated primitive preparation, interaction, intervention,
   and reader packets;
4. declared boundary conditions or preparation data;
5. which records are retained, coarse-grained, or operationally erased; and
6. universal constants already entered in the declared-input ledger.

Primitive calibration has two different allowed tracks and must never switch
mid-proof:

- **endpoint/source-completion track:** only a printed partial calibration is
  given; any phase-sensitive completion must be selected from independent
  physical principles;
- **complete-primitive track:** full primitive process tomography is supplied
  and charged; the question is whether one uniform law composes it into
  held-out complete processes without receiving those composed processes.

### 6.3 Forbidden answer imports

The candidate may not receive, without being classified as the zero-gain
control:

1. the target process tensor or Choi state;
2. the complete target transcript-probability table;
3. a future-answer or future-setting oracle not declared by the ontology;
4. separately fitted parameters for each $n$, $K$, word, or test context;
5. a real number whose uncharged digits encode the answer table;
6. gate names that silently import unprinted quantum matrices or complete
   process calibration;
7. a held-out experiment chosen only after inspecting candidate errors; or
8. a compiler whose table is extensionally equivalent to the target family
   but is not counted in $R_{\rm comp}$ or $R_{\rm unif}$.

### 6.4 Complete-process obligations

For every registered programme, the generated family must satisfy:

1. normalization and positivity;
2. convex mixing and coarse-graining consistency;
3. conditioning on every instrument outcome;
4. adaptive substitution after retained records;
5. operational containment/no signalling to an earlier programme slot;
6. identity insertion and composition consistency;
7. ancillary and composite-port consistency;
8. retained versus erased record typing;
9. all registered failure branches; and
10. agreement across overlapping subexperiments.

These are operational constraints. A retrocausal ontology may still satisfy
them; operational no signalling to the past is not an assumption of ontic
forward causation.

### 6.5 Actualization obligation

If the candidate claims that one complete history actually happens, it must
state the occurrence/sampling law and its boundary inputs. A packet that only
computes operational probabilities may enter as a predictive representation,
but may not claim a completed ontology merely by calling its output a history.

### 6.7 Held-out target ladder

The contest advances only one rung at a time:

1. scalable Clifford/stabilizer preparations, transformations, Pauli
   instruments, and adaptive classical control;
2. the same grammar plus one fixed non-Clifford resource such as $T$;
3. adaptive mid-circuit instruments and retained/erased records;
4. local multi-qubit interactions under one tensor/composition interface;
5. a fixed finite-memory non-Markov process family; and
6. only later, a relativistic local-net or QFT family.

Rung 1 prevents contextuality from being mistaken for universal quantum
hardness. Rung 2 locates the first resource displacement. Rungs 3--5 test
complete process generation. Rung 6 is closed until the finite-dimensional
contest survives.

### 6.8 Preregistered outcome classes

1. **Constructive explanatory generator:** one fixed rule passes held-out
   complete-process tests without an equivalent target-law input and reduces
   at least one invariant dependency coordinate.
2. **Exact positive rewrite:** all predictions pass, but the process law is
   merely encoded in a state, context, compiler, or boundary object; this is
   representation, not explanatory reduction.
3. **Scoped resource theorem:** every member of a frozen class pays a printed
   asymptotic coordinate or exits through a named premise.
4. **Global/context branch:** a whole-program or boundary law works, with its
   context and uniformity debt measured.
5. **Empirical deviation:** a physically motivated candidate predicts a
   preregistered departure from quantum theory.
6. **Failure/no-go:** the candidate cannot satisfy the complete operational
   contract.
7. **Ill-typed/source failure:** the proposed class, calibration, or external
   theorem does not support the claimed inference.

No favorable branch may be selected after seeing the results.

## 7. Present evidence: a trilemma, not a theorem

The source and control battery motivates, but does not yet prove, the following
frontier:

$$
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c}
\text{phase-complete future-sufficient boundary}\\
\lor\\
\text{growing context, memory, communication, or global-boundary data}\\
\lor\\
\text{loss of uniform compositional generation}
\end{array}}
$$

An empirical deviation is a fourth logical branch.

Q-Cut attacks one $R_{\rm pred}$ slice. M-SHORT attacks one regular Markov
$R_{\rm dim}$ slice. Stabilizer models show that context and positivity can
remain efficient on a restricted family. E-Comp and PC2--PC5 show that
endpoint positivity and nondivision do not select phase-complete composition.
None of those statements classifies the indivisible whole-law branch.

The decisive v17 question is therefore:

> Can a source-faithful indivisible stochastic law uniformly generate the
> scalable complete process family from less than equivalent quantum process
> data?

That is where the Barandes programme can earn explanatory content rather than
merely a positive presentation of already known predictions.

## 8. Reality and claim walls

1. Ordinary positive probabilities for complete observed transcripts already
   exist in quantum theory.
2. A process tensor is an empirical/predictive representation, not an ontology
   selection theorem.
3. Complex amplitudes need not be ontic merely because positive cuts are
   expensive.
4. A complete indivisible parent law may exist without a Markov cut; existence
   does not show uniform generation or explanatory reduction.
5. Measurement context, whole-program dependence, and global boundary data are
   not forbidden, but they are not free.
6. The laboratory programme order is not derived chronology.
7. A scalable finite-dimensional result is not QFT.
8. No metric, spacetime dimension, Einstein dynamics, or gravity conclusion
   follows from this packet.
