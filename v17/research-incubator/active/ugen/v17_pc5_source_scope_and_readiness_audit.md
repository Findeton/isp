## 2. Independent algebra audit

### 2.1 Blowtorch parity

The four Pauli images of $(x,y,z)$ are

$$
(x,y,z),\quad
(x,-y,-z),\quad
(-x,y,-z),\quad
(-x,-y,z).
$$

They are distinct and all have parity $xyz$. Hadamard sends any triple to
$(z,-y,x)$ and reverses parity. Hence the two convex kernels have disjoint
parity support even though both quantum channels are completely depolarizing.

The construction is correctly typed in two operational experiments. With the
branch record retained, the instruments are not operationally equal. After a
declared operational erasure, the channels are equal but the cube kernels
differ. No ontological erasure is inferred.

### 2.2 Infinite-order calculation

For

$$
U=(iH)(e^{-i\pi/8}T),
$$

the determinant is one and

$$
\operatorname{tr}U=\sqrt2\sin(\pi/8).
$$

Its square lies in $\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$ with norm $1/2$, so it is not an
algebraic integer. A finite-order $SU(2)$ element has root-of-unity eigenvalues
and algebraic-integer trace. The contradiction is exact and does not use a
floating-point angle test or a universality theorem.

### 2.3 Finite-carrier quantifier

The proof ranges only over finite carriers whose calibrated reversible
process group acts:

1. functorially on the full ontic simplex;

3. transformation-noncontextually on the registered process quotient; and
4. faithfully, as forced by exact tomographic adequacy.

Within that class, every stochastic automorphism is a permutation and the
image group is finite. The theorem is sound.

It does not range over:

  memory;

Those are escape branches, not counterexamples to the printed theorem.

## 6. Strongest hostile objections

### O1 — “The quantum matrices were assumed, so the conclusion is circular.”

The matrices calibrate the operational target, not the ontology. The theorem
asks whether that established process family has a finite positive functorial
realization. It does not derive quantum predictions from positivity.

### O2 — “A finite probability vector can carry a continuum.”

True in kinematics: a finite simplex contains continuously many distributions.
The theorem does not use cardinality of distributions. It uses positive
stochastic inverses and exact functorial group composition, which make every
ontic transformation a simplex automorphism and hence a permutation. Dropping
that premise enters the hidden-irreversibility or nonfunctorial branch.

### O3 — “A large enough finite model handles every experiment.”

It handles every bounded finite word list. PC5 registers an unbounded exact
word family under one fixed generator law. Approximate and bounded-depth
models remain controls, not counterexamples.

### O4 — “Transformation contextuality already proves nonclassicality.”

Not as an ontology-selection theorem. The result is relative to operational
equivalence, convexity, and an admitted ontic structure. Retained histories
and non-disjoint structures change the antecedent. PC5 charges the escape
instead of denying it.

### O5 — “The continuous control is just the wavefunction in disguise.”

That is close to the intended hostile point. Positivity alone does not remove
phase-complete predictive structure. The hemisphere model is somewhat less
trivial than a delta distribution on the wavefunction, but its oriented
continuous carrier still bears the predictive burden.

### O6 — “Montina already proves U-Gen B.”

No. Its all-unitary, support-time-symmetry, and continuous-dimension premises
must be matched independently to the future v17 class. It also does not charge
law description, context, computation, or physical implementation.

### O7 — “Barandes escapes, so PC5 is irrelevant.”

An indivisible whole-law escape is explicitly preserved. PC5 narrows what
that escape must accomplish: it must generate the unbounded complete process
family without an intermediate finite functorial carrier or a supplied
whole-program answer table. That is the U-Gen constructive question.
