   $$

\partial_t\rho
=-\nabla\!\cdot(\rho b)+\nu\Delta\rho.

$$
b_*=b-2\nu\nabla\log\rho.

Define the osmotic and current velocities

$$
u=\frac{b-b_*}{2},
\qquad

$$
u=\nu\nabla\log\rho,

## 2. Proposition N1-A — positive kinematics and continuity

### Statement

The forward and backward Fokker--Planck equations imply

$$
\boxed{
\partial_t\rho+\nabla\!\cdot(\rho v)=0.}
$$

The osmotic velocity is fixed by the instantaneous density,

$$
\boxed{
u=\nu\nabla\log\rho.}
$$

### Proof

The forward equation is

$$
\partial_t\rho=-\nabla\!\cdot(\rho b)+\nu\Delta\rho.
$$

The time-reversed process has

$$
\partial_t\rho=-\nabla\!\cdot(\rho b_*)-\nu\Delta\rho.
$$

Adding and dividing by two gives

$$
\partial_t\rho
=-\nabla\!\cdot\left(\rho\frac{b+b_*}{2}\right)
=-\nabla\!\cdot(\rho v).
$$

Subtracting the drift relation gives

$$
u=\frac{b-b_*}{2}=\nu\nabla\log\rho.
$$

QED.

### Scope

This is ordinary diffusion kinematics. It does not select $\nu$, $V$, the
initial density, the current velocity, or a quantum state.

## 3. Time-symmetric mean acceleration

Let $D$ and $D_*$ denote the mean forward and backward derivatives. For a
smooth scalar $f$,

$$
Df
=\left(\partial_t+b\cdot\nabla+\nu\Delta\right)f,
$$

and

$$
D_*f
=\left(\partial_t+b_*\cdot\nabla-\nu\Delta\right)f.
$$

Nelson's time-symmetric mean acceleration is

$$
a
=\frac12(DD_*+D_*D)X_t.
$$

For the admitted smooth class this becomes

$$
\boxed{
a
=\partial_t v+(v\cdot\nabla)v
-(u\cdot\nabla)u-\nu\Delta u.}
$$

The dynamical postulate is the mean-Newton law

$$
\boxed{
ma=-\nabla V.}
$$

The symmetric acceleration is not forced by probability theory. It is a
physical premise motivated by treating the two time directions on equal
kinematic footing.

## 4. Proposition N1-B — local Schrödinger reconstruction

### Statement

Assume on a simply connected node-free domain that:

1. the positive diffusion kinematics of Sections 1--3 hold;
2. $ma=-\nabla V$;
3. the diffusion scale is

   $$
   \nu=\frac{\hbar}{2m};
   $$

4. the current momentum is locally exact,

   $$
   mv=\nabla S.
   $$

Then, after absorbing a function of time into $S$,

$$
\psi=\sqrt\rho\,e^{iS/\hbar}
$$

satisfies

$$
\boxed{
i\hbar\partial_t\psi
=\left(-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\Delta+V\right)\psi.}
$$

Conversely, every sufficiently regular node-free Schrödinger solution on a
simply connected domain defines $\rho$, $u$, $v$, $b$, and $b_*$ satisfying
the displayed stochastic equations locally.

### Proof

Write $R=\sqrt\rho$. Since

$$
u=\nu\nabla\log\rho=2\nu\nabla\log R,
$$

one has the identity

$$
(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nu\Delta u
=2\nu^2\nabla\left(\frac{\Delta R}{R}\right).
$$

With $v=\nabla S/m$,

$$
\partial_t v+(v\cdot\nabla)v
=\frac1m\nabla\left(
\partial_tS+\frac{|\nabla S|^2}{2m}
\right).
$$

Substitute these identities into $ma=-\nabla V$. The gradient of

$$
\partial_tS
+\frac{|\nabla S|^2}{2m}
+V
-2m\nu^2\frac{\Delta\sqrt\rho}{\sqrt\rho}
$$

vanishes. Using $2m\nu^2=\hbar^2/(2m)$ and absorbing the resulting
time-only function into $S$ gives the quantum Hamilton--Jacobi equation

$$
\partial_tS
+\frac{|\nabla S|^2}{2m}
+V
-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}
\frac{\Delta\sqrt\rho}{\sqrt\rho}
=0.
$$

Together with

$$
\partial_t\rho
+\nabla\!\cdot\left(\rho\frac{\nabla S}{m}\right)=0,
$$

this is exactly the real and imaginary decomposition of the Schrödinger
equation for $\psi=\sqrt\rho e^{iS/\hbar}$. Reversing the calculation gives
the local converse. QED.

### What is and is not derived

The complex wavefunction is a compact representation of two real stochastic
fields at this scope. But the theorem receives:

1. $\nu=\hbar/(2m)$;
2. a time-symmetric acceleration rule;
3. mean Newton dynamics;
4. an irrotational current sector;
5. initial $\rho$ and $v$; and
6. an external time and Euclidean configuration geometry.

The theorem does not explain those premises merely by combining them.
