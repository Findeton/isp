## 0. Executive result

The fluctuation--response gate established a clean boundary. Ordinary-positive
detailed balance can generate a forward/reverse response once a physical
log-likelihood coordinate is supplied, but it does not generate a reversible
quantum cycle. This packet asks the stronger whole-process question:

> Can a positive law on complete actual histories, together with a physically
> sourced reversal or reciprocity identity, determine the responses to all
> licensed interventions—including coherent recombination—without receiving
> the quantum process as input?

Seven bounded conclusions survive.

1. **Whole-process path reciprocity is genuine positive physics.** A forward
   path measure, a typed reversal, and a normalized Radon--Nikodym exponent
   uniquely generate the reverse path measure. This is stronger than a
   two-outcome detailed-balance table.
2. **Reciprocity is relational, not source complete.** It determines one law
   relative to another. It does not select the forward law, the physical
   reversal, the exponent, or the reader. Infinitely many complete processes
   satisfy the same zero-affinity reciprocity identity.
3. **Positive reciprocal bridges are exact two-boundary controls.** A
   nonnegative endpoint reweighting of a reference path measure preserves its
   conditional bridges. A product endpoint weight gives an exact Doob
   transform. Reciprocity does not itself imply invariance under time
   reversal; the reference dynamics and endpoint weight remain physical
   inputs.
4. **Nonnegative route composition has no destructive cross-term.** For a
   positive bridge kernel, unresolved disjoint routes add. Nonzero positive
   route weights cannot cancel to a dark record. Contextual replacement of
   the whole measure can darken, but the replacement law then carries the
   missing answer.
5. **Path reciprocity does not determine quantum recombination.** A two-route
   family can have identical positive route probabilities, reversal map, and
   zero entropy production while its balanced-output probabilities range from
   fully bright to fully dark. The free coordinate is off-diagonal relational
   orientation.
6. **There is no general no-go against positive ontology.** An explicit
   positive phase-bearing configuration with reversible rotations and a
   positive reader reproduces a scalar cosine fringe. The construction also
   proves its own insufficiency: reciprocity selects neither the cosine reader
   nor its visibility, and the response law has been postulated.
7. **Known quantum whole-process response is doubled.** Schwinger--Keldysh,
   decoherence-functional, Gram, and pair-history languages preserve two
   oriented histories before producing positive records. Strong positivity is
   compositionally distinguished in quantum-measure theory. These are serious
   predictive-law candidates, not thereby ontologies; their source, state,
   actuality, and matter--geometry coupling remain open.

## 3. Typed whole-process interface

For a forward experiment $F$, let

$$
(\Omega_F,\mathcal F_F,P_F)
\tag{2}
$$

be a complete ordinary-positive history law. Its reverse experiment $R$ has

$$
(\Omega_R,\mathcal F_R,P_R).
\tag{3}
$$

A typed reversal is a measurable bijection

$$
\Theta:\Omega_F\longrightarrow\Omega_R.
\tag{4}
$$

Physical readers are Markov kernels

$$
K_F(r\mid\omega),
\qquad
K_R(r\mid\eta),
\tag{5}
$$

from complete histories to retained record alphabets. Their predicted record
laws are

$$
p_F(r)=\int_{\Omega_F}K_F(r\mid\omega)P_F(d\omega),
\qquad
p_R(r)=\int_{\Omega_R}K_R(r\mid\eta)P_R(d\eta).
\tag{6}
$$

A whole-process reciprocity identity has the form

$$
\frac{dP_F}
{d(\Theta^{-1})_*P_R}(\omega)
=e^{\Sigma(\omega)},
\tag{7}
$$

where $Sigma$ is an independently typed reversal-odd physical ledger when
such an interpretation is earned.

Equation (7) is stronger than a relation between two final outcomes. It acts
on the complete law. It is still not a source-complete nomology until
$P_F,\Theta,\Sigma$, and the readers descend from one physical rule.

## 4. Theorem WPR-A — exact positive reverse-process reconstruction

Let $P_F$ be a probability measure, let $\Theta$ be as in (4), and let
$\Sigma:\Omega_F\to\mathbb R$ satisfy

$$
\int_{\Omega_F}e^{-\Sigma(\omega)}P_F(d\omega)=1.
\tag{8}
$$

Define

$$
P_R(B)
=
\int_{\Theta^{-1}(B)}
e^{-\Sigma(\omega)}P_F(d\omega),
\qquad B\in\mathcal F_R.
\tag{9}
$$

**Theorem WPR-A.** Equation (9) defines a unique ordinary-positive normalized
reverse measure satisfying (7).

### Proof

Nonnegativity is immediate. Equation (8) gives $P_R(\Omega_R)=1$. Pulling
$P_R$ back to $\Omega_F$ gives

$$
(\Theta^{-1})_*P_R(A)
=
\int_A e^{-\Sigma(\omega)}P_F(d\omega).
\tag{10}
$$

Hence the Radon--Nikodym derivative of $P_F$ with respect to this pullback is
$e^\Sigma$ wherever the measures are mutually absolutely continuous.
Conversely, (7) forces (10), hence (9). $\square$

### Scientific content

This is a legitimate positive parent construction. Given a forward complete
law and a physical path ledger, it predicts the reverse complete law rather
than merely fitting a reverse endpoint probability.

It does not derive the forward law, reversal map, ledger, or reader. In a
fluctuation theorem those objects come from a Hamiltonian, bath, work
protocol, equilibrium ensembles, and measurement semantics. In U0 they must
be generated or explicitly declared.

## 6. Theorem WPR-C — finite reciprocal bridge and Doob transform

Let $X_0,\ldots,X_T$ take values in a finite set. Let the reference law be

$$
R(x_{0:T})
=
\rho_0(x_0)
\prod_{t=0}^{T-1}M_t(x_{t+1}\mid x_t),
\tag{12}
$$

and let $H(x_0,x_T)\geq0$ have

$$
Z_H=\mathbb E_R[H(X_0,X_T)]>0.
\tag{13}
$$

Define the positive whole-path law

$$
P_H(x_{0:T})
=
\frac{H(x_0,x_T)}{Z_H}R(x_{0:T}).
\tag{14}
$$

**Theorem WPR-C.** The conditional interior bridge of $P_H$ given its
endpoints is exactly that of $R$:

$$
P_H(x_{1:T-1}\mid x_0,x_T)
=
R(x_{1:T-1}\mid x_0,x_T).
\tag{15}
$$

If

$$
H(x_0,x_T)=f(x_0)g(x_T)
\tag{16}
$$

with nonnegative factors and positive denominators, then $P_H$ is Markov with

$$
g_t(x)
=
\mathbb E_R[g(X_T)\mid X_t=x]
\tag{17}
$$

and transformed transitions

$$
M_t^H(y\mid x)
=
M_t(y\mid x)
\frac{g_{t+1}(y)}{g_t(x)}.
\tag{18}
$$

Its initial law is

$$
\rho_0^H(x)
=
\frac{\rho_0(x)f(x)g_0(x)}{Z_H}.
\tag{19}
$$

### Proof

At fixed endpoints, the factor $H/Z_H$ is constant and cancels from the
conditional law, proving (15). Under (16), summing future paths from time $t$
gives (17). Substitution in (14) telescopes the factors
$g_{t+1}/g_t$, leaving (18) and (19). Normalization follows from the backward
recursion for $g_t$. $\square$

### Interpretation

Equation (14) is a real positive, two-boundary whole-process construction. It
can be reciprocal without being Markov when $H$ is not rank one. It is
invariant under path reversal only under additional conditions—for example,
when the reference law is reversal invariant and $H(x_0,x_T)$ is symmetric.
It is a serious control against any claim that whole-boundary dependence or
non-Markovianity is uniquely quantum.

Its debt is equally exact:

1. $R$ supplies the reference dynamics;
2. $H$ supplies the endpoint coupling;
3. ordered path slots and endpoints are already typed;
4. the reader family is additional; and
5. nonnegative reweighting preserves Kolmogorov additivity.

The name “Schrödinger bridge” does not mean that (14) is real-time quantum
dynamics.

## 7. Theorem WPR-D — nonnegative route kernels cannot cancel

Let $f(x),g(y)\geq0$ be boundary factors and let
$K_1,\ldots,K_m$ be nonnegative route kernels. Define the unnormalized record
weight through route $j$ by

$$
W_j
=
\sum_{x,y}f(x)K_j(x,y)g(y).
\tag{20}
$$

Opening all unresolved routes without changing their individual weights gives

$$
K_{\rm open}=\sum_{j=1}^mK_j.
\tag{21}
$$

**Theorem WPR-D.** The open-route weight is

$$
W_{\rm open}
=
\sum_{j=1}^mW_j.
\tag{22}
$$

In particular, if any $W_j>0$, then $W_{\rm open}>0$.

### Proof

Insert (21) into (20) and use linearity of the finite sum. Every summand is
nonnegative. $\square$

This is the reciprocal-bridge version of the fixed-weight darkening
obstruction. It applies only when “opening a route” means adding a disjoint
nonnegative kernel while preserving old route weights and boundary factors.

A contextual positive law can evade the theorem by changing $K_j$, $f$, $g$,
the history identification, or the reader when the whole apparatus changes.
That is physically allowed. The resulting context map must then be generated
by one source law; otherwise the target interference has merely moved into
the replacement rule.

## 8. Theorem WPR-E — identical path reciprocity, different recombination

Let the two route labels be $h=0,1$ with the same ordinary-positive law for
every $\phi$:

$$
P_{\rm path}(0)=P_{\rm path}(1)=\frac12.
\tag{23}
$$

Take identity reversal and zero reciprocity exponent. Hence every member has

Now define the predictive amplitude vector

$$
a_\phi
=
\frac1{\sqrt2}
\begin{pmatrix}
1\\ e^{i\phi}
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{24}
$$

A supplied balanced recombiner gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
p_+(\phi)
&=
\frac{1+\cos\phi}{2},\\
p_-(\phi)
&=
\frac{1-\cos\phi}{2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{25}
$$

**Theorem WPR-E.** The path probabilities, reversal, and reciprocity exponent
are independent of $\phi$, while the recombined record law ranges from
deterministically plus to deterministically minus.

### Proof

The diagonal data are

$$
|a_{\phi,0}|^2
=
|a_{\phi,1}|^2
=
\frac12.
\tag{26}
$$

Applying the balanced vectors $(1,\pm1)/\sqrt2$ to (24) gives (25). At
$\phi=0$, $p_+=1$; at $\phi=\pi$, $p_-=1$. $\square$

The missing relational object can be written

$$
D_\phi
=
a_\phi a_\phi^\dagger
=
\frac12
\begin{pmatrix}
1&e^{-i\phi}\\
e^{i\phi}&1

\tag{27}
$$

Every $D_\phi$ has the same diagonal (23). Classical path reciprocity sees
only that diagonal. Recombination reads its off-diagonal orientation.

This is not evidence that $D_\phi$ is material ontology. It proves only that

## 9. Theorem WPR-F — positive scalar fringe counterexample

To prevent an illegitimate general no-go, construct an ordinary-positive
model directly.

Let the actual configuration be

$$
\lambda\in S^1.
\tag{28}
$$

Prepare $\lambda=0$. A reversible control acts as

$$
T_\theta(\lambda)=\lambda+\theta\pmod{2\pi}.
\tag{29}
$$

For any $0\leq v\leq1$, define the positive reader

$$
K_v(+\mid\lambda)
=
\frac{1+v\cos\lambda}{2},
\qquad
K_v(-\mid\lambda)=1-K_v(+\mid\lambda).
\tag{30}
$$

**Theorem WPR-F.** Equations (28)--(30) define a normalized
ordinary-positive reversible process with

$$
p_v(+\mid\theta)
=
\frac{1+v\cos\theta}{2}.
\tag{31}
$$

For $v=1$, this is the ideal scalar two-output fringe (25).

### Proof

The rotation is bijective with inverse $T_{-\theta}$. Since
$|v\cos\lambda|\leq1$, both reader probabilities lie in $[0,1]$ and sum to
one. Substitution of the prepared and rotated configuration into (30) gives
(31). $\square$

### Why this does not close U0

The construction proves that ordinary positivity and reversible cycles are
compatible with a quantum-shaped scalar fringe. It also exposes the inputs:

1. the circle-valued configuration;
2. its sharply prepared reference;
3. the rotation action;
4. the cosine reader;
5. the visibility $v$; and
6. the identification of this response with the apparatus.

Reciprocity selects none of them. Replacing cosine by any admissible periodic
response produces another positive model. The construction has no mixed,
adaptive, Bell, contextual, eraser, material-source, or interacting
completion. It is a comparator, not a native derivation.

This theorem is important: WPR-D is not a no-go for all positive contextual
ontologies. It is a no-go only for fixed nonnegative route union. A successful
positive theory must earn the contextual response mechanism exemplified—but
not explained—by (30).

## 10. The doubled quantum whole-process control

For a supplied quantum initial state $\rho$, dynamics, and two source
histories $J_+,J_-$, the Schwinger--Keldysh generating functional has the

$$
Z[J_+,J_-]
=
\operatorname{Tr}
\!\left(
U[J_+]\rho U[J_-]^\dagger
\right).
\tag{32}
$$

Unitarity gives

$$
Z[J,J]=1.
\tag{33}
$$

Defining average and difference sources,

$$
J_r=\frac{J_++J_-}{2},
\qquad
J_a=J_+-J_-,
\tag{34}
$$

functional derivatives with respect to the two source types organize
correlations and response. In a path representation, (32) contains the
oriented pair weight

$$
\exp\!\left[
\frac{i}{\hbar}S[\phi_+]
-
\frac{i}{\hbar}S[\phi_-]
\right].
\tag{35}
$$

This is not an ordinary probability on one actual history. The two branches
are bookkeeping for a density operator, amplitude and conjugate amplitude, or
equivalent pair-history structure. KMS symmetry can relate fluctuations and
response inside this object, but it receives the action, state, quantum

The known-physics lesson is structural:

$$
\boxed{
\text{complete quantum response uses orientation between two histories,

\tag{36}

That statement is representation-level. A deeper positive ontology could

## 11. Theorem WPR-G — exact finite pair expansion

Let $A_r$ be the route set feeding record $r$, and let each route carry a
complex predictive number $a_h$. The coherent record weight is

$$
q(r)
=
\left|
\sum_{h\in A_r}a_h
\right|^2.
\tag{37}
$$

**Theorem WPR-G.** Equation (37) is exactly

$$
q(r)
=
\sum_{h,h'\in A_r}
D(h,h'),
\qquad
D(h,h')=a_h a_{h'}^*.
\tag{38}
$$

The matrix $D$ is positive semidefinite. Its diagonal is an
ordinary-positive route weight, but its off-diagonal entries need not be real
or nonnegative and are required for destructive cancellation.

### Proof

Expanding the modulus square gives (38). For every complex vector $z$,

$$
\sum_{h,h'}z_h^*D(h,h')z_{h'}
=
\left|
\sum_h z_h^*a_h
\right|^2
\geq0.
\tag{39}
$$

If all off-diagonal terms are discarded, (38) becomes a nonnegative sum and
cannot express cancellation between nonzero routes. $\square$

For mixed/open processes, $D$ need not be rank one but strong positivity
retains a Gram representation. The composition results cited in Section 2.4
make strong positivity a serious physical clue: it is stable under products
and is maximal at the cited decoherence-functional scope.

Two qualifications are mandatory.

1. This is a natural universal Gram envelope for finite quantum record laws,
   not a proved minimal ontology.
2. A positive Gaussian or other latent lift of $D$ does not explain it when
   $D$ is the covariance input and a norm-square reader is retained.

The result does not reduce a Barandes indivisible process to a chain-rule

$$
\mathcal S_{\mathcal N}(e)

\Gamma_e,
\tag{40}

The stochastic--quantum and unistochastic representation theorems make this
programme mathematically plausible. They do not supply $\mathcal S_\mathcal N$.

There is therefore no contradiction between the present result and the claim
that Hilbert space may be secondary. The missing physics is the nomological
source of the whole transition family and its intervention structure.

No theorem in this packet selects among H1--H3. Failure of one positive
construction cannot promote Hilbert ontology by elimination, and the success
of a Gram representation cannot make pair histories material by syntax.
