## 4. K-CLC exact architecture

### 4.1 Supplied structure

The formal relativistic construction assumes:

6. a commuting final-boundary measurement $E_S$;
7. the Born rule;
8. an admissible family of late hypersurfaces; and
9. existence and sequence independence of the required limits.

None is derived by the construction.

### 4.2 Final-boundary probability

Let $(Z_S,\Sigma_S)$ be the outcome space of the selected late-boundary
measurement and let $E_S(dz)$ be its projection-valued or generalized
measurement. The final Born measure is

$$
\mu_S(dz)
=
\langle\psi_0|
U_{S_0S}^{\dagger}E_S(dz)U_{S_0S}
|\psi_0\rangle.
\tag{3}
$$

One $z\in Z_S$ is taken to be the actual final boundary condition. No external
observer performs the measurement. The measurement is a mathematical rule
that defines the sample space and probability law.

Equation (3) is ordinary-positive and normalized when $E_S$ is a normalized
measurement:

$$
\mu_S(A)\geq0,
\qquad
\mu_S(Z_S)=1.
\tag{4}
$$

It is also entirely downstream of the supplied quantum law.

S_{\rm out}(y)=S\setminus J^+(y).
