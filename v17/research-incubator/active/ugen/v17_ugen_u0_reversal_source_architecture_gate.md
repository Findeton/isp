## 3. Abstract reversal-bearing experiment

Let $(E,\Sigma_E)$ be a measurable space of fully typed experimental source
packets and $(\Omega,\Sigma_\Omega)$ a measurable space of complete retained
records.

Let

$$
\Theta_E:E\to E,
\qquad
\Theta_\Omega:\Omega\to\Omega
\tag{1}
$$

be measurable involutions:

$$
\Theta_E^2=\mathrm{id}_E,
\qquad
\Theta_\Omega^2=\mathrm{id}_\Omega.
\tag{2}
$$

A complete ordinary-positive law is a Markov kernel

$$
K:E\rightsquigarrow\Omega.
\tag{3}
$$

"Markov kernel" here is only the measure-theoretic term for a conditional
probability measure indexed by the full experiment. It asserts no temporal
Markov property, restart law, intermediate state, or divisibility.

It is **reversal covariant** when

$$
K(\Theta_E e;A)
=
K(e;\Theta_\Omega^{-1}A)
\qquad
\forall e\in E,\ A\in\Sigma_\Omega.
\tag{4}
$$

Equivalently,

$$
K_{\Theta_E e}
=
(\Theta_\Omega)_*K_e.
\tag{5}
$$

The source packet $e$ must include every physical preparation, contingent
reference state, intervention program, reader orientation, and retained
record convention needed for (4) to be meaningful. A passive renaming is not
an active reversal, and an active reversal is not automatically a neutral
presentation change.

Let $J:\Omega\to\mathbb R$ be an integrable reversal-odd record statistic:

$$
J(\Theta_\Omega\omega)=-J(\omega).
\tag{6}
$$

Define its response

$$
j(e)=\int_\Omega J(\omega)K(e;d\omega).
\tag{7}
$$

## 4. Theorem ORS-A — an odd response needs an odd source distinction

If $K$ is reversal covariant, then

$$
j(\Theta_E e)=-j(e).
\tag{8}
$$

In particular, if $e$ is a reversal fixed point,

$$
\Theta_Ee=e,
\tag{9}
$$

then

$$
j(e)=0.
\tag{10}
$$

### Proof

Using (5) and the pushforward change of variables,

$$
\begin{aligned}
j(\Theta_Ee)
&=\int J(\omega)(\Theta_\Omega)_*K_e(d\omega)\\
&=\int J(\Theta_\Omega\omega)K_e(d\omega)\\
&=-j(e).
\end{aligned}
$$

If $e$ is fixed, (8) gives $j(e)=-j(e)$. $\square$

### Descriptor-blind corollary

Let

$$
d:E\to B
\tag{11}
$$

be a proposed source descriptor satisfying

$$
d(\Theta_Ee)=d(e).
\tag{12}
$$

If the proposed law factors only through $d$,

$$
K_e=\bar K_{d(e)},
\tag{13}
$$

then $K_e=K_{\Theta_Ee}$. Combined with reversal covariance, $K_e$ is
$\Theta_\Omega$ invariant and every integrable odd statistic has zero mean.

Thus a nonzero directed response cannot descend through a source interface
that has identified the physically reversed experiments.

### Exact meaning

The theorem does not demand a law-level arrow. It demands that the fully
typed physical packet distinguish the reversed cases somewhere if their
responses differ. The distinction may be:

1. a contingent source or reference state;
2. a boundary/sector condition;
3. an active intervention;
4. a reader orientation;
5. a law-level asymmetric parameter; or
6. a correction showing that the proposed reversal was not a symmetry.

An array index, program counter, or chosen drawing orientation is none of

## 5. Theorem ORS-B — symmetric ensemble cancellation

$$

\tag{14}

$$
(\Theta_\Omega)_*\bar K=\bar K
\tag{16}
$$

and every integrable reversal-odd statistic obeys

$$
\int J\,d\bar K=0.
\tag{17}

### Proof

For measurable $A$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar K(\Theta_\Omega^{-1}A)

&=\int_EK(\Theta_Ee;A)\mu(de)\\
&=\int_EK(e;A)\mu(de).
\end{aligned}
$$

The last equality uses (14). Equation (17) follows from invariance and
oddness. $\square$

## 7. Proposition ORS-D — noncommuting intervention grammar is not a selector

$$

\tag{24}

$$
\Theta_{\mathsf P}(pq)
=
\Theta_{\mathsf P}(q)\Theta_{\mathsf P}(p).
\tag{25}
$$

$$

\tag{26}
$$

$$
s(\Theta_{\mathsf P}p)=-s(p).
\tag{27}
$$

For every $a\in[-1,1]$,

$$

\frac{1+a s(p)y}{2}
\tag{28}

### Proof

then has the same proof as Theorem ORS-C. Different $a$ values are distinct
