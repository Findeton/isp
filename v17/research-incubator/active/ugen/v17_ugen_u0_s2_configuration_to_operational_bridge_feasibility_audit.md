## 4. Theorem BFA-A — observational law does not determine intervention law

### Statement

There exist two ordinary-positive finite physical models with the same complete
passive observational law and different responses to the same intervention
label. Therefore no function of the observational law alone determines the
intervention semantics.

### Witness

Let $U$ be a fair bit. Define two models on observed bits $(X,Y)$.

$$
\begin{array}{lll}
M_{X\to Y}:&X=U,&Y=X,\\
M_{Y\to X}:&Y=U,&X=Y.
\end{array}
\tag{4}
$$

Both have

$$
P(X=0,Y=0)=P(X=1,Y=1)=\frac12,
\qquad
P(0,1)=P(1,0)=0.
\tag{5}
$$

Now replace the mechanism setting $X$ by a physical clamp $X:=0$ while leaving
the other mechanism intact. Then

$$
P_{M_{X\to Y}}(Y=0\mid\mathrm{clamp}(X=0))=1,
\qquad
P_{M_{Y\to X}}(Y=0\mid\mathrm{clamp}(X=0))=\frac12.
\tag{6}
$$

The passive law (5) is identical and the physical response (6) differs.
$\square$

### Consequence

An observed configuration distribution, even if exact and complete over
passive histories, does not generate a test or transformation category. A
bridge must include the physical mechanism changed by an intervention, or one
closed parent law from which both unclamped and clamped experiments descend.

This theorem does not assume a fundamental causal DAG. The two mechanisms are
only a finite witness that intervention semantics contains information absent
from the passive pushforward.

## 5. Theorem BFA-B — bare carrier does not determine subsystem factorization

### Statement

A probability law on an anonymous carrier does not in general determine which
transformations are local to a subsystem or which product decomposition is
physical.

### Witness

Let $\mathcal C=\{0,1\}^2$ carry the uniform law. In factorization $F$, use
coordinates $(a,b)$. In factorization $G$, use

$$
c=a,
\qquad
d=a\oplus b.
\tag{7}
$$

Both are bijective identifications of the same four points with two bits and
both preserve the uniform law. A local flip of $c$ holding $d$ fixed is

$$
(a,b)\longmapsto(a\oplus1,b\oplus1),
\tag{8}
$$

which is a two-factor transformation in the $F$ coordinates. Conversely, a
local $a$ flip holding $b$ fixed changes both $(c,d)$. Thus the same carrier and
law support inequivalent locality assignments. $\square$

### Consequence

Tensor or Cartesian factorization requires physical evidence: independently
preparable source ports, restricted couplings, commuting or separately
addressable intervention families, product controls, and recombination tests.
Local distinguishability cannot be derived before the meaning of “local” is
fixed.

The witness does not say that system divisions are arbitrary in nature. It
says the divisions reside in physical coupling structure, not in the
cardinality of a bare set.

$$
R_{\rm parity}(x)=x\bmod2,
\qquad
R_{\rm block}(x)=\left\lfloor\frac{x}{2}\right\rfloor.

Both yield fair binary records, but their fibers cross. A third reader may

The bridge cannot be selected by elegance alone. Its missing coordinates have
direct empirical meanings.

### 11.1 Systems

A system boundary is supported when independently prepared source variations,
localized couplings, persistence under transport, and recombination controls

### 11.2 Interventions

An intervention is a reproducible physical replacement of a coupling or
boundary mechanism whose resource, failure, and leakage channels are included
in the parent. It is not conditionalization on an observed value.

### 11.3 Sequential structure

Sequential composition is earned when a retained boundary is complete for the
licensed continuation and restart tests agree. If future response retains
unread information from before the seam, the seam is not a division.

### 11.4 Parallel structure

Parallel composition is earned by independent source provenance,
no-interaction controls, permutation/reassembly tests, and an interacting
parent obtained by a physical coupling change. It is not inherited from a
Cartesian product.

### 11.5 Records

A record is supported by metastability or error-correcting persistence,
repeatable independent readout, controlled erasure, and a quantified
disturbance/resource ledger. Redundancy can establish operational objectivity;
it does not alone select one actual outcome.

### 11.6 Actuality

The native law must state whether one complete parent history, one stable
record, all Everett branches, a coevent, a collapse history, or another object
is actual. Normalized record probabilities do not answer this automatically.
