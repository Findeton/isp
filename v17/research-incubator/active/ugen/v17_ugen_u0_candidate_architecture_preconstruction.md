## 3. Finite controlled experiment tree

Fix a finite horizon $T$. At laboratory slot $t$ let $C_t$ be the admitted
control alphabet and $R_t$ the complete-record alphabet. A control word and
record word are

$$
c_{0:T-1}=(c_0,\ldots,c_{T-1}),
\qquad
r_{0:T}=(r_0,\ldots,r_T).
\tag{1}
$$

A direct whole-program law is a family

$$
P_{c_{0:T-1}}(r_{0:T})\geq0,
\qquad
\sum_{r_{0:T}}P_{c_{0:T-1}}(r_{0:T})=1.
\tag{2}
$$

It is **prefix autonomous** when for every cut $t$, any two complete control
words with the same prefix $c_{0:t-1}$ have the same earlier-record marginal:

$$
\sum_{r_{t+1:T}}
P_{c_{0:T-1}}(r_{0:T})
=
\sum_{r_{t+1:T}}
P_{c'_{0:T-1}}(r_{0:T})
\quad
\text{if }c_{0:t-1}=c'_{0:t-1}.
\tag{3}
$$

Equation (3) is an operational statement about freely extendible programs and
already retained records. It is not a derivation of fundamental time or
spacetime causality.

A sequential positive-memory realization consists of an initial normalized
joint measure $\nu_0(dm_0,dr_0)$ and normalized kernels

$$
K_t(dm_{t+1},dr_{t+1}\mid m_t,h_t,c_t),
\qquad
h_t=(c_{0:t-1},r_{0:t}),
\tag{4}
$$

whose product and marginalization reproduce (2). The memory may be finite,
continuous, contextual, or otherwise measurable; no form is assumed.

Equations (1)--(4) use open-loop words only to keep the theorem typed. A
finite adaptive policy is covered by treating its branch rule as the admitted
control at each history, or equivalently by applying the construction on each
consistent policy branch. No infinite-horizon, continuum-time, or

## 4. Theorem CAP-A — prefix-autonomous whole laws and history-memory laws are representationally equivalent

### Statement

At finite scope:

1. every prefix-autonomous whole-program law (2) has an exact
   ordinary-positive sequential realization with memory equal to the complete
   observed history; and
2. every sequential realization (4) whose initial state and earlier kernels
   are independent of not-yet-inserted controls induces a prefix-autonomous
   whole-program law.

### Proof

For the first direction, define the positive-support conditional

$$
k_t(r_{t+1}\mid h_t,c_t)
=
\frac{
P(r_{0:t+1}\mid c_{0:t})
}{
P(r_{0:t}\mid c_{0:t-1})
}.
\tag{5}
$$

Prefix autonomy makes the numerator and denominator independent of later
controls. Off support choose any normalized completion. Let the memory update
deterministically from $h_t$ to

$$
h_{t+1}=(h_t,c_t,r_{t+1}).
\tag{6}
$$

Initialize $m_0=h_0=r_0$ with $\nu_0$ concentrated on that identification
and with $r_0$ marginal $P(r_0)$. Thereafter set $m_t=h_t$. The initial law
times the product of (5) then telescopes to (2), exactly as in the previously
recorded HM.A theorem.

For the converse, marginalize the product of kernels (4) over every memory and
record after cut $t$. Normalization removes every later kernel. The remaining
expression contains only $\nu_0$ and kernels through $t-1$, none of which
depends on $c_{t:T-1}$. Hence the earlier-record marginal obeys (3).
$\square$

### Meaning

“Sequential memory” and “whole-boundary functional” do not define two
empirically disjoint finite theory classes when prefix autonomy holds. A
candidate earns physical distinction only from:

1. independent evidence for its carrier;
2. whether that carrier is controllable or readable;
3. its state and precision cost;
4. whether the construction used the target law;
5. its actuality claim;
6. its scaling with depth and composition; and
7. its behavior outside the calibrated experiment tree.

The history realization consumes the complete target law and earns zero
source-completion credit.

$$
P(dr_{0:T}\mid c_{0:T-1})
=
\int
P_>(dr_{t+1:T}\mid m_t,c_{t:T-1})

\tag{7}

This is positive stochastic factorization through the enlarged carrier.
$\square$

p_{xy}(q\mid\Psi)=p_x(q\mid\Psi),

p_{xy}(r\mid\Psi)=p_y(r\mid\Psi).

## 8. Theorem CAP-D — the printed stochastic marginal condition does not select Tsirelson

For bits $x,y,q,r\in\{0,1\}$, define the no-signalling box family

$$
P_E(q,r\mid x,y)
=
\frac14\left[1+(-1)^{xy+q+r}E\right],
\qquad -1\leq E\leq1.
\tag{10}
$$

Write

$$
C_{xy}=\sum_{q,r}(-1)^{q+r}P_E(q,r\mid x,y),
\qquad
\beta=\frac14(C_{00}+C_{01}+C_{10}-C_{11}).
$$

Here $\beta$ is the normalized CHSH bias. The corresponding game-winning
probability is $(1+\beta)/2$; it is not the same numerical quantity as
$\beta$.

### Statement

Every member of (10) satisfies the marginal-independence condition (8), while
its normalized CHSH bias is $\beta=E$ and its game-winning probability is
$(1+E)/2$. In particular, $E=1$ is a PR box exceeding the quantum bias bound
$1/\sqrt2$.

### Proof

For fixed $x,y,q$, summing (10) over $r$ cancels the term proportional to $E$:

$$
\sum_rP_E(q,r\mid x,y)=\frac12.
\tag{11}
$$

Likewise,

$$
\sum_qP_E(q,r\mid x,y)=\frac12.
\tag{12}
$$

Thus both marginals are independent of the remote setting and satisfy (8)
with a fixed preparation label $\Psi$. Direct substitution gives
$C_{xy}=(-1)^{xy}E$ and hence $\beta=E$. At $E=1$,

$$
P_1(q,r\mid x,y)
=
\begin{cases}
\tfrac12,&q\oplus r=xy,\\
0,&q\oplus r\ne xy,
\end{cases}
\tag{13}
$$

so every round wins, equivalently $\beta=1$. $\square$

### Consequence

At the displayed CHSH interface, stochastic marginal independence does not
restrict the no-signalling strength parameter at all. It cannot imply the
product-unitary lift (9). Some additional physical composition premise is
required.

This does not invalidate the conditional theorem

```text
COMMON QUANTUM HILBERT REPRESENTATION
+ PRODUCT LOCAL OPERATIONS
=> TSIRELSON BOUND.
```

It blocks the stronger interpretation

```text
ORDINARY POSITIVITY
+ PRINTED STOCHASTIC MARGINAL CAUSALITY
=> PRODUCT LOCAL OPERATIONS
=> TSIRELSON BOUND.
```

The second implication is not established by the cited equations.

CAP-D does not claim that the PR box satisfies a definition in which
product-local operation factorization is itself included as a primitive part
of “causal locality.” It isolates the exact logical burden: that factorization
is stronger than the displayed endpoint marginal equalities and must receive
an independent stochastic/physical justification if the bound is to count as
native rather than representation-supplied. The audited source itself lists a
future stochastic proof through its interference-correction matrices as an
open direction.

## 9. Theorem CAP-E — per-setting unistochasticity also fails to exclude the PR control

### Construction

Order the four outcome basis states as

$$
|00\rangle,|01\rangle,|10\rangle,|11\rangle.
\tag{14}
$$

For $xy=0$, let

$$
v_0=\frac1{\sqrt2}(1,0,0,1)^T,
\tag{15}
$$

and for $xy=1$, let

$$
v_1=\frac1{\sqrt2}(0,1,1,0)^T.
\tag{16}
$$

Each $v_s$ is a unit vector and can be completed to a $4\times4$ unitary
$U_s$ with $v_s$ as its first column. Starting the outcome register in its
first basis state and applying $U_{xy}$ gives exactly (13) after a
configuration-basis readout.

Equivalently, on a setting register and outcome register, the controlled
global unitary

$$
U_{\mathrm{PR}}
=
\sum_{x,y}|xy\rangle\!\langle xy|\otimes U_{xy}
\tag{17}
$$

is unitary and reproduces the PR endpoint family.

### Meaning

The PR family is not excluded by ordinary positivity or by the existence of a
global unistochastic representation. What fails is a representation of the
same correlations by one shared quantum state and independently local product
operations. The standard Tsirelson theorem proves that failure.

Thus “unistochastic” and “locally composable unistochastic with one common
state” are different premises. The latter carries the quantum correlation
boundary and cannot be attributed to stochastic marginal locality without a
separate derivation.

## 16. Empirical discriminators between physically completed architectures

Once explicit laws exist, representation equivalence can be broken only by
tests tied to their additional physical claims.

1. **Memory intervention:** directly perturb, erase, replace, or isolate the
   proposed memory carrier.
2. **Prefix test:** choose future extensions after an earlier record is
   irreversibly retained and compare its marginal across choices.
3. **Source reversal:** reverse a prepared physical source while holding
   neutral presentation fixed.
4. **Context transfer:** implement operationally equivalent controls with a
   different apparatus and test no-refit prediction.
5. **Composite closure:** combine independently calibrated sources using a new
   interaction interface hidden during construction.
6. **Multi-copy correlation:** test the principle used to exclude supraquantum
   boxes under independently prepared copies.
7. **Intrinsic P/T control:** distinguish contingent source handedness from
   interaction-level asymmetry.
8. **Resource scaling:** increase depth, system size, adaptive branching, and
