## 3. Theorem S2-A — operational-equivalence wall

### Statement

Let laws $L_1,L_2$ satisfy

$$
\mathcal O_{\mathfrak E}(L_1)
=\mathcal O_{\mathfrak E}(L_2).
\tag{3}
$$

Let $Y$ be any statistic, adaptive decision, estimator, or adjudication rule
whose inputs are the registered programmes, public packets, and complete
records from experiments in $\mathfrak E$. Then $Y$ has the same sampling law
under $L_1$ and $L_2$.

### Proof

For every fixed or adaptively chosen experiment, equality (3) gives the same
conditional distribution of the next complete record given the retained
prefix and public controls. Induction over the adaptive experiment tree gives
the same joint distribution over all settings and records. Every $Y$ is a
measurable function or randomized postprocessing of that joint record, so its
pushforward law is the same. $\square$

### Meaning

1. A hidden ontology exactly reproducing R1 cannot be selected by rerunning
   the same R1-domain experiments.
2. Better prose, visualization, or mathematical elegance is not E2 evidence.
3. Independent evidence for the hidden referent, a smaller source/resource
   burden, or a prospective deviation can still distinguish the proposal.
4. The theorem does not say empirically equivalent ontologies are
   metaphysically identical. It says the declared record experiment cannot
   decide between them.

Define the empirical residual on a held-out family $\mathfrak H$ by

$$
\Delta_{\mathfrak H}(L,R1)
=\sup_{(B,e)\in\mathfrak H}
d_{\rm TV}\!\left(
P_L(\cdot\mid B,e),P_{R1}(\cdot\mid B,e)
\right).
\tag{4}
$$

$\Delta=0$ implies no complete-record discriminator on $\mathfrak H$.
$\Delta>0$ identifies a prospective E2 target but says nothing about which
law nature chooses until the target is independently evaluated.

## 4. Theorem S2-B — positive Gaussian-reference covariance relocation

The most tempting escape from the source problem is to call amplitudes random
positive fields. The following exact construction shows both why that is
possible and why it does not by itself explain quantum physics.

### Statement

Let $\Omega=\{1,\ldots,n\}$ and let $D\in\mathbb C^{n\times n}$ be Hermitian
positive semidefinite. Let $\{A_r\}_{r\in\mathcal R}$ be a partition of
$\Omega$, and assume

$$
0<Z_D
=\sum_r\sum_{i,j\in A_r}D_{ij}<\infty.
\tag{5}
$$

There exists an ordinary-positive joint probability measure on real-doubled
field values $Z\in\mathbb C^n$ and a record $r$, constructed from a Gaussian
reference measure, whose record marginal is

$$
P_D(r)
=\frac{\sum_{i,j\in A_r}D_{ij}}{Z_D}.
\tag{6}
$$

### Construction and proof

Let $\nu_D$ be the centered complex Gaussian measure on the support of $D$
with covariance

$$
\mathbb E_{\nu_D}[Z_i\overline{Z_j}]=D_{ij}.
\tag{7}
$$

Equivalently, factor $D=VV^\dagger$, sample a standard complex Gaussian
$g$, and set $Z=Vg$. Define

$$
S_r(Z)=\sum_{i\in A_r}Z_i
\tag{8}
$$

and the joint measure

$$
\mathbb P_D(dZ,r)
=\frac{|S_r(Z)|^2}{Z_D}\,\nu_D(dZ).
\tag{9}
$$

It is nonnegative. Moreover,

$$
\sum_r\int\mathbb P_D(dZ,r)
=\frac1{Z_D}
\sum_r\mathbb E|S_r(Z)|^2
=1,
\tag{10}
$$

and

$$
\int\mathbb P_D(dZ,r)
=\frac1{Z_D}
\sum_{i,j\in A_r}
\mathbb E[Z_i\overline{Z_j}]
=P_D(r).
\tag{11}
$$

Thus the joint law is an ordinary Kolmogorov probability law. $\square$

### What the theorem earns

1. Ordinary-positive extended variables are mathematically compatible with
   every finite strongly positive Gram record law.
2. Complex material objects are unnecessary; $Z$ can be represented by real
   coordinates plus a distinguished orientation $J$.
3. Interference comes from covariance between latent components while the
   final record is ordinary positive.

### What it does not earn

1. The full kernel $D$ is exactly the covariance input in equation (7).
2. The conditional response is still

   $$
   P(r\mid Z)
   =\frac{|S_r(Z)|^2}{\sum_s|S_s(Z)|^2},
   \tag{12}
   $$

   wherever the denominator is nonzero: the norm-square rule has moved, not
   disappeared.
3. The marginal law of $Z$ under (9) is size biased relative to $\nu_D$; the
   construction is one all-at-once joint law, not “sample a free classical
   wave and independently read it.”
4. A different target/program kernel $D_{B,e}$ gives a different covariance
   unless one physical source rule generates the family.
5. The theorem supplies no prefix consistency, independent-product law,
   interacting parent, Bell-local screening, material source descent,
   resource bound, or actuality reason for $Z$.
6. Treating $Z$ as actual introduces a new ontology but no E2 prediction by
   itself.

### Corollary S2-B.1 — covariance is a zero-credit payload when target equivalent

If $D_{B,e}$ is reconstructed from the target quantum process and inserted as
the covariance of $Z$, the positive latent-field model is a compiler. Calling
$D$ noise, vacuum fluctuation, random wave, hidden context, or stochastic
geometry does not change its information content.

### Corollary S2-B.2 — the real work is a covariance/source law

A positive latent-field proposal earns native credit only if one fixed,
target-independent physical rule generates the covariance family, its
composition, contingent boundary, record coupling, and resources from E1
facts before target opening. That is the original source problem in a useful
new representation.

4. how settings can be varied operationally;
5. how source-setting correlations are generated without target advice; and
6. what held-out deviation distinguishes the proposal from R1.

Without these, “all at once” is an information-hiding location.

### Route D — standard quantum law plus objective actualization

Keep R1 quantum dynamics and add collapse, flash, mass-density, record, or

### Route E — Euclidean/reflection-positive law

analytic continuation, recover complete interventions/readers, and state what

field is not automatically sampling what happens in real time.

### Route F — operational/framework reconstruction

Purification, local discriminability, ideal compression, causal structure,
and related principles can reconstruct finite quantum theory inside a stated
comparison class. This may explain why the oriented quantum framework is
special. It does not select a material Hamiltonian, boundary state, detector,
or actual history. It is a Q-layer result, not full S2.

### Route G — Barandes-native source completion

Take ordinary-positive configurations and indivisible $\Gamma$ laws as

This route earns the programme's highest interest if one uniform rule

### Route H — discrete, lattice, graph, or causal-set carrier
