## 2. Primary source S1 — Bargmann ray representations

Valentine Bargmann,
[*On Unitary Ray Representations of Continuous
Groups*](https://doi.org/10.2307/1969831),
*Annals of Mathematics* **59**, 1--46 (1954).

Bargmann provides the mathematical framework for continuous projective
unitary representations and their central extensions. In nonrelativistic
quantum mechanics this framework explains why Galilei transformations act on
rays rather than through an ordinary representation and why a central charge
can label sectors.

G2 uses that architecture only after declaring:

1. a Hilbert carrier;
2. strong continuity;
3. projective unitarity;
4. an irreducible sector; and
5. the mass central charge.

The source does not say that classical Galilean relativity by itself selects
these quantum premises.

## 4. Primary source S3 — Feynman's real-time action formulation

[*Space-Time Approach to Non-Relativistic Quantum
Mechanics*](https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.20.367),

quantities, that alternatives are added before taking an absolute square, and

This source supports:

1. the real-time time-sliced control;

3. the free action

   $$
   S_{\rm cl}(x,t;y,0)

4. equivalence with ordinary nonrelativistic quantum mechanics.

The source describes the action-phase contribution as a postulate. Writing

$$
e^{iS/\hbar}

therefore does not derive the action, $\hbar$, the path carrier, or a physical
fine-history probability law. G2 derives the free endpoint kernel independently
from the declared representation algebra, then uses Feynman as the path-sum
cross-check.

## 6. Primary sources S5/S6 — Osterwalder--Schrader reconstruction

Konrad Osterwalder and Robert Schrader,

does not construct nature's Euclidean measure, select a vacuum, or prove that
Euclidean sample fields are the real-time ontology.

G2 therefore keeps reflection-positive Euclidean nomology live in the rival
register without declaring it fundamental.

## 7. Primary sources S7/S8 — Nelson stochastic mechanics

Edward Nelson,
[*Derivation of the Schrödinger Equation from Newtonian
Mechanics*](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.150.1079),
*Physical Review* **150**, 1079--1085 (1966), and
[*Dynamical Theories of Brownian
Motion*](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nelson/books/bmotion.pdf),
Princeton University Press (1967; web second edition 2001).

Nelson explicitly postulates Brownian motion with

$$
\nu=\frac{\hbar}{2m},
$$

continuous trajectories, no friction, and a stochastic mean-Newton law. The
construction leads locally to the Schrödinger equation under its additional
flow assumptions.

The numerical agreement between Nelson's $\nu$ and the G2 heat kernel is exact:

$$
(4\pi\nu\tau)^{-d/2}
e^{-|x-y|^2/(4\nu\tau)}.
$$

But source identity of a coefficient is not identity of theories. Nelson's
time-symmetric real-time construction requires forward and backward drifts,
current and osmotic velocities, mean acceleration, and a physical force law.
G2's Euclidean heat semigroup contains none of those selections.

N1 remains a bounded hostile positive real-time comparator only. Its ontology
and kinematics are not inherited.
