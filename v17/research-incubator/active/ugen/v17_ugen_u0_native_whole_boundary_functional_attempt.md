## 5. Attempt A — an ordinary-positive fine-history Gibbs law

The most direct positive proposal assigns one actual fine history $h$ the

$$
\mathbb P_I(dh\mid B,e)
=\frac{e^{-I_{\mathcal N}(h;B,e)}}{Z_I[B,e]}\,\mu_{B,e}(dh),
\qquad
Z_I[B,e]
=\int_{\Omega_{B,e}}e^{-I_{\mathcal N}(h;B,e)}\,\mu_{B,e}(dh).
\tag{10}
$$

Here $I_{\mathcal N}$ is real-valued or $+\infty$, and $0<Z_I<\infty$. The
record law is the ordinary coarse-graining

$$
\mathcal W_I[B,e](r)
=\mathbb P_I(A_r[B,e]\mid B,e).
\tag{11}
$$

Equations (10)--(11) are normalized, ordinary positive, and can be
all-at-once rather than Markovian. They do not assume an external time or a
trajectory geometry. They are therefore a fair positive baseline.

### 5.1 Theorem NWB-A — alternative-opening odds monotonicity

Consider two programmes $e_0$ and $e_1$. Suppose opening an unresolved
alternative changes only one record event $A_r$ by adjoining a disjoint set
$C$:

$$
A_r[e_1]=A_r[e_0]\sqcup C,
\tag{12}
$$

while the fine-history weights of every old history and every comparison
record $s$ are unchanged:

$$
e^{-I(h;B,e_1)}\mu_{B,e_1}(dh)
=e^{-I(h;B,e_0)}\mu_{B,e_0}(dh)
\quad\text{on all old events.}
\tag{13}
$$

Then, whenever $\mathcal W_I(s)>0$,

$$
\frac{\mathcal W_I(r\mid e_1)}{\mathcal W_I(s\mid e_1)}
\geq
\frac{\mathcal W_I(r\mid e_0)}{\mathcal W_I(s\mid e_0)}.
\tag{14}
$$

**Proof.** Define the unnormalized positive weights

$$
N_q(e)=\int_{A_q[e]}e^{-I(h;B,e)}\,\mu_{B,e}(dh).
\tag{15}
$$

Normalization cancels from the odds:

$$
\frac{\mathcal W_I(r\mid e)}{\mathcal W_I(s\mid e)}
=\frac{N_r(e)}{N_s(e)}.
\tag{16}
$$

Under (12)--(13),

$$
N_r(e_1)=N_r(e_0)+
\int_Ce^{-I(h;B,e_1)}\,\mu_{B,e_1}(dh)
\geq N_r(e_0),
\tag{17}
$$

while $N_s(e_1)=N_s(e_0)$. Equation (14) follows. $\square$

### 5.2 Physical consequence

A quantum interference experiment can be arranged so that opening another
unresolved route lowers the odds of a detector record relative to another
record, including an ideal dark port. Therefore a successful positive
fine-history law must violate at least one premise of NWB-A:

1. opening the route changes the weights of old histories through one
   physically contextual whole-experiment rule;
2. the “old histories” are not the same physical alternatives across the two
   contexts;
3. the record is not an ordinary union of mutually exclusive fine actual
   histories; or
4. relational cross-terms enter before the actual record is sampled.

This theorem does **not** rule out an ordinary-positive contextual
whole-boundary ontology. It rules out the simplest picture in which each
route carries a fixed positive weight and opening another route merely adds
more mutually exclusive possibilities.

### 6.1 Theorem NWB-B — positivity, normalization, and presentation descent
