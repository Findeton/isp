## 2. The theorem that actually survives

For each admissible size, Q-Cut fixes the natural
$\alpha=1/4$ Partial Matching ensemble. Alice receives
$X\in\{0,1\}^n$. Bob later receives a promised reader input $Y$ and must
produce the hidden bit with bounded error.

An admitted positive-cut realizer has:

1. input-independent shared randomness $S$;
2. a standard-Borel cut value $\Lambda$ prepared from $(X,S)$ but not $Y$;
3. a complete, already-defined Borel response family
   $\{\xi_y\}_{y\in\mathcal Y_n}$; and
4. an actual output law uniformly approximated by the screened response.

The information coordinate is

$$
C_{\rm cut}(n)
=
I(X:\Lambda\mid S).
\tag{QPA-2}
$$

Quantizing the complete finite response vector gives a finite message

$$
Q=q(S,\Lambda)
\tag{QPA-3}
$$

whose induced protocol has error at most

$$
\frac1{10}+\frac1{40}+\frac1{40}
=
\frac3{20}.
\tag{QPA-4}
$$

Because preparation does not depend on the later input,

$$
Q\perp Y\mid X,
\qquad
I(X,Y:Q)=I(X:Q).
\tag{QPA-5}
$$

The HJMR compression theorem, with truncation slack $1/20$, converts this
message into a deterministic distributional protocol of error at most
$1/5$. The natural-distribution Partial Matching lower bound at error $1/3$
then implies, for source constants $c_0>0$ and $C_{\rm H}\ge0$,

$$
40 I(X:Q)+20C_{\rm H}
\ge
c_0\sqrt n.
\tag{QPA-6}
$$

Data processing and $S\perp X$ give

$$
I(X:Q)
\le
I(X:S,\Lambda)
=
I(X:\Lambda\mid S).
\tag{QPA-7}
$$

Thus, after a realizer-independent large-$n$ threshold,

$$
I(X:\Lambda\mid S)
\ge
c\sqrt n
\tag{QPA-8}
$$

for some $c>0$.

The matched quantum protocol uses three copies of an $n$-dimensional phase
state, has error $1/16$, and has both transmitted log-dimension and cq mutual
information at most $3\log_2n$. The operational comparison is therefore

$$
I(X:Q_{\rm quantum})=O(\log n)
\quad\text{versus}\quad
I(X:\Lambda\mid S)=\Omega(\sqrt n).
\tag{QPA-9}
$$

The primary-source reconstruction and the internal reduction both survive
this re-audit. That is evidence of author-side mathematical readiness, not an

## 3. What the information coordinate means

The lower-bounded coordinate has four real virtues.

### 3.1 It is representation-resistant

Mutual information is invariant under measurable bijective recodings. Putting
all of $X$ into one exact real does not turn the preparation dependence into
one bit. Idle labels and independent public randomness also do not reduce the
charged correlation.

### 3.2 It is operationally tied to a task

The charge is not a count of imagined microscopic pieces. It is the amount of
preparation information that any positive intermediate variable must retain
if it is sufficient for a fixed family of later readers at fixed accuracy.

### 3.3 It is carrier-neutral within its class

Finite, countable, continuous, and uncountable standard-Borel carriers are
treated uniformly. The theorem does not depend on coordinate dimension,
differential entropy, or ontic cardinality.

### 3.4 It identifies a real explanatory burden

Any theory that says a positive state at the cut contains everything needed
for the later freely chosen reader cannot claim that this state retains only
$o(\sqrt n)$ preparation information on the pinned task.

These virtues justify preserving Q-Cut as a scoped foundations theorem.

## 4. What the coordinate does not mean

The term “explanatory cost” becomes misleading if it is read as a total cost.
Q-Cut does not lower-bound:

1. physical memory cells;
2. energy or thermodynamic work;
3. spatial volume;
4. preparation time;
5. precision or robustness;
6. computational time;
7. source-code or law-description length;
8. number of microscopic configurations;
9. apparatus complexity;
10. the complexity of the response family;
11. the cost of defining a different realizer for every $n$; or
12. a universe-wide nomological description.

Indeed, each admitted $n$ may have a new law-sized response table. The theorem
charges state correlation while leaving the response law free. Consequently,

$$
\boxed{
C_{\rm cut}
\text{ is retained preparation information, not total explanatory cost.}}
\tag{QPA-10}
$$

This does not make the coordinate artificial. It prevents a broader claim
than the mathematics earns.

## 5. The decisive sufficient-cut premise

Q-Cut requires a variable $\Lambda$ such that, approximately,

$$
X
\longrightarrow
\Lambda
\longrightarrow
\hat B
\quad\text{for every later registered }Y,
\tag{QPA-11}
$$

with the preparation of $\Lambda$ independent of that later $Y$.

This is a strong physical assertion. It says there is a boundary at which one
ordinary-positive random object is already sufficient for all registered
future responses. A theory can escape it in physically distinct ways:

1. no sufficient intermediate state exists;
2. the whole experiment is governed by one indivisible boundary law;
3. future controls are genuine parts of the whole-boundary condition;
4. the sufficient object is noncommutative, signed, or amplitude-like;
5. the apparent cut hides an environment or memory not included in
   $\Lambda$;
6. the setting is not independent; or
7. the accuracy advantage vanishes with scale.

Only the first four are central to the present ontology contest. The fifth is
an incompleteness diagnosis. The sixth changes the free-setting experiment.
The seventh leaves the fixed-accuracy theorem.

The theorem therefore has the logical form

$$
\text{positive future-sufficient cut}
\Longrightarrow
\text{large retained preparation information},
\tag{QPA-12}
$$

not

$$
\text{ordinary-positive reality}
\Longrightarrow
\text{large cost or impossibility}.
\tag{QPA-13}
$$
