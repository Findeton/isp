## 0. Question and exact scope

This audit asks whether four familiar identifications are necessities of
physical reality or structures that hold only in stated domains:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{physical subsystem}
&\stackrel{?}{=}
\text{Hilbert-space tensor factor},\\
\text{locality}
&\stackrel{?}{=}
\text{commuting local algebras on a pre-existing spacetime},\\
\text{composition}
&\stackrel{?}{=}
\text{Cartesian or tensor product},\\
\text{background independence}
&\stackrel{?}{=}
\text{absence of one fixed metric}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
$$

It then asks whether the first three are different manifestations of one
deeper assumption:

> Every physical partition admits an independently identifiable boundary
> object that is sufficient to screen and compose the two sides.

The investigation is regional, not temporal. It does not use a spatial cut to
prove the existence of an instantaneous state, a Markov division, or a global
time. It covers the N7--N9 records in the foundational assumption-deletion
programme and only the interface of N18 and N24 needed to keep gravity from
being smuggled into the definitions.

The allowed output is a necessity map, counterexample domain, recovery
contract, resource ledger, or discriminating experiment. It is not a new
ontology. In particular, this audit does not assert that reality is made of
operator algebras, edge modes, boundaries, histories, relations, or dressed
fields.

## 2. Four regimes that must not be conflated

The word *subsystem* refers to different mathematical structures in four
well-tested or theoretically controlled regimes.

### 2.1 Finite-dimensional quantum mechanics

For an already supplied finite-dimensional Hilbert space, a factorization

$$
\mathcal H\cong\mathcal H_A\otimes\mathcal H_B
\tag{6}
$$

gives commuting observable factors

$$
\mathcal A=B(\mathcal H_A)\otimes I_B,
\qquad
\mathcal B=I_A\otimes B(\mathcal H_B).
\tag{7}
$$

Conversely, under finite-dimensional factor conditions, mutually commuting
observable algebras that generate the full matrix algebra induce a tensor
product structure, up to the appropriate unitary equivalence. The conditions
are load bearing: accessibility, mutual commutation, generation, trivial
centre, and the finite/type-I setting.

Zanardi, Lidar, and Lloyd emphasize that different sets of operationally
accessible observables can induce different tensor-product structures on the
same abstract Hilbert space. This does not make subsystem identity arbitrary.
It makes the physical interaction and reader grammar part of what identifies
the subsystem.

### 2.2 Algebraic quantum field theory

AQFT assigns an algebra $\mathcal A(O)$ to a spacetime region $O$. The usual
locality condition is

$$
[\mathcal A(O_1),\mathcal A(O_2)]=0
\quad
\text{when }O_1\text{ and }O_2\text{ are spacelike separated}.
\tag{8}
$$

This is not normally a decomposition of the physical Hilbert space into one
factor per sharply bounded region. Local algebras in relativistic QFT are
generically type III, and entanglement is fundamentally described using
algebras and states rather than reduced density matrices for sharp local
factors.

For nested, suitably separated regions $O_1\Subset O_2$, the split property
may supply a type-I factor $\mathcal N$ with

$$
\mathcal A(O_1)
\subset
\mathcal N
\subset
\mathcal A(O_2).
\tag{9}
$$

The collar between $O_1$ and $O_2$, the state class, and nuclearity or related
phase-space conditions are not decorative. The split factor is an effective
subsystem interface at separation; it is not a proof that a sharp region is a
fundamental tensor factor.

### 2.3 Gauge theory

Gauss constraints correlate a region with its complement. On the
gauge-invariant physical Hilbert space, the naive factorization

$$
\mathcal H_{\rm phys}
\stackrel{?}{=}
\mathcal H_A\otimes\mathcal H_{\bar A}
\tag{10}
$$

can fail. A schematic charge-sector decomposition is instead

$$
\mathcal H_{\rm phys}
\subseteq
\bigoplus_q
\mathcal H_A^{q}\otimes
\mathcal H_{\bar A}^{\bar q},
\tag{11}
$$

where boundary flux or representation data must match. The exact expression
depends on the gauge theory and regulator; (11) is a typing schematic, not a
universal formula.

There are at least two legitimate interface strategies:

1. retain a gauge-invariant regional algebra, possibly with a centre labelled
   by boundary flux or charge; or
2. enlarge each side with edge variables and recover the physical space by
   matching and quotienting at the common boundary.

These are not automatically the same ontology. Casini, Huerta, and Rosabal
show that the algebra assigned to a gauge region and its centre can depend on
the boundary prescription; relative entropy and mutual information can be
more robust than a raw regional entropy. Donnelly and Freidel construct
boundary degrees of freedom that make bounded-region phase spaces
gauge-invariant and composable. The new variables are justified by the gluing
problem and surface symmetries, not by a generic instruction to add hidden
states.

### 2.4 Gravity

Diffeomorphism invariance makes local field labels gauge dependent.
Gauge-invariant excitations carrying energy-momentum require gravitational
dressing. In perturbation theory about flat spacetime, a dressed excitation
has metric dependence extending to arbitrarily long distances, and dressed
observables generically have nonlocal commutators.

Therefore the QFT pattern

$$
\text{region}
\longmapsto
\text{exact commuting local observable algebra}
\tag{12}
$$

does not transfer unchanged to gravity.

This does not imply that information cannot be localized at all. Donnelly and
Giddings construct a perturbative *gravitational splitting* in which exterior
measurements are insensitive, to the stated order, to interior information
once the relevant Poincare charges are fixed. The result suggests
charge-relative and approximate localization rather than exact factorization.
It is perturbative and background-relative; it is not a nonperturbative
subsystem theorem for the universe.

## 3. Necessity record N7 — physical subsystem equals tensor factor

The exact record is

$$
\mathfrak A_{N7}
=(S_7,D_7,T_7,R_7,\Delta_7,K_7,C_7,O_7).
\tag{13}
$$

### $S_7$ — alleged necessity

Every physically meaningful subsystem $A$ corresponds to a tensor factor
$\mathcal H_A$ in a preferred decomposition of the total state space, and
every complement is the remaining factor.

### $D_7$ — strongest theorem domain

The converse from commuting observables to a tensor product is valid in a
finite-dimensional/type-I setting when the candidate subsystem algebras have
the required factor, commutant, and generation properties. Ordinary circuit
quantum mechanics presupposes or physically engineers these conditions to
excellent approximation.

The statement is not a theorem for:

1. type-III local QFT algebras;
2. gauge-invariant spaces constrained by Gauss law;
3. topological or superselection sectors with nontrivial centres;
4. diffeomorphism-invariant gravitational observables; or
5. an unknown pregeometric root carrier.

### $T_7$ — status

**Definition/theorem inside a named finite factor model; effective empirical
regularity for many controlled laboratories; ontology postulate when promoted
universally.**

### $R_7$ — physical referent and intervention grammar

The referent is not a tensor symbol. It is a family of preparations,
independently addressable controls, nondemolition or destructive readers,
shielding operations, composition interfaces, and records whose statistics
support treating $A$ independently of its complement.

A claimed factor must therefore descend to operations that can:

1. vary $A$ while holding the complement's registered preparation fixed;
2. vary the complement while holding $A$ fixed;
3. implement joint interactions with declared ports;
4. form product preparations in a calibrated regime; and
5. test whether the alleged local operation changes remote nonselective
   records.

### $\Delta_7$ — smallest deletion

Replace “subsystem is a tensor factor” by:

> A subsystem is an operationally closed role represented by an accessible
> algebra or instrument family, together with the boundary, centre, charge,
> or gluing data needed to embed it into the parent experiment.

This deletion keeps quantum probabilities, composition, and physical
interfaces. It removes only preferred exact factorization.

### $K_7$ — known-physics recovery obligations

Any more general account must recover, in its claimed low-energy domain:

1. ordinary qubit, mode, atom, detector, and apparatus tensor products;
2. independent product preparations and local channels where observed;
3. entanglement, Bell, steering, and process-tensor predictions on their
   complete intervention grammar;
4. the AQFT split property for separated regions where its hypotheses hold;
5. superselection and charge-sector bookkeeping;
6. gauge-invariant gluing and boundary-flux matching; and
7. approximate gravitational localization in the weak-field regime.

### $C_7$ — charged replacement resources

The replacement must expose:

1. the accessible algebra or instrument family;
2. centre and superselection labels;
3. edge or reference degrees of freedom, if introduced;
4. the collar, energy, localization, and preparation costs of a split;
5. dressing and asymptotic-charge data in gravity;
6. state dependence of the subsystem assignment;
7. nonlocal control or communication used to prepare it; and
8. any target-built encoding of the complete parent process.

### $O_7$ — allowed outcomes and discriminators

1. **Exact type-I factor:** the full finite-factor tests pass.
2. **Split-effective subsystem:** a factor exists only with finite
   separation/collar and resource bounds.
3. **Centre/charge-relative subsystem:** factorization holds only after
   conditioning on or summing over boundary sectors.
4. **Dressing-relative subsystem:** exterior predictions depend only on named
   charges to a controlled perturbative order.
5. **No sufficient proper subsystem:** the complete registered parent cannot
   be screened by any allowed boundary carrier.
6. **Underdetermined:** available operations cannot distinguish multiple
   subsystem assignments.

A discriminator is a *boundary substitution test*: prepare two interior
states that agree on every frozen boundary, centre, and global-charge datum,
then ask whether every compatible exterior operation and reader has the same
distribution. A difference falsifies sufficiency of that boundary type; an
agreement at finite precision supports only the tested scope.

### N7 disposition

There is no domain-independent mathematical equivalence across the accepted
theory domains: tensor factors, type-III algebras, centre-bearing gauge
algebras, and dressed gravity are inequivalent structures. What remains open
is which structure, if any, is fundamental.

## 4. Necessity record N8 — locality equals commuting algebras on fixed spacetime

The exact record is

$$
\mathfrak A_{N8}
=(S_8,D_8,T_8,R_8,\Delta_8,K_8,C_8,O_8).
\tag{14}
$$

### $S_8$ — alleged necessity

Physical locality is exactly the assignment of mutually commuting observable
algebras to spacelike-separated regions of a pre-existing Lorentzian
spacetime.

### $D_8$ — strongest theorem domain

Microcausality is a central and empirically successful condition in AQFT on a
supplied causal spacetime. In locally covariant QFT, the theory is a functor
from a category of globally hyperbolic spacetimes and suitable embeddings to
a category of algebras. The functorial construction improves covariance, but
the spacetime objects, causal type, dimension, differentiable structure, and
metric remain inputs to that framework.

Commutation is neither a complete causal theory nor an ontology theorem. It
does not by itself specify:

1. which interventions are physically available;
2. whether a common cause or source correlation exists;
3. signalling under complete nonselective operations;
4. chronology or a causal arrow;
5. a state, vacuum, or actual history; or
6. the origin of the spacetime regions.

In gravity, gauge-invariant dressed observables obstruct exact localization
in commuting subalgebras already perturbatively.

### $T_8$ — status

**Axiom/structural theorem within fixed-background AQFT; powerful low-energy
empirical regularity; ontology postulate when used before spacetime is
derived.**

### $R_8$ — physical referent and intervention grammar

The physical referent of locality is an operational pattern:

1. interventions in one laboratory do not controllably change the complete
   nonselective records of a suitably separated laboratory;
2. influence propagates within empirically calibrated signal cones;
3. local couplings and detectors have bounded support to a declared
   approximation;
4. source correlations, postselection, and classical communication are
   separately recorded; and
5. relational positions and clocks identify the laboratories.

### $\Delta_8$ — smallest deletion

Retain AQFT microcausality as the fixed-background limit, but replace it at
the root by an operational influence and composition relation that does not
presuppose a metric:

$$
\operatorname{do}(c_A)
\not\leadsto
R_B
\quad\text{for the declared separated interface},
\tag{15}
$$

with all common causes, records, postselection, and reference systems typed.
The geometry of the separation may then be supplied, derived, or only
effective according to the candidate.

### $K_8$ — known-physics recovery obligations

1. Lorentz-covariant microcausality and local covariance in the QFT regime;
2. operational no-signalling with selective steering distinguished;
3. detector and signal propagation on calibrated light cones;
4. the time-slice and causal-factorization results where their hypotheses
   hold;
5. cluster and finite-energy behavior at the relevant approximation;
6. gauge-theory long-range constraints and soft/charge sectors; and
7. weak-field gravitational propagation and causal response without an
   undeclared preferred foliation.

### $C_8$ — charged replacement resources

Any generalized locality must charge:

1. relational rods, clocks, signals, or material landmarks;
2. source and preparation independence;
3. gauge choice and dressing;
4. boundary conditions and asymptotic structure;
5. finite detector support and switching tails;
6. approximation order in Newton's constant or inverse distance;
7. state dependence and code-subspace restrictions; and
8. any metric or causal graph inserted as a hidden input.

### $O_8$ — allowed outcomes and discriminators

1. **Exact AQFT locality on a supplied background.**
2. **Operational locality stronger than algebraic commutation** for the
   complete registered instruments.
3. **Approximate emergent microcausality** from a more general parent.
4. **Charge/dressing-mediated nonlocality with no operational signalling.**
5. **Genuine measurable departure** from QFT locality.
6. **No derived geometry or underdetermination.**

Discriminators must compare complete interventions, not equal-time
commutators alone. The relevant tests are changes in remote nonselective
record laws, distance/collar scaling of residual correlations, dressing- and
charge-sector dependence, and consistency across at least two physical
reference systems.

### N8 disposition

Commuting local algebras on supplied spacetime remain a mandatory QFT limit.
They are not established as the root definition of locality, and gravity
provides a concrete obstruction to their naive exact continuation. This does
not prove that locality is emergent; it identifies the theorem boundary.

### $D_9$ — strongest theorem domain

Cartesian composition is correct for independently specified classical

**Definition and successful composition rule in named classical and

## 6. Necessity record N18 — background independence is merely no fixed metric

The exact interface record is

$$
\mathfrak A_{N18}
=(S_{18},D_{18},T_{18},R_{18},\Delta_{18},K_{18},C_{18},O_{18}).
\tag{19}
$$

### $S_{18}$ — alleged necessity

A theory is background independent exactly when its metric is dynamical or no
single metric is fixed.

### $D_{18}$ — theorem domain

General relativity makes the metric dynamical and is background independent
in a much stronger sense than QFT on one fixed metric. Locally covariant QFT
can compare theories across a category of metrics and uses relative Cauchy
evolution to describe response to metric changes. Nevertheless, the category
still supplies globally hyperbolic manifolds, dimension, differentiability,
orientation/time-orientation, causal type, and admissible embeddings.

Therefore “no fixed metric” does not entail “no inherited geometric
carrier.”

### $T_{18}$ — status

**Programme principle with several inequivalent formal realizations, not one
binary theorem.**

### $R_{18}$ — physical referent and grammar

The referent is whether rods, clocks, causal relations, boundary locations,
topology-sensitive records, and gravitational responses are generated or
supplied. Coordinate invariance alone is not enough; a fixed but covariantly
described structure remains fixed.

### $\Delta_{18}$ — smallest deletion

Replace the binary label by a background ledger. For every carrier field or
structure, record one of:

```text
nomological / contingent / control / calibrated / derived / target-imported
```

The ledger must include at least manifold, topology, dimension,
differentiable structure, signature, causal orientation, boundary type,
asymptotic structure, metric, connection, volume element, and localization
map.

### $K_{18}$ — recovery obligations

1. diffeomorphism-invariant classical GR predictions;
2. locally covariant QFT on controlled backgrounds;
3. equivalence-principle and local Lorentz/position-invariance tests;
4. relational observables tied to physical rods and clocks;
5. weak-field/post-Newtonian/radiative limits; and
6. consistency of regional gluing with constraints and charges.

### $C_{18}$ — charged resources

Every fixed topology, dimension, boundary, asymptotic condition, reference
field, gauge choice, foliation, and code subspace is charged even if the
metric is varied.

### $O_{18}$ — outcomes and discriminators

The useful outputs are degrees of background dependence, not a slogan. A
candidate can be metric-dynamical but manifold-fixed; relational but
topology-fixed; emergent only below a scale; or fully silent because it has
not generated operational geometry. Cross-background transfer without
refitting and agreement between different physical reference systems are
required discriminators.

### N18 disposition

The identification is too weak by definition. Removing a fixed metric is one
advance, not a complete proof of background independence.

## 8. The shared sufficient-boundary hypothesis

The four regimes suggest that the common concept, if there is one, is not a
tensor factor. It is an **operational screening and gluing relation**.

Consider two laboratory regions $R_-$ and $R_+$ joined at a candidate
interface $\Sigma$. Freeze:

1. all independently available preparations and interventions on each side;
2. all complete readers and failure/null records;
3. source and apparatus lineage;
4. gauge/presentation equivalences;
5. boundary and asymptotic conditions; and
6. resource and approximation tolerances.

A candidate boundary object $B_\Sigma$ is **independently physical** only if
its type and values are fixed by at least one of:

1. direct boundary preparation or readout;
2. an independently calibrated constraint or conserved charge;
3. a source law transferred from other experiments;
4. a symmetry with a verified material action; or
5. a gluing theorem whose inputs do not contain the held-out parent answer.

It is not independently physical if it is manufactured by encoding the
complete target process, wavefunction, process tensor, solved geometry, or
future record.

### 8.1 Screening sufficiency

Let $x,x'$ be two admissible interior preparations on $R_-$ with the same
candidate boundary data,

$$
B_\Sigma(x)=B_\Sigma(x').
\tag{22}
$$

The boundary is exterior-screening sufficient for a frozen experiment family
if, for every compatible exterior control $c_+$ and complete exterior reader
$F_+$,

$$
P(F_+\mid \operatorname{do}c_+,x)
=
P(F_+\mid \operatorname{do}c_+,x').
\tag{23}
$$

The equality may be exact, conditional on a centre/charge sector, or valid to
a printed error and perturbative order. Those scopes may not be merged.

### 8.2 Compositional sufficiency

## 10. Gauge and gravity controls against false conclusions

### 10.1 Edge modes do not prove boundary ontology

Adding edge variables can make a regional phase space gauge invariant and
support gluing. It does not prove that the added variables are independent
material constituents. They may encode how descriptions on two sides meet.
Their surface symmetry charges and measurable boundary consequences must
decide their physical status.

### 10.2 A centre is not ignorance about an ordinary factor

A nontrivial centre labels sectors that local gauge-invariant operations
cannot coherently mix under the declared algebra. Treating it as an ordinary
classical random variable can be useful, but the sector structure and allowed
operations must be retained. A direct sum over sectors is not one unnoticed
tensor product.

### 10.3 Dressing nonlocality is not automatic signalling

Long-range gravitational or electromagnetic dressing and nonlocal
commutators do not alone create a controllable superluminal channel. Complete
preparations, constraints, available operations, and readers determine
signalling. N8 must preserve this distinction.

### 10.4 Approximate gravitational splitting is not exact locality

If exterior measurements depend only on total charges to first order, then
interior quantum information is screened at that order. This does not erase
the long-range field, identify an exact tensor factor, or prove the result
nonperturbatively.

### 10.5 Failure of factorization is not unification

Gauge constraints and gravitational dressing show that a naive product can
fail. They do not generate a common matter--geometry law, solve the problem of
time, or select Barandes's ontology.

## 11. Concrete discriminating programme

The following tests turn the audit into physical questions without opening a
candidate theory.

### D1 — observable-induced subsystem swap

On one finite carrier, freeze two independently implementable control/reader
algebras that induce inequivalent tensor decompositions. Test whether one is
selected by interaction stability, autonomous preparation, noise locality,
and held-out composition rather than by a coordinate choice.

**Discriminates:** preferred physical subsystem versus representation-relative
factorization.

### D2 — split-collar scaling

For nested QFT regions, vary the collar while keeping source, detectors, and
energy constraints fixed. Track the resources and error required to realize
independent product preparations or a type-I intermediary.

**Discriminates:** exact sharp factorization versus controlled split-effective
subsystems.

### D3 — gauge boundary-sector substitution

Prepare interior states with the same boundary flux/charge sector but
different gauge-invariant interior information. Compare all exterior
gauge-invariant readers, then open a registered cross-boundary interaction.

**Discriminates:** exterior charge screening versus full compositional
sufficiency. A later difference can coexist with earlier screening.

### D4 — algebra/edge prescription comparison

For a finite lattice-gauge control, compute and measure complete records under
two boundary algebra prescriptions and an extended-edge construction. Raw
regional entropy may differ; mutual information, relative entropy, charge
distributions, and complete gluing predictions should identify the invariant
content.

**Discriminates:** boundary convention from physically transferable data.

### D5 — triple-gluing mutant

Freeze three regions, pair interfaces, orientations, charge sectors, and
readers. Compare both parenthesizations in (18), including null/failure
records and gauge quotienting.

**Discriminates:** strict composition, missing edge/charge data, anomaly, or
higher coherence.

### D6 — gravitational charge-equivalence test

Within a controlled weak-field calculation, choose two interior quantum
states with equal total Poincare charges but different internal information.
Compare the complete exterior gravitational observables to the registered
perturbative order, then vary the dressing prescription without changing the
physical asymptotic charges.

**Discriminates:** gauge presentation, charge-relative localization, and
genuine exterior access to interior information. It is presently a theory
test, not an authorized experiment.

### D7 — reference-system covariance

Define the same operational separation using two physical reference systems.
After including each reference's recoil, noise, and gravity, compare the
complete relational records.

**Discriminates:** physical locality from a hidden coordinate or frame.

### D8 — one-parent matter--geometry boundary test

For the future common MG0 experiment, hold public source/apparatus data fixed
and compare coherent and incoherent matter preparations with the same coarse
mass profile. Require each complete entrant to generate both matter-sensitive
and gravity-sensitive records, boundary charges, and reciprocal response from
one law.

**Discriminates:** expectation-source, actual-configuration source,
branch-conditioned source, quantum mediator, and more primitive joint laws—
but only after the MG0 entrant-completeness gate is independently satisfied.

## 16. Exact findings

### F1 — no universal tensor-factor theorem

No theorem in the audited domains establishes the implication

$$
\text{physical subsystem}
\Longrightarrow
\text{preferred exact tensor factor}
\tag{28}
$$

without domain-specific premises. Exact tensor-factor results survive in their
finite/type-I domain; AQFT, gauge theory, and perturbative gravity require
different or qualified subsystem structures.

### F2 — microcausality is a limit, not a root derivation

AQFT microcausality is a mandatory successful low-energy structure on a
supplied spacetime. It does not derive the spacetime, physical intervention
grammar, or gravitational locality.

### F3 — product and gluing are distinct

Products provide one gluing type. Gauge and gravitational constraints require
boundary/charge/dressing data, and E-Comp warns that local quotient agreement
need not fix an interacting or sequential parent.

### F4 — the common concept is operational screening, not one carrier

The strongest representation-neutral shared question is whether a candidate
interface makes all external complete-record predictions constant on its
interior equivalence classes and supports a target-blind gluing rule. Known
physics realizes different graded versions of this concept, often only within
a fixed state class, charge sector, code subspace, collar, or perturbative
order.

### F5 — background independence requires a ledger

“No fixed metric” does not report the status of manifold, topology, dimension,
causal type, boundary, reference system, or asymptotic structure. A structure-
by-structure origin ledger is required.

### F6 — one-parent matter--geometry remains open

Gravitational dressing and constraints undermine naive exact independence of
matter and geometry. They do not supply the missing common law. MG0's
one-parent requirement remains an admission gate, not a result.

### F7 — the highest-leverage next test

The most informative bounded experiment is not “does a subsystem exist?” It
is:

> After freezing a boundary type and every physically measurable charge,
> centre, dressing, reference, and resource datum, can two different interiors
> be substituted without changing every compatible exterior record, and can
> the same interface correctly predict a held-out reconnection and triple
> gluing?

This simultaneously tests screening, composition, and answer import while
remaining neutral about the boundary's ontology.
