## 0. Binding question

SPB asks:

> Does one independently identified physical interface object screen a
> complete controlled experiment and support held-out composition without
> encoding the target process?

This is not the question whether a complete process can be represented by
some mathematical state. It can always be represented by its whole history,
its full response table, or a predictive quotient constructed from the target
law. SPB asks whether nature supplies a smaller *physical* interface whose
identity and operations are established independently of the held-out answer.

The temporal and regional arms are separately scored. No common boundary type
is assumed.

## 1. No-answer and ontology walls

The contract forbids the following shortcuts.

1. A process matrix, process tensor, channel, scattering matrix, transfer
   matrix, wavefunction, density operator, or boundary algebra is not called
   physical merely because it predicts records.
2. The whole past, complete target table, minimal predictive quotient, or
   target-trained latent state cannot earn independent-descent credit.
3. Failure to reject a difference is not evidence of screening. Screening
   requires a predeclared equivalence test with an upper uncertainty bound.
4. A system causal break does not erase external memory.
5. Active reset is not metaphysical erasure. Information transferred to a
   controller, reservoir, old record, or nearby mode remains in the
   source-closure ledger.
6. Equality of spectra, determinant, trace, or conjugacy class is not equality
   of responses at one labelled physical boundary.
7. A port-basis mixing is not gauge merely because a transplantation matrix
   exists. It must be a declared presentation symmetry or a calibrated
   physical adapter.
8. Pairwise gluing is not associativity. A direct three-module record is
   required.
9. Tomography of the connected target is not prediction by composition.
10. A synthetic fixture can verify analysis conformance but cannot award a
    physical rung.
11. Passing one apparatus does not establish a universal boundary ontology,
    fundamental Markovianity, tensor-factor ontology, or Hilbert ontology.
12. Failing the registered boundary candidates does not prove that no
    boundary exists in nature.

The maximum positive claim is scoped physical sufficiency for the frozen
apparatus, interventions, reader family, power/frequency/time regime, and
uncertainty budget.

## 2. Complete attempted-run object

For either arm, an attempted run is a record

$$
\omega=(j,p,c,b,f,r,n,q),
$$

where:

- `j` identifies the immutable apparatus/configuration instance;
- `p` is the earlier-history or interior preparation;
- `c` is every controller command and random seed;
- `b` is the candidate-boundary intervention and its outcome;
- `f` is the held-out future, exterior, or reconnection policy;
- `r` is the scientific reader transcript;
- `n` is the nuisance/calibration/environment transcript; and
- `q` is the run-quality state, including abort, timeout, invalid, leakage,
  overwritten, stale-calibration, and hardware-failure states.

The sample space `Omega` contains successful and failed attempts. The scored
law is normalized on `Omega`; postselection is a reported conditional, never
the only law.

Every clock time, scheduler decision, reference phase, reset acknowledgement,
temperature/drift monitor, topology change, and data-loss event used in the
analysis must occur in `omega` or be listed as inaccessible source debt.

## 3. Boundary candidate type

A boundary candidate is a tuple

$$
\mathfrak B=(B,\mathsf{Prep}_B,\mathsf{Instr}_B,
\mathsf{Read}_B,\sim_B,\mathsf{Cost}_B).
$$

`B` is the proposed interface object. `Prep_B`, `Instr_B`, and `Read_B` are
physical preparation, intervention, and readout families. $\sim_B$ is the
predeclared presentation/equivalence relation. `Cost_B` records information,
precision, energy, memory, time, and apparatus costs.

The five predicates are:

$$
\begin{aligned}
D(\mathfrak B)&:\quad \text{target-independent physical descent},\\
S(\mathfrak B)&:\quad \text{complete-record screening},\\
C(\mathfrak B)&:\quad \text{target-blind held-out composition},\\
I(\mathfrak B)&:\quad \text{implemented intervention and readout grammar},\\
X(\mathfrak B)&:\quad \text{no-refit held-out transfer}.
\end{aligned}
$$

A sufficient physical boundary at the full SPB scope satisfies all five. The
five coordinates are also reported separately so that, for example, a real
carrier that screens but does not compose is not discarded or overstated.

## 4. Screening is interventional equivalence

Let `h,h'` be two registered histories/interiors that prepare equivalent
boundary instances, $B(h)\sim_B B(h')$. For every held-out policy `f` and
boundary instrument `i`, define the complete future/exterior laws

$$
P_{h,i,f}(d\omega_F),\qquad P_{h',i,f}(d\omega_F).
$$

Screening requires the upper simultaneous confidence bound on a frozen
distance `d` to satisfy

$$
U_{1-\alpha}\!\left[d(P_{h,i,f},P_{h',i,f})\right]
\le \epsilon_{h,h',i,f}
\tag{SPB-S}
$$

for every registered pair and held-out policy. `epsilon` is derived from the
independent boundary-preparation, reader, and drift validation budget and is
frozen before held-out records are opened. It is not enlarged after a failed
comparison.

The primary record distance is total variation on the complete finite
transcript. Proper log-score regret and a likelihood-ratio/e-value diagnostic
are secondary. Continuous waveform records must first use a frozen measurable
binning or density model; the binning cannot be selected from the difference
being tested.

SPB-S is an equivalence claim, not a null-hypothesis nonrejection. Every
excluded or unmeasured output weakens the scope and is printed.

## 5. Composition is a direct held-out comparison

Suppose independently calibrated modules or intervals `A`, `B`, and `C` have
boundary descriptions `B_A`, `B_B`, and `B_C`. A frozen composition rule
predicts

$$
\widehat P_{ABC}
=
\mathsf{Comp}(B_A,B_B,B_C)
$$

without fitting the directly connected run. The direct physical composite
produces `P_ABC`.

Composition passes only if:

1. the prediction is normalized on the complete attempt space;
2. its upper predictive-distance bound is below a predeclared tolerance;
3. both bracketings, where defined, agree within their propagated calibration
   budget;
4. both agree with the directly measured three-part composite; and
5. the same rule transfers to a held-out policy/band without retuning.

An algebraic associativity theorem is a control. It does not replace the
direct physical comparison.

## 6. SPB-T — temporal system--memory arm

### 6.1 Exact physical architecture type

The candidate apparatus is `SPB-T-Q2`:

$$
\mathcal A_T=(S,M,E_\perp,C,R,\mathcal L),
$$

where `S` is one addressable superconducting transmon, `M` one independently
addressable dominant memory transmon, `E_perp` every residual quantum and
classical environment not identified with `M`, `C` the complete controller
and random-seed register, `R` the readout chain, and `L` the leakage/loss
carrier.

The final evaluation pin must identify the physical device, qubit addresses,
calibration epoch, Hamiltonian-control family, firmware, pulse compiler,
readout resonators, reset mechanism, data schema, and raw-data destination.
No moving cloud-device name suffices.

### 6.2 Frozen boundary-candidate lattice

The candidates are nested but not silently promoted:

| code | boundary candidate | interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| `T-B0` | freshly re-prepared `S` only | ordinary system-only Markov control |
| `T-B1` | joint physical carrier `S+M` | primary compact noncommutative candidate |
| `T-B2` | `S+M` plus frozen classical controller/reference register `C_B` | tests controller/reference memory |
| `T-B3` | predeclared finite growing record of depth `k` | bounded history candidate with explicit memory cost |
| `T-ANTI-W` | reconstructed full process matrix | predictive-object anti-control; no descent credit |
| `T-ANTI-H` | complete past transcript or target predictive quotient | target-built anti-control; no descent credit |

The Hilbert/operator representation of `T-B1` is the standard-QM benchmark
description of a physically named carrier. Passing it would establish an
effective physical boundary for this apparatus, not that Hilbert space is
fundamental. Failure would not license adding `E_perp` after seeing the
held-out residual; any enlarged candidate requires a new physical-descent
audit and fresh pin.

### 6.3 Past-policy pairs

The final pin must freeze at least the following types of pair.

1. **Memory-different pair:** two histories followed by the same system causal
   break produce the same fresh `S` preparation but independently verified
   different states of `M`. This should defeat `T-B0` under at least one
   future policy if the apparatus genuinely carries memory.
2. **Boundary-equal/history-different pair:** identity history versus a
   nontrivial calibrated pulse/interaction and inverse, or another pair that
   prepares operationally equivalent `S+M` states while leaving distinct
   physical histories. This tests `T-B1` against heating, controller, leakage,
   and residual-bath memory.
3. **Controller-different pair:** equal `S+M` preparation with different
   recorded controller/reference histories. This distinguishes `T-B1` from
   `T-B2`.
4. **Depth mutant:** histories agreeing on the last `k` registered boundary
   records but differing earlier. This tests `T-B3` without assuming finite
   Markov order.

Equivalence of boundary preparations is certified on calibration/validation
runs before the held-out future labels are opened. The destructive validation
copies are not the scored continuation runs.

### 6.4 System causal break

At the candidate cut the system input is measured or discarded, actively
reset, and prepared in one of a frozen informationally complete set of states.
The output preparation is independent of the input and of the measurement
outcome except through an explicitly recorded randomized assignment.

The complete break transcript contains the pre-reset outcome, reset attempts,
leakage classifier, prepared-state setting, feedback latency, failure state,
and controller record. A projective measurement without independent
repreparation is not a complete causal break.

### 6.5 Memory grammar

Each history pair is crossed, where physically valid, with these independently
calibrated instruments on `M`:

| code | operation | physical purpose |
|---|---|---|
| `M-ID` | no intended change | baseline memory carriage |
| `M-READ` | informationally complete instrument with outcome retained | identifies readable memory and back-action |
| `M-X` | calibrated toggle or orthogonalizing unitary | tests causal sensitivity to memory state |
| `M-RAND-R` | Pauli randomization with seed retained | distinguishes random control from erasure |
| `M-RAND-U` | same randomization with seed inaccessible to the licensed future | operational depolarization test; disposal carrier recorded |
| `M-RESET` | measurement/active reset to a frozen reference state | bounded erasure/reset test, including reservoir/controller cost |
| `M-ISO` | independently validated detuning/echo/isolation during continuation | tests whether future dependence is carried through `M` coupling |
| `M-RESET-ISO` | reset followed by isolation | strongest named-memory removal control |

`M-RAND-U` and `M-RESET` do not mean information disappears from the universe.
They state that the seed or old state is unavailable to the licensed future
apparatus. Any physical carrier to which it is transferred remains in the
resource and source-closure ledger.

### 6.6 Held-out future policies

The final pin must include mutually incompatible continuations, not repeated
readout of one observable. At minimum:

1. free/coupled evolution at preregistered held-out durations;
2. an exchange-sensitive Ramsey or iSWAP-family continuation;
3. a conditional-phase/CZ-family continuation;
4. final informationally complete `S` and sampled `M` readers; and
5. one adaptive policy conditioned on an earlier retained system record.

Every policy produces a complete joint record, including no-click, leakage,
reset failure, invalid classifier, and old-record branches.

### 6.7 Temporal screening questions

The arm answers these in order.

1. Does the causal break make the fresh `S` state sufficient? If no, `T-B0`
   fails and external memory is operationally present.
2. Do `M-X`, `M-RESET`, and `M-ISO` change the future in the direction
   predicted from independently calibrated `M` operations? If no, the named
   memory has not been physically descended as the relevant carrier.
3. After conditioning on equivalent `S+M` preparations, do different histories
   give equivalent complete futures under every held-out policy? If yes,
   `T-B1` screens at scope.
4. If not, does the predeclared controller/reference register close the cut?
5. If not, does a fixed finite record depth close it without growing with the
   target family?
6. If no allowed candidate closes it, is the correct outcome no proper
   boundary or underdetermination given the available controls?

### 6.8 Temporal composition test

Three independently calibrated intervals `A`, `B`, and `C` act on the same
candidate carrier. Training runs determine each interval's registered
instrument map and its uncertainty without using the connected held-out
records. The maps are linked through the candidate boundary and predict the
complete joint record law for the direct `ABC` run.

Both $(A\circ B)\circ C$ and $A\circ(B\circ C)$ are computed from the
same frozen maps. The directly executed `ABC` circuit is the held-out target.
The maps may be represented with standard quantum instruments, but a separate
full process tensor fitted to `ABC` is forbidden as the prediction.

The test is repeated after one registered memory intervention at each seam.
This prevents an accidental good fit under one idle boundary from masquerading
as compositional sufficiency.

## 7. SPB-R — regional microwave arm

### 7.1 Exact physical architecture type

The candidate apparatus is `SPB-R-MW3`:

$$
\mathcal A_R=(I,I',A,B,C,V,Q,G,N),
$$

where `I,I'` are physically different interchangeable interiors; `A,B,C` are
three independently measurable multiport modules; `V` is the physical seam
connector; `Q` is any physical transplantation/basis adapter; `G` is the
coherent microwave source and VNA-grade reader; and `N` is the complete loss,
load, calibration-plane, drift, and assembly nuisance carrier.

The final pin must bind serials, cable/joint topology, optical lengths,
impedances, connector orientations, loads, adapters, calibration standards,
VNA settings, source power, frequency grid, temperature, assembly order, and
raw complex record schema.

### 7.2 Regional boundary candidate

Within a frozen linear, time-stationary, single-mode operating regime, the
primary candidate is

$$
\mathfrak B_R=
\left(S_B(\nu),\mathcal P,\mathcal R,
\mathcal Q_{\rm port},\mathsf{Cost}_R\right),
$$

where `S_B(nu)` is the calibrated full complex multiport relation at a common
reference plane, `P` and `R` are coherent port preparation and reader
families, and `Q_port` is the frozen quotient by port relabellings and reference
phase conventions.

Linearity and time stationarity are empirical scope conditions. They must be
validated across the frozen power, waveform, and time window. If
intermodulation, hysteresis, or memory is detected, the simple frequencywise
`S_B` candidate fails or narrows; a higher-order response object cannot be
added after held-out opening.

### 7.3 Fixed-interface equality

Two interiors screen the same exterior only after their boundary records are
expressed at a literally common physical interface. The primary comparison is

$$
S_I^{\rm common}(\nu)
\stackrel{?}{\simeq}
S_{I'}^{\rm common}(\nu)
$$

on the held-out grid and for coherent multiport superpositions.

For a transplantation relation

$$
S_I=T^{-1}S_{I'}T,
$$

three cases are kept distinct.

1. If `T` is only a coordinate relabelling/reference-phase convention already
   in `Q_port`, it may be quotiented.
2. If `T` mixes physical ports, its realizability in the admitted apparatus
   category must first be proved. Calibrated input/output adapters implementing
   the required transformation must be inserted and their losses, gain, noise,
   phases, and extra ports included. A scalar normalization does not make a
   general invertible transplantation unitary.
3. If no physical adapter exists in the admitted apparatus category,
   conjugacy is an invariant control, not fixed-interface screening.

Trace, determinant, pole set, or eigenvalue agreement alone never passes this
test.

### 7.4 Exterior reader completeness at registered scope

For a validated linear `m`-port device, the full complex response to a basis of
port excitations determines every coherent superposition at that frequency.
SPB-R nevertheless holds out:

1. multiport coherent superpositions with independent relative phases;
2. broadband pulses reconstructed from held-out frequency bins;
3. at least two source powers inside the validated linear range; and
4. time-separated reruns testing drift/stationarity.

This catches channel-phase, calibration-plane, nonlinearity, and memory errors
that can hide in one-port sweeps.

### 7.5 Physical reconnection and star-product prediction

Partition each independently measured module's ports into exterior and seam
sets. For modules `A` and `B`, the connector `V(nu)` imposes frozen linear seam
relations between outgoing amplitudes of one and incoming amplitudes of the
other. The composite prediction `A star_V B` is defined as the unique exterior
input--output relation obtained by solving

$$
b_A=S_A a_A,\qquad b_B=S_B a_B
$$

together with the connector seam equations. Frequencies at which the seam
system is singular or nonunique are registered anomaly outputs, not silently
dropped.

The independently measured module matrices and connector calibration are the
only prediction inputs. The full connected response is held out and measured
directly after physical assembly.

### 7.6 Three-region/two-bracketing test

For independently measured `A,B,C` and connectors `V_AB,V_BC`, compute

$$
(S_A\star_{V_{AB}}S_B)\star_{V_{BC}}S_C
$$

and

$$
S_A\star_{V_{AB}}(S_B\star_{V_{BC}}S_C).
$$

Both must agree with each other where defined and with the same directly
measured three-module physical network. Algebraic associativity is checked as
a conformance theorem; agreement with direct records is the scientific test.

At least one held-out assembly changes an interior while keeping the common
boundary candidate fixed. This couples substitution to gluing rather than
running two unrelated demonstrations.

## 9. Data split and no-refit rule

Because no exact apparatus packet presently exists, numerical shot counts and
frequency grids are not invented here. The final evaluation pin must freeze
them after an openly labelled pilot/calibration stage and before main held-out
acquisition.

The immutable split must contain:

1. **calibration** — determines instruments, boundary preparation error,
   connector response, nuisance bounds, and power analysis;
2. **training** — estimates the permitted segment/interface maps;
3. **validation** — checks normalization, uncertainty coverage, stability,
   and the predeclared equivalence tolerance; and
4. **held-out** — future policies, coherent superpositions, frequency bins,
   interior substitutions, and direct composites unavailable during fitting.

The split assignment is generated and committed before main acquisition. If a
hardware intervention makes the original split invalid, the run is invalid or
the entire changed apparatus receives a fresh pin. The held-out set is never
recycled as training for the same claim.
