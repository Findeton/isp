## 1. Audit method

The audit treats every proposed boundary as a fallible empirical hypothesis,
not as a piece of notation. It applies four attacks.

1. **Delete the boundary premise.** Ask whether the advertised conclusion was
   already placed in a process tensor, scattering matrix, fitted nuisance
   model, or chosen quotient.
2. **Change the referent while preserving predictions.** Ask whether an
   operationally equivalent representation changes the alleged physical
   carrier.
3. **Intervene on the carrier.** Ask whether a real, typed operation changes
   the held-out complete records as the candidate says it should.
4. **Reconnect without refitting.** Ask whether separately descended pieces
   predict a directly assembled whole.

Each issue below receives one of four dispositions:

- `BOUND`: already blocked or typed by the contract;
- `PIN-DUTY`: must be instantiated at exact apparatus scope;
- `SCOPE`: limits the possible award but does not invalidate the experiment;
- `FAIL-CLOSED`: prevents an empirical pin until repaired by physical input.

## 3. SPB-T hidden-assumption and mutant audit

### 3.1 Tensor factorization is not an ontological award

Writing the selected hardware as `S + M + E_perp + C` uses an engineered
control decomposition. It does not assert that nature fundamentally factors
into qubits. The decomposition is admitted only because the proposed device
offers separately calibrated ports and interventions on `S` and `M`.

The empirical question is whether any member of the frozen candidate lattice
screens the registered future. If it does, the award is relative to those
physical controls and records. If residual degrees of freedom remain relevant,
the result is a larger boundary, unresolved memory, or no registered proper
boundary—not a post-hoc enlargement.

**Disposition:** `BOUND`, with device-specific addressability a `PIN-DUTY`.

### 3.2 A system causal break can disturb the memory

A measure-and-prepare operation on `S` can act back on `M` through residual
coupling, measurement photons, drive crosstalk, Stark shifts, controller
latency, or shared resonators. Treating it as `do(S=s)` while silently assuming
`M` unchanged would corrupt the central intervention.

The exact run packet must therefore include:

1. memory tomography immediately before and after every causal-break branch;
2. a matched-duration idle and sham-measurement control;
3. drive, readout-resonator, leakage, and crosstalk calibration;
4. ordering variants when physically feasible; and
5. the measured break-on-memory map in the prediction parent.

The contract's complete attempted-run object can carry these records, but the
apparatus pin must instantiate them.

**Disposition:** `PIN-DUTY`.

### 3.3 Read is not a passive look

Reading `M` may decohere, reset, heat, or condition it. `M-READ` must be typed
as an instrument with both a classical outcome and a post-read state, not as a
noninvasive query. Screening after read and screening without read are
different branches.

**Hostile mutant:** replace `M-READ` by a mathematical marginal that produces
the same logged bit but no physical backaction. The scorer must reject it as a
different experiment.

**Disposition:** `BOUND` in the grammar; implementation is a `PIN-DUTY`.

### 3.4 Reset and erasure move information

Active reset does not annihilate information. It exports entropy and records
to resonators, transmission lines, controller memory, heat, or a larger bath.
A post-reset future can depend on those destinations even when the logical
memory qubit is in its ground state.

The run packet must distinguish:

- logical `M` reset;
- controller-record deletion or retention;
- resonator ring-down;
- bath relaxation and thermal repopulation;
- reset failure and leakage;
- isolation performed before versus after reset; and
- energy, latency, and discarded-record costs.

`RESET-ISO` is the strongest registered branch, not metaphysical erasure.
Persistent dependence after it is a scientific outcome.

**Disposition:** `BOUND`; exact destinations and costs are a `PIN-DUTY`.

### 3.5 Randomization needs retained seeds

Averaging over unlogged random operations can mimic erasure while the seed or
controller remains a perfect memory. The seed is part of the attempted-run
record. At least two analyses are required: seed-retained and operationally
unavailable-to-the-future. The latter requires a physical access argument, not
deleting a column during analysis.

**Hostile mutant:** hide the seed from the analyst but leave it accessible to
the device controller. The branch must not receive erasure credit.

**Disposition:** `BOUND`.

### 3.6 Isolation is a measured operation, not a label

Detuning or echoing `M` does not prove disconnection. Residual `ZZ`, drive
crosstalk, bus modes, readout resonators, leakage levels, and control wiring
remain possible channels. The pin must specify an operational isolation
budget over the relevant frequency and time regime and retain failure to meet
that budget.

**Disposition:** `PIN-DUTY`.

### 3.7 Pairwise boundary matching can be vacuous

Two past policies can be declared to have the same boundary only because the
candidate was reconstructed from the entire target process. The contract
blocks this by freezing a candidate lattice and matching through independently
implemented boundary preparations/readers. The final pin must also require a
nontrivial separation certificate showing that the past records differ while
the boundary-equivalence upper bound closes.

**Hostile mutant:** duplicate the same past program under two labels. It must
fail the separation certificate.

**Disposition:** `BOUND`; quantitative separation is a `PIN-DUTY`.

### 3.8 Segment maps can become response tables

Tomographically complete maps for every program and time can store the target
rather than explain it. The temporal composition test is admitted only if the
segment family, parameter sharing, and resource ledger are frozen before the
three-segment held-out program. Per-program process tensors and full-past
states remain anti-controls.

**Hostile mutant:** allocate a distinct segment map to every held-out control
word. It may predict but must be scored as target-built compression failure.

**Disposition:** `BOUND`.

### 3.9 Finite futures cannot prove universal Markov order

Passing the registered held-outs proves screening only for the frozen process
family, time range, interventions, and uncertainty. It does not prove that the
candidate is a universal state or that memory vanishes at all later times.

**Disposition:** `SCOPE`.

## 4. SPB-R hidden-assumption and mutant audit

### 4.1 A scattering matrix is regime-relative

A frequencywise matrix `S(nu)` is sufficient for coherent exterior fields
only inside a validated linear, time-stationary, single-mode regime. It need
not capture saturation, frequency conversion, hysteresis, time variation,
internal initial conditions, nonlinear mixing, spontaneous noise, or hidden
ports.

The pin must validate power scaling, superposition, stationarity, bandwidth,
mode content, and noise. Detection of higher-order response does not license a
post-hoc larger object; it yields the registered `R7` outcome or a fresh gate.

**Disposition:** `BOUND`; validation thresholds are a `PIN-DUTY`.

### 4.2 Similarity is not fixed-interface equality

The audited four-port transplantation relation uses an invertible `T_4`, but
direct calculation shows `T_4^dagger T_4` is not proportional to the identity.
No scalar normalization turns it into a passive lossless port mixer.

Therefore the published conjugacy is a valuable mathematical control, not an
already executed fixed-port substitution. A future adapter must be shown to
belong to the admitted apparatus category and its extra ports, loss, gain,
noise, reference planes, and calibration must be charged.

**Hostile mutant:** normalize `T_4`, call it unitary, and compare matrices in
different bases. The audit rejects this mutation.

**Disposition:** `BOUND`.

### 4.3 One-port equality is real but composition-poor

The fixed-contact one-port experiment gives a genuine bounded example of
different interiors with the same scalar exterior response. It cannot test
multiport phase relations or a nontrivial seam algebra. It is a positive
substitution control and must not be collaged with another experiment to
claim a passed multiport gluing arm.

**Disposition:** `SCOPE`.

### 4.4 Lossy laboratory modules exceed the unitary graph theorem

The generalized star-product theorem supplies a clean comparator for its
mathematical domain. Real microwave modules and adapters are lossy. The SPB-R
prediction is therefore defined operationally by solving the measured linear
seam equations, not by silently asserting that every measured matrix is a
unitary quantum-graph matrix. The theorem is a conformance control where its
hypotheses hold.

**Disposition:** `BOUND`.

### 4.5 Singular seam frequencies are outcomes

Near resonances, the seam equations can be singular, nonunique, or badly
conditioned. Dropping those bins after seeing the composite would bias the
test. The pin must freeze a conditioning threshold from calibration, flag
registered singular/anomaly outcomes, and propagate uncertainty rather than
replace inverses ad hoc.

**Disposition:** `PIN-DUTY`.

### 4.6 Direct gluing must use one physical composite

Agreement of the two algebraic bracketings is not an empirical test if both
are calculated from the same inputs. They must also predict the same directly
assembled three-module network, with assembly order, connector orientation,
loads, torque, calibration plane, and elapsed time retained.

The two bracketings describe calculations on one network; they are not two
different physical histories and earn no fundamental associativity ontology.

**Disposition:** `BOUND`.

### 4.7 External drives need complete phase control

Measuring one port at a time determines coherent superpositions only under
the validated linear model and a stable shared reference. Independent
relative-phase multiport drives, time-separated repeats, held-out bins, and
broadband reconstructions are needed to attack phase-reference and drift
errors.

**Disposition:** `PIN-DUTY`.

### 4.8 Hidden loads, stubs, and reference planes are boundary candidates

A dangling stub, imperfect termination, cable delay, connector mode, or
calibration-plane shift can make an apparently identical interface fail under
reconnection. Such structure is not dismissed as nuisance merely because it
is inconvenient. It either belongs in the interface, is bounded below the
registered tolerance, or causes screening/composition failure.

**Disposition:** `BOUND`.

  the phenomenon. The margin must be set from downstream predictive and
  apparatus requirements, not observed disagreement.

**Disposition:** contract coherent; exact evaluation statistics are
`FAIL-CLOSED` until pilot and apparatus inputs exist.
