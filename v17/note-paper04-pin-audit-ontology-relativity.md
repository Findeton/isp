## 2. Ontology and relativity reconstruction

### 2.1 External parameter, physical clock, and temporal explanation

The comparator label `s` can play several roles: indexing a state family,
ordering a laboratory schedule, or switching an interaction. It is not an
observable merely because it appears in equations. A clock reading is instead
an outcome of a physical instrument. The distinction is load-bearing because
an autonomously implemented controller may replace an externally timed switch,
whereas merely deleting `s` from a function signature cannot.

Operational elimination is accordingly a dependency claim. After calibration
is frozen, the held-out predictor must obtain complete reader probabilities
from one clock-neutral parent without receiving `s`, a program counter, a path
rank, a hidden winding number, or target labels. This can show that `s` is
redundant for a bounded family of registered predictions. It cannot show that
the universe has no time, that one clock is fundamental, or that the parent is
the unique ontology.

### 2.2 Supplied causal order versus clock records

Paper 03's typed frontier determines which localized operations can compose.
That ordering remains supplied comparator structure. Paper 04 may construct
correlations between physical clock records and positions within a licensed
procedure, but it may not infer the procedure's arrows from increasing clock
values or from categorical source/target notation.

This distinction survives even if two clocks agree perfectly. Clock order can
track a supplied causal order without explaining it. Conversely, causal order
can exist where a chosen clock stops, recurs, or has insufficient support.
Thus a stopped clock establishes the boundary of that temporal frame, not the
end of physical change.

### 2.3 Reading, record, division, and actuality

A reading is an instrument outcome. A retained record is a later accessible
physical carrier of that outcome. A Barandes division event is a system-relative
conditioning permission in an indivisible stochastic law. An actual occurrence
is an ontological fact not selected by any conditional-probability table.

None of these notions implies another. The pin correctly requires all four
record/division combinations to be represented or excluded by theorem:

| Readable record | Complete division | Seat-O interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| yes | yes | a recorded reading may also form a lawful complete cut |
| yes | no | a durable log may omit future-relevant history |
| no | yes | a lawful system-relative cut need not leave a clock record |
| no | no | an ordinary unresolved intermediate process |

Clock conditioning must therefore retain the complete Paper-03 history and
adaptive context. Equal current readings do not justify a Markov checkpoint
when licensed future response still depends on earlier records.

### 2.4 Barandes scope

Barandes's published indivisible-stochastic formulation assumes a fixed
configuration space for the model and laws of the form
`p(i,t | j,t0)` at target and allowed conditioning times. Division events are
allowed conditioning times for the particular system, not universal spacetime
atoms. The target time can be treated as a free variable, including choices
earlier than the conditioning time, so the notation alone does not select a
fundamental arrow.

Consequently, renaming `t` as a clock outcome would not derive relational
time. A Barandes-style Route S must show how target/conditioning indices arise
from, or become operationally redundant relative to, physical clock records.
It must also retain indivisibility away from actual division events. The pin
states exactly these limits.

### 2.5 Same path, different paths, and local calibration

The positive affine relation

$$
\tau_B=a\tau_A+b,\qquad a>0,
$$

is a units-and-origin comparison on one supplied comparator path and one
preregistered good-clock window. It is not a universal relation between all
clocks. Finite resolution, drift, readout disturbance, and interaction enter
through the frozen conditional error model.

Clocks on different paths may accumulate different proper times. Forcing an
affine same-reading fit across those paths would destroy precisely the
relativistic effect a later gravity theory must explain. Paper 04 may use such
proper-time differences as declared comparator controls, but it neither
derives the paths nor the metric that assigns their proper times.

### 2.6 Temporal-frame transformations

A physical clock change acts on the complete operational description: states,
observables, records, readers, support, and correlations. It is not exhausted
by substituting one scalar symbol for another. For noisy or coarse clocks the
change may lose information and need not have an inverse. Equality on one
state or one marginal reader cannot certify frame equivalence.

This is compatible with quantum-reference-frame results, where descriptions
of states, measurements, and dynamics transform together and may exhibit
frame-dependent entanglement or temporal localization. It does not imply that
all clock choices are equivalent. A genuine multiple-choice outcome is a
scientific result, not an implementation failure.

### 2.7 Parametrization versus explanation

Given a complete target evolution, one can often add a clock factor and a
constraint to encode the whole trajectory in a stationary object. Exact
recovery then proves a representation theorem. It does not show that the
factorization was independently selected, that the constraint is a law of
nature, or that an external time was physically gauge.

The pin's strongest distinction is therefore correct:

```text
reverse-engineered parent + exact reduction
    -> formal parametrization representation;

independently frozen parent + held-out complete predictions with no external
parameter dependency
    -> bounded operational redundancy;

either result
    -/-> fundamental timelessness or unique ontology.
```

### 2.8 Finite, periodic, stopped, recurrent, and interacting clocks

A real clock is only good relative to a state class, window, resolution, and
task. Finite clocks recur or lose resolution. A repeated reading cannot label
distinct cycles unless a physical record carries cycle information; that
record enlarges the clock packet and consumes resources. A path index is not
an admissible substitute.

Interaction and readout disturbance are physical, not nuisances to be silently
deleted. At least one positive construction must include a nonzero clock-system
coupling or readout disturbance and track effects in both directions. A
time-nonlocal reduced equation or lossy temporal frame is an allowed outcome.

### 2.9 Relativity, spacetime, and gravity refusals

The supplied comparator already contains Lorentzian signature, worldlines,
localization regions, causal order, and AQFT structure. Paper 04 can test
whether physical clocks recover comparator predictions without consuming a
scalar schedule. It cannot derive any supplied geometric input.

Specifically, success would not establish:

- an orientation or thermodynamic arrow;
- a global time function or preferred slicing;
- dimension, signature, distance, duration from a dynamical metric, or
  spacetime emergence;
- a universal configuration ontology or actual history;
- matter sourcing geometry or geometry changing later matter; or
- Einstein dynamics, curvature, conservation, or quantum gravity.

Gravitationally interacting clock models remain controls on a declared metric.
Their reference-frame-dependent localization cannot be promoted to a derived
matter-geometry law.
