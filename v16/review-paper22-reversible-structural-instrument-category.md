## 3. Independent reconstruction

The mathematically viable core can be reconstructed without relying on the
candidate's narrative as follows.

For an atomic Paper 13D boundary sort `s`, define a homogeneous positive
source groupoid

\[
 \mathsf{Src}^{\ge2}_s.
\]

An object is a finite component family with a marked active subset `A` of at
least two nonempty components, with every active component in a boundary
`B_s(I_alpha)`.  Spectators may be carried as separately typed tensor factors.
A morphism is a component bijection together with the Paper 13D occurrence
bijections and local port flips, transporting the active/spectator mark and
all boundary fields.  It may not turn an active component into a spectator or
change `s`.

On this groupoid the two child assignments are

\[
 T_s(X)=\boxtimes_{\alpha\in A\sqcup E}X_\alpha
\]

and

\[
 F_s(X)=
 \Phi_s^A\!\left(\boxtimes_{\alpha\in A}X_\alpha\right)
 \boxtimes\boxtimes_{\epsilon\in E}X_\epsilon.
\]

The first retains the marked active partition and has no new cross-active
bond.  The second is the one accepted simultaneous `A`-ary fusion, forgets
that accessible partition, and pushes forward every fresh cross-pair outcome.
Paper 13D covariance supplies their action on presentation morphisms.  For at
least two nonempty active components, their complete future contracts differ
even when all realized cross bonds happen to be zero; structural identity is
therefore predictive behavior, not a decoded printed bit.

For each such fixed source, let `P_X` be the cross-active unordered-pair set.
The uniform seed state is the tensor product over `P_X`, and its Householder
preparation is natural because a presentation isomorphism only permutes the
pair coordinates.  The tensor query retains `X` and computes `T_s(X)`.  The
fusion query retains `X` and the complete seed while computing `F_s(X,xi)`.
Those retained values are precisely the reversible complement demanded by
no-hiding.  Controlled inversion returns the query, seed, and complement to
their common blank values while retaining the same route-independent source.

The fused-witness projector is constant on the complete fused query subspace.
Conjugating it by the controlled query gives the relative mode phase on the
blank-query fiber.  Hence the first mixer, query, inverse, and second mixer

After recombination, a source-indexed isometry `W_X` sends each mode to an

\[

\[

## 5. Mandatory fresh attacks and additional semantic controls

### C-F1 — heterogeneous active boundary sorts

**Result: decisive failure.**  The explicit `B_1^0/B_2^0` source in Section 2
is admitted but has no accepted fused child.  This defeats typed totality and
point-free naturality on the advertised source groupoid.

### C-F2 — isomorphic spectator

Take a homogeneous active pair and add a spectator whose complete boundary
value is isomorphic to one active component.  Because the active mark is part
of the source object and is transported, no source automorphism may exchange
the spectator with that active component.  The mode law remains `C_phi`, and
the active conditioned law is unchanged after marginalizing the spectator.

**Result: pass.**  The result relies on rooted/marked, not unrooted, orbits.

### C-F3 — nontrivial automorphism

Take two identical active components with identical internal fields, so the
source has a component-swap automorphism.  The automorphism permutes the
cross-pair coordinates, fixes the uniform seed state and Householder vector,
and transports tensor/fusion witnesses.  The commit uses the mass of the full
history orbit, while `C_phi` is source independent.

**Result: pass on a common sort.**  Neither the route odds nor total mass is
divided or multiplied by the automorphism order.

### C-F4 — restriction to one active component

Start with two nonempty active components and delete all occurrences of one.
The printed positive source category requires at least two components, so the
restriction leaves its domain.  The paper names a degenerate control family
but gives no objects, morphisms, query maps, commit maps, or natural
restriction transformation for it.  It also does not exhibit the admitted
type transport needed to prove that the singleton tensor boundary and the
one-component fusion target are predictively identical.

**Result: fail as a theorem; repairable.**

### C-F5 — restriction to zero active components

Delete every active occurrence.  With no spectators, the tensor result is the
monoidal unit.  Paper 13D's adjudicated zero-component fusion is an arrow from
that unit to the unique empty atomic boundary `B_s(empty)`.  Equality of the
underlying singleton sets does not identify these boundary types in the free
typed execution category, and Paper 22 supplies no invertible alignment of
their complete future tests.  Therefore the assertion that the structural
quotient simply merges `T` and `F` is not proved.

**Result: fail.**  A declared empty-family alignment or a different
degenerate target is required.

### C-F6 — two distinct sources with the same accessible fused child

Choose two different component partitions of the same occurrence union and
seeds that yield the same accessible fused fields and bonds.  The query is
safe: it retains `X` and the seed, so the two inputs have orthogonal
complements and can be uncomputed.  The commit is also safe **as a family
indexed by a classical source**.

If, however, all `W_X` are promoted to one coherent isometry on
`direct-sum_X |X>`, equation (12)'s exhaust label `c_{m,[H]}` is not
source-indexed and the two source basis states can have the same output.
That direct-sum map would not be isometric unless `X` is retained or the
exhaust is refined to `c_{X,m,[H]}`.

**Result: scoped pass.**  The candidate constructs per-source instruments,
not a coherent superposition-changing instrument over source objects.

### C-F7 — two disjoint active marks inside one source

Let one underlying component family contain disjoint subsets `A_1` and
`A_2`.  To state that both instruments commute, a source object must carry
both marks and both outcome/seed/register families.  The printed source type
contains one marked subset `A` and calls its complement spectators.  Thus the
two putative maps are defined relative to two different source objects,
`(X,A_1)` and `(X,A_2)`, rather than as endomorphisms or a commuting square on
one object.

**Result: fail for the internal commuting claim.**  External tensoring of
disjoint source objects remains valid.

### C-F8 — simultaneous versus staged fusion

For three common-sort active components, compare the single `A`-ary commit
with fusion of the first pair followed by fusion with the third.  The staged
process contains an extra traversed boundary and fresh-seed stage, hence is a
different Paper 13D trace.  Paper 22 uses only the first process and never
equates the two.

**Result: pass with permanent scope.**  No fusion associativity or fusion
algebra follows from the instrument.

### C-F9 — several seeds in one physical orbit

Let several labeled cross-pair seeds push forward to the same physical child
orbit.  Replacing the orbit mass by one seed representative would
undernormalize it.  The stated commit instead uses the square root of the sum
`Gamma_F([H]|[X])` and an orthogonal exhaust label per physical orbit, so the
accessible trace recovers the sum after partial trace.

**Result: pass for the abstract commit isometry.**  The optional phrase that
this is realized "directly" from the uniform seed state is stronger than the
given construction; a concrete coherent aggregation would have to retain a
complement on the unused seed subspaces.  No such stronger realization is
needed for the fiberwise instrument law reviewed here.

### C-F10 — local port-frame automorphism

Apply a Paper 13D local `X/Y` port swap on one active occurrence.  It
transports the complete component value and query witness, while the scalar
endpoint color and unordered cross-pair seed law transform according to the
accepted action.  The fused-type projector is defined by target type rather
than a port coordinate.

**Result: pass on the homogeneous source subcategory.**
