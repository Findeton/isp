# Twenty-five routes to the relational selector law

## A result-neutral Paper 17F investigation

### Abstract

Paper 13D supplies one accepted typed indivisible conditional law
\(\Gamma_D\). Papers 17B--17E show exactly what it does not supply: an
autonomous occurrence law \(\Pi_{\rm phys}\) on point-free unmarked process
complexes. Without that law, Paper 17 cannot generate an objective ensemble
from which operational chronology and dimension can be tested.

This paper investigates twenty-five possible selector principles drawn from
the internal constraints of the current theory, stochastic and quantum
physics, statistical mechanics, quantum gravity, cosmology, information
theory, and the alternative of one actual universe without a fundamental
ensemble. The investigation is deliberately output-blind: no chronology,
dimension, manifold, metric, curvature, or gravity result is used to choose a
law.

Five exact anti-circularity results organize the search. On a finite connected
replacement graph, every positive candidate \(\Pi\) can be made:

1. the stationary law of a local reversible Markov kernel;
2. the squared ground-state amplitudes of a positive frustration-free
   replacement Hamiltonian;
3. the diagonal Born weights of infinitely many structural wavefunctions;
4. the unique maximum-relative-entropy distribution for a suitably chosen
   reference measure; and
5. the output of a tagged whole-law selector with exact child recovery.

Therefore stationarity, detailed balance, a unique positive ground state,
Hilbert-space representation, maximum entropy, and selector packaging do not
select \(\Pi\) unless their kernel, operators, amplitudes, reference measure,
or primitive odds are fixed independently.

The strongest classical architecture found is a **point-free groupoid Gibbs
selector**. On every bounded sector it assigns

\[
 \Pi_{\Lambda,\theta}([\chi])
 =Z_{\Lambda,\theta}^{-1}
  \nu_{\mathcal G}([\chi])
  \exp[-S_\theta([\chi])],
\]

where \(\nu_{\mathcal G}\) is an explicitly declared groupoid reference
measure, \(S_\theta\) is a finite point-free local motif action modulo exact
relations and boundary coboundaries, and the same law is specified
operationally by fixed-exterior replacement odds. It is covariant, has no
hidden birth order or dormant carrier, preserves every conditional Paper 13D
law, and exposes rather than hides its physical couplings. Exact deletion can
relate couplings across size and fusion arity, but the Paper 17B
antichain/star family proves that it does not fix even the first fusion
activity.

The strongest genuinely new partial-selection mechanism is a
**Gamma-native branching structural fusion algebra**. If independently
derived point-free structural types obey finite indecomposable fusion rules

\[
 a\otimes b=\bigoplus_c N_{ab}^{c}c,
\]

their Frobenius--Perron dimensions determine normalized resolved-channel odds

\[
 p(c,\alpha\mid a,b)=\frac{d_c}{d_ad_b}.
\]

Associativity then makes final probabilities independent of bracketing. This
derives nonuniform probabilities from discrete compositional support rather
than fitting a continuous activity. Paper 13D's current structural fusion is
deterministic, so it does not yet possess the required branching algebra.
Even a successful fusion bootstrap would still need a root/size law and the
propensity for fusion to occur.

The ambitious quantum alternative is a quantal local specification or
decoherence functional across process complexes. It becomes physically
distinct from the classical selector only if cross-complex interference is
defined and observable. Otherwise it is a diagonal quantum lift that adds
phases without new predictions. Known quantum principles determine the form
of probabilities given a state more readily than they select the state.

No investigated principle uniquely fixes the actual coupling values from the
accepted theory alone. Three honest options remain:

1. adopt the groupoid Gibbs specification as an enlarged objective law and
   treat its surviving couplings as new physical constants, to be reduced by
   consistency or calibrated without seeing dimension outputs;
2. supply a quantal occurrence state and boundary condition as genuinely new
   nomological data; or
3. follow the Barandes-compatible contingent-state route, treat one actual
   process complex as cosmological state data, and give up the claim that
   \(\Gamma_D\) probabilistically selected its dimension.

Before choosing among them, the successor should first test whether
Gamma-stable structural sectors generate a branching fusion algebra. This is
the only route found that could replace some continuous selector parameters
by exact algebraic data.

## 2. What counts as a successful selector

A selector proposal is scientifically live only if it passes all of these
criteria.

### 2.1 Referent and covariance

It acts on physical isomorphism classes of complete unmarked process
complexes, retains generator multiplicity and incidence, and is invariant
under the accepted presentation groupoid. Orbit mass is pushforward mass, not
representative mass.

### 2.2 Exact conditional recovery

For every supported marked experiment,

\[
 \widehat\Gamma(H\mid\chi,a)=\Gamma_D(H\mid\chi,a).
\]

No history-dependent selector term may alter the accepted conditional sector.

### 2.3 No hidden time

The law may use complete structural replacement cells or whole histories. It
may not treat a syntax enumeration, growth order, filling list, or Markov
checkpoint as physical chronology.

### 2.4 No dormant structure

Varying process size or generator occurrence must change the realized carrier.
A fixed maximal product carrier plus enable bits is a failed control.

### 2.5 Locality without assumed geometry

Any locality claim is a finite dependence rule in typed relational
replacement space. It cannot use spatial distance, causal cones, dimension,
or a continuum neighbourhood.

### 2.6 Finite and infinite consistency

Every finite restriction is positive and normalized. Local odds are
cycle-integrable. Deletion and projective restrictions commute with the law.
The unbounded theory has an existence theorem and either uniqueness or an
explicit phase/boundary-condition family.

### 2.7 Output blindness

No parameter, phase, cutoff, locality radius, or boundary state is chosen by
looking at chronology, dimension, manifoldlikeness, signature, metric,
curvature, or resemblance to \(3+1\) spacetime.

### 2.8 Ontological clarity

The proposal states whether \(\Pi\) is:

- objective propensity in an enlarged law;
- a contingent cosmological state;
- an epistemic distribution over one actual complex; or
- merely a computational sampling device.

These interpretations are not interchangeable.

## 3. Five anti-circularity theorems

Let \(V\) be the finite vertex set of one connected bounded physical
replacement graph, and let \(\pi:V\to\mathbb R_{>0}\) be any normalized
full-support distribution.

### Theorem 1 -- every positive law has a local reversible realization

For every connected undirected replacement graph on \(V\), there exists a
local Markov kernel \(K\) with support only on edges and self-loops such that
\(\pi\) is reversible and stationary.

#### Proof

Choose symmetric positive edge conductances \(c_{xy}=c_{yx}\) sufficiently
small that

\[
 \sum_{y\sim x}c_{xy}\pi(y)\leq1
\]

for every \(x\). Define

\[
 K(y\mid x)=c_{xy}\pi(y)\quad(y\sim x),
\]

\[
 K(x\mid x)=1-\sum_{y\sim x}c_{xy}\pi(y).
\]

Then

\[
 \pi(x)K(y\mid x)
 =c_{xy}\pi(x)\pi(y)
 =\pi(y)K(x\mid y).
\]

Detailed balance implies stationarity. \(\square\)

### Corollary 1 -- stationarity is not selection

Finding a reversible structural dynamics with stationary law \(\pi\) is not
an explanation of \(\pi\) unless the conductances and transition rule were
fixed independently. The same statement holds even more trivially for the
global resampling kernel \(K(y\mid x)=\pi(y)\).

### Theorem 2 -- every positive law has a unique frustration-free ground state

Let \(\{|x\rangle:x\in V\}\) be an orthonormal structural basis and define

\[
 |\Psi_\pi\rangle=\sum_{x\in V}\sqrt{\pi(x)}|x\rangle.
\]

For every edge \(x\sim y\), set

\[
 |v_{xy}\rangle
 =\sqrt{\pi(y)}|x\rangle-\sqrt{\pi(x)}|y\rangle,
\qquad
 h_{xy}=|v_{xy}\rangle\langle v_{xy}|,
\]

and

\[
 H_\pi=\sum_{x\sim y}h_{xy}.
\]

Then \(H_\pi\geq0\), it is frustration free,
\(H_\pi|\Psi_\pi\rangle=0\), and graph connectedness makes its kernel
one-dimensional.

#### Proof

Each edge vector is orthogonal to \(|\Psi_\pi\rangle\). Conversely, a vector
\(|w\rangle\) in every edge kernel obeys

\[
 \frac{w_x}{\sqrt{\pi(x)}}
 =\frac{w_y}{\sqrt{\pi(y)}}
\]

on every edge. Connectedness makes the ratio constant on \(V\). \(\square\)

### Corollary 2 -- a unique positive ground state is not selection

Any prechosen positive \(\pi\) can be encoded as the unique ground-state
weights of a local positive Hamiltonian. A Rokhsar--Kivelson or
Perron--Frobenius construction explains the law only when the local operators
are independently physical; otherwise their coefficients contain \(\pi\).

### Theorem 3 -- every classical law has infinitely many quantum lifts

For arbitrary phases \(\phi_x\),

\[
 |\Psi_{\pi,\phi}\rangle
 =\sum_x\sqrt{\pi(x)}e^{i\phi_x}|x\rangle
\]

has structural Born probabilities \(\pi(x)\). A diagonal density operator

\[
 \rho_\pi=\sum_x\pi(x)|x\rangle\langle x|
\]

does also.

### Corollary 3 -- quantization is not state selection

Hilbert space, the Born rule, Gleason-type noncontextual representation,
envariance, dilation, or decoherence can constrain how a supplied state yields
probabilities. They do not choose the amplitudes \(\sqrt\pi\), their phases,
or the density operator. A quantal selector needs independently defined
cross-complex interference experiments and a state or boundary principle.

### Theorem 4 -- every positive law is a maximum-relative-entropy answer

For any full-support reference distribution \(\nu\), maximizing

\[
 -D(q\|\nu)
 =-\sum_xq(x)\log\frac{q(x)}{\nu(x)}
\]

over normalized \(q\) gives \(q=\nu\). More generally, exponential-family
constraints give

\[
 q_\theta(x)\propto\nu(x)e^{-\sum_a\theta_aO_a(x)}.
\]

Choosing \(\nu=\pi\) therefore makes any \(\pi\) the unique unconstrained
maximum-relative-entropy law.

### Corollary 4 -- maximum entropy is not a fundamental selector

Maximum entropy or maximum caliber is an inference rule after a sample space,
reference measure, and constraints are supplied. It may calibrate a selector;
it cannot select those inputs without another physical principle.

### Theorem 5 -- a selector with child recovery is equivalent to its branch law

For child complexes \(x_j\) with normalized accepted laws \(\Gamma_j\), every
tagged whole-law selector with disjoint sectors and exact conditional recovery
has the form

\[
 \Gamma_\Sigma(j,H)=p_j\Gamma_j(H)
\]

for one probability vector \(p\), and every such vector defines a selector.

### Corollary 5 -- correct typing is not numerical selection

A genuine sum carrier solves the dormant-memory and type problems. It does not
determine \(p\). The primitive branch odds remain the new physical input.

## 4. The strongest classical candidate: a groupoid Gibbs selector

### 4.1 Why this architecture is natural

Paper 13D already supplies:

- a finite presentation groupoid on every bounded carrier;
- a free typed physical execution grammar modulo exact relations;
- point-free generator and motif orbits;
- tensor, fusion, deletion, and restriction operations; and
- normalized conditional history laws.

A classical local specification uses precisely these structures and adds only
what is missing: relative occurrence odds.

### 4.2 Finite groupoid reference measure

For one bounded sector \(\Lambda\), let \(\mathscr C_\Lambda\) be the finite
groupoid of presented unmarked process complexes and their presentation
isomorphisms. A natural finite reference is groupoid cardinality,

\[
 \nu_{\mathcal G}([\chi])\propto
 \frac{1}{|\operatorname{Aut}(\chi)|}.
\]

Equivalently, on a fixed labeled carrier, equal reference weight per presented
diagram pushes forward to a class weight proportional to its orbit size;
the common labeling factorial cancels at fixed size and leaves the inverse
automorphism factor.

This factor is invariant under equivalence of finite presentation groupoids
and is the familiar origin of symmetry factors in combinatorial and Feynman
expansions. It is nevertheless a **declared reference measure**. Covariance
alone does not prove that presented diagrams have equal prior weight.

### 4.3 Local motif action

Freeze a point-free structural dependence radius \(k\). Let
\(O_1,\ldots,O_A\) be a complete basis of rooted generator and incidence motif
counts in that radius, reduced modulo:

- accepted diagram relations;
- presentation covariance;
- boundary coboundaries; and
- exact implementation-equivalence equations.

For real couplings \(\theta=(\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_A)\), define

\[
 S_\theta(\chi)=\sum_{a=1}^A\theta_aO_a(\chi).
\]

The bounded selector is

\[
 \boxed{
 \Pi_{\Lambda,\theta}([\chi])
 =\frac{
  |\operatorname{Aut}(\chi)|^{-1}e^{-S_\theta(\chi)}
 }{
  \sum_{[\psi]\in\Lambda}
  |\operatorname{Aut}(\psi)|^{-1}e^{-S_\theta(\psi)}
 }.
 }
\]

Every finite law is positive and normalized.

### 4.4 Operational local odds

For a marked fixed-exterior replacement cell \(C[x]\leftrightarrow C[y]\),
the physical ratio is

\[
 \log r_\theta(C[y]\leftarrow C[x])
 =-\bigl[S_\theta(C[y])-S_\theta(C[x])\bigr]
 +\log\frac{\nu_{\mathcal G}(C[y])}
                 {\nu_{\mathcal G}(C[x])}.
\]

Because this is a potential difference, reciprocity and every cycle equation
hold automatically. Point-free motifs give covariance. A finite structural
dependence radius gives spectator independence for truly disconnected marked
replacements.

Global automorphism factors require care when several identical unmarked
components can be exchanged. The correct local test marks the replacement
site and uses the rooted one-hole stabilizer. An isomorphic disconnected
spectator can enlarge a global automorphism group by a wreath-product
factorial, so naive cancellation of global symmetry factors is false.
Unmarked global multiplicity and marked local spectator independence are
different statements.

### 4.5 Exact Paper 13D recovery

Define

\[
 \widehat\Gamma_{\Lambda,\theta}([\chi],[H]\mid[a])
 =\Pi_{\Lambda,\theta}([\chi]\mid[a])
  \Gamma_D([H]\mid[\chi],[a]),
\]

with typed support restriction for \([a]\). Since the selector factor is
independent of \(H\), every accepted Paper 13D conditional law is recovered
exactly.

### 4.6 What is fixed and what is not

The architecture fixes:

- the physical sample objects;
- automorphism accounting once the reference measure is adopted;
- local-odds composition;
- cycle consistency;
- exact child recovery; and
- a finite coupling census after \(k\) is frozen.

It does not fix:

- the locality radius \(k\);
- the couplings \(\theta_a\);
- disconnected root weights;
- the correct unbounded phase or boundary condition; or
- whether the reference should be groupoid cardinality rather than another
  independently physical measure.

### 4.7 Deletion is a renormalization constraint

Let \(D_n\) be uniform physical occurrence deletion. Projective consistency
requires

\[
 (D_n)_*\Pi_{n,\theta_n}=\Pi_{n-1,\theta_{n-1}}.
\]

Marginalizing a local exponential family can generate every interaction
allowed by symmetry. Therefore a finite generator-only action need not be
closed under deletion. One must either:

- prove closure of a finite family;
- retain the generated motif tower;
- use a latent projective representation; or
- treat deletion as a genuine renormalization flow.

This is a mathematical physics issue, not an implementation issue.

For unnormalized class weights (w_n), exact deletion closure is equivalently
the transfer equation

\[
 \sum_{[\chi']}
 D([\chi']\to[\chi])
 \frac{w_{n+1}([\chi'])}{w_n([\chi])}
 =\frac{Z_{n+1}}{Z_n},
\]

where the right side must be independent of the retained class
\([\chi]\). This is stronger than matching aggregate counts and is the correct
finite equation to solve for candidate activities.

### Theorem 6 -- deletion relates fusion arities but does not fix fusion activity

In the Paper 17B restricted antichain/star family, let \(q_n\) be the
probability of the simultaneous \(n\)-star at fixed \(n\ge2\). For
\(n\ge3\), deletion maps antichain to antichain and star to star. Hence
projective consistency implies

\[
 q_n=q_{n-1},
\]

or equivalently one size-independent odds \(\lambda=q/(1-q)\). At the
one-component endpoint the two alternatives collapse or cease to be a
two-class test, so no equation fixes \(q\). Every \(\lambda>0\) remains
admissible.

The accepted Paper 13D geometric size marginal belongs to its restricted
primitive-family experiment. It cannot be inserted as the size law of this
star/fusion selector sector without a new cross-sector theorem. The result
above is conditional at each size and proves only the projective relation
among shape odds.

### Corollary 6

Deletion can collapse an infinite arity profile to fewer couplings, but it
does not select their values. This is the smallest exact model of what a full
projective selector analysis can realistically accomplish.

### Theorem 7 -- branching fusion support can select channel odds

Let \(\mathcal T\) be a finite set of point-free structural types with unit
\(1\), and suppose independently derived mutually exclusive fusion channels
have multiplicities

\[
 a\otimes b=\bigoplus_cN_{ab}^{c}c,
 \qquad N_{ab}^{c}\in\mathbb N.
\]

Assume these data form a finite indecomposable based ring. Its normalized
positive Frobenius--Perron dimension satisfies

\[
 d_1=1,
 \qquad
 d_ad_b=\sum_cN_{ab}^{c}d_c.
\]

Assign each resolved multiplicity channel
\((c,\alpha)\), \(1\leq\alpha\leq N_{ab}^{c}\), the probability

\[
 \boxed{
 p(c,\alpha\mid a,b)=\frac{d_c}{d_ad_b}.
 }
\]

It is normalized by the dimension equation. For a triple fusion, the total
probability of final type \(d\) through the left bracketing is

\[
 \frac{d_d}{d_ad_bd_c}
 \sum_xN_{ab}^{x}N_{xc}^{d}.
\]

Associativity of the fusion multiplicities makes this equal to the
right-bracketed expression

\[
 \frac{d_d}{d_ad_bd_c}
 \sum_yN_{bc}^{y}N_{ay}^{d}.
\]

Thus the final channel probabilities are independent of serialization. A
categorified associator satisfying the pentagon would strengthen this
marginal statement to coherent path identification.

The Fibonacci support gives the smallest vivid example:

\[
 \tau\otimes\tau=1\oplus\tau,
 \qquad
 d_\tau=\frac{1+\sqrt5}{2},
\]

\[
 p(1\mid\tau,\tau)=d_\tau^{-2},
 \qquad
 p(\tau\mid\tau,\tau)=d_\tau^{-1}.
\]

The nonuniform odds are fixed by integer support and positivity, not by a
fitted real coupling.

This channel law is stochastic, not automatically quantum. Fusion categories
in known quantum physics also carry associators, phases, and interference
data, but the Frobenius--Perron dimension ratios alone are ordinary positive
probabilities. ISP should add complex amplitudes only if a typed
cross-channel recombination experiment demands them.

### Corollary 7 -- why this does not yet solve \(\Pi_{\rm phys}\)

Paper 13D's current structural fusion is deterministic union. Its equation is
only multiplicative and retains positive-character freedom. The theory would
first have to derive presentation-independent stable structural types,
branching multiplicities, associativity, and a pentagon object from accepted
Gamma experiments.

Even then, Frobenius--Perron coherence selects the output channel *conditional
on a fusion*. It does not determine:

- whether fusion occurs rather than no fusion;
- the relative mass of primitive, staged, and simultaneous processes;
- root and disconnected-sector weights;
- the global size law; or
- infinite-volume phase data.

It is therefore the strongest exact partial selector found, not a completed
cosmological measure.

### 4.8 Finite versus unbounded law

The accepted ontology retains complete process traces. If a repeatable stable
operation \(F\) has factorized occurrence activity \(a>0\), the trace words
\(1,F,F^2,\ldots\) contribute the subseries

\[
 \sum_{k\geq0}a^k.
\]

It converges only for \(a<1\). In a free repeatable alphabet with activities
\(a_j\), the corresponding word subseries requires
\(\sum_ja_j<1\). The endpoint identity \(F^2=\mathrm{id}\), when it occurs,
does not remove this divergence: the two-step process trace remains physically
different from the empty trace. Equal stable-word weights are therefore
already nonnormalizable before large-complex entropy is considered.

For unbounded process complexes, define local conditional specifications on
every finite replacement region and seek a DLR-style or projective limit. The
same local rules may yield:

- no global law;
- one global law; or
- multiple phases distinguished by boundary or tail data.

A successful selector must report which case occurs. Selecting one phase by
the desired dimension is forbidden.

## 5. Routes 1--7: constraints internal to the relational theory

### Route 1 -- exact Paper 13D consistency

The accepted category supplies exact equations, not numerical occurrence
weights. Passing to the relation lattice removes activities that are pure
presentation, identity insertion, coherence, or an exact stable-monoid
relation. Boundary coboundaries remove source/target normalization from local
odds.

This is indispensable but insufficient. The parallel contrasts

\[
 U\quad\hbox{versus}\quad D\circ Q^0
\]

and simultaneous versus staged fusion survive. Their conditional kernels are
deliberately physically different, so no accepted equality can remove them.

**Verdict:** necessary reduction; not a selector.

### Route 2 -- complete character-space elimination

A finite exact census can test whether additional coherence, restriction, or
closure equations annihilate every non-boundary character. Paper 17E gives
the correct quotient

\[
 \mathcal X^{\rm gen}=L^\perp/\operatorname{im}d.
\]

Radius-\(k\) motif indicators extend the same calculation. If the quotient
became zero and the replacement graph were connected, multiplicative local
weights would be unique up to normalization.

The explicit Paper 17B \(\lambda\)-family survives covariance, tensor
separation, deletion, and marked recovery. Consequently the inherited
equations do not annihilate the character space.

More sharply, if simultaneous fusion arities \(3,\ldots,N\) are admitted,
the primitive/staged direction and the independent contrasts

\[
 (n-1)e_{\Phi_2}-e_{\Phi_n},
 \qquad 3\leq n\leq N,
\]

give

\[
 \dim\mathcal X^{\rm gen}\geq N-1
\]

before stable-word and contextual-motif activities are counted. Generator
counts are not complete either: a balanced four-input binary fusion tree and
a comb have the same generator multiset but different point-free incidence
motifs.

**Verdict:** exact diagnostic with a negative current result; future anomaly
equations could reduce but have not selected the law.

### Route 3 -- endogenous structural selector

A genuine selector uses a sum carrier

\[
 \bigsqcup_j\{j\}\times\mathsf H(x_j)
\]

rather than dormant copies of every child. This is the cleanest way to place
different process structures inside one enlarged configuration space.

The selector-equivalence theorem then gives

\[
 \Gamma_\Sigma(j,H)=p_j\Gamma_j(H).
\]

Thus it internalizes the odds but does not derive them. A deeper mechanism
could determine \(p\), but that mechanism would itself be the new selector
law and must pass the same test.

**Verdict:** best typing architecture; numerical law still missing.

### Route 4 -- exact physical symmetry

If an automorphism of the complete selector exchanges child sectors while
preserving exterior data and law, invariance makes their probabilities equal.
More generally, symmetry leaves one independent weight per child orbit.

Presentation covariance has already been quotiented. Nonisomorphic physical
complexes such as a tensor antichain, a fusion star, a primitive, and a staged
queried process are in different orbits. No accepted symmetry exchanges them.

**Verdict:** compelling and exact where available; intrinsically partial.

### Route 5 -- point-free local replacement odds

This is the strongest operational formulation. It asks directly for

\[
 r(C[y]\leftarrow C[x])
\]

while holding the complete typed exterior fixed. It does not invent an
intermediate temporal state and can be stated on the physical replacement
graph.

Local odds reconstruct a finite global law exactly when reciprocal and
cycle-integrable. Their physical origin remains to be supplied. The groupoid
Gibbs candidate is one explicit origin.

**Verdict:** preferred language of an actual selector; values remain new.

### Route 6 -- cycle, spectator, and implementation consistency

Zero cycle holonomy makes local odds a global potential. Spectator
independence prevents distant disconnected structure from changing a marked
local ratio. Implementation independence prevents two presentations or
selector mechanisms for the same physical replacement from defining
different odds.

These are homogeneous consistency equations. If one positive solution exists,
multiplication by any surviving compatible character gives another. The
fusion character proves that current equations leave a family.

**Verdict:** powerful falsifier and parameter reducer; not presently unique.

### Route 7 -- occurrence locality

Generator locality is the smallest ansatz. Finite motif locality is the more
general controlled alternative. Unrestricted contextual dependence restores
essentially one potential per bounded physical class.

No metric neighbourhood is available. A structural locality radius must be
defined by typed incidence distance in the complete replacement diagram. The
choice of radius is a physical hypothesis. Exact deletion may generate larger
motifs, so a small radius must prove closure rather than rely on truncation.

**Verdict:** a necessary model-class decision; minimal radius is preferred for
falsifiability but not selected by current law.

## 6. Routes 8--13: dynamics and quantum physics

### Route 8 -- structural transition dynamics

One may add a kernel \(K(y\mid x)\) on process complexes and define
\(\Pi\) as stationary. Theorem 1 proves that every positive \(\Pi\) admits
such a local kernel. Therefore the kernel must be motivated independently.

Moreover, an auxiliary iteration count is not physical chronology. A
structural Monte Carlo kernel can be a sampler for a whole-law measure without
describing how the universe evolves.

There is one sharper subcandidate. If an independently physical primitive
modal-adjacency operator \(A\) exists, Perron--Frobenius theory gives

\[
 Ar=\rho r,
 \qquad
 \ell^{T}A=\rho\ell^{T},
\]

\[
 K_{xy}=\frac{A_{xy}r_y}{\rho r_x},
 \qquad
 \pi_x=\frac{\ell_xr_x}{\ell\cdot r}.
\]

For symmetric \(A\), \(\pi_x\propto r_x^2\). This is the Parry or
maximum-path-entropy measure on the associated edge shift, and it really does
choose one finite law from one fixed operator.

Its blocker is physical rather than algebraic. Present replacement edges were
introduced as comparisons of selector odds, not as modal transitions.
Duplicating an edge, subdividing one physical rewrite into two bookkeeping
steps, changing the primitive move basis, or moving a finite cutoff generally
changes the Perron vector. On an unbounded graph a normalizable positive
Perron vector may not exist. The proposal becomes compelling only if the
theory independently derives a unique groupoid-weighted primitive adjacency
operator and proves refinement invariance and projective consistency.

**Verdict:** useful realization or sampler; circular as a selector unless
\(K\) is new independently tested physics. The Perron--Parry specialization is
a live unique-selector candidate conditional on a canonical physical modal
operator.

### Route 9 -- detailed balance and non-equilibrium stationarity

Detailed balance yields edge ratios

\[
 \frac{\Pi(y)}{\Pi(x)}
 =\frac{K(y\mid x)}{K(x\mid y)}.
\]

It is attractive because it directly supplies local odds. But Theorem 1
shows that any positive law can be represented this way. Paper 13D supplies
neither a structural kernel nor universal reverse structural interventions.

One-way non-equilibrium kernels weaken the constraint further: stationary
currents may circulate and stationarity requires solving the whole kernel.

**Verdict:** conditional candidate after a physical kernel exists; no current
selection.

### Route 10 -- complex occurrence amplitudes

A quantal selector would assign amplitudes to complete process complexes. A
local integrable form is

\[
 \frac{\mathcal A(C[y])}{\mathcal A(C[x])}
 =\exp\!\left[-\frac12\Delta S(C;x,y)+i\alpha(C;x,y)\right].
\]

For a single-valued vertex amplitude, phase sums around every replacement
cycle must be multiples of \(2\pi\). Nontrivial holonomy instead makes
replacement paths physical quantum alternatives and requires a larger
history theory.

The joint amplitude would also need a conditional amplitude realization of
Paper 13D. Barandes's stochastic--quantum correspondence does not provide a
unique one. Per-complex magnitudes and phases remain free under normalized
conditional predictions.

**Verdict:** coherent ambitious option only if cross-complex interference is
made operational; otherwise an unnecessary lift of a classical law.

### Route 11 -- decoherence functional over structures

A decoherence functional

\[
 \mathcal D((\chi,H),(\chi',H'))
\]

could assign quantum measure to structural histories. Probabilities for a
structural partition require suitable decoherence. Stable records may help
produce it.

Closed-system histories theory makes the missing input explicit: decoherence
depends on both dynamics and an initial state or boundary condition. It
explains when alternatives can be assigned probabilities, not why their
diagonal weights have one particular value.

**Verdict:** valid future quantum framework; adds a state and off-diagonal
kernel rather than selecting them.

### Route 12 -- unitarity, positivity, and reflection-type constraints

Normalization, strong positivity, complete positivity under spectator
extension, and composition can rule out candidate amplitudes. Reflection
positivity could support reconstruction only after an independently justified
reflection or time-splitting structure exists.

Such constraints normally define a convex or algebraic admissible set. They
rarely choose one state. Paper 13D has no pregeometric reflection operation
and no structural amplitude anomaly.

**Verdict:** important bootstrap gates; no isolated selector presently.

### Route 13 -- quantum-information principles

Noncontextual probability representation, operational distinguishability,
channel capacity, redundancy, and envariance all begin with supplied states,
channels, or tensor factorizations. Gleason-type theorems characterize
probability assignments by density operators; they do not choose the density
operator. Envariance constrains Born weights relative to supplied Schmidt
coefficients; it does not choose those coefficients.

Weighting complexes by distinguishability, information capacity, stable
record count, or response magnitude would be a new fitness principle and may
bias the desired emergent outputs. Algorithmic probability similarly depends
on a description language and is naturally epistemic rather than an exact
point-free propensity.

**Verdict:** supplies constraints and calibration observables; no fundamental
state selection.

## 7. Routes 14--18: statistical and combinatorial physics

### Route 14 -- Gibbs/DLR local specification

This is the mathematical home of the leading classical candidate. Strictly
positive local conditional laws can be represented by local potentials once
the dependence graph and reference measure are supplied. The potential values
remain couplings, and an infinite specification can have multiple Gibbs
phases.

The replacement-odds formulation is preferable to assuming a temporal Markov
property. Its locality is structural and its complete exterior can retain all
law-relevant context.

**Verdict:** strongest available selector framework; coupling and phase
selection remain physical inputs.

### Route 15 -- entropy versus action

The number of process complexes may grow rapidly with generator count. A
Boltzmann suppression can compete with this combinatorial entropy and create
convergent, critical, or condensed phases.

Neither branch counting nor conditional-history entropy is neutral. Private
refinement can alter counts, and

\[
 \sum_H\Gamma_D(H\mid\chi)=1
\]

makes the ordinary conditional partition sum trivial. Rényi entropy,
collision probability, or predictability would be new selector observables
with an arbitrary sign and scale.

**Verdict:** crucial normalizability and phase analysis after an action is
chosen; entropy does not choose the action.

### Route 16 -- intrinsic renormalization and criticality

Deletion already acts as a physical restriction map. A more general
point-free coarse-graining could define a flow on occurrence couplings.
A unique attractive fixed point with no relevant selector directions would
be a genuine selection mechanism.

At present no unique coarse-graining beyond exact deletion is defined.
Marginalization generates allowed motif interactions, fixed points depend on
theory space and scheme, and relevant directions normally survive. Choosing a
critical point because it later resembles spacetime would violate output
blindness.

**Verdict:** promising long-term reducer of coupling space; not executable as
selection now.

### Route 17 -- exchangeable and projective families

Finite-set covariance removes auxiliary labels, while projectivity supplies
consistent restrictions. Representation theorems then express laws through
latent kernels or random measures. They parameterize rather than select those
kernels.

Dense exchangeable graph laws, sparse graphex laws, and unimodular rooted
limits illustrate that even the meaning of exchangeability and sampling must
be chosen with the desired sparsity regime. The exact Paper 17B \(\lambda\)
family is already exchangeable and projective.

**Verdict:** indispensable consistency architecture; large residual latent
law family.

### Route 18 -- causal-set sequential-growth inspiration

Discrete general covariance and a Bell-type spectator/causality condition in
classical sequential growth lead to a broad family of coupling constants, not
one dynamics. Later work extends the family when zero transition
probabilities are permitted.

The useful lesson is the ratio principle: spectators should not affect a
local physical alternative. The unsuitable import is fundamental sequential
birth. Paper 17 cannot infer time from the order in which a sampler constructs
a complex.

**Verdict:** strong precedent for covariant local-odds families and for
remaining couplings; not a direct ISP law.

## 8. Routes 19--22: quantum gravity, cosmology, and bootstrap

### Route 19 -- sums over combinatorial structures

Spin foams, tensor models, group field theory, dynamical triangulations, and
causal-set path sums all attach weights or amplitudes to combinatorial
histories and then quotient auxiliary labels. They show that a varying-size,
background-independent sum is technically and conceptually possible.

The nearest template for the present problem is group field theory. Its
Feynman expansion produces complexes with local vertex activities, propagator
weights, and diagrammatic symmetry factors. Abstracting away its geometric
group labels suggests

\[
 \Pi_{\theta,b}([\chi])
 \propto
 b([\chi])\prod_a z_a^{N_a(\chi)}
 =b([\chi])\exp\!\left[-\sum_a\theta_aN_a(\chi)\right].
\]

This is an excellent *form* for a selector, but the comparison also exposes
what has not been derived: a field rank, kinetic term, interaction list,
couplings, contour, vacuum, and boundary state. Importing a simplicial rank or
a Lorentz group would preselect dimension or geometry.

There is also a fundamental base-measure fork. If one begins with labeled
diagrams and pushes their measure to isomorphism classes, the usual symmetry
factor motivates

\[
 b_{\rm grp}([\chi])=|\operatorname{Aut}\chi|^{-1}.
\]

If the physical sample space consists primitively of unlabeled orbits, equal
orbit counting instead gives

\[
 b_{\rm orb}([\chi])=1.
\]

Point-freeness alone does not choose between them. The base measure must be
derived from an independently accepted construction or retained as part of
the law. Calling the automorphism factor "covariance" would hide this choice.

**Verdict:** strongest external confirmation of the groupoid Gibbs form;
neither its base measure nor its activities are uniquely selected.

### Route 20 -- coherence, refinement, and anomaly cancellation

This is the most promising route to a deductive selector. Suppose two
physically certified construction diagrams have the same complete external
experiment. If the equality is independent evidence rather than a desired
property imposed after the fact, the selector must assign compatible weights
to the two composites. In a local model those equalities become algebraic
equations among the activities.

The correct programme is:

1. enumerate only the operationally justified 2-cells of the accepted typed
   experiment category;
2. derive their equations on local odds or amplitudes;
3. quotient redundant activities, coboundaries, and normalization gauges;
4. solve the remaining positivity, gluing, and projective-consistency
   equations; and
5. report the exact dimension of the surviving coupling space.

A unique positive solution would constitute genuine selection. A finite
solution set would be a major reduction. A positive-dimensional solution
space would prove that new constants or state data remain.

The current accepted category is deliberately free enough to distinguish
physical staged, simultaneous, and fused processes. A positive character can
therefore assign independent activities to its generators while respecting
composition and tensoring. Ordinary covariance and monoidality alone do not
fix those activities. Declaring all refinements equivalent would be worse: it
could erase the very occurrence and co-onset distinctions Paper 17 needs.

The gravity analogy is anomaly cancellation, not Einstein's equations.
Quantum field theories often restrict matter content or couplings by demanding
that independently required symmetries survive quantization. Here the
analogous constraints must come from native operational equivalences. One
must not manufacture enough 2-cells to force a preferred answer.

**Verdict:** highest-priority derivational research route; the needed native
coherence cells have not yet been established, so no activity is presently
fixed.

### Route 21 -- cosmological state and boundary proposals

Quantum cosmology teaches that a dynamical equation and a state of the
universe are distinct inputs. No-boundary, tunnelling, and related proposals
attempt to specify the second by regularity, a contour, or a boundary
condition. A dimension-neutral ISP analogue could be a distinguished root
functional on closed process complexes.

The direct analogue is not yet executable. Paper 13D has typed finite
processes and conditional histories, but no primitive vacuum-to-complex
creation arrow, cap/cup structure, structural action, or cross-complex
interference functional. A formal closed trace such as

\[
 W(f)=\operatorname{Tr}K_f
\]

can be cyclic and tensor-multiplicative for endomorphisms, yet it requires a
canonical closure and trace measure and applies only to a restricted class of
arrows. Choosing those ingredients is already choosing a state.

No-boundary physics may later motivate a selector once the theory possesses
its own creation and closure operations. Importing a Euclidean metric action
or a preferred simplicial boundary today would circularly assume the geometry
Paper 17 is meant to discover.

**Verdict:** legitimate future state-selection route; presently untyped and
necessarily new nomological data.

### Route 22 -- lessons from general relativity

General covariance by itself does not determine a probability measure over
geometries. Lovelock-type uniqueness, hypersurface-deformation closure,
soft-graviton consistency, and gravitational anomaly cancellation become
powerful only after a manifold dimension, metric field, differentiability,
locality, and often a low-derivative action have been supplied.

They cannot be run backward to select pregeometric process complexes without
importing those assumptions. Paper 16's declared gravitational controls are
therefore targets and falsifiers, not selector priors.

The transferable lesson is narrower and important: independently required
symmetries and composition laws can sharply reduce a candidate action. That
lesson is already captured by Route 20.

**Verdict:** no direct pregeometric selector; supports the native
coherence/anomaly programme and forbids geometry-shaped priors.

## 9. Routes 23--25: data, ontology, and no-ensemble alternatives

### Route 23 -- autonomous calibration from structural records

If Routes 1--22 reduce the selector to a finite identifiable family
\(\{\Pi_\theta\}\), the remaining constants could be calibrated from genuine
structural records. This is not post-hoc selection if the calibration corpus,
observables, likelihood, and held-out validation are frozen before any
chronology or dimension computation.

The data must be physically stronger than one realized complex with no
sampling interpretation. Examples would be repeated independently prepared
structural experiments, factorized cosmological sectors justified by the
law, or stable records whose conditional distribution under
\(\Pi_\theta\times\Gamma_D\) is identifiable. The calibration must use raw
point-free structural facts, not estimators of dimension, manifoldlikeness,
metric fit, or gravitational success.

Current Paper 13D records are conditional records inside a supplied complex.
They do not constitute repeated draws of complexes and therefore cannot yet
estimate selector activities.

**Verdict:** honest way to determine a small residual parameter family, but
the requisite structural sampling records do not yet exist.

### Route 24 -- the Barandes state/law fork

Barandes's ontology separates an indivisible transition law from the actual
configuration and trajectory on which it operates. It does not require the
law to select its own initial or cosmological state.

That gives three precise interpretations:

1. if \(\chi\) is an enlarged configuration, then \(\Pi\) is a contingent
   cosmological state unless a further structural transition law prepares it;
2. if \(\chi\) labels alternative laws, then \(\Pi\) is a hyper-law and lies
   outside the present indivisible process; and
3. if structural creation and ordinary histories are united in one larger
   indivisible law, then \(\Pi\) may be genuinely nomological.

The first option is conservative and scientifically coherent. It means that
Paper 17 can ask what chronology and dimension the actual complex has, but
not what dimension \(\Gamma_D\) statistically predicts. The third option is
the desired long-term theory, but it requires new structural dynamics rather
than a wrapper distribution.

**Verdict:** strongest ontological diagnosis; contingent state is an honest
fallback, while a nomological selector requires an enlarged indivisible law.

### Route 25 -- one actual complex without a fundamental ensemble

One may reject \(\Pi_{\rm phys}\) entirely and treat one point-free complex
\(\chi_*\) as the actual relational history. Intrinsic response, order,
local finiteness, and some descriptive scaling tests can be applied directly
to \(\chi_*\).

This route does not provide typicality, generative probabilities, ensemble
error bars, or a claim that \(\Gamma_D\) selected the observed dimension. A
deterministic law \(\delta_{\chi_*}\) does not solve the problem; it merely
restates the unexplained actual state as a selector.

The valuable output is a class of **selector-robust statements**: properties
that hold for every complex in a support class, or finite-sample bounds that
do not depend on an assumed cosmological measure. Those results remain valid
under future selector choices.

**Verdict:** viable descriptive research programme and logical fallback; not
a probabilistic selector or explanation of dimensional selection.

## 11. The concrete candidate families

### 11.1 Candidate A -- Gamma-native branching fusion law

Derive a finite based fusion ring from point-free Gamma-stable structural
sectors, then apply Theorem 7 to its resolved channels. This is the only
candidate that currently offers exact nonuniform odds without inserting a
real-valued coupling, prior distribution, or desired geometric target.

It is a **partial** selector. It governs which structural output channel
occurs conditional on a certified branching fusion. To become a whole-complex
law it must be combined with independently derived fusion/no-fusion, root,
size, tensor-component, and trace-word propensities. Those remaining pieces
could take the Gibbs form below without altering the algebraically fixed
channel ratios.

### 11.2 Candidate B -- stochastic relational field law

For every finite exterior sector \(\Lambda\), define

\[
 \Pi_{\Lambda,\theta,\eta}([\chi])
 =\frac{1}{Z_{\Lambda,\theta,\eta}}
 b_\eta([\chi])
 \exp\!\left[-\sum_{a=1}^{m}\theta_aO_a([\chi])\right],
\]

where:

- \(O_a\) are a frozen finite basis of point-free bounded-radius motif
  counts, including all interactions generated by exact marginalization;
- \(b_\eta\) explicitly records the base-measure branch rather than hiding
  it;
- exact categorical identities, fusion/deletion restrictions, and boundary
  coboundaries have already been quotiented;
- the same law is reconstructed from complete-exterior replacement odds; and
- infinite-volume states are the DLR measures of this specification.

The preferred parameterization uses positive activities \(z_a=e^{-\theta_a}\)
and a separate size/root fugacity. No sign or magnitude is chosen by a desired
emergent geometry.

This is classical but not Markovian in physical time. It is a probability law
on whole process complexes. It is fully compatible with an indivisible
conditional \(\Gamma_D\).

### 11.3 Candidate C -- quantal relational field law

Replace scalar probabilities by a strongly positive decoherence functional

\[
 \mathcal D_\Lambda(A,B),
 \qquad
 \mathcal D_\Lambda(\Omega,\Omega)=1,

\]

defined on events of complete process complexes and compatible with groupoid
quotient, restriction, tensor composition, and exact conditional recovery.
Diagonal values give a quantum measure, not an ordinary additive selector.

This candidate is warranted only if a physical experiment can exhibit
cross-complex interference. If all accessible structural alternatives
decohere exactly, its diagonal restriction is Candidate B plus surplus phase
data. A complex amplitude is not evidence of quantumness by itself.

Quantum restriction must also be typed before claiming projectivity. Tracing
over deleted structure generally produces a mixed state; coherently summing
over its extension fiber instead produces phase-dependent interference. These
are different physical operations and generate different effective
interactions. A future quantal selector must declare which one the accepted
deletion experiment realizes.

### 11.4 Candidate D -- contingent cosmological state

Specify a normalized \(\Pi_0\) or one actual \(\chi_*\) as state data, not as
a consequence of \(\Gamma_D\). The conditional law remains universal while
the structural state is contingent, analogous to an initial quantum state.

This is ontologically clean and may be the correct answer. Its cost is that
dimensional selection is conditional on \(\Pi_0\) rather than predicted by the
accepted law alone.

### 11.5 Candidate E -- canonical modal-entropy law

Derive one positive groupoid-weighted operator \(A\) from physically primitive
structural alternatives, then use its Perron--Parry measure. Unlike a fitted
Gibbs family this could yield unique finite weights without numerical
activities.

This is the most economical unique-selector option found, but it is also the
most vulnerable to a hidden algorithm. Its admission gates are exact:

1. the modal edges are operationally physical, not attack or rewrite syntax;
2. accepted implementation refinements induce the same physical operator;
3. presentation and automorphism treatment are derived;
4. tensor spectators do not alter unrelated odds;
5. finite Perron laws commute with deletion; and
6. the unbounded operator is positive recurrent with a unique normalizable
   physical state, or its phase data are explicit.

Failure of any gate demotes \(A\) to a sampler and Candidate E to a
representation of an inserted law.

## 12. Ranked scientific programme

The investigation recommends this order.

### Priority 1 -- Gamma-native branching fusion bootstrap

Construct candidate structural types from presentation-independent stable
record sectors. Ask whether complete Gamma-supported fusions yield genuine
integer output multiplicities. Then test unit, involution, finite rank,
indecomposability, associativity, and pentagon coherence. If the gates pass,
compare the accepted conditional channel probabilities with the derived
Frobenius--Perron probabilities.

This route gets first priority because it can create inhomogeneous algebraic
equations and exact probabilities, rather than only homogeneous equalities
among pre-existing activities.

### Priority 2 -- native coherence census

Before inventing numerical activities, freeze a finite mathematical census of
all independently justified operational equivalences, deletion squares,
fusion squares, tensor exchanges, and boundary gluings. Solve the resulting
activity equations. Do not add equivalences merely because they reduce the
coupling count.

### Priority 3 -- freeze the minimal stochastic family

If a continuous family survives, define the smallest point-free groupoid
Gibbs specification containing it. Carry both orbit and groupoid base measures
until an independent descent theorem distinguishes them. Prove bounded
normalization, exact deletion, local-odds integrability, and infinite-volume
existence before computing any emergent observable.

### Priority 4 -- determine whether the residual parameters are constants or state

Classify each surviving parameter by operational counterfactual:

- if changing it changes the universal structural law in every preparation,
  it is a coupling;
- if it varies between independently preparable closed sectors under one
  universal law, it is state data; and
- if no physical preparation distinguishes the alternatives, it is gauge.

### Priority 5 -- autonomous calibration, if available

Only a previously frozen corpus of genuine structural selector records may fit
the residual values. The dimension battery remains cryptographically and
procedurally sealed until fitting and model choice finish.

### Priority 6 -- quantum promotion only on interference evidence

Construct a quantal selector only after defining one typed structural
interference experiment that no positive classical \(\Pi\) can reproduce.
Then freeze the decoherence-functional axioms and boundary state independently
of spacetime outputs.

### Priority 7 -- retain the honest fallbacks

If neither activities nor state can be selected, pursue selector-robust
theorems on one actual complex and report cosmological contingency rather than
forcing a fake derivation.

## 15. Conclusion

The investigation did not discover a miracle principle by which an accepted
conditional law chooses its own cosmological occurrence state. That is not a
failure of the theory. Known physics normally distinguishes law from state,
and covariance from dynamics.

It did identify a compelling next construction. First test whether
Gamma-stable sectors carry a genuine branching fusion algebra whose
Frobenius--Perron dimensions fix local channel odds. Then treat the remaining
whole-complex selector as a **dimension-neutral relational field law on
complete point-free process complexes**, expressed by complete-exterior local
odds and a groupoid Gibbs specification. Its base measure, local motif basis,
couplings, and phase must remain explicit. Native coherence and anomaly
equations should reduce that data; autonomous structural calibration may
determine a finite residue. A quantal promotion is justified only by
structural interference, not by the word "quantum" or by attaching complex
phases.

If this programme still leaves an initial state, the Barandes-compatible
answer is to say so: \(\Gamma_D\) is the indivisible conditional dynamics and
the structural cosmological state is additional contingent data. The theory
can then investigate the actual complex without pretending it was uniquely
selected.

The immediate next scientific object is therefore not a Python sampler and
not a dimension estimator. It is a frozen mathematical **coherence-and-local-
odds selector classification**, reviewed before implementation and kept
strictly blind to all spacetime outcomes.
