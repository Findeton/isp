## 6. Mandatory common hostile attacks

### H1 — representative mass

Replace an orbit pushforward by one representative's mass on a nontrivial
orbit. The reviewer must show the normalization or covariance consequence.

### H2 — automorphism multiplicity collapse

Use a history with repeated atoms and graph automorphisms. Test whether the
paper retains physical occurrence multiplicity while quotienting labels.

### H3 — internal fixed-point orbit

Use an atom fixed by $X\leftrightarrow Y$. Verify that it contributes once,
not twice, while a two-element orbit contributes the sum of both masses.

### H4 — naked symmetric intervention

At a swap-symmetric history, compare `do(X=1)` with its transported
`do(Y=1)`. Determine whether only the complete marked packet or intervention
orbit descends.

### H5 — history-only quotient

Quotient the history but leave the marked slot, reader, or context untransported.
The resulting probability must not be accepted as physical.

### H6 — graph enumeration order

Permute atoms and the ordering of unordered pairs while preserving the
endpoint structure. Every probability and experiment profile must agree.

### H7 — bond endpoint sever

Move one bond without transporting its endpoints. The resulting structure is
not the same physical orbit.

### H8 — per-size table replacement

Replace the endpoint rule at one cardinality while keeping small checked sizes
unchanged. The all-size theorem must fail.

### H9 — uniform-deletion failure

Delete one atom and its incident bonds. Verify the remaining labeled and
physical laws are exactly the lower-cardinality laws.

### H10 — hidden order

Let a bond probability depend on tuple index, insertion order, or a selected
linear extension. This must violate exchangeability/covariance.

### H11 — fixed dormant carrier

Embed all histories in a fixed infinite or maximum-size carrier with active
bits. Determine whether that changes the frozen physical sample space.

### H12 — conditioning as intervention

Use the common-source row to compare $P(Y\mid X)$ with
$P(Y\mid\operatorname{do}X)$. The difference must be exact.

### H13 — incomplete reader false zero

Use the parity reader for $E\to(X',Y')$. Verify that it cancels while the
complete reader detects response.

### H14 — mediator deletion

Test total $X\to Z_Y$ response and the residual after independently holding
$E'$ fixed. The paper must not label total response as direct.

### H15 — context aggregation

Average the $U_X$ response over $c$ before retaining its sign. Verify that a
scalar can erase the exact context reversal.

### H16 — bond response orientation

For both equal-color and unequal-color endpoints, toggle one endpoint color.
The signed change must reverse while its magnitude remains $7/25$.

### H17 — reversible swap miscalled erasure

Transport the record projectors through the reversible record-label swap. It
must remain readable.

### H18 — true eraser admitted

Apply the noninjective reset to both record sectors. It must fail the stable
record theorem and remain outside the licensed grammar.

### H19 — record implies division

Hold $r$ fixed and vary omitted $h$. The future $t$ profile must disprove
future sufficiency of the record-only frontier.

### H20 — division implies record

Use mode $D$ and verify a complete positive frontier exists although no new
record register is written.

### H21 — native cut Markovization

Solve $C=KB$ exactly. Any positive-kernel claim on the declared carrier dies
if $K$ has negative entries.

### H22 — state enlargement after failure

Add a phase, branch label, history identifier, or cache after computing the
negative kernel. It may be an explicit enlarged-carrier control but may not
erase the native result.

### H23 — mode-as-law menu

Check that $U,R,D$ are values of one sampled typed field with one fixed joint
law. Conversely, test whether any result selects a mode probability after
inspection.

### H24 — parameter retuning

Change the rotation seed, fair priors, equal mode weights, bond probabilities,
or geometric cardinality law to improve a later behavior. The reviewed law
must then be recognized as changed.

### H25 — dimension leakage

Search every definition and proof for a dimension, metric, coordinate,
lattice, target scaling law, or desired spacetime outcome used to construct or
select $\Gamma_*$.

### H26 — actuality smuggling

Check that sampling language, a record value, or one illustrated history is
not promoted to an actual history without a selection rule.

## 10. Verdicts

Each report returns exactly one:

- `ACCEPT` — no semantic counterexample and all assigned duties pass;
- `ACCEPT-WITH-FIXES` — the law survives, with bounded proof/prose repairs
  that move no probability, definition, parameter, or outcome; or
- `REJECT` — at least one semantic counterexample defeats a promoted
  coordinate or makes the law undefined/inconsistent.

Each report also prints the full product outcome vector. A failure in one
coordinate does not automatically erase independent coordinates, but a
referent or normalization failure blocks every dependent coordinate.

## 11. Adjudication rule

Adjudication independently reconstructs every decisive finding against the
unchanged paper. It may narrow prose. It may not change a primitive
probability, equation, group action, experiment interface, future grammar,
or parameter.

Any such mathematical change is retuning. The unit stops and asks the user;
there is no automatic Paper 13B-v2 chain.

Only a terminal mathematical `ACCEPT` on unchanged law semantics authorizes a
new Rust implementation pin. Until then, no code path exists.
