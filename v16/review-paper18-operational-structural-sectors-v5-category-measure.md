### M2. Version-4 dependent sum and finite-kernel theorem -- pass

The dependent sum \(\mathcal C=\coprod_x\mathcal C_x\), measurable
projection \(p\), declared total sigma algebra \(\Xi\), trace algebras
\(\Xi_x\), and total measurable target \(\tau\) are coherently typed.  They
do not assume equal fibers, a global numbering, countable generation, or a
standard-Borel structure.

The Definition-10A event condition is equivalent to measurability of

\[
 x\longmapsto\int_{\mathcal C_x} f(\kappa)\,\mu_x(d\kappa)
\]

for every nonnegative total \(\Xi\)-measurable \(f\).  Indicators give the
event condition, finite nonnegative combinations give simple functions, and
monotone convergence gives arbitrary nonnegative functions.  Conversely,
indicators recover every event map.  This proof needs no standard-Borel or
regular-conditional-probability theorem.

### M4. No post-hoc coarsening and refinement -- symmetric and pass

The gauge (V4.7) leaves \(\Xi\) and \(\mathcal O\) fixed and therefore is

### M5. Equal trace algebras do not determine the total bundle -- pass

Let \(X\) be uncountable standard Borel and
\(\mathcal C=X\times\{0,1\}\).  The product-Borel sigma algebra and the
countable-modification algebra

\[
 \Xi_c=\{p^{-1}(B)\mathbin\triangle N:
 B\in\mathcal B(X),\ N\subseteq\mathcal C\text{ countable}\}
\tag{M.1}
\]

both induce the full discrete sigma algebra on every two-point fiber.  Their
total event algebras differ: \(X\times\{0\}\) is product Borel but is not in
\(\Xi_c\).

The difference is not cosmetic.  The product-Borel algebra is countably
generated.  If \(\Xi_c\) had countably many generators
\(p^{-1}(B_n)\mathbin\triangle N_n\), then outside the countable set
\(\bigcup_n p(N_n)\) all generators would treat the two points in each fiber
identically.  Their generated sigma algebra could not contain either point
singleton there, contradicting (M.1).  Thus \(\Xi_c\) is not countably
generated.  Equality of all trace algebras neither identifies the total
sigma algebra nor supplies an accepted bundle isomorphism.

### M6. Product-Borel predeclared-index rejection -- pass

Freeze the product-Borel bundle and physical descriptor

\(1/3\) on a non-Borel set of first coordinates and \(2/3\) off that set,
then

\[
 x\longmapsto\mu_x(\ell^{-1}\{0\}\cap\mathcal C_x)
\]

is nonmeasurable.  The event \(\ell^{-1}\{0\}\) belongs to the frozen
\(\Xi\), so Definition 10A rejects the family.  Deleting it afterward is
exactly the forbidden post-hoc coarsening.

### M7. Countable-modification coarse bundle -- correctly accepted only
without a physical uniform index

For (M.1), every event has the form
\(E=p^{-1}(B)\mathbin\triangle N\).  With the same displayed non-Borel
point weights, the event map \(x\mapsto\mu_x(E_x)\) equals
\(\mathbf1_B(x)\) away from the countable set \(p(N)\).  Its exceptional
values occur only on a countable Borel set, so the event map is measurable.
Thus this is a valid kernel on the frozen coarse referent.

Every point singleton is a total event.  Hence each fiber has two positive
point events and pointwise branching holds.  But
\(X\times\{0\}\notin\Xi_c\), and no physical uniform index descriptor is
in \(\mathcal O\).  The displayed set-theoretic index is therefore not the
value of a physical total event, and the model has no descriptor-coherent
“index 0 versus index 1” branch on a nontrivial region.  Adding that index
after the measure is the forbidden refinement.

This is not a hidden failure of the coarse referent.  It is a different
measurable physical proposal from M6, exactly as the v4 adjudication required.

### M9. Pointwise, descriptor-coherent, and target branching -- pass with the
stated implication scope

All four levels are typed separately:

1. set-level plurality uses only inequivalent physical fiber classes;
2. pointwise branching uses two disjoint positive events of \(\Xi_x\);
3. descriptor-coherent branching uses one frozen physical descriptor and
   fixed disjoint descriptor events over a measurable region; and
4. target branching uses two disjoint positive target events.

For \(E_i=\ell^{-1}(D_i)\), the kernel axiom makes

\[
 g_i(x)=\mu_x(E_i\cap\mathcal C_x)
\]

measurable, so \(\{g_i>0\}\) and their intersection are measurable.  The
positivity-set assertion in (V5.2) is therefore correct.

The exact implication structure is:

\[
 \text{descriptor coherent on }B
 \Longrightarrow \text{pointwise at every }x\in B,
\]

and

\[
 \text{target branching at }x
 \Longrightarrow \text{pointwise branching at }x,
\]

because disjoint target events pull back under \(\tau\) to disjoint resolved
events of the same positive pushforward mass.  Pointwise branching need not
imply either stronger statement, and descriptor-coherent same-target
branching need not imply target branching.  Thus “separate” is correctly
read as non-equivalence, not mutual logical independence.

The product vector's unqualified branching coordinate is expressly the
pointwise predicate at at least one complete input.  No current coordinate is
promoted.

### M10. Physical descriptors under presentation and bundle isomorphism --
pass

become descriptors.  Under an accepted measurable bundle isomorphism over

A bare fiberwise bijection, a representative relabeling, or a sheet swap that
does not transport the physical descriptors and experiments is not an
equivalence of frozen referents.

## 3. Fresh semantic countermodels

### F1. Connected double cover with no physical global sheet species -- pass

Let \(\mathsf S=\mathbb T\), the unit circle with its Borel sigma algebra
and multiplication, and let \(X=\mathbb T^2\).  Define

\[
 \mathcal C=\{(a,b,z)\in\mathbb T^3:z^2=a\},
 \qquad p(a,b,z)=(a,b),\qquad \tau(a,b,z)=ab.
\]

Give \(\mathcal C\) its Borel sigma algebra and each two-point fiber the
uniform measure.  Finite-cover fiber sums of Borel indicators are Borel, so
this is a resolved kernel.  Its target kernel is \(\delta_{ab}\), with unit,
finite words, associativity, and \(d=1\).

Topologically, the pullback of \(z\mapsto z^2\) is a connected two-fold
cover and has no continuous global sheet numbering.  Local sheet descriptors
exist and swap on chart overlaps.  An auxiliary Borel or set-theoretic
numbering may exist, but it is not thereby a transition-compatible physical
descriptor.  The frozen contract may have pointwise branching everywhere
without promoting that auxiliary numbering to a universal channel species.

### F2. Same fiber measures, inequivalent total bundles even with a benign
kernel -- pass

On \(X=[0,1]^2\) and \(\mathcal C=X\times\{0,1\}\), give channel 0 the
Borel weight

\[
 q(a,b)=\frac14+\frac a2
\]

and channel 1 the complementary weight.  This is a valid kernel on both the
product-Borel total bundle and the coarse algebra (M.1); every fiber measure
and fiber sigma algebra is identical in the two models.  Nevertheless the
product contract can freeze the uniform index descriptor, whereas the coarse
contract cannot, and the total sigma algebras are not isomorphic by the
countable-generation test in M5.  The difference between referents therefore
does not depend on manufacturing a nonmeasurability failure.

### F3. Borel-varying one-, two-, and three-channel fibers -- pass

Let \(\mathsf S=\mathbb R_{\geq0}\), \(X=\mathsf S^2\), let \(K\) be the
middle-thirds Cantor set, and put

\[
 n(a,b)=1+\mathbf1_K(a)+\mathbf1_{\{b>a\}}(a,b).
\]

Take

\[
 \mathcal C=\{((a,b),k):0\leq k<n(a,b)\}
\]

with its trace Borel sigma algebra.  On each fiber assign weights
proportional to \(2^{-k}\), and target every channel at \(a+b\).  The
normalizing factor takes only three Borel-selected values, so every total
event map is Borel.  The target kernel is deterministic addition with
\(d=1\).  Pointwise branching occurs exactly where \(n\geq2\); a predeclared
slot descriptor is coherent only on regions where the corresponding slot
exists.  This tests a genuinely varying dependent sum without a global fixed
fiber.

### F4. Descriptor-coherent same-target branching without target branching
-- pass

On a finite discrete base, give every relevant fiber two inequivalent
channels, freeze a physical trace-parity descriptor taking values
\(0,1\), assign both positive mass, and map both to the same target.  The
parity events witness descriptor-coherent and pointwise branching on the
whole declared region, while the target law is deterministic.  This shows
that neither pointwise nor descriptor-coherent branching implies target
branching, without supplying any probabilities from the descriptor itself.

### F5. Target branching forces pointwise branching -- pass

Let one fiber have any finite positive resolved measure and let its target
pushforward give positive mass to disjoint target events \(T_0,T_1\).  The
fiber events

\[
 (\tau^{-1}T_0)_x,\qquad(\tau^{-1}T_1)_x
\]

are disjoint, measurable, and have the same positive masses.  Thus a model
with target branching but no pointwise resolved branching is impossible.
This confirms the implication scope stated at the start of the report and
does not collapse the two predicates into equivalence.

### F6. Nonconstant measurable gauge on a frozen descriptor contract -- pass

On \(\mathsf S=\{\mathbf1,a\}\), let two \((a,a)\)-channels of common
target \(a\) have masses \(1,2\).  Then
\(\mathsf M_{a,a}=3\delta_a\), \(d(a)=3\), and the resolved law is
\((1/3,2/3)\).  Freeze any independently physical descriptor separating the
two classes.  For \(h(\mathbf1)=1,h(a)=5\), (V4.7) changes the masses to
\(5,10\) and the character to \(d^h(a)=15\), but the resolved law remains
\((1/3,2/3)\) and the target law remains deterministic.  The descriptor
contract is unchanged, demonstrating that gauge and referent change are
different operations.

### F7. Non-standard cylinder fiber with coherent finite-coordinate
descriptors -- pass

Let \(I\) be uncountable and, over a nonunit input of a finite sector space,
take \(\mathcal C_x=\{0,1\}^I\) with its cylinder sigma algebra and directly
supplied fair product measure.  Freeze selected coordinate maps as physical
descriptors.  Each coordinate's two cylinder events have probability
\(1/2\), giving descriptor-coherent and pointwise branching, while point
singletons need not be measurable.  A constant target gives no target
branching.  The construction uses direct event probabilities and no regular
conditional probability or disintegration.
