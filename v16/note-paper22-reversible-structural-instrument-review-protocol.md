## 2. Mutual blindness

Each reviewer writes exactly one assigned report and may not read, message,
summarize, or inspect another seat's work before all three reports are frozen.
No reviewer stages or commits files.

## 3. Common questions

Every seat must decide independently:

1. Are the source, query, commit, complement, record, and dependent child
   carriers completely typed?
2. Does the no-hiding argument distinguish reversible query from irreversible
   accessible fusion correctly?
3. Is the two-mode law genuinely attached to operationally different
   structural transformations rather than renamed output bits?
4. Does exact uncomputation remove every route-distinguishing field before
   recombination?
5. Is the complete instrument normalized and does conditioning recover the
   exact Paper 13D children?
6. Are presentation covariance, orbit mass, spectators, restrictions, and
   future composition valid at the claimed scope?
7. Are the positive outputs whole-process instrument components or only
   target configurations?
8. Does the candidate honestly leave opportunity/activity, root, actuality,
   ensemble, chronology, and dimension unconstructed?

The candidate is not rejected merely because it is a newly postulated local
instrument. It is rejected if the new postulate is ill-typed, internally
inconsistent, noncovariant, unnormalized, circularly selected, or fails an
advertised theorem.

## 9. Adjudication rule

Reports are evidence, not terminal acceptance. After all three are frozen, a
separate adjudication must reproduce every decisive counterexample, reconcile
scope disagreements, and bind exact report hashes.

If the candidate is accepted, only its local triggered scope is inherited by
Paper 23. Activity, root, ensemble, chronology, dimension, metric, and
actuality remain closed unless separately constructed.
