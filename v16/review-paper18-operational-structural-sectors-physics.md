## 2. Theorem reconstruction and physical scope

The distinction between a theorem about the general contract and a theorem
about the accepted physical law is essential here.

1. **Theorem 1 is a general existence countermodel.** The inverse system
   \(\{0,1\}^n\) proves only that finite bounded spaces do not entail a
   discrete global completion. It does not show that the accepted Paper 13D
   restriction system exists or has a nonatomic completion.
2. **Theorem 2 is the one accepted-law theorem relevant to this seat.** Paper
   13D fixes a single simultaneous-fusion generator and target boundary sort,
   unions occurrences, forgets the component partition, retains internal
   bonds, and samples fresh cross-component bond values. Its trace semantics
   retain the realized fused target value. Forgetting realized values and
   marks therefore maps all such histories to one unmarked syntax/target;
   retaining the bond values gives the already normalized
   \(\mathbf\Gamma_D\) history partition. This establishes the stated fork.
3. **Theorems 3 and 4 are conditional general-contract theorems.** They say
   what follows from a separately physical finite positive associative
   kernel and character. No such accepted physical kernel has been
   constructed, so these theorems derive no current channel propensity.
4. **Theorem 5 is a general representational gauge theorem.** Its physical
   conclusion is only non-identifiability when all complete experiments
   consume the invariant \(\mathsf P\). It does not prove that a future
   \(\mathsf M\) and \(d\) lack separate observables.
5. **Theorem 6 is a general representation no-go.** Writing any associative
   Markov convolution as \(\mathsf M=\mathsf P,d=1\) proves that a fitted
   positive-convolution representation is not a selection explanation. It
   does not forbid a measure with independent physical provenance.
6. **Theorem 7 is a general identifiability theorem.** Conditioning on the
   fact that composition occurred removes the occurrence propensity \(q\),
   so a unique conditional output kernel cannot determine whether
   composition occurs. It does not construct a physical \(q\) or
   \(\Pi_{\rm phys}\).

Thus the present accepted-law result is narrow: the current simultaneous
fusion realizes the structural/history fork. The finite, countable,
continuous, hybrid, and contextual analyses are contract branches, not

### CM-O1 — stable history with no selector-level structural branch

Let the unmarked channel set be the singleton \(\{\kappa\}\). Its history
fiber is \(H=\{H_0,H_1\}\), with
\(\Gamma(H_1\mid\kappa)=p\), \(0<p<1\). History \(H_i\) writes the record

two histories are physically distinguishable and have indefinitely stable

\(u(H_0)=u(H_1)=\kappa\). There is one unmarked structural channel and no
conditional law over two structural targets.

This is the required stable-history/nonstructural branch. It confirms that
stable occurrence of an emergent value does not alone establish
selector-level branching and supports O3–O5.

### CM-O2 — one continuous law, arbitrarily many base measures

On \(S=[0,1]\), let the physical law be

\[
 \Pi(A)=\int_A 2x\,dx.
\]

For every \(|\theta|<1\), define the probability reference measure

\[
 \nu_\theta(dx)=w_\theta(x)dx,
 \qquad w_\theta(x)=1+\theta(2x-1).
\]

Then the same physical law has density

\[
 \frac{d\Pi}{d\nu_\theta}(x)=\frac{2x}{w_\theta(x)}.
\]

All event probabilities agree for every \(\theta\), while the displayed base
measure and density change. No observation consuming only \(\Pi\) identifies
\(dx\) or any \(\nu_\theta\). This is the required continuous-law/arbitrary-
base-measure countermodel and validates O13 and the provenance firewall.

### CM-O3 — a perfectly normalized selector tuned to dimension four

### CM-O4 — two contingent cosmological states with identical conditional Gamma

Let \(X=\{\chi_A,\chi_B\}\) and retain exactly the same accepted

### CM-O5 — irreducibly contextual composition

Let sectors, outputs, and the complete exterior all be bits and define

\[
 c=a\oplus b\oplus\xi.
\]

For fixed \(a,b\), exteriors \(\xi=0\) and \(\xi=1\) demand opposite

Averaging over \(\xi\) requires an extra distribution on exteriors and hence

### CM-O6 — stable record and complete division vary independently

### CM-O7 — phases without an interference observable
