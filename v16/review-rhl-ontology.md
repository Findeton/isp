### 3.1 The record/law/boundary circle

The construction contains a real mutual dependence:

- a record is durable only relative to the licensed future law;
- the law is indexed by recorded boundary conditions; and
- the actual history is extended only at those stable boundaries.

This is not a contradiction if the candidate is specified jointly as, for
example,

`(Reg, D, division subcategory, record algebras, recoverable continuation class, actualization postulate)`.

The paper effectively makes that joint specification by enriching selected
boundaries and then restricting future morphisms to an append-only class. It
does **not** derive the division catalogue, show that the jointly specified
object is unique, or prove a self-selecting fixed point. The phrase “one common
law” is therefore defensible as an existence construction, but any reading in
which durable records first derive the very law used to define their durability
would be circular. The repair is to publish the joint datum explicitly and
state an existence/extension condition for every actualization step.

### 3.2 One actual history without a global valuation

Let `C_r` be the commutative record algebra at an actually occurring stable
boundary `r`. An actual fact assignment can be represented by a character (or
selected minimal record sector) `v_r` on `C_r`. When `r` is included in a later
record boundary `s`, compatibility requires `v_s` restricted along the record
embedding to equal `v_r`. This is a compatible family only on the actual record
net. It does not define a valuation on the full noncommutative observable
algebra and does not assign outcomes to unperformed incompatible experiments.

Consequently the description neither assumes a Kochen–Specker-forbidden global
value assignment nor a many-worlds branch ontology. It is also weaker than a
maximally fine path: between actual records there is no ontic choice among the
fine fillings used in `D`. What is missing is a proof that the actual record net
always admits another compatible extension. The paper correctly calls this
actualization a postulate; it should also call compatibility/existence part of
that postulate rather than an achieved theorem.

### 3.3 Barandes inspiration and the point/tick audit

The Barandes comparison is accurately narrow. Barandes' primary construction
uses an old-fashioned trajectory in a **fixed configuration space**, with
configuration space explicitly a fixed kinematical/ontological ingredient and
with target and division times. Hilbert-space quantities are secondary and
gauge dependent. See [Barandes, *Quantum Systems as Indivisible Stochastic
Processes*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.21192), especially the abstract and
Sections 2 and 5. RHL inherits the ontology/representation inversion, not the
fixed configuration space, time parameter, or fine trajectory.

I found no hidden ontic point or tick in the RHL definitions. The finite basis
rows, CNOT applications, route labels, and matrix products occur only in the
result-neutral receipts. Categorical composition orders boundaries locally but
does not supply a universal clock or a foliation. One sentence is risky:
“becomes the next actual fact” can sound like a globally enumerated update.
Replace it with “extends the compatible actual record web” and say explicitly
that extension order may be partial. This is a wording repair, not a kill.

## 4. Locality audit

### 4.1 Fixed-factor no-signalling survives

For a fixed tensor factorization `A tensor B`, let `Lambda_A` be any CPTP map.
For every Bob observable `b`,

`Tr[((Lambda_A tensor id_B)(rho_AB))(I_A tensor b)]`

equals

`Tr[rho_AB(Lambda_A^*(I_A) tensor b)] = Tr[rho_AB(I_A tensor b)]`,

because trace preservation is equivalent to
`Lambda_A^*(I_A)=I_A`. This proves invariance of Bob's **unconditioned**
marginal. It does not prohibit conditional steering after Alice's outcome is
revealed, and it assumes the factorization and Bob algebra are already fixed.

I independently rebuilt the Bell-state control in exact arithmetic and added
three Alice channels: dephasing, a bit flip, and a reset channel. Bob's marginal
was exactly `diag(1/2,1/2)` before and after each channel. Thus no mandatory
remote-dependence kill is triggered at fixed factorization.

### 4.2 Proposition 6 is a typing lemma, not a dynamics theorem

The proposition's logic is correct. If Bob's old observable is `b in B` and the
post-occurrence state is a functional `omega'` on a new algebra `A'`, then the
quantity called Bob's later expectation is undefined until a physical map
`j_B:B -> A'` is supplied. The exact state `diag(3/4,1/4)` gives `3/4` under the
identity calibration and `1/4` under the swapped calibration.

What the receipt does **not** prove is that both maps are physically admissible.
Bare equal dimension permits them; a calibrated apparatus, a locality functor,
or covariance requirement may select one. Nor is ambiguity unavoidable: for a
size-changing boundary one may declare `A'=B tensor C`, append a fixed ancilla,
and use `j_B(b)=b tensor I_C`. That is a valid transport and defines Bob. It is
simply new physical law data.

Accordingly Proposition 6 is best classified as:

- a theorem about the typing of expectations;
- an exact underdetermination witness at bare boundary typing; and
- an avoidable ambiguity once an independently calibrated algebra transport or
  an equivalence theorem is supplied.

It is not a no-signalling theorem, a signalling result, or a no-go theorem for
dynamic subsystems.

### 4.3 Dynamic Alice/Bob attempt

I tried the minimal construction the paper would need. Assign each boundary
`Sigma` an operational algebra `A(Sigma)`. Assign a candidate Bob subalgebra
`B(Sigma) subseteq A(Sigma)`. For every physical occurrence
`M:Sigma -> Sigma'`, require a law-generated Heisenberg transport
`j_B^M:B(Sigma)->A(Sigma')`, natural under gluing and calibration, and require
pre-contact Alice interventions to leave every transported Bob expectation
invariant.

RHL supplies boundary types and functionals but no `B(Sigma)`, no calibrated
separation relation, no natural `j_B^M`, and no equivalence class under which
different embeddings are observationally identical. The construction can be
added by hand, as the ancilla example shows, but it cannot be recovered from
the candidate. Therefore the correct disposition is exactly
`RHL-BLOCKED-AT-DYNAMIC-LOCALITY`. Absence of a typed Bob is not a safety
theorem.

## 6. Hamiltonian audit

The conceptual disposition survives, with one technical narrowing.

1. Once a **selected** strongly continuous one-parameter unitary group
   `U(t)` on a Hilbert sector is fixed, Stone's theorem gives a unique
   self-adjoint generator (possibly unbounded, with its domain fixed by the
   group). Selection of the sector, clock, and representation remains physical
   model data; the generator is not nonunique after that group is fixed.
2. A bare completely positive semigroup is not enough for the paper's
   “Lindbladian” sentence. The standard GKSL result requires a quantum
   dynamical semigroup with trace preservation and appropriate continuity;
   the familiar bounded GKSL form is immediate in finite dimension or under
   uniform/norm continuity. General strongly continuous infinite-dimensional
   semigroups have unbounded-generator and domain qualifications. See the
   primary results [Gorini–Kossakowski–Sudarshan,
   1976](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.522979) and [Lindblad,
   1976](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01608499).
3. If the unsliced indivisible law has no selected one-parameter factorization,
   neither a Hamiltonian nor a Lindblad generator is fundamental. This is
   correct.

Barandes supplies an additional compatible reason for representational
caution: under his time-dependent Hilbert-space gauge transformations the
Hamiltonian transforms as a gauge potential and is not itself a gauge-invariant
observable. RHL does not inherit Barandes' external time, so this is precedent,
not an RHL derivation.

The sentence that the Hamiltonian “regains its classical philosophical role”
is an interpretation, not a consequence of Stone's theorem. It is acceptable
if labeled as such. RHL constructs no clock sector, Hamiltonian, energy law, or
Hamiltonian reconstruction in this paper.
