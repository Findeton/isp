## 3. Common check 4 -- Proposition V6.1

### Descriptor-coherent branching implies pointwise branching

Let descriptor-coherent branching hold on \(B\ni x\). For the frozen
descriptor \(\ell\) and disjoint descriptor events \(D_0,D_1\), the total
events \(\ell^{-1}D_i\) are disjoint and measurable. Their fiber sections
belong to \(\Xi_x\), and (V5.2) gives each positive \(\mu_x\)-mass. They are
therefore witnesses to (V5.1).

### Target branching implies pointwise branching

Let disjoint target events \(D_0,D_1\) have positive target probability.
Their total preimages under measurable \(\tau\) are disjoint events of
\(\Xi\), and the exact pushforward identity gives positive resolved
probability to both fiber sections. The resolved probability density with
respect to \(\mu_x\) is

\[
 \frac{d(\tau\kappa)}{d(a)d(b)},
\]

which is finite and strictly positive everywhere. Consequently a resolved
event has positive normalized probability exactly when it has positive
\(\mu_x\)-mass. The two preimages therefore satisfy (V5.1). If target
branching is stated directly with the unnormalized pushforward
\(\mathsf M_x\), the same conclusion follows immediately from (V4.2).

### Both converses fail

- The finite two-channel same-target control has pointwise resolved
  branching and a deterministic target law. Thus pointwise branching does
  not imply target branching.
- Instantiate the countable-modification coarse bundle with
  \(\mathcal O\) containing no channel-separating descriptor. Every fiber
  has two positive measurable points, but no descriptor in the frozen
  contract tracks them across a region. Thus pointwise branching does not
  imply descriptor-coherent branching.

These are failures of converse implications only. They supply neither
measure provenance, occurrence propensity, nor actualization.

## 4. Common check 5 -- observable freeze and sigma attacks

Let \(X\) be an uncountable standard Borel input space and
\(\mathcal C=X\times\{0,1\}\), with both channel points over \(x\) having
the same target. For a non-Borel \(V\subset X\), take

\[
 w(x)=\begin{cases}1/3,&x\in V,\\2/3,&x\notin V,\end{cases}
 \qquad
 \mu_x=w(x)\delta_{(x,0)}+(1-w(x))\delta_{(x,1)}.
\]

On the product-Borel bundle with a predeclared physical sheet descriptor,
the event \(X\times\{0\}\) has nonmeasurable probability \(w(x)\). The
family is not a kernel. Deleting that event after observing the failure is
forbidden post-hoc coarsening.

The legitimate coarse proposal can be realized exactly as

\[
 \Xi_c=\left\{
 (A_0\times\{0\})\cup(A_1\times\{1\}):
 A_i\in\mathcal B(X),\quad A_0\mathbin\triangle A_1
 \text{ is countable}
 \right\}.
\tag{P.1}
\]

It is a sigma algebra, contains all saturated base cylinders, induces the
full algebra on each two-point fiber, admits point-local events, and omits
the uniform sheet-0 event. For an event in (P.1), its probability is

\[
 w(x)\mathbf1_{A_0}(x)+(1-w(x))\mathbf1_{A_1}(x).
\]

Outside the countable set \(A_0\mathbin\triangle A_1\), this is the common
Borel indicator; on that set it is only a countable modification. Hence it
is measurable, so the family is a valid kernel on this different frozen
contract. Adding the external sheet index after seeing the family is
forbidden post-hoc refinement. Neither attack changes the verdict on the
original referent, and no global channel numbering is forced.

## 5. Common check 6 -- total bundle, kernel, pushforward, and character

Write \(x=(a,b)\) and \(g(\kappa)=d(\tau\kappa)\). For a fiber event
\(F\in\Xi_x\), choose any total extension and set

\[
 \mathsf P^{\rm res}_x(F)
 =\frac1{d(a)d(b)}\int_F g\,d\mu_x.
\]

This is extension independent, countably additive, and measurable on every

makes its total mass one. Thus (V4.5) is a resolved probability kernel, not
merely a normalized pointwise family.

\[
\begin{aligned}
 (\tau_*\mathsf P^{\rm res}_x)(A)

   \int_{\tau^{-1}A}d(\tau\kappa)\,\mu_x(d\kappa)\\
 &=\frac1{d(a)d(b)}\int_A d(c)\,\mathsf M_x(dc)

## 8. Projectivity, occurrence propensity, and selector residue

Uniform deletion remains

\[
 K_{n+1,n}(x,y)=\frac{m(x,y)}{n+1},
\]

not raw multiplicity. The primary projectivity condition is
\(P_nK_{n,m}=P_m\), with orbit, inverse-automorphism, and labeled bases kept
distinct. The binomial count laws are projective for every
\(p\in[0,1]\), as are mixtures, so deletion does not generally identify a
parameter or boundary state. Deletion is not resolved-channel composition.

A conditional resolved or target law also does not determine whether
composition occurs. Distinct positive \(q(a,b,\xi)\) give the same law
conditional on composition and different whole laws; at \(q=0\), the
conditional is additional counterfactual structure.

After all such ingredients are analyzed, the remaining family of complete
occurrence laws is the version-2 selector residue \(\mathfrak R\). The
pre-measure contract \(\mathfrak O_{\rm ch}\) neither equals nor determines
that residue. No vector-space dimension is assigned to \(\mathfrak R\)

## 10. Six fresh semantic countermodels

These are additional to the frozen controls. The first two explicitly
separate both converse implications in Proposition V6.1.

1. **Nonatomic target branching without descriptor coherence.** At one
   input, let \(\mathcal C_x=[0,1]\) with Lebesgue measure and let
   \(\tau(t)=c_0\) for \(t<1/3\) and \(\tau(t)=c_1\) otherwise. Freeze no
   nonconstant resolved-channel descriptor in \(\mathcal O\). The target
   probabilities are \(1/3\) and \(2/3\), and their preimages give
   pointwise branching despite zero point masses. Descriptor-coherent
   branching is absent because the frozen descriptor family has no
   separator. This directly realizes target branching implying pointwise
   branching while pointwise branching does not imply descriptor coherence.

2. **Descriptor-coherent nonatomic branching with one target.** Let
   \(X=[0,1]\), \(\mathcal C=X\times[0,1]\), and give every fiber density
   \(2t\,dt\). Freeze the descriptor
   \(\ell(x,t)=\mathbf1_{[0,1/2]}(t)\), and give every channel the same
   target. Its two descriptor events have masses \(1/4\) and \(3/4\) for
   every input. Branching is descriptor coherent and therefore pointwise,
   while the target law is deterministic. This refutes pointwise branching
   implying target branching.

3. **Strictly different branching loci in a varying finite bundle.** Let
   \(X=\mathbb R\). Use one channel for \(x\leq0\) and two equally weighted
   channels for \(x>0\). Freeze a physical slot descriptor on the two-point
   region. Give both slots the same target for \(0<x\leq1\), but distinct
   targets for \(x>1\). The pointwise and descriptor-coherent branching
   locus is \((0,\infty)\), while the target-branching locus is
   \((1,\infty)\). All bundle strata and probability maps are Borel.

4. **Atomless resolved nonidentifiability at fixed target law.** At one
   input, take \(\mathcal C=[0,1]^2\), \(\tau(t,u)=t\), and compare
   \(dt\,du\) with \(dt\,2u\,du\). Both resolved kernels push forward to
   uniform \(dt\) on the target, but their probabilities for events in the
   second coordinate differ. The target kernel therefore determines no
   resolved conditional distribution even on a fixed standard-Borel
   contract.

5. **Projective random graphs with a free parameter.** Push the labeled
   Erdős--Rényi law \(G(n,p)\) to unmarked graph classes and delete a
   uniformly chosen physical vertex. The resulting family is projective for
   every \(p\in[0,1]\). Distinct \(p\)'s give distinct projective laws, so
   exact deletion covariance does not identify the whole selector.

6. **One channel contract and a continuum selector residue.** Fix one
   \(\mathfrak O_{\rm ch}\), all its conditional channel laws, and two
   disconnected primitive whole complexes \(\chi_0,\chi_1\) with the same
   child conditional laws. The whole laws
   \[
    \Pi_t=t\delta_{\chi_0}+(1-t)\delta_{\chi_1},
    \qquad 0\leq t\leq1,
   \]
   agree on every fixed conditional channel experiment and differ on whole
   occurrence. They give a continuum of selector-residue candidates without
   changing \(\mathfrak O_{\rm ch}\), and none selects an actual history.
