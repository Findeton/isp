### 2.2 Aligned futures and the congruence

Two process complexes $\chi,\chi'$ are **alignable** when some
presentation morphism $g$ transports the source type of $\chi$ onto
that of $\chi'$; $g$ then transports every legal future arrow of
$\chi$ to a legal future arrow of $\chi'$ and every equivariant
reader of $\chi$ to one of $\chi'$ (Paper 13D Thm 2 supplies
transport of laws along $g$).

> **Definition 2.1 (predictive-sector congruence).** Complexes
> $\chi,\chi'$ are predictively equivalent, written $\chi\sim\chi'$,
> when they are alignable by some $g$ and for every aligned pair of
> futures $(f,f'=gfg^{-1})$ and every aligned pair of equivariant
> readers $(R,R'=gRg^{-1})$,
> $$g_*\,R_*\,\mathbf\Gamma_D(\chi)\;=\;R'_*\,\mathbf\Gamma_D(\chi').$$
> The equivalence uses *all* admitted readers, led by the complete
> reader (identity on the outcome fiber); no reader family is chosen
> to force a quotient (pin control 13 discharged by construction).

> **Lemma 2.2 (equivalence).** $\sim$ is reflexive, symmetric, and
> transitive on process complexes.
>
> **Proof.** Reflexivity: $g=\mathrm{id}$. Symmetry: transport by
> $g^{-1}$, using functoriality $g_*g_*^{-1}=\mathrm{id}$ of the
> pushforward (Thm 2). Transitivity: given aligning $g$ for
> $\chi\sim\chi'$ and $h$ for $\chi'\sim\chi''$, the composite $hg$
> aligns $\chi$ to $\chi''$; for aligned triples the reader laws
> compose through the intermediate presentation, and equality at the
> two legs gives equality of the endpoints. ∎

> **Theorem A (congruence).** $\sim$ is compatible with every
> operation of the census:
> 1. *(tensor)* if $\chi_\alpha\sim\chi'_\alpha$ for a finite family,
>    then $\boxtimes_\alpha\chi_\alpha\sim
>    \boxtimes_\alpha\chi'_\alpha$;
> 2. *(simultaneous fusion)* if $\chi_\alpha\sim\chi'_\alpha$ for a
>    finite same-sort family, then
>    $\Phi_s(\boxtimes_\alpha\chi_\alpha)\sim
>    \Phi_s(\boxtimes_\alpha\chi'_\alpha)$;
> 3. *(futures and composition)* if $\chi\sim\chi'$ then
>    $f\circ\chi\sim gfg^{-1}\circ\chi'$ for every legal future $f$
>    of $\chi$, with the composite complexes again congruent;
> 4. *(restriction/deletion)* deleting the same uniformly selected
>    occurrence from congruent complexes gives congruent complexes;
> 5. *(stable words and erasure)* applying the same stable word, or
>    the eraser, to congruent complexes gives congruent complexes.
>
> Consequently the quotient
> $\mathsf{Sect}_{13D}=\{\text{complexes}\}/\!\sim$ exists, and
> tensor and single simultaneous fusion descend to well-defined
> operations on it wherever the sorts match.

#### Proof

The kernel of the law is equivariant (Thm 2) and structurally
recursive (§7.3), and the physical quotient is formed before any
reader (§9.1). We verify each clause.

*Tensor.* The tensor kernel is the product of factor kernels (§7.3,
§10.1) and the tensor reader is the product reader. Aligning each
factor by $g_\alpha$, the product map $g=\boxtimes_\alpha g_\alpha$
aligns the tensors, transports futures componentwise, and conjugates
product readers to product readers. Product laws agree because each
factor agrees.

*Fusion.* The n-ary generator draws one fresh seed per cross-component
pair, independently of everything else (§6.1, §10.2), and redraws no
within-component bond. Transport by $g$ relabels occurrences,
exchanges ports locally where $\tau$ says so, and carries the fresh
cross-pair seed set bijectively onto the corresponding set; each seed
has the same uniform law on $[25]$ and the bond threshold rule (§6.3)
is port-swap covariant because $d_i=e'_i$ is a scalar relation fixed
by swaps. Hence the fusion kernels correspond under $g$ exactly, the
corresponding histories are identified by $g$, and the orbit
pushforwards agree. Component permutation invariance of the generator
(§10.2) makes the result independent of the enumeration used to match
families.

*Futures.* Convolution: the composite kernel is the convolution of
generator kernels along the shared boundary (§7.3); transporting the
whole diagram by $g$ transports each generator and the gluing, so
both sides convolve termwise-equal kernels.

*Restriction.* Deletion removes the deleted occurrence's factors,
incident bonds, seeds, and addresses exactly (§14, Thm 7); applying
the same deletion to two complexes aligned by $g$ leaves them aligned
by the restricted $g$, with all retained factors untouched.

*Stable words and erasure.* Each stable generator is a bijection of
its declared target carrying records by a label bijection (§11.1–11.2)
or a deterministic field map; the eraser is deterministic (§11.3).
Conjugating by $g$ commutes with each, so the pushed laws continue to
agree. ∎

## 3. Stage 2 — orbit-summed multiplicity descent

### 3.1 Labeled construction probabilities and the orbit pushforward

For a complex $\chi$ with stabilizer $G_\chi$, the labeled
construction probabilities are the values
$\widetilde{\mathbf\Gamma}(\chi)(H)$ on presented histories. Their
physical masses are the full-orbit sums (Paper 13D §9.1):

$$
M_\chi([H])=\sum_{H'\in G_\chi H}\widetilde{\mathbf\Gamma}(\chi)(H').
$$

Representative mass — the single term $\widetilde{\mathbf\Gamma}
(\chi)(H)$ — is forbidden as a physical quantity; it generally omits
orbit translates and fails normalization (pin control 14 discharged:
only the orbit sum is ever used below). Automorphism-fixed points are
retained inside their orbits with their actual multiplicity.

> **Definition 3.1 (descent).** The multiplicity data of $\chi$ is
> the function $M_\chi$ on its physical history cells, together with
> the cell partition itself. The descended data of the class
> $[\chi]\in\mathsf{Sect}_{13D}$ is the common value of $M_\chi$ over
> the class, when it is constant on the class.

> **Theorem B (multiplicity descent).** On the certified fixtures,
> $M$ is constant on $\sim$-classes; equivalently, congruent
> complexes have isomorphic cell partitions with matching masses.
>
> **Proof.** If $\chi\sim\chi'$ are aligned by $g$, transport by $g$
> maps presented histories of $\chi$ bijectively onto presented
> histories of $\chi'$ (equivariance of every kernel, Thm 2), maps
> stabilizers onto stabilizers, hence orbits onto orbits, and
> preserves each labeled probability because $g$ carries the entire
> seed product (public bits fair, private $\eta$'s, uniforms, and
> cross-pair seeds) onto an identically distributed product. Therefore
> $M_{\chi'}(g[H])=M_\chi([H])$ for every cell. Since the congruence
> compares laws through *some* aligning $g$, and the descended data
> are invariant under precomposition with the stabilizer (each
> stabilizer element preserves orbits and their sums), the descended
> data are independent of the chosen alignment: two alignings differ
> by a stabilizer translate. ∎

### 4.2 Closure test on the certified fixtures

> **Theorem C (source-sort tower).** At the unbonded atomic sorts
> ($B_0$, $B_1^0$, $B_1^r$, and the erased/stable post-endpoint sorts
> restricted to their certified words), simultaneous fusion is the
> deterministic union of occurrence sets (Paper 13D §10.2: no bonds
> to draw). Hence for the size ladder
> $a_n:=[U_{\varnothing}(\{1,\dots,n\})]$ at the source sort,
> $$a_m\otimes a_{m'}=a_{m+m'},\qquad N_{a_m,a_{m'}}^{a_k}=\delta_{k,m+m'}.$$
> The fused class is unique for every pair: multiplicity is exactly
> $1$ into the sum-size class and $0$ elsewhere.
>
> **Proof.** Determinism of $\Phi_s$ at unbonded sorts makes the fused
> complex a function of the component complexes; its class is the
> unique class of a size-$(m+m')$ primitive fixture (Propositions
> A.1–A.2 fix which one). Uniqueness of the target class is the
> multiplicity statement. ∎

> **Corollary C.1 (closure fails finitely; tower closes countably).**
> On any finite subcollection of the certified source-sort fixtures
> containing some $a_m$ with $m\ge1$, multiplication escapes the
> subcollection ($a_m\otimes a_m=a_{2m}$, strictly larger size,
> Proposition A.1). The only fusion-closed finite subfamilies are the
> trivial ones $\{a_0\}$. On the countable ladder
> $\{a_n:n\ge0\}$ multiplication closes with unit $a_0$ and is
> associative — union of finite sets is associative — and
> presentation-invariant by Theorem A/B.

> **Theorem D (bonded-sort classification).** At the bonded sorts
> ($B_2^0$, $B_2^r$, and their certified continuations), fusion
> carries existing bonds and draws fresh cross-pair seeds (§6.3,
> §10.2). The same deterministic-union structure holds at the level
> of occurrence carriers, and the bond field lives inside the cells:
> for the bonded ladders $b_n$ (any fixed bonded family),
> $$N_{b_m,b_{m'}}^{b_{m+m'}}=1,\quad\text{all other }N=0,$$
> identically to Theorem C. Consequently:
> 1. closure **fails** on every finite nontrivial subcollection of
>    certified fixtures (escape to larger size);
> 2. closure **holds** on the countable ladders, forming a
>    commutative unital semiring isomorphic to $(\mathbb N,+)$ with
>    $a_n\mapsto n$;
> 3. this semiring is **indecomposable**: its only additively closed,
>    multiplication-absorbing subsemiring is trivial, since $a_1$
>    generates every $a_n$ and absorption by any ideal containing
>    $a_d$, $d\ge2$, would require $a_{d+1}$ inside it, contradicting
>    closure under adding $a_1$ repeatedly;
> 4. it admits **no involution**: a fusion-ring involution requires
>    $N_{x,y}^{\mathbf 1}\ge1$ for $y=x^*$, i.e. the unit inside
>    $x\otimes y$; in the ladder $x\otimes y$ contains the unit
>    $a_0$ only for $x=y=a_0$, so no element $a_m$, $m\ge1$, has a
>    dual;
> 5. therefore the bonded-sort structures are classified the same way
>    as the source-sort ones: **not a finite closure; a countably
>    generated free unital commutative semiring; not a fusion ring.**

#### Proof

Clauses 1–3 repeat Theorem C and Corollary C.1's arguments, with the
bond mechanism supplying no additional classes (the previous
paragraph). Clause 4: suppose an involution existed; taking
$x=a_1$ there must be $x^*$ with $N_{a_1,x^*}^{a_0}\ge1$; but every
product lands in $b_{1+k}$, $k\ge0$, and $b_1$ occurs only from
$(b_1,b_0)$, so $N_{a_1,y}^{a_0}=0$ for all $y$ — contradiction. ∎
