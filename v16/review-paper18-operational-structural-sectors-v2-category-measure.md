## 3. M1–M22

### M1 — profile-arrow groupoid

**Pass.** For a profile \(\mathcal P_s\), the identity arrow is the identity
functor on \(\mathsf E(s)\) together with identity outcome maps. If
\(\Phi=(F,\eta):\mathcal P_s\to\mathcal P_t\), its specified typed inverse
uses \(F^{-1}\) and the inverse outcome maps. Exact pushforward equality
inverts because measurable isomorphisms have measurable inverses.

For composable \((F,\eta)\) and \((G,\theta)\), composition is

\[
 (G\circ F,\,e\mapsto\theta_{F(e)}\circ\eta_e).
\]

It is total, invertible, typed, natural, and probability preserving.
Associativity is ordinary functor and map associativity. Naturality of the
composite follows by pasting the two naturality squares. Thus Proposition 1
constructs the claimed groupoid and natural outcome transports.

### M2 — partial-correspondence attacks

**Pass.** Every version-1 partial-span attack is excluded at the definition.
A partial relation pairing only a matching subcatalogue is neither a total
functor nor invertible. In particular, the old three-profile pattern cannot
give its first or second arrow when the middle profile contains an unmatched
experiment. If two genuine profile isomorphisms do exist, their composite in
M1 is again a profile isomorphism, so transitivity holds.

### M3 — totality, null outcomes, and diagnostic readers

**Pass.** \(\mathsf E(s)\) contains every admitted complete experiment,
including its preparation, intervention slot and value, process word,
complete or equivariant diagnostic reader, and full outcome space. The
functor \(F\) is total on that category. Each \(\eta_e\) is an isomorphism of
the whole measurable outcome space, not merely of the positive-probability
support. Consequently zero-probability outcomes remain in the transported
space and an omitted intervention, reader, or null outcome fails Definition
4 before probabilities are compared.

The phrase “reader identity” is read as including the declared typed meaning
of its outcome object; an arbitrary relabeling that changes that meaning is
not an allowed typed profile arrow.

### M4 — protected physical and operational identity

**Pass.** Definition 3 makes experiment arrows preserve every typed field and
physical incidence. Definition 4 additionally fixes boundary type, context,
intervention identity, reader identity, process composition, tensoring, and
the accepted presentation action. A mere probability-space bijection that
changes any of those data is not a profile isomorphism.

### M5 — the sector groupoid and its descriptors

**Pass.** An identity in \(\mathsf{Sect}(B)\) is
\((\mathrm{id}_s,\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal P_s})\). Inverses and composites are
formed componentwise from the presentation and profile groupoids;
compatibility is preserved because the profile transport is induced by the
same accepted presentation transport. Hence all arrows are invertible and
composition is associative.

Taking an isomorphism class removes only presentation. The descriptor still
contains \([s]_{\mathcal G}\), so a profile isomorphism between behaviorally
degenerate but nonisomorphic physical structures cannot erase their
different first coordinates. This constructs the sector referent schema; it
does not populate the bounded census.

### M6 — reconstruction of \(\mathsf{Bnd}\)

**Pass.** Objects are the declared triples
\((\partial B,\iota_B,\xi_B)\). An arrow is a literal typed marked inclusion,
not a presentation index or stochastic deletion. Arrow equality is
componentwise equality. Literal identity embeddings preserve all required
data, and the composite of two injective type-, incidence-, context-, mark-,
and experiment-preserving embeddings has the same properties. Componentwise
composition supplies associative composition and the identity laws.

### M7 — contravariant marked restriction

**Pass at contract level.** For \(j:B'\hookrightarrow B\), pullback sends a
sector object to its literal marked subinterface and restricts its profile to
the full experiment subcategory supported there. On an arrow \((g,\Phi)\),
it restricts the presentation transport, experiment functor, and natural
outcome maps. The mark and support-preservation requirements make that arrow
well typed.

Literal pullback gives

\[
 \operatorname{res}_{\mathrm{id}_B}=\mathrm{id},\qquad
 \operatorname{res}_{j\circ k}
 =\operatorname{res}_k\circ\operatorname{res}_j.
\]

Transporting both an inclusion and its data by an accepted presentation
change gives the stated equivariance square on objects and arrows. The
“provided” clause correctly leaves the physical extraction open: objects
that do not support all required pullbacks do not constitute a valid
extracted bounded sector system.

### M8 — exact pullback versus stochastic deletion

**Pass in both directions.** Marked restriction is a deterministic
contravariant functor along a literal physical landmark. It is not a random
transition and performs no marginalization through an incomplete division.
Unmarked deletion is instead a Markov kernel on point-free class spaces and
tests projectivity of measures. A deletion kernel cannot select a
representative for exact restriction; a marked pullback cannot replace
uniform deletion probabilities.

### M9 — groupoid-valued inverse limit

**Pass with the scope stated in Section 2.** The coherent reading can be
reconstructed explicitly. A global object consists of objects \(x_B\in
\mathsf{Sect}(B)\) and, for every \(j:B'\hookrightarrow B\), an isomorphism

\[
 \phi_j:\operatorname{res}_j(x_B)\xrightarrow{\sim}x_{B'}
\]

with \(\phi_{\mathrm{id}}=\mathrm{id}\) and the usual cocycle

\[
 \phi_{j\circ k}
 =\phi_k\circ\operatorname{res}_k(\phi_j).
\]

A morphism is a family \(u_B:x_B\to y_B\) commuting with every \(\phi_j\).
Every component is invertible, so these objects and morphisms form a
groupoid. This is the pseudo-limit/descent groupoid named by Definition 8.
No claim is made that the physical diagram or its limit has been extracted.

### M10 — standard and non-standard inverse-limit controls

**Pass.** The countable binary tower has limit
\(\{0,1\}^{\mathbb N}\), a standard Borel Cantor space carrying the
nonatomic fair product measure.

For a fresh non-standard control, let \(I\) be uncountable, take finite
levels \((\mathbb Z/3)^F\) for \(F\Subset I\), and use coordinate
projections. The inverse limit is \((\mathbb Z/3)^I\) with its cylinder sigma
algebra. Every cylinder-measurable set depends on at most countably many
coordinates, so no countable family generates all coordinate cylinders.
It is not standard Borel. Uniform product measure is nonatomic: any positive
measurable set can be split into three positive parts by an unused
coordinate. Coordinatewise addition gives a measurable autonomous closed
composition. Definition 9 can report this model without forcing it into the
standard-Borel or contextual branches.

### M11 — five completion axes

**Pass with hierarchical-label scope.** Existence/closure, cardinality,
measurable type, physical measure type, and topology answer different
questions. Contextuality may coexist with any local cardinality. A standard
Borel space acquires no “nonatomic” label until a measure is supplied. A
hybrid presentation does not itself choose a reference measure or topology.

For a groupoid-valued completion, cardinality is understood point-freely on
isomorphism classes. Within the measurable axis, “finite discrete” and
“countable discrete” are the more specific reports even though they are also
standard Borel. This reading avoids conflation and does not create a new
physical coordinate.

### M12 — channel groupoid and history fibers

**Pass with the fibration wording scope.** Accepted channel isomorphisms
retain source, target, physical occurrences, incidence, structural mode, and
multiplicity. A nontrivial automorphism can exchange two occurrences without
turning multiplicity two into one. Its induced Paper 13D transport acts on
the corresponding history fibers and pushes the conditional law forward.

An invariant measurable coarse output \(J\) descends through this action and
has exactly \(J_*\mathbf\Gamma_D\). A non-invariant presentation coordinate
does not define a point-free output. Coarsening may merge history values, but
it cannot turn their pushforward probabilities into new independently
selected unmarked channels. Definition 11 supplies an equivariant indexed

### M13 — finite-word convolution closure

**Pass.** The strengthened definition rejects pairwise-finite kernels whose
iterates diverge. A fresh hostile model makes the distinction exact. Let

\[
 \mathsf S=\{\mathbf1\}\sqcup\mathbb N_{>0},\quad
 \nu(\{n\})=\frac{6}{\pi^2n^2},\quad f(n)=n,
\]

use the required delta unit, and for nonunits define

\[
 \mathsf M_{a,b}=f(a)f(b)\nu.
\]

Every pair measure is nonzero and finite. For three nonunits, either
bracketing contains

\[
 \int f(n)\,\nu(dn)
 =\frac6{\pi^2}\sum_{n\ge1}\frac1n=\infty.
\]

The two extended measures can agree as \(\infty=\infty\), but neither is a
finite convolution. Definition 13(3) rejects the model, and item (4) requires
finite-measure equality for every finite word.

### M14 — Theorems 3 and 4 on arbitrary measurable spaces

**Pass.** No standard-Borel theorem is used. For a kernel on an arbitrary
measurable space and nonnegative measurable \(d\), simple-function
approximation followed by monotone convergence shows that

\[
 (a,b)\mapsto\int_A d(c)\mathsf M_{a,b}(dc)
\]

is measurable. Division by positive measurable \(d(a)d(b)\) gives a kernel;
equation (1) gives total mass one, and the delta unit is preserved.

For any finite word and bracketing, multiplication of the pairwise factors
telescopes to

\[
 \frac{d(z)}{\prod_i d(a_i)}
\]

times the corresponding raw final measure. Definition 13 makes those raw
measures finite and equal for all bracketings. Integrating the common
nonnegative factor over an arbitrary measurable set gives equality of the
final probability measures. The argument is not restricted to atoms,
simple functions, or bounded \(d\).

### M15 — finite resolved Frobenius–Perron specialization

**Pass.** In the Fibonacci based ring

\[
 \tau^2=\mathbf1+\tau,
\]

normalization gives \(d(\mathbf1)=1\), and positivity selects the unique
positive root \(d(\tau)=\varphi\) of \(x^2=1+x\). Resolved counting gives
probabilities \(1/\varphi^2\) and \(1/\varphi\), whose sum is one. More
generally,

\[
 \sum_{c,\alpha}\frac{d_c}{d_ad_b}
 =\frac{\sum_cN_{ab}^cd_c}{d_ad_b}=1.
\]

Summing resolved slots gives the stated target-sector probability. The
candidate correctly requires the fusion ring and resolved counting measure
to have independent physical provenance.

### M16 — finite-word measure/character gauge

**Pass.** For an \(n\)-input word, every intermediate \(h\)-factor cancels.
The transformed raw final measure is the original one multiplied by

\[
 \frac{\prod_{i=1}^n h(a_i)}{h(z)},
\]

which is independent of the bracketing. The theorem separately assumes that
every such transformed word measure is finite, so the divergent-iterate gap
does not reappear. Kernel measurability follows by integrating the measurable
function \(h^{-1}\) against \(\mathsf M\). The unit, positive character, and
exactly invariant \(\mathsf P\) follow by substitution.

### M17 — normalized-representation no-go

**Pass at exactly its stated scope.** An associative Markov convolution has

that representability discovered after choosing \(\mathsf P\) does not
select it.

It does not prove that an independently derived physical channel measure
cannot yield a unique character or conditional law. It does not select the
probability that composition occurs and does not construct the whole
selector. The candidate makes all of these scope boundaries explicit.

### M18 — absence of hidden standard-Borel or reference measure assumptions

**Pass.** Definition 13 begins with an arbitrary measurable space. The proofs

### M19 — marginal associativity versus path coherence

**Pass.** A fresh countermodel demonstrates independence. Let the marginal
sector law be deterministic addition on \(\mathbb Z/3\). Retain path
automorphisms in \(C_3=\langle g\rangle\) and set

\[
 \alpha(1,1,1)=g,
 \qquad
 \alpha(a,b,c)=1\quad\text{otherwise}.
\]

The marginal convolution is associative for every finite word. For the
quadruple \((1,1,1,1)\), the two pentagon composites are \(g^2\) and 1, so
pentagon coherence fails. Definition 13 explicitly says its marginal theorem

### M20 — roots, spectators, and wreath products

**Pass as an open construction obligation.** Consider a three-lobed object
whose isomorphic lobes each have internal automorphism group \(C_2\). The
unrooted lobe symmetry is

\[
 C_2\wr S_3
\]

of order \(2^3 3!=48\). A physical root distinguishing one lobe has a
stabilizer of order 16. Inverse-automorphism weighting therefore changes by
a factor of three between the rooted and unrooted conventions. Likewise,
\(m\) identical disconnected spectators contribute
\(|\operatorname{Aut}X|^m m!\) to a wreath-product automorphism group.

These factors do not justify changing rooted local odds. They must cancel by
a proved tensor-spectator theorem or remain different declared base choices.
Version 2 registers the hazard, preserves physical occurrence multiplicity,
and claims no composition closure or physical measure before the test.
