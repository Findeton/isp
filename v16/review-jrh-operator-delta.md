## 3. Three objects that must remain separate

### CLOSED — the exact CP boundary instrument

The finite Z instrument is a fully specified object.  Each branch is CP by its
Kraus form, the effects sum to identity, and complete branch blocks are affine.
Its normalized Choi state is separable, so it is entanglement breaking.  These
properties do not transfer automatically to a history functional or local
weight ansatz.

### PARTIAL — the general history functional

At fixed boundary types the formula is coherent.  Require

`V[h]: H_(B-) -> H_(B+)`

for every `h` in one summation, with `rho_boundary >= 0`, and define
`K_A=sum_(h in A)a[h]V[h]`.  Then

`D(A,B)=Tr(K_A rho K_B^dagger)`

is Hermitian and biadditive.  It is also strongly positive automatically:
with `X_A=sqrt(rho) K_A^dagger`, the matrix `D(A,B)=Tr(X_A^dagger X_B)` is a
Gram matrix.  This is an indirect negative selection result: **strong
positivity alone places no further restriction on the local weights once the
class operators are well typed**.

The same formula makes every branch update `rho -> K_A rho K_A^dagger` CP.
It does **not** automatically make a partition an operational instrument.  An
exact counterexample is

```text
rho = P0,  K0=P0,  K1=2P1.
```

For this boundary state, `D(0,0)=1`, `D(1,1)=D(0,1)=0`: the partition is
exactly decoherent and normalized.  But
`K0^dagger K0 + K1^dagger K1 = diag(1,4)`, not identity; the family is neither
trace preserving nor trace-nonincreasing on every input.  Therefore the
sentence that a CP **instrument** emerges at exact division needs the additional
operator condition

`sum_alpha K_alpha^dagger K_alpha = I`

on every allowed input boundary sector (or the correctly typed deterministic
higher-order analogue).  Exact decoherence supplies ordinary probabilities for
the registered `rho`; it is not operator completeness.

### OPEN — the concrete local-weight ansatz

`a[h]=product_v exp(-I_v[h]/2+i Theta_v[h])` is a nonempty parameterization,
not selected dynamics.  Nothing exact selects `I_v`, `Theta_v`, `V[h]`, the
vertex set, or the measure.  The paper says so.  In particular, evaluating two
functions on one matter-geometry history does not yet prove mutual
backreaction; at least one independently calibrated matter datum must change a
relation/transport response, and that response must change a held-out later
observable.
