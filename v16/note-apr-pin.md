## 1. The one-box question

> Can one construct a genuinely point-free, atomless physical regional
> referent whose parthood, overlap, boundaries, causal influence, and
> comparison maps are fixed by one compositional process law up to operational
> null directions, rather than supplied as decorative kinematics?

APR is a forcing-boundary and referent unit. It does not claim to construct
gravity, a metric, a manifold, matter species, a continuum limit, or a unique
dynamics. Its job is to determine whether the first missing noun in those
claims—physical region—can be made precise and dynamically faithful.

The unit targets a self-consistent pair

    (atomless causal regional doctrine, compositional process law)

and not a bare lattice of names. The regional doctrine types the law; the law's
complete future behavior determines which regional distinctions are physical.

## 2. Ontology is a gated coordinate, not prose decoration

Every APR theorem, receipt row, outcome, and hostile review must state its
level.

### 2.1 Candidate ontology

The ontic candidate is one actual, partial, compatible web of durable
relational facts. Its bounded parts are regions. A region is not a set of
pre-existing spacetime points and is not a finite graph cell.

The candidate permits:

- parthood, overlap, disjointness, contact, and causal influence among bounded
  relational facts;
- one actual horizontal continuation or division outcome where actualization
  is licensed;
- indefinitely refinable regions, if atomlessness survives the law's
  operational quotient;
- durable records as actual facts.

It does not posit:

- manifold points, lattice sites, graph vertices, global slices, or ticks;
- many simultaneously actual histories;
- a wave function, Hilbert space, decoherence functional, Hamiltonian, or
  category as a second substance;
- a metric, connection, curvature, or volume before those roles are earned;
- actualization as an emergent theorem. Actualization remains a postulate.

Unrecorded alternatives are arguments of the law, not additional actual
worlds. Ideal points, when they appear in a representation, are filters or
limits reconstructed from regions and are not promoted to beables.

### 2.2 Candidate law

The law assigns compositional future profiles to bounded relational fillings.
It must distinguish passive redescription from physical continuation, retain
interference until a stable record division, and generate every calibrated
prediction used to identify regions.

The microscopic law type remains open. It may be represented by an indivisible
multi-region process, a strongly positive history functional, a boundary
instrument family, or another equivalent construction. APR may establish
which regional distinctions such a law can support; it may not declare that a
particular representation is fundamental.

### 2.3 Representation

Finite prefix algebras, ideals, matrices, Hilbert spaces, graphs, cospans,
class operators, kernels, Python objects, and receipt rows are representations
or exact verification devices. A finite fixture cannot prove fundamental
discreteness or a continuum limit.

The atomless prefix representation used below is an existence witness. The
physical referent is the faithful image selected by complete future profiles,
not the syntax of binary words.

## 3. Priced principles and their drop controls

APR separates mathematical consequences from principles.

### P0 — operational identity of regions

Two candidate regional distinctions count as the same physical fact exactly
when every licensed continuation and calibrated future record treats them the
same.

This is a regional identity-of-indiscernibles principle. It is not a theorem.
Dropping it leaves hidden, forever-indiscernible regional facts as a live
ontology.

### P1 — presentation covariance

Renaming, subdividing, regrouping, or reslicing a description of the same fact
does not change predictions. Dropping P1 permits presentation-dependent
physics.

### P2 — compositional closure

Compatible bounded fillings glue, disjoint spectators compose, and predictions
do not depend on an artificial intermediate cut. Dropping P2 permits
lookup-table families without one law.

### P3 — dynamic locality

The subsystem assigned to a region by internal construction agrees with the
subsystem invariant under calibrated changes in its exterior. This is a
Fewster–Verch-shaped principle, adapted to the regional law.

Dynamic locality alone has a constant-law false positive. APR therefore adds
support faithfulness: physically distinct regions must have some licensed
future discriminator unless they lie in the operational null quotient.

### P4 — atomlessness

No nonzero physical region is irreducible under parthood. APR tests this after
the predictive quotient. It does not infer atomlessness from the absence of
atoms in one presentation.

Dropping P4 leaves atomic and mixed regional ontologies live.

### P5 — complete-continuation scope

A null or causal-absence theorem is licensed only relative to a declared
continuation and intervention catalogue. If completeness is not proved, the
verdict is upper-bound-only or priced, never absolute.

## 5. Candidate atomless regional algebra

APR's result-neutral existence representation is the Boolean algebra K of
finite unions of prefix cylinders over a binary alphabet. A cylinder w
contains every continuation of the finite word w. Canonical elements are
finite prefix-free antichains.

Operations are exact:

- zero is the empty antichain;
- one is the empty-word cylinder;
- meet is prefix intersection;
- join is union followed by prefix reduction;
- complement is finite cylinder splitting and reduction;
- A ≤ B iff A ∧ complement(B) = 0.

Every nonzero cylinder w splits into the disjoint nonzero cylinders w0 and w1.
Every nonzero finite union contains such a splittable cylinder. Thus K is
atomless by a symbolic theorem, not by a finite census.

The ideal/frame completion is a representation of arbitrary joins. APR must
keep three notions distinct:

1. mereological atomlessness of physical regions;
2. nonatomicity of a later extensive valuation;
3. nonatomicity of an event/history algebra.

None is inferred from either of the others.

The mandatory negative control quotients K by a homomorphism onto the
two-element Boolean algebra. The syntax is atomless while the quotient is
atomic. Therefore every positive APR result must re-test atomlessness after
the operational quotient.

## 6. Regional relations

Candidate regions carry the following result-neutral predicates.

### 6.1 Parthood and overlap axioms

- A ≤ A; transitivity; antisymmetry after operational quotient.
- zero ≤ A ≤ one.
- meet is greatest lower bound; join is least upper bound.
- overlap(A,B) iff A ∧ B is nonzero.
- disjoint(A,B) iff A ∧ B is zero.
- finite distributivity and exact complement in the chosen representation.

### 6.2 Contact axioms

Contact C is a candidate symmetric relation satisfying:

- C(A,B) implies A and B are nonzero;
- symmetry;
- monotonicity: C(A,B) and A ≤ A', B ≤ B' imply C(A',B');
- overlap implies contact;
- contact distributes over finite joins in each argument.

Contact is not causal precedence and does not yet define distance.

### 6.3 Causal-influence axioms

The derived or priced relation A ≺ B must be:

- irreflexive at the calibrated record grain unless a causal-cycle outcome is
  explicitly registered;
- invariant under presentation refinement;
- monotone under enlarging source and target regions;
- compatible with gluing and disjoint spectators;
- supported by an intervention-to-record discriminator;
- independent of mere correlation.

If influence cycles survive, APR does not silently force a partial order. It
returns CYCLIC-BLOCK, a fused-region branch, or a higher-order-process debt.

## 7. Two arrows and structured gluing

APR represents boundaries and fillings with a structured-cospan double
category.

- Objects are physical boundary types.
- Vertical arrows are passive comparisons/refinements of the same physical
  boundary fact.
- Horizontal arrows are candidate physical fillings or continuations between
  boundaries.
- Two-cells certify compatibility of a vertical redescription with a
  horizontal filling.
- Horizontal composition is pushout-shaped gluing along a common boundary.
- The monoidal product is disjoint union.

The exact fixture layer may use finite presentations, but the type discipline
is binding: a vertical refinement creates no record; a horizontal extension
may.

The following are hard failures:

- treating a refinement as growth;
- allowing a horizontal event to be erased by a name change;
- gluing predictions without a common physical boundary type;
- using one arrow type for both operations.

## 8. Complete future profiles and the physical quotient

For every candidate boundary or region i, let Phi_i collect all calibrated
predictions under the declared licensed future fillings. In a finite exact
representation this is a linear or affine map. Define

    N_i = kernel(Phi_i)
    Q_i = V_i / N_i.

For a continuation family, the stable null is the largest
continuation-invariant subspace contained in the present null space. It is
computed by iterative removal in finite fixtures and defined by intersection
over all licensed future words in the theorem statement.

Two histories, boundary facts, or regional distinctions are physically equal
under P0 exactly when their classes in the complete future-profile quotient
agree.

The physical regional algebra is not K itself. It is the faithful image of

    Supp_M : candidate region A ↦ Dyn_M(A),

## 9. Comparison-map classification

For two presentations or histories that later meet a common boundary, every
comparison receives exactly one diagnosis:

1. DERIVED — the law's continuations determine the comparison on every fed
   quotient direction.
2. NULL-QUOTIENT — rival lifts differ only inside the complete stable null.
3. PRICED — rival lawful comparisons move a calibrated prediction; the choice
   is additional law data.
4. INCONSISTENT — no comparison satisfies gluing, covariance, and the common
   future profiles.

Calling an unfed direction gauge after one preparation is forbidden. It is
null only after the complete continuation test.

### 9.1 Mandatory inherited RHL discriminator

The construction must rebuild, independently, the common-boundary span

    V_a = V_b = C^2,    V_c = C^3,

with unit vector e0 and marked vector p=e1.

Comparison A maps both marked vectors to e1. Comparison B maps the a-marked
vector to e1 and the b-marked vector to e2. Both are local unit-preserving
isometries.

The marked cross term is 1 in A and 0 in B. The calibrated coherent screen

    s = (1/4) || J_ca p + J_cb p ||^2

is exactly 1 in A and 1/2 in B. APR must diagnose the pair as PRICED unless a
new common future law derives one comparison or proves their difference null.

A changed-object mutant replacing e1 by e2 in one continuation must move the
diagnosis or be rejected. A scorer that only checks local isometry is invalid.

## 10. Dynamic locality and the constant-law trap

For a region A define:

- Kin_M(A): the subsystem generated by fillings wholly internal to A;
- Dyn_M(A): the subsystem unchanged by every calibrated modification in the
  causal exterior of A, with common boundary data fixed.

The dynamic-locality target is a natural isomorphism Kin_M(A) ≅ Dyn_M(A).

A constant law makes both sides trivial for every A and passes that equality.
APR therefore requires dynamic faithfulness:

- some internal intervention changes a later calibrated record;
- different physical subregions have different support profiles somewhere in
  the held-out family;
- erasing the regional relation or replacing it by a resource-matched blind
  input moves the held-out prediction;
- the faithful image remains atomless.

Dynamic locality without these controls earns no physical referent.

## 11. Operational causal influence

For admissible interventions I_A and calibrated stable records Z_B,

    A ↝ B

means that two choices of I_A, with common past and boundary data fixed, induce
different predictions for Z_B in the joint law.

The absence of influence is absolute only if the intervention and record
catalogues are complete at the stated scope. Otherwise APR reports
UPPER-BOUND-ONLY or PRICED.

Disjoint support must imply the registered no-signalling/spectator condition.
This is not yet changing-factorization no-signalling for a dynamical geometry;
APR only supplies the regional referent and interface required to phrase that
future theorem.

## 12. Minimal predictive boundaries

A boundary retains exactly the equivalence classes of interior fillings that
some licensed exterior can distinguish. It is sufficient if no exterior
prediction depends on finer interior data, and minimal if deleting any class
changes at least one calibrated continuation.

APR will compare:

- a hand-declared boundary;
- the complete future-profile quotient boundary;
- a lossy coarsening;
- a redundant refinement;
- an exterior-catalogue extension.

The derived boundary is licensed only at the declared continuation scope.
Extension instability is a priced catalogue debt, not concealed.

## 13. E-37 family-level irreducibility game

No finite fixture can defeat every enlarged-state lookup table. APR therefore
freezes a family-relative game.

Construct matched regional families SHARE_n and SPLIT_n that agree under a
declared blind projection pi but differ in whether two interiors share a
physical subregion or only share a name.

- One uniform process rule is trained/fixed on registered members.
- Held-out members are nonisomorphic regional presentations.
- The blind classes B0, B1, and B2 receive matched state dimension, history
  depth, calibration access, and parameter count.
- Every blind rule factors through pi and therefore cannot inspect the
  regional relation that distinguishes SHARE from SPLIT.
- The physical rule must generate, rather than receive, the held-out screen.

The result is always class-relative. A successful exclusion says the regional
variable is irreducible against the frozen parity classes, not against every
possible encoding.
