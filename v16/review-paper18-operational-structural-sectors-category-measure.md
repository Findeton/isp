## 2. Decisive result

The conditional measure theorems are mostly sound, but the object to which
they would be applied is not yet a constructed categorical/measurable
contract. Three independent defects matter:

1. Definition 2 does not define what a typed intervention/reader
   correspondence is, or require identity, inverse, totality, or closure
   under composition. Consequently behavioural indistinguishability is not
   proved to be an equivalence relation, while Definition 3 uses a response
   profile class.
2. Definition 4 supplies pairwise maps but imposes neither identity nor the
   functorial equation
   \(r_{B',B''}r_{B,B'}=r_{B,B''}\), and it does not prove descent through the
   accepted presentation groupoid. Thus the displayed inverse limit is not
   yet the limit of a defined functor.
3. The supposedly general regime list and Definition 10 exclude autonomous,
   closed, non-standard measurable inverse limits. The listed primary
   classifications are also not mutually exclusive: contextuality is a
   closure property, whereas finite/countable/standard-Borel/hybrid are
   cardinal or measurable-type properties.

The first defect invalidates the current coordinate
`P18-SECTOR-REFERENT-CONTRACT-CONSTRUCTED`. The corrections are not a unique
wording change: one must choose a category/groupoid of profile
correspondences, choose groupoid-compatible restriction objects (maps,
correspondences, or kernels), and change the sector-space outcome contract.
Under the frozen verdict rule this is `REJECT`, not `REVISE`.

## 3. First decisive semantic counterexample

The first decisive counterexample occurs at Definition 2, before the inverse
limit and channel theorems.

Take a single intervention type and deterministic response coordinates. Let

\[
 \operatorname{Prof}(S)=\{s_0\},\qquad
 \operatorname{Prof}(T)=\{t_0,t_1\},\qquad
 \operatorname{Prof}(U)=\{u_1\},
\]

with response values

\[
 p(s_0)=p(t_0)=0,
 \qquad
 p(t_1)=p(u_1)=1.
\]

Under the ordinary relation/span meaning of “correspondence,” which the
candidate does not exclude, the correspondence \(s_0\leftrightarrow t_0\)
makes every paired response equal, and the correspondence
\(t_1\leftrightarrow u_1\) does likewise. Their composite is empty and there
is no equality-preserving \(S\)-to-\(U\) correspondence. Hence the displayed
criterion permits \(S\equiv_\Gamma T\) and \(T\equiv_\Gamma U\) without
\(S\equiv_\Gamma U\).

If “correspondence” was intended to mean a total invertible typed natural
isomorphism, transitivity would follow by composition, but none of those
requirements is stated and the intervention/reader objects and their
composition category are not defined. Therefore
\([\operatorname{Prof}_\Gamma(s)]\) has no determined equivalence-class
meaning in the frozen candidate. This is a countermodel to the stated
contract, not a proposed repair.

## 4. M1–M18 reconstruction

### M1 — literal structure, gauge, and behavioural degeneracy

Definition 1 correctly excludes reader labels, serialization indices,
caches, and inferred dimension. Definition 2 uses the accepted Paper 13D
presentation groupoid for kinematic gauge, and Definition 3 retains
\([s]_{\mathcal G}\) separately from the response profile. Thus the intended
separation is sound: behavioural degeneracy does not erase a nonisomorphic
physical object.

This is only a schema. The response-profile class is not defined for the
reason above, and no bounded census or descent theorem establishes that every
field in an extracted interface has literal Paper 13D provenance. Therefore
the intention passes, but the claimed constructed referent contract does not.

### M2 — behavioural equivalence

**Fail.** Reflexivity, symmetry, and transitivity follow only if the typed
correspondences contain identities, are invertible, preserve the complete
typed catalogue, and are closed under composition. The candidate states none
of these. The counterexample in Section 3 realizes the resulting
nontransitivity. Pairwise equality of response numbers is not by itself an
equivalence relation on typed experiments.

### M3 — restrictions and presentation actions

**Fail.** The paper requires neither

\[
 r_{B,B}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathsf S_B}
 \quad\text{nor}\quad
 r_{B',B''}\circ r_{B,B'}=r_{B,B''},
\]

and states no equivariance square from presented interfaces to their
groupoid orbits. These are not consequences of the word “restriction.”

A concrete accepted-groupoid obstruction is an unrooted interface containing
two occurrence slots whose presentations are exchanged by the nontrivial
element of \(S_2\). Give the two transported field values 0 and 1. The
unmarked physical datum is the orbit of the multiset \(\{0,1\}\). A proposed
map that keeps “the first occurrence” returns 0 from one representative and
1 from its swapped representative, so it does not descend to the orbit.
Paper 13D's uniform deletion is a covariant stochastic operation; it does not
automatically provide this deterministic point-free restriction. The set of
admissible subscopes and the kind of restriction arrow must be constructed
before an inverse limit exists.

### M4 — Theorem 1 and the accepted Paper 13D system

**Theorem valid; inherited implication sharply limited.** For
\(\mathsf S_n=\{0,1\}^n\) with terminal-coordinate deletion, compatible
tuples are exactly binary sequences. The inverse limit has continuum
cardinality. The fair Bernoulli cylinder measure is nonatomic.

This proves only an existence theorem about inverse systems. It does not show
that the accepted Paper 13D bounded sector spaces are finite, that their
restriction maps form a functor, that their inverse limit is nonempty, that
it is standard Borel, or that a physical measure on it is Bernoulli or even
exists. Paper 13D has finite occurrence carriers but arbitrarily long finite
execution syntax; finite carriers alone do not establish a finite bounded
sector census. The candidate mostly respects this limitation and correctly
leaves the actual completion unconstructed.

### M5 — standard-Borel completion

**Fail as a claim of generality.** Standard Borel is a useful branch, but it
is an extra assumption. Let \(I\) be uncountable and direct the finite subsets
\(F\Subset I\) by inclusion. Put

\[
 \mathsf S_F=\{0,1\}^{F}
\]

with coordinate projections. The inverse limit is \(\{0,1\}^{I}\) with its
cylinder sigma algebra. Every measurable set depends on at most countably
many coordinates, so the sigma algebra is not countably generated and the
space is not standard Borel. The fair product measure is nonatomic: any
positive measurable set can be split using a coordinate outside the
countable set on which it depends.

Coordinatewise XOR supplies a measurable, deterministic, associative
convolution with unit. This branch is autonomous and closed, not contextual,
but it is absent from Definition 5 and the five-regime list. Contextual
composition itself is not silently excluded—the paper correctly retains it
as a refusal of autonomous closure—but the “most general” convolution
contract applies only after the unproved standard-Borel restriction.

### M6 — unmarked channel equivalence and automorphisms

The channel definition explicitly retains every generator occurrence,
incidence, and occurrence multiplicity and permits equivalence only through
an accepted structural isomorphism. This correctly prevents an automorphism
from turning a two-occurrence object into a one-occurrence object.

It does not yet determine numerical multiplicity. If \(S_2\) exchanges two
physical resolved channels, resolved counting gives 2, orbit counting gives
1, and finite-groupoid cardinality can give \(1/2\). Definition 11 correctly
keeps these provenances separate, so the current physical channel measure
must remain unconstructed.

### M7 — measurable-kernel definition

Measurability, pairwise nonzero finiteness, the two-sided unit, and
associativity on every measurable set are stated. The standard-Borel
restriction is stronger than the kernel calculations require and causes the
M5 exclusion.

There is a second closure gap. Pairwise finiteness does not make an iterated
convolution finite, while the associativity equation is permitted to read
\(\infty=\infty\). On
\(\mathsf S=\{\mathbf1\}\sqcup\mathbb N\), let
\(\nu(\{n\})=2^{-n}\), \(f(n)=2^n\), use the required delta unit, and for
nonunits set

\[
 \mathsf M_{a,b}=f(a)f(b)\nu.
\]

Every pair measure is finite. For three nonunits, both bracketings give the
same extended measure because
\(\sum_n f(n)\nu(\{n\})=\infty\), but every set of positive \(\nu\)-measure
has infinite triple mass. Thus the written axioms admit a kernel that is not
closed in finite measures. The current
`P18-COMPOSITION-CLOSURE-UNPROVEN` coordinate is therefore necessary.

### M8 — Theorem 3

**Pass under the stated hypotheses.** For measurable positive \(d\), the
standard kernel-integration lemma makes

\[
 (a,b)\longmapsto\int_A d(c)\,\mathsf M_{a,b}(dc)
\]

measurable. Division by the positive measurable function \(d(a)d(b)\)
preserves measurability. Equation (1) gives total mass one, hence finiteness.
The unit also survives:
\(\mathsf P_{\mathbf1,a}=\mathsf P_{a,\mathbf1}=\delta_a\).
This is a theorem about any kernel meeting the contract, not evidence that
Paper 13D supplies such a physical kernel.

### M9 — Theorem 4 on arbitrary measurable sets

**Pass.** Define the two composed measures

\[
 Q_L(A)=\int\mathsf M_{a,b}(dx)\mathsf M_{x,e}(A),\qquad
 Q_R(A)=\int\mathsf M_{b,e}(dy)\mathsf M_{a,y}(A).
\]

Definition 10 gives \(Q_L=Q_R\) on every measurable set, not merely on
atoms. Equality of measures permits integration of the nonnegative measurable
function \(d\mathbf1_A\). Cancellation of the intermediate \(d(x)\) or
\(d(y)\) yields exactly the two iterated \(\mathsf P\) measures. The proof
therefore covers arbitrary measurable \(A\) and unbounded positive \(d\);
the resulting Markov compositions are finite.

This proves equality of final marginals only. It says nothing about an
associator between retained path objects or pentagon coherence.

### M10 — finite Frobenius–Perron specialization

**Pass with its stated provenance scope.** In a finite indecomposable based
fusion ring, the normalized positive Frobenius–Perron character is unique.
The character equation is

\[
 d_a d_b=\sum_c N_{ab}^{c}d_c.
\]

With resolved counting measure, each resolved slot \((c,\alpha)\) has mass
\(d_c/(d_ad_b)\), and summing over
\(1\leq\alpha\leq N_{ab}^{c}\) normalizes the law. If only the target sector
is observed, its mass is \(N_{ab}^{c}d_c/(d_ad_b)\).

The formula per \(\alpha\) additionally assumes a physically derived
resolved-channel set with equivariant counting measure. A basis of a fusion
space, an orbit count, and inverse-automorphism groupoid weight do not supply
that assumption. The candidate says this explicitly enough and does not
apply the specialization to the accepted law.

### M11 — Theorem 5

**Pass.** The stated finiteness hypothesis makes every transformed pair
measure admissible. Positivity and measurability are immediate. Since
\(h(\mathbf1)=1\), the unit is unchanged. In a triple composite, the
intermediate \(h\)-factor cancels, and equality for arbitrary measurable sets
follows by integrating the original equal composed measures against
\(h(z)^{-1}\mathbf1_A(z)\). Direct substitution proves the transformed
character equation. The displayed cancellation proves exact invariance of
\(\mathsf P\).

### M12 — completeness of the gauge family

The \(h\)-family is complete only in a precisely bounded sense. Fix the same
measurable sector space and the same strictly positive conditional kernel
\(\mathsf P\). If \((\mathsf M,d)\) and \((\mathsf N,e)\) both represent it,
then, wherever the measures are finite,

\[
 \mathsf N_{a,b}(dc)
 =\frac{e(a)e(b)}{e(c)}\mathsf P_{a,b}(dc)
 =\frac{h(a)h(b)}{h(c)}\mathsf M_{a,b}(dc),
 \qquad h=e/d.
\]

Thus every scalar refactorization of this kind is in the family. It is not a
complete catalogue of representational degeneracy: measurable isomorphisms,
sector refinements with identical pushforward, different resolved groupoid
presentations, and different disintegrations over unobserved channels are not
\(h\)-transforms on a fixed \(\mathsf S\).

### M13 — Theorem 6 and its no-go scope

**Pass with explicit hidden assumptions.** An associative Markov kernel is a
finite nonzero positive kernel; its unit and associativity transfer to
\(\mathsf M=\mathsf P\), and \(d=1\) satisfies the character equation. The
theorem proves that normalized-convolution representability alone cannot
explain a probability law.

To call this a selection no-go one must assume that only representability was
offered as the explanation, that \(\mathsf M\) was fitted after \(\mathsf P\),
that no independent channel census or measure provenance exists, and that
the sector space and conditioning event are held fixed. It does not prove
that an independently derived physical \(\mathsf M\) cannot select a unique
conditional law. It also says nothing about operation-occurrence propensity
or the whole selector. The candidate observes these limitations.

### M14 — exhaustiveness of the five branches

**Fail.** The uncountable-index inverse limit in M5 is closed, autonomous,
measurable, and non-standard; it fits none of the five realized outcomes.
Moreover:

- `CONTEXTUAL-NONCLOSED` is orthogonal to cardinality and measure type, so a
  contextual system may also be finite, countable, continuous, or hybrid;
- “nonatomic” is a property of a measure, not of a bare standard-Borel
  sector space, and the physical channel measure is currently unconstructed;
- a standard-Borel space can carry atomic, nonatomic, or mixed measures; and
- `HYBRID` has no criterion making it disjoint from a disjoint-union
  presentation of a standard-Borel space.

The branches are useful tests but are neither mutually exclusive nor
exhaustive at the declared level.

### M15 — topology, groupoids, and physical measure

**Pass.** A topology supplies Borel sets, not a distinguished physical
measure. A groupoid need not possess a Haar system, and existence does not
imply physical uniqueness.

For a fresh provenance control, take the pair groupoid
\(G=[0,1]\times[0,1]\). Both Lebesgue measure and the full-support Radon
measure \(2x\,dx\), copied onto each range fiber, give continuous left Haar
systems. The same topology and groupoid therefore admit distinct Haar
systems. Nothing in the groupoid says which one counts physical channels.
This is a measure-provenance failure even though Haar systems exist.

### M16 — associator and pentagon

**Pass as a distinction; no coherence is constructed.** Let the marginal
sector law be deterministic addition on \(\mathbb Z_2\). Retain path
automorphisms in \(C_3=\langle g\rangle\), and define the normalized
associator by

\[
 \alpha(1,1,1)=g,
 \qquad
 \alpha(a,b,c)=1\ \text{otherwise}.
\]

The marginal output is associative. For the quadruple \((1,1,1,1)\), the
two pentagon paths give \(g^2\) and 1, so pentagon coherence fails. This is an
associative marginal with a genuine path-level coherence failure. Theorem 4
cannot exclude it. The candidate correctly requests a separate associator
and pentagon test and correctly leaves full coherence unproven.

### M17 — spectators, braiding, roots, and wreath products

The registered hazards are real and remain open construction obligations.
If a rooted local channel \(C\) is tensored with \(m\) isomorphic disconnected
spectators \(X\), then typically

\[
 \operatorname{Aut}(C\boxtimes X^{\boxtimes m})
 \supseteq
 \operatorname{Aut}(C)\times
 \bigl(\operatorname{Aut}(X)\wr S_m\bigr).
\]

Inverse-automorphism weighting introduces a factor
\(1/(|\operatorname{Aut}X|^m m!)\). Unless an exact rooted/tensor
cancellation theorem is proved, a disconnected spectator changes a local
odds ratio. Rooting can also break an automorphism that exists in the
unrooted object. Braiding may exchange two physical occurrences without
reducing their count. The candidate flags all of these and promotes no
measure or coherent composition, so the surviving coordinate is
`P18-COMPOSITION-CLOSURE-UNPROVEN`.

## 7. Required positive controls 37–43

### 37. Finite discrete unique positive character — PASS

Use the Fibonacci based ring

\[
 \tau^2=\mathbf1+\tau.
\]

With \(d(\mathbf1)=1\), positivity forces
\(d(\tau)=\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2\), the unique positive root of
\(x^2=1+x\). The resolved probabilities are
\(1/\varphi^2\) and \(1/\varphi\), which sum to one.

### 38. Countable continuous character family — PASS

For \((\mathbb N_0,+)\) and
\(\mathsf M_{m,n}=\delta_{m+n}\), every \(d_r(n)=r^n\), \(r>0\), is a
normalized positive character. This is character nonuniqueness; all these
characters happen to induce the same deterministic conditional kernel.

### 39. Finite-level nonatomic completion — PASS, with a missing branch

The candidate's countable binary inverse system is valid. CM3 gives the
stronger uncountable-index version: finite levels and a nonatomic completion
that is not standard Borel, exposing the missing outcome class.

### 40. Hybrid sector model — PASS

Take

\[
 \mathsf S=\mathbb Z_2\times\mathbb R_{\ge0},
 \qquad
 (a,x)\star(b,y)=(a+b\bmod2,x+y).
\]

The deterministic convolution is associative and standard Borel, but a rule
on the discrete \(\mathbb Z_2\) label supplies no measure on the continuous
coordinate. The candidate makes exactly this distinction.

### 41. Contextual refusal of closure — PASS

On \(\{0,1\}\) with unit 0, let the only nontrivial product be

\[
 \mathsf M^{\xi}_{1,1}
 =p_\xi\delta_0+(1-p_\xi)\delta_1,
\]

with \(p_0=1/3\) and \(p_1=2/3\). Each fixed-context law is associative, but
no autonomous kernel equals both. If \(\xi\) cannot be absorbed as declared
physical state, `CONTEXTUAL-NONCLOSED` is the correct refusal.

### 42. Gauge-related representatives — PASS

Use the two-point associative hypergroup with unit 0 and

\[
 \mathsf P_{1,1}=\tfrac12\delta_0+\tfrac12\delta_1.
\]

Start from \(\mathsf M=\mathsf P,d=1\). For \(h(0)=1,h(1)=2\), Theorem 5
gives

\[
 \mathsf M^h_{1,1}=2\delta_0+\delta_1,
 \qquad d^h(0)=1,\quad d^h(1)=2.
\]

Both representatives return the same half-half \(\mathsf P_{1,1}\), while
their raw measures and characters differ.

### 43. Normalized representation without provenance — PASS

For the same two-point hypergroup, replace \(1/2\) by any fitted
\(p\in[0,1]\):

\[
 \mathsf P_{1,1}=p\delta_0+(1-p)\delta_1.
\]

It remains associative, and \(\mathsf M=\mathsf P,d=1\) represents it. The
parameter \(p\) has merely been inserted into the normalized convolution;
no physical resolved-channel measure selected it. This confirms Theorem 6's
provenance no-go.

## 10. Terminal assessment

The paper has a useful and largely correct conditional measure-theoretic
core. In particular, it correctly separates channel support from measure,
proves normalization and arbitrary-set marginal associativity, exposes the
measure/character gauge, and refuses to treat normalized hypergroup
representability as a selection mechanism.

Those successes do not construct the claimed sector referent. The undefined
profile correspondence, nonfunctorial restriction schema, missing
non-standard measurable branch, and finite-composition closure gap are
semantic defects in the frozen mathematical object. They cannot be repaired
inside this review, and no implementation or downstream result can cure
them. Mathematical promotion therefore stops with `REJECT`, while all
surviving coordinates above remain preserved.
