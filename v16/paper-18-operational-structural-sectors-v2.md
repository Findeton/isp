## 3. Complete bounded profiles as a groupoid

### Definition 1 -- bounded marked exterior

A bounded marked exterior is a tuple

\[
 B=(\partial B,\iota_B,\xi_B),
\]

where \(\partial B\) is a complete accepted boundary type, \(\iota_B\) is a
finite family of literal typed physical marks or subinterface embeddings,
and \(\xi_B\) is every native context needed to type experiments supported
inside the marks. Marks are physical addresses or landmark structures, not
enumeration indices.

Let \(\mathsf{Bnd}\) be the small category whose arrows

\[
 j:B'\hookrightarrow B
\]

are literal injective maps on boundary fields, occurrence sets, incidence,
record sectors, context, and marks. They must preserve every declared type
and invariant and carry each experiment supported in \(B'\) to the same
physical subexperiment in \(B\). Equality is componentwise equality of these
maps. Identities are literal identity inclusions and composition is
componentwise composition of embeddings. Presentation changes form an
action groupoid on this category.

### Definition 2 -- physical interface datum

At \(B\), an interface datum is

\[
 s=(B,\mathcal I_s,\mathcal R_s,\mathcal C_s),
\]

where \(\mathcal I_s\) is complete incidence and occurrence data,
\(\mathcal R_s\) is the family of stable records with licensed transports,
and \(\mathcal C_s\) is the native context. Every field is literal accepted
law data. Reader names, caches, inferred dimensions, and serialization
positions are excluded.

Kinematic equivalence is exact transport by the accepted presentation
groupoid and is denoted \(s\cong_{\mathcal G}t\).

### Definition 3 -- complete experiment category

For an interface \(s\), \(\mathsf E(s)\) is the small typed category whose
objects are every admitted complete experiment supported by \(s\): complete
exterior, native context, preparation, intervention slot and value, process
word, complete or equivariant reader, and outcome space. Its arrows are only
accepted experiment isomorphisms preserving every typed field and physical
incidence. Composition and identities are inherited literally from the
accepted experiment category.

The complete profile

\[
 \mathcal P_s=\operatorname{Prof}_{\Gamma}(s)
\]

assigns to every object of \(\mathsf E(s)\) its complete normalized outcome
law and transports that law along accepted experiment isomorphisms.

### Definition 4 -- profile isomorphism

A profile isomorphism \(\Phi:\mathcal P_s\to\mathcal P_t\) consists of:

1. a total invertible typed functor
   \(F:\mathsf E(s)\to\mathsf E(t)\), with a specified typed inverse;
2. for every experiment \(e\), a measurable outcome-space isomorphism
   \(\eta_e\);
3. exact pushforward equality
   \[
    (\eta_e)_*\mathcal P_s(e)=\mathcal P_t(F(e));
   \]
4. naturality of \(\eta\) under every experiment isomorphism; and
5. preservation of boundary types, contexts, intervention identity,
   reader identity, composition, tensoring, and accepted groupoid action.

Partial relations, non-total spans, and correspondences between selected
subcatalogues are not profile isomorphisms.

### Proposition 1 -- profile isomorphisms form a groupoid

Complete profiles and the isomorphisms of Definition 4 form a groupoid
\(\mathsf{Prof}^{\Gamma}_B\).

#### Proof

The identity functor and identity outcome maps give identity arrows. The
specified inverse functor and inverse outcome maps preserve pushforward
equalities and naturality. Composition of total invertible typed functors and
measurable outcome isomorphisms is total, invertible, typed, natural, and
probability preserving. Associativity follows from ordinary functor and map
composition. \(\square\)

### Definition 5 -- bounded sector candidate

A bounded candidate descriptor is the ordered pair

\[
 \sigma_B(s)=\bigl([s]_{\mathcal G},\mathcal P_s\bigr)
\]

with the second coordinate understood up to isomorphism in
\(\mathsf{Prof}^{\Gamma}_B\). Behavioral indistinguishability means profile
isomorphism. It does not quotient two nonisomorphic physical structures,
because their first coordinates remain different. The profile isomorphism
class is now defined by Proposition 1 rather than by an unspecified pairwise
correspondence.

Let \(\mathsf{Sect}(B)\) be the groupoid whose objects are literal compatible
pairs \((s,\mathcal P_s)\) and whose arrows are compatible pairs

complete interface data and \(\Phi\) is the induced profile isomorphism. Its

For every marked inclusion \(j:B'\hookrightarrow B\), exact pullback of

### Theorem 3 -- positive-character normalization

Let \(d:\mathsf S\to\mathbb R_{>0}\) be measurable, with
\(d(\mathbf1)=1\), and suppose

\[
 d(a)d(b)=\int d(c)\,\mathsf M_{a,b}(dc)
\tag{1}
\]

for every pair. Then

\[
 \mathsf P_{a,b}(dc)
 =\frac{d(c)}{d(a)d(b)}\mathsf M_{a,b}(dc)
\tag{2}
\]

is a normalized measurable Markov kernel with the same unit.

#### Proof

Kernel integration makes the weighted measure measurable in \((a,b)\).
Positivity is immediate, and equation (1) makes its total mass one. The unit
follows from \(d(\mathbf1)=1\). \(\square\)

### Theorem 4 -- finite-word bracketing independence

For every finite input word, every binary bracketing of iterated
\(\mathsf P\) gives the same final probability measure.

#### Proof

For any fixed bracketing, intermediate \(d\)-factors telescope. The resulting
measure on final output \(z\) is the corresponding iterated
\(\mathsf M\)-measure multiplied by

\[
 \frac{d(z)}{\prod_i d(a_i)}.
\]

Definition 13 makes the iterated \(\mathsf M\)-measure finite and independent
of bracketing. Integration against the nonnegative measurable final factor
therefore gives equal probability measures. \(\square\)

### Corollary 1 -- finite Frobenius--Perron branch

For a finite indecomposable based fusion ring with independently physical
integer multiplicities, the unique normalized positive
Frobenius--Perron character solves (1). Each resolved slot
\((c,\alpha)\), \(1\leq\alpha\leq N_{ab}^{c}\), has probability

\[
 \mathsf P(c,\alpha\mid a,b)=\frac{d_c}{d_ad_b}.
\]

The target-sector probability is
\(N_{ab}^{c}d_c/(d_ad_b)\). This branch is conditional on deriving the
finite fusion data and resolved counting measure.

### Theorem 5 -- measure/character gauge

Let \(h:\mathsf S\to\mathbb R_{>0}\) be measurable with
\(h(\mathbf1)=1\). Suppose every transformed finite-word convolution below
is finite. Define

\[
 \mathsf M^h_{a,b}(dc)
 =\frac{h(a)h(b)}{h(c)}\mathsf M_{a,b}(dc),
 \qquad
 d^h(c)=h(c)d(c).
\]

Then \(\mathsf M^h\) is a finite positive measurable convolution,
\(d^h\) is its positive character, and its induced kernel is exactly
\(\mathsf P\).

#### Proof

Every intermediate \(h\)-factor cancels. An n-input word acquires only the
external factor \(\prod_i h(a_i)/h(z)\), independent of bracketing. The
assumed finiteness gives Definition 13. Direct substitution gives the
character equation and exact cancellation in (2). \(\square\)

The theorem demonstrates one representational gauge. It is not claimed to
classify refinements, measurable isomorphisms, disintegrations, or every
possible representation degeneracy.

## 8. Exact projectivity through deletion

### Definition 15 -- deletion Markov system

Let \(\mathsf U_n\) be the point-free unmarked class space at size \(n\).
An exact deletion system is a family of Markov kernels

\[
 K_{n,m}:\mathsf U_n\rightsquigarrow\mathsf U_m,
 \qquad n\geq m,
\]

satisfying identity and Chapman--Kolmogorov equations

\[
 K_{n,n}=\mathrm{id},
 \qquad
 K_{n,m}=K_{n,k}K_{k,m}.
\]

A family of sector laws \(P_n\) is projective exactly when

\[
 P_nK_{n,m}=P_m
\tag{3}
\]

for every admitted \(n\geq m\). Equation (3), not an untyped weight formula,
is primary.

### Definition 16 -- uniform one-occurrence deletion

For finite occurrence classes, let

\[
 m(x,y)
 =\#\{v\in\operatorname{Occ}(x):[x-v]=y\}.
\]

Uniform deletion is

\[
 K_{n+1,n}(x,y)=\frac{m(x,y)}{n+1}.
\tag{4}
\]

The multiplicity counts physical occurrences, including
automorphism-exchanged occurrences. It satisfies
\(\sum_y m(x,y)=n+1\).

If \(P_n(y)=w_n(y)/Z_n\) relative to equal orbit base and \(w_n(y)>0\),
equation (3) is equivalent to

\[
 \sum_x\frac{m(x,y)}{n+1}
 \frac{w_{n+1}(x)}{w_n(y)}
 =\frac{Z_{n+1}}{Z_n}.
\tag{5}
\]

If instead \(w_n(y)=u_n(y)/a_y\) with
\(a_y=|\operatorname{Aut}y|\), the ratio in (5) is replaced by

\[
 \frac{a_y}{a_x}\frac{u_{n+1}(x)}{u_n(y)}.
\tag{6}
\]

Labeled-object bases have their own factorial convention and must be derived
separately. Raw multiplicity \(m\), normalized deletion \(K\), orbit weight,
and groupoid weight may not share one undefined symbol.

### Proposition 2 -- projectivity need not select a unique law

For binary-color count classes,

\[
 P_n^{(p)}(k)=\binom nkp^k(1-p)^{n-k}
\]

is projective under uniform deletion for every \(p\in[0,1]\). Mixtures over
\(p\) are also projective. Exact projectivity constrains a family but need
not select its parameter or boundary state.

### Theorem 7 -- occurrence propensity is not fixed by a conditional channel law

Let \(\mathsf P_{a,b}\) be any fixed conditional output law. For every

Any two distinct strictly positive \(q_1,q_2\) give the same output law
conditional on composition and different composition-occurrence
propensities. Hence the conditional channel law does not determine whether
composition occurs.

At \(q=0\), the conditioning event is null and a displayed conditional
kernel is additional counterfactual structure not identified by the whole
