# Structural occurrence characters and the missing selector primitive

## A Paper 17E physics-first census

### Abstract

Paper 13D defines a point-free conditional history law on a selected typed
execution diagram. Papers 17B--17D showed that this law does not determine the
relative occurrence weights of different unmarked process complexes. This
paper performs the next exact construction requested by Paper 17D.

First, it separates two mathematical objects that must not be conflated. The
presentation groupoid removes labels from one process complex. A physical
replacement graph instead compares two whole complexes obtained by placing
parallel subdiagrams in the same complete typed exterior. Its path-groupoid
completion is only bookkeeping for occurrence odds; it is not a new temporal
dynamics or a claim that the replacements are executable interventions.

Second, on every bounded Paper 13D sector we construct the exact
generator-local occurrence-character quotient. If \(G\) is the finite set of
unmarked generator orbits, \(L\) is the lattice generated by accepted
equalities of parallel diagrams, and \(d\) is the boundary coboundary map,
then the locally observable logarithmic activity space is

\[
 \mathcal X_{N,M}
 =L^\perp/\operatorname{im}d.
\]

It is nontrivial. Three independent parallel-diagram contrasts survive:
primitive \(U\) versus queried composition \(D\circ Q^0\), and simultaneous
fusion versus staged binary fusion independently at arities three and four.
Thus fusion activity is not an isolated scalar accident: its arity profile and
the primitive/staged activity are already independent physical occurrence
constants. This census is exact for generator-local characters. Without a
locality-range axiom, contextual motif weights form an additional, larger
freedom and must not be silently declared absent.

Third, we prove a structural-descent no-go theorem. For every fixed Paper 13D
experiment \(e\), forgetting the values in any history \(H\) always returns
the same unmarked execution complex \(\chi(e)\). Hence

\[
 (u_e)_*\Gamma_D(e)=\delta_{[\chi(e)]}.
\]

No existing atomic outcome, tensor outcome, fusion bond, stable-future value,
eraser result, response contrast, or physical-history quotient supplies a
branch between distinct process complexes. The separately declared
cardinality law \(2^{-(n+1)}\) fixes only the size marginal of the restricted
empty-program primitive family and leaves structural activities free at each
size.

Finally, we define the smallest honest new selector object: a point-free
whole-law coproduct whose histories are a tagged disjoint union of child
history spaces and whose realized history contains only the selected child.
This avoids dormant-memory growth and a hidden birth order. But an exact
equivalence theorem shows that a selector with child-law recovery is precisely
the missing probability vector in another form. Symmetry fixes equal weights
only when a genuine physical automorphism exchanges the complete alternatives;
it cannot equate nonisomorphic tensor and fusion complexes. The construction
therefore identifies the right new law without pretending to derive its
couplings.

## 2. Three different relational objects

### 2.1 The presentation groupoid

For a presented unmarked process complex \(\chi\), let
\(\mathcal G_{\rm pres}\) contain its accepted finite-set relabelings,
component permutations, and typed presentation maps. Its orbit \([\chi]\)
is the physical point-free complex.

Arrows of \(\mathcal G_{\rm pres}\) assert sameness of physical referent. They
must preserve:

- every generator occurrence with multiplicity;
- the complete typed incidence diagram;
- tensor versus fusion structure;
- simultaneous versus staged operations; and
- the external typed boundary.

The presentation groupoid cannot delete a fusion, replace a primitive by a
queried composite, or turn a stable future into an identity.

### 2.2 The bounded unmarked sector

Fix positive integers \(N\) and \(M\), and fix a finite set of admitted
external boundary-object orbits. Let
\(\mathsf C_{N,M}\) be the set of point-free Paper 13D process complexes
whose occurrence carriers have size at most \(N\), whose diagrams have at
most \(M\) nonidentity physical generator vertices, and whose external
boundaries lie in the fixed finite set.

External preparation, intervention, and reader marks are forgotten. Their
values do not create distinct autonomous process activities. The unmarked
diagram retains the kind and typed position of every physical generator and
the physical slots on which a compatible mark could act.

For fixed \(N,M\), this sector has finitely many generator, boundary, and
diagram orbits. Boundedness is used only to make the census finite. It is not
a physical cutoff and earns no infinite-volume law.

### 2.3 Typed replacement contexts

A one-hole unmarked context \(C[-]\) is a point-free typed string diagram
whose hole has a fixed interface

\[
 S\longrightarrow T.
\]

For parallel unmarked subdiagrams \(x,y:S\to T\), both \(C[x]\) and \(C[y]\)
have the same complete exterior boundary. A **replacement cell** is the
presentation orbit

\[
 [C;x,y].
\]

The bounded replacement graph \(\mathcal R_{N,M}\) has the physical classes
\([C[x]]\) as vertices and a pair of formally opposed edges for every
admitted replacement cell.

The reverse edge exists so a probability ratio can be inverted. It does not
assert that an agent can physically transform \(C[x]\) into \(C[y]\), or that
such a transformation occurs later in time.

### 2.4 The replacement path groupoid is bookkeeping

Formally adjoining concatenation and cancellation of replacement paths gives
the path groupoid \(\Pi_1(\mathcal R_{N,M})\). A positive local-odds assignment
is a multiplicative map

\[
 r:\Pi_1(\mathcal R_{N,M})\to\mathbb R_{>0}.
\]

This groupoid transports ratios in structural comparison space. It is not a
subcategory of \(\mathsf{Exec}_D\), not a future grammar, and not an emergent
clock.

## 3. Exact generator-local character census

### 3.1 Generator and object orbits

Let \(G_{N}\) be the finite set of unmarked physical generator orbits that
occur in \(\mathsf C_{N,M}\), after presentation covariance and external-mark
forgetting. It includes the admitted orbits of:

- primitive \(U\);
- query \(Q^0\) and continuation \(D\);
- recorded query \(Q^r\) and continuation \(R_c\);
- simultaneous fusion, indexed by its physical sort and unordered component
  family;
- stable entry \(\iota\);
- nonidentity stable-future maps, modulo their accepted exact relations; and
- executable erasure \(E_r\).

Identity and symmetric-coherence arrows are not physical activity generators.
They receive weight one.

Let \(O_N\) be the finite set of boundary-object orbits. For
\(g\in G_N\), write \(s(g),t(g)\in O_N\), and let

\[
 \partial:\mathbb Z^{G_N}\to\mathbb Z^{O_N},
 \qquad
 \partial e_g=e_{t(g)}-e_{s(g)}
\]

be the boundary incidence map.

### 3.2 Count vectors and relation lattice

Every unmarked diagram \(f\) has a generator-count vector

\[
 n(f)\in\mathbb N^{G_N}.
\]

For every accepted exact equality \(f=f'\) of parallel diagrams, include

\[
 n(f)-n(f')
\]

in the relation lattice \(L_N\subseteq\mathbb Z^{G_N}\).

Symmetric-monoidal associativity, unit laws, braiding coherence, finite-set
covariance, and permutation of one simultaneous fusion's input family do not
change physical generator multiplicities. They therefore contribute the zero
count relation. Any extensional relation internal to the stable-future
endomorphism monoid is retained in \(L_N\) rather than ignored.

Because every accepted equality is parallel,

\[
 L_N\subseteq\ker\partial.
\]

### 3.3 Logarithmic activities

A generator-local positive character has the form

\[
 \rho_\alpha(f)=\exp\!\bigl(\alpha\cdot n(f)\bigr),
 \qquad \alpha\in\mathbb R^{G_N}.
\]

It descends to accepted physical diagrams exactly when

\[
 \alpha\cdot\ell=0
 \quad\text{for every }\ell\in L_N.
\]

Thus the pre-boundary character space is \(L_N^\perp\).

### 3.4 Boundary coboundaries

For a boundary potential \(\beta:O_N\to\mathbb R\), define

\[
 (d\beta)_g=\beta(t(g))-\beta(s(g)).
\]

Then every diagram \(f:S\to T\) satisfies

\[
 (d\beta)\cdot n(f)=\beta(T)-\beta(S).
\]

Hence \(d\beta\) multiplies every diagram with the same complete exterior by
the same factor. No fixed-exterior replacement odds can observe it. Since
relations are parallel, \(\operatorname{im}d\subseteq L_N^\perp\).

### Theorem 1 -- generator-local occurrence-character quotient

The exact space of generator-local logarithmic activities observable by
fixed-exterior replacements in the bounded sector is

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal X_{N,M}^{\rm gen}
 =L_N^\perp/\operatorname{im}d .
 }
\]

Its dimension is

\[
 |G_N|-\operatorname{rank}L_N-\operatorname{rank}d.
\]

#### Proof

Multiplicativity under tensor and composition makes logarithms additive, so a
generator-local character is linear in the count vector. It respects every
accepted diagram equality exactly when it annihilates \(L_N\). Two such
activities give identical ratios on every pair of parallel diagrams exactly
when their difference is a boundary coboundary on each connected
object-generator component. Quotienting gives the displayed space. The rank
formula follows because \(\operatorname{im}d\subseteq L_N^\perp\). \(\square\)

### 3.5 Three explicit surviving directions

The quotient is not merely abstract. It contains at least the following
fixed-exterior contrasts whenever the indicated carriers are admitted.

#### A. Primitive versus queried composition

Paper 13D explicitly keeps

\[
 U_J:B_0(I)\to B_2^0(I)
\]

different from

\[
 D\circ Q_J^0:B_0(I)\to B_2^0(I).
\]

Their count-vector difference is

\[
 e_D+e_{Q^0}-e_U.
\]

It is boundary-neutral and is not an accepted relation. Its activity measures
the relative occurrence propensity of an indivisible primitive and a
physically staged queried process with the same exterior sorts.

#### B. Ternary simultaneous versus staged fusion

For three compatible nonempty components, compare one simultaneous ternary
fusion

\[
 \Phi_3:
 B_s(I_1)\boxtimes B_s(I_2)\boxtimes B_s(I_3)
 \longrightarrow B_s(I_1\sqcup I_2\sqcup I_3)
\]

with two staged binary fusions having the same source and target. Paper 13D
explicitly retains the additional traversed boundary in the staged process.
The count contrast is

\[
 2e_{\Phi_2}-e_{\Phi_3}.
\]

Consequently total fusion number \(N_\Phi\), and more finely the relative
activities of fusion arities, are observable and not boundary coboundaries.

#### C. Quaternary simultaneous versus staged fusion

For four compatible nonempty components, compare one simultaneous
quaternary fusion with any fixed three-step binary fusion tree having the same
source and target. The count contrast is

\[
 3e_{\Phi_2}-e_{\Phi_4}.
\]

This is linearly independent of both the primitive/staged contrast and the
ternary fusion contrast because it has a unique \(\Phi_4\) coordinate. The
argument extends to every admitted arity.

These three count contrasts are linearly independent. No current conditional
probability equation fixes their occurrence odds.

Stable-future activities are deliberately not added to this lower bound. The
exact quotient already handles them through \(L_N\): if the accepted future
monoid equates a finite word with the identity, that relation constrains or
may trivialize its positive character. No presentation of the future monoid
is silently replaced by free syntax here.

### Corollary 1 -- fusion freedom is not isolated

The fusion parameter \(\lambda\) found by Paper 17B is one projection of a
larger occurrence-character space. Fixing one total fusion activity alone
would leave the primitive/staged activity, relative fusion-arity activities,

### 3.6 What this census does not prove

Theorem 1 is complete only under the generator-local ansatz. A general local
specification may depend on a bounded point-free neighbourhood of the replaced
cell. Examples include adjacency of two fusion vertices, whether a fusion is
followed by an eraser, or a typed incidence motif crossing the replacement
boundary.

For a declared locality radius \(k\), the bounded sector contains finitely
many rooted motif orbits. Their indicator vectors can be appended to the
generator-count matrix, and the same relation/coboundary quotient computes
the exact radius-\(k\) character space. Without declaring \(k\), there is no
finite theorem saying that generator counts exhaust all physical weights.

This is a physics choice, not a computational inconvenience. Locality itself
must be stated before its coupling census can be complete.

## 4. Exact replacement-odds reconstruction

### 4.1 Edge log odds

Assign each oriented physical replacement edge \(e:x\to y\) a real number

\[
 \omega(e)=\log r(y\leftarrow x).
\]

Reciprocity is \(\omega(\bar e)=-\omega(e)\). Cycle integrability is

\[
 \sum_{e\in c}\omega(e)=0
\]

for every closed replacement path \(c\).

### Theorem 2 -- exactness of finite local odds

On a finite replacement graph, reciprocal local odds arise from a positive
global occurrence weight if and only if their log odds have zero sum around
every cycle. The reconstructed log weight is unique up to one additive
constant on each connected component.

#### Proof

This is the exactness criterion for a real one-cochain on a graph. Choose a
root in each component and integrate \(\omega\) along a path. Zero cycle sums
make the result path-independent. Exponentiation gives a positive weight.
Any two potentials with the same edge gradient differ by a componentwise
constant. \(\square\)

### 4.2 Full bounded freedom

If no locality, spectator, or implementation-equivalence constraint is
imposed, an arbitrary positive weight on the vertices is admissible. For a
replacement graph with \(|V|\) vertices and \(c\) connected components, its
integrable edge-log-odds space has dimension

\[
 |V|-c.
\]

Generator and motif ansatzes select structured subspaces of this full
potential space. They do not follow from graph integrability itself.

### 4.3 Spectator and implementation equations

A physical selector must impose additional equalities.

1. **Spectator independence.** For a disconnected complex \(z\), the edges
   \(x\leftrightarrow y\) and
   \(x\boxtimes z\leftrightarrow y\boxtimes z\) have equal odds unless a
   declared interaction crosses the exterior.
2. **Implementation independence.** Two replacement cells that descend to
   the same complete physical local alternative have equal odds after exact
   transport.
3. **Restriction consistency.** Deleting an unobserved occurrence or
   restricting a finite exterior commutes with the local specification when
   the deleted data are outside its declared dependence neighbourhood.

These are linear equations on log odds. They reduce the admissible space but
do not generally set it to one point.

For every admitted Paper 13D experiment \(e\), the map \(u_e\) is constant:

\[
 u_e(H)=\chi(e)
 \quad\text{for every }H\in\mathsf{Hist}(e).
\]

It therefore descends through the physical history quotient and satisfies

\[
 \boxed{
 (u_e)_*\Gamma_D(e)=\delta_{[\chi(e)]}.
 }
\]

#### Proof

Paper 13D defines histories recursively from the already chosen syntax of
\(e\).

- An atomic history varies source and target values of one fixed generator.
- A composite history retains values along one fixed composite syntax tree.
- A tensor history varies factor histories inside one fixed tensor family.

  retains the eraser generator in the execution trace.

No recursion clause adds, deletes, or substitutes a generator according to a
stochastic outcome. The physical quotient uses the stabilizer of the same
experiment and therefore cannot join histories belonging to a different
execution diagram. Hence \(u_e\) is constant before and after quotienting.
\(\square\)

## 6. The smallest honest selector extension

### 6.1 A whole-law coproduct, not a future generator

Let \(x_1,\ldots,x_k\) be distinct point-free child complexes compatible with
one complete exterior specification \(B\). Introduce a new whole-law selector
object

\[
 \Sigma_B\langle x_1,\ldots,x_k\rangle.
\]

It is not an arrow \(x_i\to x_j\), not a Markov birth step, and not a member
of \(\mathsf{Fut}_{\rm stable}\). Its history space is the tagged disjoint
union

\[
 \mathsf H(\Sigma_B)
 =\bigsqcup_{j=1}^k\{j\}\times\mathsf H(x_j).
\]

A realized history \((j,H)\) contains the selected child history and a
distinguishable structural tag \(j\) at the selector boundary. Whether that
tag becomes a stable record under a licensed future grammar is a separate
theorem. The realized history contains no boundary values, seeds, records, or
inactive copy of any unselected child.

### 6.2 Exact child recovery

For a positive probability vector \(p=(p_1,\ldots,p_k)\), define

\[
 \Gamma_\Sigma(j,H)=p_j\Gamma_{x_j}(H).
\]

Then

\[
 P_\Sigma(S=j)=p_j,
 \qquad
 \Gamma_\Sigma(H\mid S=j)=\Gamma_{x_j}(H).
\]

This preserves every accepted conditional child law exactly.

### Theorem 4 -- selector equivalence theorem

For fixed child laws with disjoint tagged structural sectors, every normalized
selector law satisfying exact child recovery is uniquely of the displayed
form for one probability vector \(p\). Conversely, every probability vector
defines such a selector law.

#### Proof

Marginalize any selector law over each child history fibre to obtain \(p_j\).
Exact child recovery gives
\(\Gamma_\Sigma(j,H)=p_j\Gamma_{x_j}(H)\). Normalization makes \(p\) a
probability vector. The converse is immediate. \(\square\)

### Corollary 3 -- a selector slot does not select its odds

Adding a tagged coproduct solves the **typing** problem of structural
alternatives. It does not solve the **dynamics** problem of their relative
weights. The vector \(p\), or equivalent local odds, is precisely the missing
physical datum.

This prevents a new round of formalism from being mistaken for new physics.

### 6.3 No dormant-memory criterion

The selector is genuine only if branch descent satisfies all of the following.

1. The realized carrier is the selected child carrier, not a product of all
   child carriers plus an enable bit.
2. Unselected child seeds and records are absent, not hidden or ignored.
3. Child restriction reproduces the full accepted history law, not merely a
   chosen reader marginal.
4. Refining a child implementation without changing its physical complex does
   not change the branch odds.
5. The structural tag is readable and stable under a declared future grammar,
   while an executable eraser control lies outside that grammar.

### 6.4 Symmetry can fix only genuine symmetry orbits

Suppose an exact physical automorphism of the complete selector object
permutes child sectors and preserves its exterior, law, and structural
reader. Invariance forces equal probabilities within each orbit of that
permutation action.

For two exchanged sectors, this gives \(p_1=p_2=1/2\).

But a tensor antichain and a fusion star are nonisomorphic physical complexes:
their incidence, target typing, and generator content differ. No presentation
automorphism exchanges them. Symmetry therefore supplies no reason to set
their odds equal.

### 6.5 Nested selectors and cycle equations

If the same alternatives can be assembled through different nested selector
constructions, implementation independence requires the resulting branch
ratios to agree. For local replacements these are exactly the reciprocal and
cycle equations of Theorem 2.

Thus a coherent family of selector coproducts can reconstruct a global
\(\Pi_{\rm phys}\), but only after its primitive edge odds and disconnected
root weights are supplied or derived.
