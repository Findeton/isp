## 2. Decisive issue

Version 4 correctly constructs a measurable kernel on a varying
resolved-channel bundle. Its event/integration lemma, target pushforward,
normalization, and gauge theorem all survive review.

The failed clause is its stronger assertion that every coarse sigma algebra
must either expose the global channel-index event or remove pointwise
branching. A strict coarse total sigma algebra can:

- induce a full discrete sigma algebra on every two-point fiber;
- admit point-local total events separating both channel objects;
- make every admitted event probability measurable in the input;
- retain positive pointwise branching at every input; and
- omit a uniform cross-input event called “channel 0.”

A non-Borel weight attached to that omitted external label is not a
nonmeasurable physical probability. It is not an event of the declared
measurable theory. Therefore the coarse model is a legitimate measurable
bundle unless a globally coherent channel descriptor was independently
declared physical before the measure was chosen.

The version-4 protocol incorrectly required rejection of every such coarse
variant, and version-4 Section 4.1 incorrectly said coarsening necessarily
removes all resolved branching. This is a scope defect in the intended
control, not a failure of the measurable-kernel theorem.

## 3. Physical and mathematical resolution required

A successor must distinguish:

1. **pointwise resolved branching:** two positive measurable channel events
   in one input fiber;
2. **measurable family branching:** one or more total measurable events that
   track channel properties across inputs; and
3. **externally imposed labels:** set-theoretic identifications across fibers
   absent from the declared physical sigma algebra.

The total bundle, its sigma algebra, and every claimed physical channel
descriptor must be frozen before the channel kernel. A predeclared
descriptor cannot be removed after seeing its nonmeasurable probability.
Conversely, an external label absent from the frozen observable algebra
cannot be used after the fact to declare a valid kernel nonmeasurable.

No global trivialization or uniform channel numbering should be imposed in
general. Such a requirement would wrongly exclude nontrivial measurable
bundles, continuous channel families, and channels whose only invariant
identity is local.
