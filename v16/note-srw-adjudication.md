## 4. Why “bundle weld” fails

`RewriteSpan` is a labelled before/after graph pair with an internal-vertex
correspondence, created vertices, and an allowed matrix-entry relation. It has
no interface/match object, edge or port persistence morphism, gluing rule,
composition operation, associator, order record, or fusion law. The four
objectwise `FiberSpec` recipes likewise provide dimensions after a sector is
chosen, not a functor on rewrite identities and composites.

The exact overlap counterfixture does not prove that nature requires
commuting events. It proves that SRW's local gates permit multiple inequivalent
completions: chosen order, recorded order, associator/order holonomy, or fused
joint event. Since no completion is supplied, two locally valid typed arrows
do not define one successor.

The reciprocal statement inherits this limit. A left inverse of one isometry
is an exact matrix lemma; it cannot promote an uncomposed pair into a
“reciprocal weld.”

## 5. Geometry and backreaction ruling

The target-sector dimension and transition support do operational work in the
chosen representation. The target graph's **spatial adjacency** does not. A
minimal typed circuit retaining input/output wires, allowed support, transport
entries, and named output effects reproduces every registered future
probability after deleting the internal spatial edges. The graph was used to
name an effect, not to generate an otherwise unavailable dynamical response.

This is a class-relative eliminability result for the registered fixture. It
does not prove that all finite relational geometry is eliminable. It proves
that SRW has not yet made adjacency irreducible.

Nor is there backreaction. The process state and coupling angle never select a
different rewrite, and no invariant property of the output adjacency alters a
later transport beyond a compilable named effect. SRW supplies an architecture
slot for relational change plus process transport, not the two-way chain

```text

```
