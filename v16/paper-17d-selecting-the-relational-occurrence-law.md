# Selecting the relational occurrence law

## A physics-first investigation of \(\Pi_{\rm phys}\)

### Abstract

Paper 13D supplies a complete typed indivisible stochastic law conditional on
one selected process complex. Papers 17B and 17C proved that this conditional
law does not determine how often different unmarked process complexes occur.
Every compatible autonomous extension has the form

\[
 \widehat\Gamma(\chi,H\mid a)
 =\Pi_{\rm phys}(\chi\mid a)\,\Gamma_D(H\mid\chi,a),
\]

and multiplication by an arbitrary positive point-free function of \(\chi\)
leaves all accepted conditional predictions unchanged. The missing object is
the occurrence law \(\Pi_{\rm phys}\).

This paper asks what could determine it without importing a hidden time,
background geometry, preferred dimension, or post-hoc continuum target. We
prove three structural results. First, normalization of \(\Gamma_D\) contains
no occurrence evidence: its total likelihood is one in every selected
complex. Second, the remaining multiplicative freedom is a positive-character
space; accepted composition laws fix \(\Pi_{\rm phys}\) only if that character
space is trivial. It is not trivial in the present theory because fusion count
defines an explicit character. Third, positive local replacement odds define
a unique finite occurrence law precisely when they are reciprocal,
cycle-integrable, and connect the physical replacement graph. This identifies
the smallest missing physical datum more sharply than a guessed global action.

The closest route internal to the present ontology is an endogenous structural
selector: one larger point-free experiment would have to produce alternative
process structures as recorded outcomes, and its branch law would have to
descend to implementation-independent local replacement odds. Paper 13D does
not yet do this; its execution diagram is fixed before \(\Gamma_D\) is
evaluated. The best noncircular alternatives are an independently justified
local specification with a uniqueness theorem, cancellation of all residual
characters by exact consistency relations, or preregistered microscopic
calibration. Maximum entropy, decoherence, quantum dilation, covariance,
exchangeability, criticality, no-boundary cosmology, and a preferred
four-dimensional output do not select the law by themselves.

The result is therefore constructive but not a selection claim:

## 2. Exact location of the missing physics

### 2.1 Disintegration

Every compatible positive joint law has, on positive-mass classes, the form

\[
 \widehat\Gamma([\chi],[H]\mid[a])
 =\Pi([\chi]\mid[a])\,
  \Gamma_D([H]\mid[\chi],[a]).
\]

For an exogenous protocol, \([a]\) may restrict the support to complexes in
which its typed addresses exist, but it may not alter ratios inside that
support. Thus

\[
 \Pi([\chi]\mid[a])
 =\frac{
  \mathbf 1_{[a]\in\mathsf A([\chi])}\Pi_{\rm phys}([\chi])
 }{
  \sum_{[\chi']}
  \mathbf 1_{[a]\in\mathsf A([\chi'])}\Pi_{\rm phys}([\chi'])
 }.
\]

The missing nomological or cosmological datum is exactly
\(\Pi_{\rm phys}\), equivalently

\[
 S_{\rm proc}([\chi])
 =-\log\Pi_{\rm phys}([\chi])+\text{constant}
\]

on positive support.

### 2.2 Theorem 1 -- normalized \(\Gamma_D\) has no occurrence evidence

For every admitted selected complex and protocol,

\[
 Z_\Gamma([\chi],[a])
 :=\sum_{[H]}\Gamma_D([H]\mid[\chi],[a])=1.
\]

Therefore the rule

\[
 \Pi([\chi])\propto Z_\Gamma([\chi])
\]

assigns the same unnormalized number to every admitted complex. It cannot
produce the fusion/antichain ratio, and on an infinite full-support execution
space it is generally not normalizable.

#### Proof

The displayed equality is the normalization axiom for each conditional
history law. No cross-complex comparison occurs in that axiom. \(\square\)

### 2.3 Consequences

The following attempts cannot extract a neutral scalar occurrence propensity
from the accepted conditional law.

- Summing conditional probabilities gives one.
- Averaging them gives a base-measure-dependent number.
- Counting private seeds promotes an implementation representation to
  ontology and is not invariant under refinement.
- Conditional entropy, response magnitude, record production, or stability is
  a new selector functional, not a consequence of normalization.
- The matrix \(R\) fixes relative structure inside its selected primitive but
  not the overall activity or phase of a different process complex.
- Multiplying a whole conditional amplitude sector by a nonzero complex
  number leaves normalized predictions inside that sector unchanged.

This is why the issue is physics rather than missing algebra.

## 3. The positive-character obstruction

### 3.1 Ratios of admissible weights

Fix one typed boundary sector and let \(W_0\) and \(W_1\) be two strictly
positive occurrence weights obeying the same point-free tensor and certified
gluing rules. Define

\[
 R(\chi)=\frac{W_1(\chi)}{W_0(\chi)}.
\]

Whenever independent tensor or certified composition is defined and both
weights have the same boundary factor,

\[
 R(\chi\otimes\psi)=R(\chi)R(\psi),
\]

and analogously for the certified gluing operation. Thus \(R\) is a positive
character of the physical process-composition structure. Taking logarithms,

\[
 L(\chi):=\log R(\chi)
\]

is additive.

### 3.2 Theorem 2 -- character-space criterion

Composition, covariance, and conditional recovery select a unique positive
occurrence law, up to normalization in each disconnected support sector, only
if every positive point-free character compatible with the accepted physical
relations is trivial.

#### Proof

If \(W_0\) and \(W_1\) are two admissible weights, their ratio is a compatible
positive character. If all such characters are trivial, the two weights are
proportional and normalization makes them equal. Conversely, any nontrivial
compatible character \(R\) turns one admissible weight \(W_0\) into the
distinct admissible weight \(RW_0\) wherever the result is normalizable.
\(\square\)

### 3.3 Current theory fails the uniqueness criterion

Let \(N_\Phi(\chi)\) count physical fusion occurrences. It is point-free and
additive under the accepted tensor and composition rules. For every
\(\lambda>0\),

\[
 R_\lambda(\chi)=\lambda^{N_\Phi(\chi)}
\]

is a nontrivial positive character. Therefore the current character space is
not trivial. The antichain/star family is its smallest visible orbit:

\[
 \frac{\Pi_\lambda(\chi_n^\star)}
      {\Pi_\lambda(\chi_n^\otimes)}=\lambda.
\]

This formulation generalizes the one-parameter counterexample. Every
independent additive process invariant supplies a possible logarithmic
activity unless a new relation, conservation theorem, or observation fixes
it.

### 3.4 What exact consistency could still accomplish

Suppose accepted equivalences yield relations among generator counts,

\[
 \sum_\gamma A_{j\gamma}N_\gamma=0.
\]

For an exponential character

\[
 R(\chi)=\exp\!\left(\sum_\gamma
 \alpha_\gamma N_\gamma(\chi)\right),
\]

each physical relation imposes

\[
 \sum_\gamma A_{j\gamma}\alpha_\gamma=0.
\]

If the relation matrix has full rank after removing normalization and genuine
boundary terms, all activities are fixed. If it has a null space, that null
space is precisely the residual selector freedom. This provides a concrete
test for anomaly- or coherence-based selection. At present the fusion
direction survives.

## 4. Local odds instead of a guessed global action

### 4.1 The physical replacement graph

For each complete typed exterior boundary \(B\), define a graph
\(\mathcal R_B\):

- vertices are physical process classes compatible with \(B\);
- an edge \(x\leftrightarrow y\) means that one certified bounded region is
  replaced while the complete exterior and its typed attachment are held
  fixed; and
- edges are quotient objects, not addresses in a serialization.

A local occurrence specification assigns a positive directed ratio

\[
 \mathfrak r_B(y\leftarrow x)>0.
\]

The intended meaning is

\[
 \mathfrak r_B(y\leftarrow x)
 =\frac{\Pi_{\rm phys}(y\mid B)}
        {\Pi_{\rm phys}(x\mid B)}.
\]

### 4.2 Theorem 3 -- finite local-odds reconstruction

Let \(\mathcal R_B\) be finite. A positive local specification reconstructs a
global weight on each connected component if and only if:

1. **reciprocity**
   \[
   \mathfrak r_B(x\leftarrow y)
   =\mathfrak r_B(y\leftarrow x)^{-1};
   \]
2. **cycle integrability** for every closed replacement cycle
   \(x_0\to x_1\to\cdots\to x_k=x_0\),
   \[
   \prod_{i=0}^{k-1}
   \mathfrak r_B(x_{i+1}\leftarrow x_i)=1.
   \]

On a connected replacement graph the normalized distribution is unique.
On a disconnected graph one independent positive constant remains for every
connected component before normalization.

#### Proof

Choose a reference vertex \(x_0\) in one connected component and define

\[
 W(x)=\prod_{e\in p:x_0\leadsto x}\mathfrak r(e)
\]

along any path \(p\). Reciprocity and the cycle condition make this product
independent of the chosen path. Every specified edge ratio is then recovered.
Any second solution has constant ratio to \(W\) on the component. Normalizing
fixes that constant when the component has finite total weight. Conversely,
ratios derived from any global positive weight satisfy reciprocity and have
unit product around every cycle. \(\square\)

### 4.3 Why this is the smallest useful missing object

A global table \(\Pi_{\rm phys}(\chi)\) hides the physical source of its
numbers. Local odds expose it. They let the theory ask separately:

- what makes a fusion occurrence more or less probable than its absence;
- whether the answer depends on a remote spectator;
- whether different constructions of the same replacement agree;
- whether all cycles integrate to one law; and
- whether finite laws extend consistently to unbounded process spaces.

For context-independent generator insertion,

\[
 \mathfrak r(\text{insert }\gamma)=\lambda_\gamma,
\]

so Paper 17B's \(\lambda\) is exactly the first local odds parameter.

This construction is local in **structural replacement space**, not Markovian
in time. The complete exterior can include all law-relevant relational
context, and no intermediate happening is promoted to a restartable state.
The proposal therefore preserves native nondivision unless an independent
complete-frontier theorem licenses a temporal cut.

### 4.4 Infinite-volume warning

Finite cycle integrability does not guarantee one infinite-volume law. A
projective or Dobrushin--Lanford--Ruelle-style specification may possess:

- no global measure;
- one global measure; or
- several phases compatible with identical local conditional rules.

Therefore a successor must prove existence and either uniqueness or an honest
phase/boundary-condition family. Locality alone is not selection.

## 5. The most theory-native route: endogenous structural selection

### 5.1 The key idea

The current \(\Gamma_D\) is evaluated only after a process complex has been
chosen. To make it select process occurrence, the theory would need one larger
physical experiment \(\Sigma\) whose possible recorded outcomes genuinely
have different process structures.

For two local alternatives \(x\) and \(y\), suppose there were stable outcome
sectors \(s=0,1\) and point-free branch maps

\[
 d_s:\{H\in\mathsf H(\Sigma):S(H)=s\}
 \longrightarrow \mathsf H(\chi_s),
 \qquad \chi_0=x,\quad\chi_1=y.
\]

One could try to define

\[
 \mathfrak r(y\leftarrow x)
 =\frac{\Pr_\Sigma(S=1)}{\Pr_\Sigma(S=0)}.
\]

This would turn a conditional branch probability inside one accepted whole
law into an occurrence ratio between structures.

### 5.2 Why Paper 13D does not yet provide it

Paper 13D lets outcomes change boundary values inside a fixed typed execution
diagram. Fusion may create a new recorded bond value, but the fusion generator
itself was already part of the selected diagram. No admitted outcome says
"this generator occurrence exists" versus "this occurrence does not exist."

Thus the present law distinguishes:

\[
 \text{outcome of a selected operation}
 \quad\text{from}\quad
 \text{occurrence of the operation itself}.
\]

The first is supplied; the second is the missing law.

### 5.3 Exact internal-selector descent contract

An endogenous selector would have to satisfy all of the following.

1. **Common carrier.** Both structural alternatives are outcomes in one
   declared physical configuration space, not two externally selected runs.
2. **Genuine structural descent.** The branch map deletes no dormant physical
   machinery that remains law-relevant; a disabled fixed-memory branch is not
   varying process size.
3. **Stable branch record.** A complete reader distinguishes the structural
   alternatives under the declared future grammar.
4. **Exact child recovery.** Conditioned branch histories reproduce the
   accepted history law of the corresponding child complex.
5. **Implementation independence.** Two selector mechanisms realizing the
   same physical replacement give the same local odds.
6. **Spectator independence.** A disconnected remote spectator does not alter
   the local ratio except through a separately declared interaction.
7. **Cycle integrability.** Odds from different sequences of local structural
   choices reconstruct one global law.
8. **No external setting substitution.** A laboratory coin or pre-existing
   program label cannot masquerade as the autonomous selector.
9. **No hidden birth time.** The selector is a whole-law structural
   alternative, not a Markov growth step indexed by code order.
10. **Projective closure.** Finite branch laws restrict consistently and admit
    the required physical limit.

### 5.4 Root-state residue

Even a successful endogenous selector may determine only ratios among
structures reachable from a common parent. If the replacement graph has
several components, or if all constructions start from a distinguished unit
or null boundary, the relative weights of those roots remain boundary-state
data.

Choosing a unique unit as an initial source would be a relational analogue of
a cosmological boundary condition. It may be a legitimate new postulate, but
it is not a theorem of the present conditional law.

### 5.5 Detailed-balance variant

If a future theory supplied an independently physical kernel on process
structures,

\[
 K(y\mid x),
\]

then a reversible stationary law would obey

\[
 \Pi(x)K(y\mid x)=\Pi(y)K(x\mid y),
\]

and hence

\[
 \mathfrak r(y\leftarrow x)
 =\frac{K(y\mid x)}{K(x\mid y)}
\]

on bidirectionally supported edges. Kolmogorov's cycle condition for those
ratios is exactly the integrability condition of Theorem 3. On a finite
irreducible replacement graph, the stationary law is unique.

This is a genuine possible selector, but the kernel must be physically
motivated before its stationary law is examined. Paper 13D contains no kernel
whose states are process complexes. Its interventions alter mechanisms inside
a fixed complex. A response magnitude cannot be silently reinterpreted as
\(K(y\mid x)\), and universal bidirectional laboratory manipulability is not
required by Barandes's ontology.

A one-way structural kernel could still possess a stationary law, but then
local odds do not follow from the displayed reversible ratio and the kernel's
whole stationary problem must be solved. In either case, the kernel is new
physics rather than an algebraic extraction from current response tensors.

## 6. What Barandes's ontology implies

Barandes separates three ingredients:

- a fixed configuration space, which supplies kinematics;
- fixed transition probabilities, which supply nomological dynamics; and
- a standalone probability distribution, which he calls contingent and
  informational or epistemic.

That distinction supports the occurrence-gauge diagnosis. A transition law
does not ordinarily select a standalone distribution. Combining them creates
joint probabilities, but neither factor follows from the other.

There are two honest Barandes-compatible options.

1. **Contingent-state option.** Treat \(\Pi_{\rm phys}\) as cosmological state
   data rather than a further universal law. Then different universes or runs
   may have different process marginals while sharing \(\Gamma_D\).
2. **Enlarged-nomology option.** Put process structure itself into one larger
   fixed configuration space and supply a new indivisible transition law on
   it. That new law may yield structural branch probabilities, but it is new
   physics, not a consequence of the old \(\Gamma_D\).

Barandes's stochastic--quantum correspondence does not change this
conclusion. Hilbert spaces, amplitudes, density matrices, and dilations can
represent stochastic dynamics, but the representation does not determine a
missing standalone distribution. Decoherence likewise depends on the full
state and dynamics; it explains suppression of interference between selected
coarse histories, not why one process complex had a particular prior weight.

### 6.1 The cosmological interpretation fork

Before further construction the programme must choose, explicitly, between
two interpretations of \(\Pi_{\rm phys}\).

- **Objective propensity.** \(\Pi_{\rm phys}\) is part of an enlarged law and
  assigns physical chances to whole process complexes. Paper 17 may then make
  ensemble predictions, but a new selector law is required.
- **Epistemic cosmological state.** \(\Pi_{\rm phys}\) represents uncertainty
  about one actual complex. It may be updated by observations, but dimension
  inferred from it is not a dimension dynamically selected by
  \(\Gamma_D\).

A one-universe ontology does not erase this fork. If probabilities over
complexes are used to claim typical chronology or dimension, their physical
or epistemic status must be stated. If no such probabilities are fundamental,
Paper 17 must analyze the one actual complex from records rather than claim an
autonomous ensemble law.

## 7. Quantum-physics routes

### 7.1 Sum over histories

A Feynman-style law would assign complex amplitudes to complete process
histories and sum them before squaring. This is a possible new law, not a
deduction from \(\Gamma_D\). It requires:

- a sample space and measure;
- an action or local amplitudes;
- all activities and phases;
- a boundary state or contour prescription;
- normalization or a constructive definition; and
- an operational cross-complex interference test.

The accepted matrix \(R\) supplies a real amplitude motif inside one primitive
sector. It does not fix the magnitude or phase between sectors.

### 7.2 Decoherent histories

Closed-system histories formulations make the separation explicit: the
decoherence functional depends on both dynamics and an initial condition.
Decoherence permits probabilities for suitable coarse histories; it does not
choose the initial state or the microscopic action. Records may diagnose
decoherence but do not supply process occurrence weights.

### 7.3 Bootstrap and consistency

Unitarity, positivity, analyticity, crossing, and symmetry can strongly
restrict a quantum law. Modern bootstrap programs usually produce an allowed
region; a unique extremal theory occurs only with additional assumptions.
Before spacetime, analyticity and crossing are not even intrinsic ISP inputs.

The transferable lesson is nevertheless valuable: build the full convex or
character space of admissible selectors first, then ask whether exact
consistency collapses it to an isolated point. Do not choose an extremal point
because its macroscopic output is attractive.

### 7.4 Anomaly cancellation

An anomaly is a genuine selection mechanism because an inconsistent quantum
theory is not an alternative physical theory. Green--Schwarz cancellation,
for example, drastically restricts otherwise possible theories. The ISP
analogue would be an exact failure of groupoid descent, tensor coherence,
normalization, or composition unless activities satisfy specific equations.

This route is promising only if the anomaly is independently demonstrated.
Calling non-manifoldlike output an anomaly would be output targeting.

### 7.5 Euclidean and reflection-positive routes

A positive Gibbs-like process weight could be tested for a reconstruction of
a Hilbert-space quantum theory. But reflection positivity presupposes a
reflection or time-splitting structure not presently derived. Even when it
holds, it constrains an already supplied Euclidean measure; it does not create
that measure from \(\Gamma_D\).

### 7.6 Born-rule and envariance arguments

Born-rule derivations from Hilbert-space symmetry address how probabilities
depend on amplitudes once a quantum state and its tensor factorization are
given. They do not select the state, its cross-complex amplitudes, or the
process action. Applying a Born rule to \(\Pi_{\rm phys}\) would therefore
move the question from missing probabilities to missing amplitudes without
fixing their magnitudes or phases.

### 7.7 Quantum Darwinism and pointer-state stability

Environment-induced selection identifies stable, redundantly recorded pointer
observables relative to a supplied state, interaction, and system--environment
split. It is highly relevant to why some recorded happenings become objective.
It does not determine how frequently the underlying process complexes occur.
Weighting complexes by their redundancy would be a new preference for
record-rich worlds and would violate the output-neutral selector firewall.

### 7.8 Ground-state or least-action selection

The phrases "ground state" and "least action" do not define a law until the
Hamiltonian or action has already been supplied. In a path integral,
stationary-action configurations are approximations to an integral whose
measure, action, couplings, contour, and boundary state are inputs. In the
present theory \(S_{\rm proc}\) is precisely what is missing, so minimizing it
cannot define it.

## 8. Statistical and inferential routes

### 8.1 Maximum entropy and maximum caliber

Given a base law \(q(\chi)\) and frozen expectations \(\bar F_i\), maximizing
relative entropy yields

\[
 \Pi_{\boldsymbol\alpha}(\chi)
 =\frac{q(\chi)}{Z(\boldsymbol\alpha)}
 \exp\!\left[-\sum_i\alpha_iF_i(\chi)\right].
\]

This is a principled inference rule after \(q\), the observables, and their
expectations are supplied. If \(F_i=N_i\) are generator counts, the Lagrange
multipliers are exactly the missing activities. Maximum caliber therefore
organizes empirical information; it does not derive the information.

### 8.2 Exchangeability

Exchangeability removes arbitrary labels. De Finetti- and Aldous--Hoover-type
representation theorems express exchangeable laws as mixtures of latent
parameters or kernels. They classify covariant families but do not select one
mixing measure or kernel. Ergodicity removes mixture uncertainty only after an
extremal law has been chosen; it does not name the extremal law.

### 8.3 Stationarity

If an independently defined transformation \(T_\Gamma\) acted on process
laws, one could seek

\[
 \Pi=T_\Gamma\Pi.
\]

Uniqueness would then require an ergodicity or contraction theorem. The
current \(\Gamma_D\) maps a selected experiment to history probabilities; it
does not map a process law to another process law. Defining \(T_\Gamma\) is
therefore the new physics. Stationarity without a fixed transformation is
empty: for any desired \(\Pi\), one can construct a reset kernel having it as
the stationary law.

### 8.4 Microscopic calibration

Calibration is the strongest honest route if repeated autonomous physical
motifs can be identified before geometry. For independent or proven
exchangeable antichain/star trials, if \(k\) of \(m\) trials contain fusion,
the binary likelihood is

\[
 L(\lambda)\propto
 \left(\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda}\right)^k
 \left(\frac1{1+\lambda}\right)^{m-k},
\]

with interior maximum

\[
 \widehat\lambda=\frac{k}{m-k}.
\]

This estimates a physical activity rather than deriving it. It is admissible
only if the theory proves:

- point-free identification of each motif;
- autonomous generation rather than laboratory selection;
- no pseudoreplication inside one correlated history;
- an ergodic or replication theorem;
- apparatus and context independence; and
- preregistration before dimension or manifold tests.

For one universe, local repetition is not automatically an ensemble. A
homogeneity or ergodic theorem is additional physics.

### 8.5 Bayesian model comparison is not occurrence dynamics

Observed data \(D\) may update beliefs over candidate complexes,

\[
 P(\chi\mid D)\propto P(\chi)\Gamma_D(D\mid\chi).
\]

This can identify which model best explains records. It does not determine
the prior \(P(\chi)\), nor does an observer's posterior become the universe's
autonomous occurrence law.

### 8.6 Typicality

Typicality results state that most states relative to a specified measure on a
specified subspace have some property. They require the subspace, constraint
surface, and typicality measure in advance. A claim that manifoldlike
complexes are "typical" would therefore presuppose the very measure under
investigation.

### 8.7 Algorithmic probability and simplicity

Algorithmic priors assign larger weight to shorter descriptions relative to a
chosen universal machine and coding. Complexity changes by bounded constants
between universal machines, but those constants become finite multiplicative
changes in process odds and are not physically negligible for a fundamental
law. More importantly, no accepted theorem identifies a programming language
with the physical presentation groupoid. Algorithmic simplicity remains an
epistemic prior, not a point-free occurrence law.

### 8.8 Anthropic and observer selection

Conditioning on the existence of records, observers, or a habitable
large-scale geometry may update an observer's posterior over candidate laws.
It cannot provide the unconditioned autonomous law without a prior. It also
targets precisely the macroscopic output the programme is meant to predict.

## 9. Gravity-inspired routes

### 9.1 Einstein's methodological lesson

General covariance alone does not determine Einstein's equation. The strong
uniqueness results associated with general relativity require substantial
physical assumptions: a metric carrier, locality, differential structure,
low derivative order, a conservation identity, dimension, and appropriate
matter coupling. Those assumptions are meaningful only after spacetime is in
place.

The pregeometric analogue is not to import the Einstein--Hilbert action. It is
to state an equally explicit list of intrinsic assumptions and calculate the
remaining character space. The local-odds and cycle-integrability contract is
the correct present-level version of that strategy.

### 9.2 No-boundary and tunneling proposals

Hartle--Hawking and Vilenkin propose different cosmological boundary
conditions for a wave function after a gravitational configuration space and
action have been supplied. Their disagreement is instructive: a constraint
equation does not by itself select one state.

A relational no-boundary analogue might assign a distinguished amplitude or
weight to process complexes with no incoming physical boundary. But it would
still have to specify:

- what counts as no boundary in the typed category;
- the process action or local odds;
- whether disconnected closed components are allowed;
- a contour or positive normalization rule; and
- how the proposal avoids selecting geometry by construction.

It is a candidate boundary postulate, not a deduction from \(\Gamma_D\).

### 9.3 Causal-set sequential growth

Classical sequential growth is the closest structural precedent. Discrete
covariance and a causality condition yield a broad family of dynamics with
free couplings. The construction demonstrates that labels can be gauge while
growth probabilities remain physical. It also demonstrates the present
point: covariance and causality do not usually fix the occurrence law.

Importing sequential growth would add Markov births and a stage parameter.
That conflicts with the whole-law, non-Markovizing objective unless the
sequential description is proved to be only a gauge representation of a
covariant whole-history measure.

### 9.4 Causal-set actions

The Benincasa--Dowker action is built to approximate dimension-specific
continuum curvature. It is valuable as a later held-out effective test, but it
cannot select a dimension-neutral pregeometric \(\Pi_{\rm phys}\) without
putting dimension and geometric locality into the selector.

### 9.5 CDT, spin foams, and group field theory

These programs confirm that a sum over combinatorial structures needs more
than covariance:

- CDT supplies simplicial Lorentzian building blocks, dimension, topology
  restrictions, a Regge action, and a causal setup, then studies a phase
  diagram.
- Spin-foam and group-field theories require amplitudes, measures, field
  content, interactions, and couplings.
- GFT renormalization can reveal fixed points and condensate phases, but the
  microscopic model and coarse-graining scheme are inputs and multiple phases
  can occur.

They inspire techniques for normalization and phase analysis. Their geometric
ingredients cannot be used to choose the ISP law before dimension is tested.

### 9.6 Asymptotic safety

A unique ultraviolet fixed point could constrain a law only after the field
space and renormalization transformation are independently defined. Relevant
directions remain empirical parameters, and truncation or scheme choices must
be controlled. The present theory has no intrinsic coarse-graining map on
process complexes, so “choose the fixed point” is not yet an executable
principle.

### 9.7 Thermal time

The thermal-time hypothesis derives a flow from a state on an observable
algebra. That is potentially relevant after \(\Pi_{\rm phys}\) exists. It
cannot select \(\Pi_{\rm phys}\), because the state that generates the modular
flow is the missing input.

## 10. New selector routes and their exact status

### Route A -- character annihilation by physical relations

## 14. Strongest present conclusion

The present theory does not contain a hidden formula for
\(\Pi_{\rm phys}\). Its conditional normalization actively proves why the
formula cannot be recovered by summing over Paper 13D outcomes. Barandes's
ontology reinforces, rather than removes, this separation: fixed transition
law and standalone state are different ingredients.

The investigation nevertheless identifies a precise next object. Do not guess
an action over all complexes. Construct the point-free local replacement
groupoid and seek physical odds on its primitive edges. Those odds must be
derived by an endogenous structural selector, fixed by exact consistency, or
calibrated as new constants. Reciprocity and cycle integrability then decide
whether they define one global occurrence law; projective and uniqueness
theorems decide whether that law survives without a cutoff.

> There exists a larger indivisible relational experiment in which distinct
> process structures are genuine stable outcome sectors, and its conditional
> branch probabilities descend, independently of implementation, to an
> integrable local occurrence specification.

not yet part of Paper 13D. If it fails, the honest alternative is to introduce
and microscopically calibrate process activities as new physical constants.

## 15. Primary literature and scope

- J. A. Barandes, *Quantum Systems as Indivisible Stochastic Processes*:
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.21192

  The primary ontology is a fixed configuration space with fixed transition
  laws and a contingent standalone distribution. The Hilbert representation
  is secondary. This supports the law/state separation used here.

- J. A. Barandes, *The Stochastic--Quantum Correspondence*:
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.10778

  Supports the existence and nonuniqueness of Hilbert-space representations;
  it does not select a missing autonomous process marginal.

- J. B. Hartle, *The Quantum Mechanics of Closed Systems*:
  https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9210006

  Decoherence of closed-system histories depends on both dynamics and an
  initial condition; it does not remove state selection.

- J. B. Hartle, *Spacetime Quantum Mechanics and the Quantum Mechanics of
  Spacetime*: https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9304006

  Supplies the histories/decoherence-functional comparison without implying a
  unique process action or cosmological state.

- W. H. Zurek, *Probabilities from Entanglement, Born's Rule from Envariance*:
  https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0405161

  Constrains probabilities relative to a supplied entangled quantum state; it
  does not select cross-complex amplitudes or the state itself.

- W. H. Zurek, *Decoherence, Einselection, and the Quantum Origins of the
  Classical*: https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0105127

  Supports the pointer-state and environmental-redundancy comparison while
  retaining state, interaction, and factorization inputs.

- J. B. Hartle and S. W. Hawking, *Wave Function of the Universe*:
  https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.28.2960

  A boundary-state proposal after geometric histories and a Euclidean action
  are supplied, not a derivation of a dimension-neutral process measure.

- A. Vilenkin, *Quantum Creation of Universes*:
  https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.30.509

  A distinct tunneling boundary proposal. The plurality of proposals
  illustrates that a dynamical constraint does not alone select the state.

- R. D. Sorkin, *Quantum Mechanics as Quantum Measure Theory*:
  https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9401003

  Supplies the quantum-measure alternative to classical additivity but not
  cross-complex amplitudes for the present theory.

- F. Dowker, S. Johnston, and S. Surya, *On Extending the Quantum Measure*:
  https://arxiv.org/abs/1007.2725

  Demonstrates that an infinite histories measure may fail to extend even

- D. P. Rideout and R. D. Sorkin, *A Classical Sequential Growth Dynamics for
