## 5. Exact scale and weighting obstructions

### Theorem 1 — conformal-density degeneracy

Suppose a point process has intensity measure

$$
d\Lambda=\rho\,dV_g
$$

in $d$ dimensions. For constant $c>0$, put

$$
g'=c^2g,
\qquad
\rho'=\frac{\rho}{c^d}.
$$

Positive conformal scaling preserves chronological order, while
$dV_{g'}=c^d dV_g$. Therefore

$$
\rho' dV_{g'}=\rho dV_g.
$$

The complete point-process order law is unchanged, although every proper time
is multiplied by $c$. Absolute scale and duration are thus unidentifiable
without an independently fixed intensity or clock calibration.

### Proposition 3 — arbitrary weights can fake a dimension statistic

For positive node weights define a weighted ordering fraction

$$
r_w
=
\frac{
2\sum_{i<j,\ i\prec j}w_iw_j
}{
(\sum_iw_i)^2-\sum_iw_i^2
}.
$$

Take three nodes with only $a\prec b$ and $c$ incomparable to both. If
$w_a=w_b=t,w_c=1$, then

$$
r_w=\frac{t}{t+2}\longrightarrow1.
$$

If $w_a=w_b=1,w_c=t$, then

$$
r_w=\frac1{2t+1}\longrightarrow0.
$$

The order is fixed throughout. Therefore arbitrary positive weights can tune
a weighted relation statistic across almost its whole range. A
$\Gamma$-derived weight may enter a geometric estimator only after a physical
volume/intensity theorem, not merely because the weight is invariant and
additive.

## 6. Finite non-identifiability

At fixed finite sample size, order data need not determine a continuum

states that for every $K$, error tolerance, and Lorentzian-distance target,
there are normalized spatially compact Cauchy slabs whose finite $K$-point

the stated Lorentzian distance. A flat-cylinder version shows the same issue
inside vacuum solutions: sufficiently long normalized flat cylinders produce
the total $K$-chain order with probability approaching one while their
timelike diameters separate without bound.

This is not a universal no-go for continuum recovery. It proves that a finite
fit requires an explicitly restricted target class, regularity and scale
controls, a convergence notion, and multi-size evidence. It also bars the
claim that one finite order uniquely determines a metric.

$$

\sqrt{1+\theta}

1,&\theta=0,\\[0.6em]
