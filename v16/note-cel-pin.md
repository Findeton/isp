## 4. Record permanence

Bare covariance `T^dagger(A) subset A` is not permanence for a general channel.
CEL distinguishes:

- algebra covariance/classicality preservation;
- mathematical zero-error recoverability of the record partition;
- recovery using a branch label that may be lost under coarse-graining;
- recovery by a readout/operation actually licensed in `G`; and
- continuation-stable licensed recoverability under every future word in the
  registered grammar.

For a finite classical flag partition, exact recoverability means that the
output supports of distinct record sectors remain disjoint, possibly after a
licensed relabeling. For a reversible unitary normalizer this reduces to a
permutation of sectors. A reset channel is the mandatory negative control:
its Heisenberg action stays inside the diagonal algebra while it merges both
sector states.

Redundancy is global. Erasing one copy while another survives proves survival
of one registered erasure, not permanence. A positive permanence claim needs
an algebraic all-word certificate for the declared post-write grammar; a
catalogue enlargement that can erase the last copy must demote it.

Actualization is outside this criterion and remains a postulate.
