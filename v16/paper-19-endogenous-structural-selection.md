# Endogenous structural selection from an indivisible relational law

## Why a conditional Gamma does not yet choose the structure of its own world

### Abstract

Paper 13D constructs a point-free indivisible stochastic law
\(\mathbf\Gamma_D\) for complete typed experiments. Paper 18 constructs the
general measurable contract needed to ask whether whole relational process
structures themselves have physical occurrence probabilities, without
assuming that those structures form a discrete set. The remaining question is
whether the accepted law actually supplies such probabilities.

This paper gives a negative but operationally useful answer. Every accepted
Paper 13D experiment contains a fixed execution arrow before its stochastic
law is evaluated. Structural recursion randomizes complete boundary and
history values but never the execution syntax itself. Simultaneous fusion has
one resolved unmarked fusion channel; its fresh cross bonds are values in the
history fiber. Therefore the current \(\mathbf\Gamma_D\) contains no
nontrivial endogenous structural selector.

We formulate and prove an endogenous-selector theorem: a larger indivisible
law would determine structural channel probabilities if one independently
fixed parent experiment had a point-free measurable outcome map onto genuine
child structures, omitted dormant alternatives, recovered every child
conditional law, and glued consistently across complete exteriors. Merely
postulating such a parent is representation, not derivation. Local odds must
also satisfy cocycle, rooted-spectator, normalizability, and exact deletion
conditions. For the current law a positive fusion-activity family survives all
available constraints, so the whole selector is not unique.

One part of Paper 17 does survive this obstruction. Within any fixed
positive-mass process complex, Gamma-relative operational precedence depends
only on the accepted conditional law and is invariant under every compatible
selector. The frequency and global organization of those complexes are not
invariant; an exact one-parameter family changes their ordering statistics.
Thus scoped conditional precedence is selector-robust, while a complete
chronology-valued ensemble and dimension selection remain blocked.

The result assumes neither a lattice nor a discrete event web. It identifies
the smallest missing physical input: a genuine structural transition or
branching law, or an explicitly contingent cosmological state.

\[
 (\operatorname{Proc})_*

 =\delta_{[f_{\widehat e}]}.
\tag{3}
\]

Here \(\operatorname{Proc}_{\widehat e}\) is the constant forgetful map on

not a diagnostic reader extracting hidden program metadata from a naked

#### Proof

Proceed by structural recursion on the free execution syntax.

- An identity history is the one-entry trace at its fixed object.
- An atomic generator history retains the fixed source and target values of
  that generator.
- A composite history glues histories of the two already fixed arrows.
- A tensor history is the component-indexed family of histories of the fixed
  factor arrows.
- A fusion history retains the fixed tensor source and fixed fused target of
  the one chosen fusion arrow.

At every clause, stochastic seeds alter only legal boundary or history values.
No clause samples an alternative syntax tree or generator occurrence set.
Induction proves (3). \(\square\)

## 5. Endogenous structural selection

### Theorem 2 -- endogenous pushforward

Assume that the family \(\mathfrak S_x\) satisfies:

1. point-free covariance;
2. total measurable structural descent;
3. complete channel coverage;
4. structural separability;
5. no dormant alternative machinery;

\[
 \mu_x=(s_x)_*\mathbf\Gamma_{\Sigma_x}

\[
 \widehat\Gamma_x(d\kappa,dH)
 =\mu_x(d\kappa)
  \mathbf\Gamma_D(dH\mid\kappa,x)

The pushforward \(\mu_x\) in this theorem is already a probability kernel. In
Paper 18's measure/character notation it admits the honest representation

#### Proof

Measurability and finiteness of (4) follow by pushforward of the normalized
parent law through the total measurable map. Covariance makes the law

the support the declared physical channel support rather than a reader

#### Proof

## 6. Local odds and measurable cocycles

### 6.1 Finite replacement control

For a fixed complete exterior \(B\), let \(\mathcal R_B\) be a finite graph
whose vertices are genuine structural alternatives and whose oriented edges
are independently physical reversible replacements. Let
\(r(e)>0\) be the proposed odds ratio along an edge.

### Theorem 4 -- finite local-odds reconstruction

There exists a positive weight \(w\) on each connected component satisfying

\[
 \frac{w(t(e))}{w(s(e))}=r(e)
\tag{6}
\]

if and only if

\[
 r(e^{-1})=r(e)^{-1}
\]

and the product of \(r\) around every closed replacement cycle is one. When
it exists, \(w\) is unique up to one positive normalization constant per
connected component.

#### Proof

Necessity follows by telescoping weight ratios around a cycle. For
sufficiency, choose one root in each component and define \(w(v)\) as the
product of edge ratios along any path from the root to \(v\). Unit cycle
products make this path independent. Changing the root value multiplies the
whole component by one constant. \(\square\)

### 6.2 Non-discrete extension

The finite theorem is a control, not a global discreteness assumption. On a
measurable replacement equivalence relation, the analogue of \(r\) is a
positive Radon--Nikodym cocycle

\[
 c(x,z)=c(x,y)c(y,z).
\tag{7}
\]

Equation (7) is necessary local consistency. Existence and uniqueness of a
global quasi-invariant probability measure additionally require a reference
measure or physical kernel, normalizability, and boundary conditions. A
cocycle alone does not select those data.

### 6.3 Current Paper 13D obstruction

Paper 13D supplies interventions on boundary fields inside a fixed execution.
It supplies no executable transition whose source is one process syntax class
and whose target is another. Therefore its possible process diagrams do not
yet form a physical replacement graph. Drawing a graph between syntactically
similar diagrams would be an analyst's move set, not a Gamma-derived

## 7. Spectators, automorphisms, and locality

### 7.1 Rooted locality

Suppose an interior replacement \(u\leftrightarrow v\) is supported inside a
complete exterior with a physically retained root or hole. For a remote
independent spectator \(s\), locality requires

\[
 \frac{w(v\boxtimes s)}{w(u\boxtimes s)}
 =\frac{w(v)}{w(u)}
\tag{8}
\]

relative to the same rooted reference convention.

### Proposition 1 -- unrooted automorphism factors are not local odds

If \(s\) is isomorphic to one of several components, the unrooted
automorphism group can gain a permutation or wreath-product factor. Raw
class-probability ratios can therefore acquire a combinatorial multiplier
even when the rooted local replacement law is unchanged.

Consequently spectator locality must be tested on rooted one-hole
stabilizers or equivalent complete exteriors. It cannot be imposed by
canceling unrooted \(|\operatorname{Aut}|\) factors informally.

#### Proof

In an unrooted disjoint union of \(k\) isomorphic components, permutations of
the components contribute an \(S_k\) automorphism factor. Marking the replaced
hole removes precisely the permutations that exchange it with remote copies.
The remaining stabilizer carries the local symmetry. \(\square\)

## 9. Deletion and the exact selector family

### 9.1 Antichain/star control

For every \(n\ge2\), let:

- \(e_n^\otimes\) be the tensor of \(n\) independent singleton component
  processes; and
- \(e_n^\star\) be the same family followed by one simultaneous n-ary
  fusion.

Both are point-free process classes. Uniform component deletion maps each
family at size \(n\) to the same family at size \(n-1\), after empty
components are removed.

For every \(\lambda>0\), define

\[
 \Pi_\lambda(e_n^\otimes\mid n)=\frac1{1+\lambda},
 \qquad
 \Pi_\lambda(e_n^\star\mid n)=\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda}.
\tag{10}
\]

For a whole normalized control, fix any preregistered probability law
\(q_n\) on \(n\ge1\), common to every \(\lambda\), and put

\[
 \Pi_{\lambda,q}(e_n^\otimes)
 =\frac{q_n}{1+\lambda},
 \qquad
 \Pi_{\lambda,q}(e_n^\star)
 =\frac{q_n\lambda}{1+\lambda},
\tag{11}
\]

with zero mass outside this control support. The common \(q\) is comparison
data, not a derived cosmological size law.

### Theorem 5 -- exact projective nonuniqueness

The family (10) is normalized, point-free, tensor-spectator neutral under the
declared component exterior, and projectively consistent under uniform
component deletion for every \(\lambda>0\). Every member recovers the same
accepted Paper 13D conditional history law on each chosen process complex.
For every fixed \(q\), equation (11) gives distinct normalized whole control
laws with the same size marginal and the same child conditionals.

#### Proof

Normalization is immediate. Presentation covariance follows because only the
unmarked process class is used. Deletion sends tensor to tensor and star to
star, so the two class masses are unchanged between successive sizes. The
conditional history law is fixed separately on each class and does not depend
on its marginal mass. A remote tensor spectator changes neither the presence
of the one fusion occurrence nor the odds in the rooted component exterior.
At the terminal deletion from \(n=2\) to \(n=1\), the tensor and one-component
fusion descriptions have the same unmarked child class; both masses therefore
push forward to the unique size-one class with total probability one.
Summing (11) first over the two classes and then over \(n\) gives
\(\sum_nq_n=1\); its size marginal is \(q\) for every \(\lambda\).
\(\square\)

### Corollary 4 -- projectivity does not select fusion activity

Exact deletion constrains \(\lambda\) to be common across sizes in this

The theorem proves that the solution set of the presently tested formal

### 10.1 Gamma-native branching fusion

**Status:** not realized by the current law.

The route would be compelling if one parent had multiple genuine resolved

### 10.2 Executable structural forward/reverse rates

**Status:** absent.

Paper 13D has reversible or irreversible operations on boundary fields, and

### 10.3 Coherence or anomaly closure

**Status:** insufficient.

Accepted coherence leaves \(N_\Phi\) invariant. Adding an equation that
changes it would identify physically distinct traces and retune the referent.

### 10.4 Groupoid Gibbs/DLR plus deletion

**Status:** a valid family architecture, not a selected law.

\[

### 10.5 Canonical transfer operator

**Status:** absent.

The accepted law defines kernels on histories for fixed arrows, not a
positive operator between arrow classes. Any such operator presently requires
a new physical move set and transition rates. Engineering it to have a
desired eigenmeasure would be circular.

### 10.6 Covariant growth

**Status:** not imported.

Causal-set-style growth can strongly constrain a law but assumes a structural

### 10.7 Quantum structural measure

**Status:** dormant.

Paper 13D contains amplitude-level representations of fixed conditional
processes, but no complete experiment recombines inequivalent execution
structures. Complex structural phases would therefore be empirically idle.
A quantal selector also requires strong positivity and extension to the

### 10.8 Contingent cosmological state

**Status:** admissible but unselected.

One may openly append a normalized projective structural state. It would
close equation (1) as state data, not as a consequence of
\(\mathbf\Gamma_D\).

### 10.9 Structural calibration

**Status:** no current trials.

Calibration requires one physical parent that repeatedly realizes genuine

## 11. The Barandes interpretation

Barandes describes a fixed stochastic transition law on a configuration
space and distinguishes it from a standalone probability distribution. This
supports two coherent extensions.

1. Enlarge the physical configuration space so that relational process
   structure is itself a configuration variable, then construct one new
   indivisible transition law on that enlarged space.
2. Retain the current transition law and supply a contingent structural
   state separately.

The first option is closer to deriving a selector, but a transition law alone
still need not choose its initial or cosmological state. A unique stationary
measure would become persuasive only if the structural transition operator,
ergodic regime, and boundary independence were independently physical.

The accepted Paper 13D law realizes neither extension yet.

## 12. Quantum and gravity lessons

### 12.1 Quantum theory

Quantum combs and process matrices can type higher-order transformations
without assuming one global causal order. Purification can represent mixed
processes as parts of reversible larger processes. These principles constrain
admissible process composition but do not choose the pure structural state or
cosmological boundary condition.

Quantum measure theory permits nonclassical interference among histories, but
extension from finite cylinder events to the full covariant event algebra is
an additional theorem and can fail. Decoherence can make alternatives behave
classically without selecting their prior weights.

### 12.2 Quantum gravity

Causal-set growth, group field theory, spin foams, dynamical triangulations,
and related sums over structures demonstrate serious ways to make structure
dynamical. They normally supply causal order, simplex type, group data, field
rank, action, couplings, or boundary state as inputs. Paper 19 may learn from
their consistency machinery but cannot import those inputs before the
selector has chosen its own structural type.

Cylindrical consistency is essential once a coarse-graining map exists. The
map itself must be selected by the dynamics; choosing it because a fixed point
looks four-dimensional is circular.
