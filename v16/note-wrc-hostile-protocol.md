## The eliminability doctrine under review

A methodological proposal arrived after the Papers 3--7 reports froze. Its
correct core is now a mandatory audit object, not an adopted WRC result:

1. Against an unrestricted adversary, every finite input/output table can be
   compiled into a lookup table or enlarged state. Absolute finite-fixture
   non-eliminability is therefore vacuous.
2. A meaningful irreducibility claim must predeclare a constrained blind
   adversary class, resource bounds, one uniform candidate rule, a family of
   carriers or graphs, and held-out members/interventions.
3. Failure of that declared class licenses only class-relative exclusion.
4. DISC's 10,380-row sweep excludes its registered memoryless walk classes at
   the third tick. DISC explicitly tested no memory-bearing null. Thus it
   establishes memory/record dependence at its scope, not automatically
   dynamic geometry.
5. Paper 20 evolves a state and record on a fixed 27-cell carrier. A record-fed
   transport is not yet a rule that rewrites the relational carrier, computes
   transport from the rewritten graph, and receives reciprocal matter
   backreaction.

A reviewer may improve or refute this doctrine, but may not replace it with
either extreme: “any finite fixture proves ontology” or “no constrained finite
fixture can ever supply evidence.” The latter is also false: finite exact
models can exclude a predeclared bounded class.
