## 1. The question, and why this axis is the one that answers it

The corpus's division event has three actors. Its arena has nine. Its field
has order three. It declares three link directions. Four threes, and until
now no measurement had moved one of them independently of the others.

NDEP moved the actor count and reported that every numeral it tested
travelled with the square root of that count rather than with the count
itself. It also said, in its own voice, why that report could not be the
whole story:

> at every one of them q is the square root of the actor count exactly

So on that axis the field order and the actor count march together, and no
three-point table over those arenas can separate them.

This unit moves the other coordinate. The arena is fixed at every row of
every table below -- nine actors, the field of order three, its four parallel
classes, three declared link directions, twenty-seven cells -- and the only
thing that moves is the number of actors in one division event.

That is the whole instrument, and it is the mirror image of the parent's.
Because the field order stands still while the event size moves, any quantity
that MOVES here is not carried by the field order, and any quantity that
STANDS STILL here is not carried by the event size. The two units together
separate the event size from the field order for the first time in the
corpus.

What this axis cannot do is say which of the standing coordinates carries a
numeral that stands still. At fixed actor count, field order and link count,
the reading in which the parent's numeral does not move is simultaneously the
actor-count reading, the field-order reading and the link-count reading. So
NEEDS-3 in this paper means exactly one thing -- not carried by the event
size -- and names no other carrier. Section 9 states this where the words are
defined and the reading walls scan this text for any sentence that would
claim more.

Two slots are scored, not one, and each gets its own word, per the parent's
engraving:

> A law's STATEMENT is LAW-IN-N when the parent's theorem, as stated, holds at the new arena point

A law's STATEMENT is LAW-IN-A when the parent's theorem, as stated, holds at
every feasible event size, and BREAKS when it is evaluable at one and false
there. A law's NUMERAL is LAW-IN-A when the declared a-only reading
reproduces the measurement at every feasible arity and some feasible arity
separates that reading from the constant one; it is NEEDS-3 when the constant
reading is what survives, so the numeral is not an arity function; it is
BREAKS when neither reads the measurement.

## 9. Law 6 -- the three-actor counting theorem, and the arity at which it has no force

SEC-2 found that a division event spanning a seam can be lawful, at a target
that declares the cross links the event realises, and that it is never free.
Its reason was a counting argument about three actors:

> at most two of which can join the sectors, since a triangle admits no proper two-colouring

so a group that opens no pair inside a sector must double a link the union
already carries, and a doubling is what buys a free item.

The union is rebuilt here from the arrangement alone -- 15 carriers, 54

unit's own delivered census by a route sharing no code with it:

> Of the 288 seam-spanning groups, 216 leave the dictionary alive once the target declares the cross links the event realises

and

> The 72 that stay dead are the groups that also open a pair inside one sector

The theorem's hypothesis generalises exactly, and the general form is what
the last column measures. Every pair of a group is one of three things: a
cross pair, a new pair inside a sector, or a doubling of a pair the union
already carries. The cross pairs are a cut of the complete graph on the
group's actors, so at most the maximum cut of that graph can be cross, and
the rest -- the complement of the maximum cut -- must lie inside one sector.
That complement reads 0, 1, 2 and 4. At three actors it is one, which is the
parent's own sentence; at four and five it is larger, so the theorem holds
there a fortiori.

**At two actors every one of the 36 seam-spanning groups opens no pair inside
a sector and doubles nothing.** The complement of the maximum cut is zero
because a single edge is two-colourable, so a seam-spanning event of two
actors is exactly one cross pair and nothing else. Its whole footprint is the
crossing.

That is the configuration the parent priced at zero free items and stamped a
control, for a reason it stated in terms:

> no three-actor group can produce it

The stamp is exactly right at the parent's arity, where this unit measures
that no group of that size produces it. At two actors every seam-spanning
group produces it. The free-item counts themselves are that unit's
measurement and are not recomputed here; what is measured here is the
configuration its rows are about, and the arity at which a driven event can
realise the row it could only construct by hand. The obstruction that made
gluing unmotivated at three actors is empty at two, and what fills it instead
is a question for the unit that owns the update rule.

## 10. Does anything select three?

The pin asks whether any pre-registered internal principle selects the
event size. The honest answer is that the corpus never registered one. What
it did is make commitments inside constructors, and each of those commitments
is a principle that was never stated. Five of them are named here and each is
evaluated by measurement at every event size the arena admits.

The standard for the word SELECTS is stated before the table, because it is
the one place a reader could be misled. A principle selects the parent's
arity only if it DISCRIMINATES: only if it admits that arity and rejects the
others among the worlds this unit actually built and measured. That the
parent's structure can be recovered from records generated at the parent's
arity would be identifiability, which is a different property and is never
promoted to selection here; nor is a principle credited for being merely
self-consistent at the arity it came from. Every row below is a measurement
at nine candidate event sizes, and the column that matters is the one that
says which other sizes the principle rejects.

| candidate principle | admits | selects the parent arity alone |
|---|---|---|
| cover_at_R_equals_L | 3, 4, 6 | no |
| round_completeness | 1, 3, 9 | yes |
| saturation_at_the_budget | 3, 4, 6 | no |
| saturation_is_maximality | 3 | yes |
| subgroup_order_available | 1, 3, 9 | yes |

**3 of the 5 candidate principles admit the parent's arity and no other
nontrivial one.** None of the five was pre-registered. Two of them -- that every actor divides in every round, and that a block's
size is an order the translation group actually realises -- admit exactly
the divisors of the actor count and exactly the subgroup orders, which at
this arena are the same three numbers. The second is named for what it
measures and no more: it asks whether a subgroup of that order EXISTS, which
is weaker than asking that the events themselves be cosets, and at the
parent's arity the event universe is every triple of actors while only the
lines are cosets. The third principle, that the budget is the maximum
attainable incidence, admits the parent's arity alone.

The licensed form of the answer is a conditional, and the assumptions do the
selecting. IF every round must be a complete partition of the actors into
proper nontrivial coset blocks of the arena's translation group, THEN the
event size is the field order. Each clause of that antecedent is one of the
rows above, measured rather than assumed: completeness admits the divisors,
coset blocks admit the subgroup orders, and properness removes the two
trivial sizes. Drop any one clause and the conclusion goes: without
completeness the two-actor and four-actor worlds are buildable, and the whole
of this paper is the measurement of what they contain; without the coset
clause the four-actor world satisfies everything the parent's covering rule
asks of it. The conditional is the result, and it is conditional on
assumptions no unit of the corpus ever registered.

The mechanism common to the first two is the arena's own subgroup lattice.
The realised orders are one, the field order, and the field order squared;
at a prime field order the actor count has no other divisors either. So
under those added assumptions a = q is what the arena leaves once the
trivial sizes are excluded.

The licensed statement of the answer is therefore narrow, and it is worth
putting plainly. In present ISP the event size remains an INDEPENDENT FREE
DECLARATION: nothing pre-registered anywhere in the corpus selects it, and
nothing measured here does either. It becomes non-independent only under
assumptions this unit names and the corpus never made. The two principles
that fail to select would admit an event of four actors as readily as three,
and the reader is entitled to know that the list of five is this unit's,
assembled after the fact from what constructors quietly assume.

procedure must refuse rather than default, and two at the statement slot.
Every one comes out as forced, and a recipe that short-circuits the procedure

The a-only reading is fixed by rule and not fitted per law. A single datum at

## 12. Scope, and what is not claimed

Two and four actors are built entire: every grouping, the whole actor
lattice, the complete event universe for the floor, the whole symmetric group
at the declared grouping window and the declared permutation window over
every distinct prefix. Five actors is a declared window, and the window's
limit is published rather than hidden: its multi-round corpus would carry
4782969 histories against a declared cap of 100000 and is not reached, so
every five-actor row in this paper comes from the single-round census, the
complete floor search or the union census, and none from a driven history.

The ladder is searched to a declared bound, and every achievable-budget set
above is a set within that bound.

No value at the parent's arity is imported as a result. The parent's numbers
enter as anchors and as targets for this unit's own constructor, and the
SEC-2 free-item counts are that unit's measurement and are not recomputed
here.

This unit measures the transport of six published laws along the event-size
axis. It does not measure whether the event size is selected by anything, and
no sentence here says it is: what section 10 reports is which unstated
constructor commitments would select it if they were declared, and nothing
measured here selects it without the arena. No count anywhere above is converted into a
measure: no distribution over event sizes, over groupings or over histories
is declared anywhere in this unit, and every fraction above is stamped
COUNTING-ONLY.

## 13. The successor register

Six questions are left open and named.

The five-actor multi-round corpus is the obvious one. It is a large but
finite computation and it would close the only arity whose driven rows are
missing.

The saturation decision is now owed on two axes rather than one. NDEP left it
open at non-prime field order; this unit finds the same word ambiguous at
four actors, where the budget and the maximum differ by one. A successor
carrying the grammar in either direction must decide what the word is to
mean, and this unit names both readings and chooses neither.

The idle actor has no theory. At every arity that does not divide the actor
count a round leaves actors that divide in no event, and the corpus's
grammar, its record law and its ladder were all written where that could not
happen. Whether the idle actor is a defect of the transport or an object the
theory should have had is not decided here.

The sharpened floor is attained at every arity measured here and at every
actor count the parent measured. That is two axes of evidence and still not a
theorem.

The obstruction that SEC-2's counting theorem supplies is empty at two
actors, and this unit does not say what replaces it. Whether a two-actor
crossing is a lawful autonomous event requires the price the unit that owns
the update rule computes, not the pair combinatorics this one measures.

And the arena's subgroup lattice is what makes the two selecting principles
indistinguishable here. The registered successor is ARITY AT SIXTEEN ACTORS,
and the registration has to say which notion of subgroup it means, because
the obvious one does not separate anything. The translation group of the
sixteen-actor plane is elementary abelian of rank four, so its subgroup
orders are one, two, four, eight and sixteen -- every divisor of the actor
count. Divisibility and subgroup-order availability therefore still coincide
there. What DOES separate at sixteen actors is the linear reading: the
cosets of subspaces over the arena's own field have sizes one, four and
sixteen only, so an event required to be a linear coset can have size four
while an event required only to divide the actor count can have size two or
eight. The successor is pre-registered in that form and is not attempted
here. Until it is built, the conditional of section 10 is relative to an
arena in which its two clauses cannot be told apart.
