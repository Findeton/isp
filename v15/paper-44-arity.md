## 1. The question, and why this axis is the one that answers it

The corpus's division event has three actors. Its arena has nine. Its field
has order three. It declares three link directions. Four threes, and until
now no measurement had moved one of them independently of the others.

NDEP moved the actor count and reported that every numeral it tested
travelled with the square root of that count rather than with the count
itself. It also said, in its own voice, why that report could not be the
whole story:

> at every one of them q is the square root of the actor count exactly

So on that axis the field order and the actor count march together, and no
three-point table over those arenas can separate them.

This unit moves the other coordinate. The arena is fixed at every row of
every table below -- nine actors, the field of order three, its four parallel
classes, three declared link directions, twenty-seven cells -- and the only
thing that moves is the number of actors in one division event.

That is the whole instrument, and it is the mirror image of the parent's.
Because the field order stands still while the event size moves, any quantity
that MOVES here is not carried by the field order, and any quantity that
STANDS STILL here is not carried by the event size. The two units together
separate the event size from the field order for the first time in the
corpus.

What this axis cannot do is say which of the standing coordinates carries a
numeral that stands still. At fixed actor count, field order and link count,
the reading in which the parent's numeral does not move is simultaneously the
actor-count reading, the field-order reading and the link-count reading; those
three readings coincide here and this axis cannot separate them. So NEEDS-3
in this paper means exactly one thing -- not carried by the event size -- and
names no other carrier. Section 12 states this where the words are defined and
the reading walls scan this text for any sentence that would claim more.

There is a second thing the axis cannot do, and it is worth saying before any
result rather than after. Every row here is taken at one actor count, one
field order and one link count, all three of which the theory leaves free.
Every measurement in this paper is therefore one point of a three-dimensional
free fibre, and no sentence here is licensed as a statement about the ISP
family unless it is a theorem that holds at every arena. Exactly one result
below is of that kind and it is labelled where it appears; everything else is
a measurement at the declared arena.

Two slots are scored, not one, and each gets its own word, per the parent's
engraving:

> A law's STATEMENT is LAW-IN-N when the parent's theorem, as stated, holds at the new arena point

A law's STATEMENT is LAW-IN-A when the parent's theorem, as stated, holds at
every feasible event size, and BREAKS when it is evaluable at one and false
there. A law's NUMERAL is LAW-IN-A when the declared a-only reading
reproduces the measurement at every feasible arity and some feasible arity
separates that reading from the constant one; it is NEEDS-3 when the constant
reading is what survives, so the numeral is not an arity function; it is
BREAKS when neither reads the measurement.

### 2.1 The transport rule, and the object it creates

A round at event size a is a MAXIMAL PACKING of the nine sites by disjoint
blocks of a, with the remainder idle. Where the event size divides the actor
count the remainder is empty and this is exactly the parent's partition, so
the rule is a strict extension of the committed constructor rather than a
different constructor that happens to agree on a count; the run checks that
at the parent's arity the two routes return the same objects and not merely
the same number of them.

Where the event size does not divide the actor count, the round carries
actors that divide in no event at all. That is the arity axis's own new
object, and the committed grammar has no name for it. It follows that the
worlds this unit builds at two, four and five actors are EXTENSIONS of the
committed theory and not readings of it: the packing rule is a declaration
this unit makes, it is carried as its own row of the arena table, and every
per-law word below is scored WITHIN THE DECLARED EXTENSION FAMILY and is
licensed in no wider form. No sentence in this paper says what ISP does at
two actors; what the measurements say is what the extension family does
there. It is the first thing the sweep produces and it is not a technicality:
it is the measured content of the sentence "the round is a partition", which
is a commitment the corpus made in a constructor and never stated as a law.

The scope word is attached on two different grounds and the difference should
be stated rather than smoothed over. Most rows stand on the packing rule: the
grouping census, the corpora, the ladder and the forcing census all consume
maximal packings, and at two, four and five actors those packings leave an
idle remainder. Two rows do not use the packing rule at all -- the
information floor enumerates every subset of the nine actors of the given

that the EVENT SIZE is off the parent's, which is what makes them extension
results too. The scoping is conservative in both cases and never narrower

One predicate of the parent's is not reused as it stands, and the reason
